# Aspect: brief

One of wave 3's four standard templates: the local real estate agency (`business`, not one of the original designs). Its motion is quiet and purposeful: a front door that swings open onto each home as it comes into view, soft reveals.

`aspect` is a working name: in real estate a home's aspect is the way it looks out, its first impression. Searched on Framer, Webflow, Squarespace and the real estate theme sellers: no template product is called Aspect. The fictional agency below has its own name.

## Concept

**Concept.** An independent agency that shows you in. The site is calm and editorial: a warm text serif over a plain Scandinavian grotesk, fog-colored paper, one deep madrona red and a touch of brass. Every home opens the way a viewing does, at its front door: each listing's photo comes into view behind a painted door with its house number, which swings open onto the house. A listing reads like a short feature story written by the broker who walked through it, every card says where the home stands as an agent's window card does, the agency's sales stand in a ledger with their asking and sold prices, the brokers are named people with the neighborhoods they know, and asking what your home is worth is never more than a tap away.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Halvorsen Pike Real Estate**, an independent agency of five brokers in a brick storefront at 214 Tidewater Lane (an invented street) in Fairhaven, the 1890s brick quarter of **Bellingham, Washington**, on Bellingham Bay, ninety minutes north of Seattle. Its brokers walk through every home they list before they write a word about it, and the write-up is theirs, not a template's.
- **The people:** founded in 2011 by **Kari Halvorsen** (managing broker, who grew up in a Sunnyland bungalow and spent eight years at a Seattle brokerage) and **Dana Pike** (broker, a former carpenter who reads a house's bones on the first walk-through). **Mateo Ruiz** (first homes and condos, Spanish and English), **June Takahashi** (the south side and the waterfront) and **Elliot Marsh** (sellers' marketing and photography) joined later.
- **Where:** six neighborhoods mostly, Fairhaven, South Hill, the Lettered Streets, Columbia, Edgemoor and Sunnyland, and the shore of Lake Whatcom when asked.
- **The listings:** five on the market (a 1924 Craftsman bungalow in Columbia, a 1908 foursquare with a bay view on South Hill, a Victorian cottage in the Lettered Streets under contract, a 1962 rambler on the Edgemoor bluff, a Fairhaven townhome) and one sold last month (a Sunnyland cottage, sold over asking in six days). The ledger lists the year's sales.
- **The offer:** a free valuation, with a broker's visit and a written opinion of price within three working days, based on the sales around it; a write-up of each home room by room; open houses on Sundays; a commission agreed in writing before listing.
- **Practical:** the office is open Monday to Friday 9 to 5:30 and Saturday 10 to 3; viewings any day by request, answered within one working day. Licensed in Washington State; a member of the Northwest Multiple Listing Service.

The name was searched: no agency or broker called Halvorsen Pike, in Bellingham or elsewhere. Bellingham is used by no other template.

The same structure suits an independent real estate agency or brokerage, a single broker or a team of two, a rentals and property management office, an agency selling holiday homes or country houses, or an estate agent in Europe or Australia (see What varies for how prices are said). The agent rewrites the listings, the neighborhoods, the brokers, the figures and the legal lines.

## Trends it draws on

These come from fourteen real estate sites opened in November 2026: independent and design-led agencies in the UK, the US and Australia, and the agencies and developers in Awwwards' Real Estate category of 2025 and 2026 (realestateabbey.com, Honorable Mention December 2025; fame-estate.com, Honorable Mention July 2025; realevate.agency, Site of the Day September 2026; lenvalproperties.com, Honorable Mention October 2026, a developer). WebFetch reads a page's text; the fonts, colors and libraries below come from each site's own HTML and stylesheets, read with curl. themodernhouse.com refused the connection (403) and fantasticfrank.com twice (429). Each trend was seen on at least two of the sites.

2. **Stone, sand and clay grounds with one deep, earthy color; the navy of the older prestige look is fading.** inigo.com (sand #ccbba7, clay #d0b0a4, moss #475447 and berry #744e59 on a pale #dfdbdc), realestateabbey.com (#f2eee9 with a slate #6c7e85 and an olive black #424a3a), kayburton.com.au (blush #f4eeed, tan #ccab8d and a brown #683d32), jelliscraig.com.au (sand #f1ece4 and #eae3d7, ink #1d252d, a sage #8ec6b5), fame-estate.com (an oxblood #7a0c07). Your Home Value"). On a listing: inigo.com ("Request viewing" and "Register for similar homes"), aucoot.com ("Book a viewing", with the contact preference).

Their motion: Swiper carousels (aucoot.com, jelliscraig.com.au, kayburton.com.au, realestateabbey.com, fame-estate.com), Barba page transitions (aucoot.com, kayburton.com.au, realestateabbey.com, lenvalproperties.com), Lenis smooth scrolling (fame-estate.com, lenvalproperties.com), loaders counting to 100% (fame-estate.com, lenvalproperties.com). Aspect keeps the smooth scrolling and leaves the rest out.

**What makes it its own:**
- **the open door:** the walk-through of trend 3 begins where a real one does. Each listing's photo arrives behind its front door: a painted panel drawn in CSS from the theme's colors, with two inset panels, a brass knob and the house's number on a brass plate. As the photo comes into view, the door swings open on its left hinge, in perspective, and the house is shown; on the home page's cards the doors open one after another, on a listing's page and in the hero once on load. Each listing chooses its door's paint among three of the theme's colors (madrona, overcast or Salish deep), so a row of cards reads like a street of front doors. With no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode there is no door: the photo is shown plain;
- **the window card:** each listing card carries its state on a band across its top edge, the way an agent's window card does: "New this week", "Open Sunday 1–3", "Under contract", "Sold in 6 days";
- **the sold ledger:** the year's sales in a ruled ledger on Salish deep, each row led by its house number, then its street, its neighborhood, its asking price, its sold price in brass, its days on the market and its broker, under three figures (homes sold, the share of the asking price, the median days).

**What it leaves out:**
- a search bar and filter form over a listings feed (Upstart has none: listings are records), a map embed, a mortgage calculator, a chat bubble, review widgets and portal badges;
- sun, light, shadows and the time of day as an effect (the architecture template Gnomon's), an hour shown as you scroll (Ember's), a line drawing itself (Chalk's), a picture flying from a list into its page (Encore's), anything that scrolls sideways (Reel's), a blueprint or measured-drawing look (Trestle's), folds that open (Seam's pleats), an opening envelope or fanned cards (Kindly's), rings around a center (Beacon's, Trestle's, Mend's);
- carousels that move by themselves, page transitions, loaders and splash screens, a marquee or ribbon (Corner's), italic accent words in headings;
- stock real estate pictures: handshakes, keys held out, sold signs, couples with boxes, drone shots with graphics over them.

## Type

Both families are files of the template, under the SIL Open Font License, from Google Fonts, as variable `woff2` files subset to Latin and Latin Extended (for Norwegian and Spanish names), with their italics, declared in `styles/site.css`.

| Role | Font | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Headings | Petrona | 300 at display sizes and 400 below 2 rem, never bold; the hero at `clamp(2.75rem, 5vw + 1rem, 5.25rem)`, `leading-[1.04]`, `tracking-[-0.015em]`, `text-balance`; section headings `clamp(1.875rem, 2.5vw + 1rem, 3.25rem)`; sentence case; no italic words in headings |
| Addresses, prices, house numbers | Petrona | 400; a listing's address as its title; prices with `lining-nums tabular-nums`, 1.5 rem on a card, 2.5 to 3 rem on a listing's page and in the ledger's sold column; the house number on the door's plate and leading each ledger row |
| Pull quotes | Petrona | 300 italic, 1.75 to 2.5 rem: a listing's opening line and clients' words |
| Body | Albert Sans | 400, 1.0625 rem, `leading-relaxed`, `text-base-content/80`, measure `max-w-[64ch]`; a listing's story at 1.125 rem |
| Facts, labels, buttons | Albert Sans | 500 to 600, 0.8125 rem; the facts row (3 beds · 2 baths · 1,860 sq ft) in 500 with `tabular-nums`; the window card's band and the eyebrows in 600 uppercase at 0.75 rem with `tracking-[0.08em]`; buttons 600 at 1 rem, sentence case |

Petrona is a text serif with a slightly narrow, calligraphic build and a long range of weights: light at display sizes it has the soft, bookish warmth of the Copernicus and Caslon headings above, and its figures sit well in prices and house numbers. Albert Sans is a grotesk drawn after Scandinavian modernist lettering, plain and open, the role Söhne and Diatype play on those sites, and a nod to the Halvorsens' Norwegian Bellingham.

**If a sibling template takes them:** Brygada 1918 (headings) with Instrument Sans (body); or Gelasio (headings) with Karla (body). All four are on Google Fonts under the OFL, and none is used by waves 1 and 2.

## Motion

Quiet, and always about going in. Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode nothing moves and everything is in its place: no door, every photo plain and replaceable, every row of the ledger shown. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too.

- **The open door** (`data-door` on a photo's frame; `scripts/door.js`, Motion):
  - in the part, the frame holds the `<img>` and, after it, a door: a `<span class="door" aria-hidden="true">` with two inset panels, a knob and a plate holding the house number (the record's `number`, rendered by Liquid, so the text stays in the template). Its paint is `data-paint="primary"`, `"secondary"` or `"neutral"`, from the record's `door` field. The stylesheet keeps the door out of sight (`visibility: hidden`): a decoration over a photo the page already shows, so edit mode and a page with no script show the photo plain;
  - in visitor mode, `start` shows each door closed over its photo (`visibility: visible`, `rotateY: 0`), the frame given `perspective: 1400px` and the door `transform-origin: left center`; when the frame comes into view (Motion's `inView`, `amount: 0.45`) the door swings open, `rotateY: [0, -104]`, 1.1 s, `ease: [0.65, 0, 0.35, 1]`, while a shade on its face deepens (`opacity: [0, 0.35]`) as it turns from the viewer; at the end it is hidden again and the photo is whole. Cards in a row open 120 ms apart (`data-door-stagger` on their list). `data-door="load"` (the hero and a listing's lead photo) opens once the page has loaded, after 0.3 s;
  - each door opens once per visit of the page; `stop` hides the doors and takes off the styles it set, and `start` gives every value where it starts from (`rotateY: [0, -104]`), so the preview's second rendering opens them again cleanly;
  - under reduced motion, `start` does nothing: no door at all;
  - on a phone the door is the same, at the card's width; its number plate scales with it.
- **Reveals** (`data-reveal`, `scripts/reveal.js`, Motion's `inView`): a section's main elements rise a little (`y: [16, 0]`, `opacity: [0, 1]`, 0.8 s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`), their children 70 ms apart under `data-reveal="stagger"`, as in the site-scripts skill; the ledger's rows come in this way, top to bottom.
- **The status filter** on the homes page (CSS only): chips for All, For sale, Open this weekend, Under contract and Sold are radio inputs; `:has()` keeps the cards whose `data-status` matches (an open house is `data-open`). "All" is checked by default, so every home shows with no script and in edit mode. The statuses are a fixed set; neighborhoods are browsed on their own page rather than filtered, so adapting never means writing a rule per neighborhood. A card hidden and shown again by the filter keeps its door open.
- **The request, filled in from the link** (`data-prefill`, `scripts/prefill.js`): "Ask Kari for a valuation" leads to `/sell?with=kari-halvorsen`, a neighborhood's "What's my home worth here?" to `/sell?hood=fairhaven`; the script checks the matching option. It writes no text and does not move; with no script nothing is checked in advance.
- **Smooth scrolling** (`scripts/smooth-scroll.js`): Lenis, light (`lerp: 0.12`, `anchors: true`), so the neighborhoods' index glides to each one; anchored sections carry `scroll-margin-top` for the sticky header.
- **The header** is sticky on fog paper at 95% with a hairline under it; it does not change as you scroll.

No carousel that moves by itself, no page transition, no loader, no WebGL. Libraries: Motion and Lenis, pinned on jsDelivr, under MIT. Everything else is CSS.

## Listings, offers and what stays outside

Upstart has no listings feed, no calendar, no payments and no embeds, so:
- **the listings are records the maker keeps,** typed or pasted in the Content tab, with their status changed by hand when a home goes under contract or sells. A brokerage that must show the whole MLS through an IDX search links to its own search page from a "Search all homes" button: a link that leaves the site, never its widget;
- **a viewing and a valuation are requests,** sent through the forms, which a broker answers by phone or email; the pages say so before and after sending;
- **no saved searches or alerts:** "Tell us what you're looking for" sends a wish list a broker answers by hand;
- **no map:** "Get directions" links to the address on a map site; the neighborhoods are described in words and photos;
- **no 3D tour or video embed:** a "Take the 3D tour" link opens the maker's tour page;
- **no mortgage calculator:** prices are given as they are; the questions say to ask a lender;
- **the legal lines are the maker's:** the license, the MLS, Equal Housing Opportunity and the disclaimer are in the footer as built; the guide tells the agent to ask for the real license number and never invent one.

## What varies

The choices the agent asks when the maker's messages leave them open, page type by page type, the recommended one first. The prices, the figures, the commission's rate, the license numbers, the hours and the open houses are facts: the agent asks for them once the page is built, or leaves them out rather than guess.

### Home (`/`), with the choices for the whole website

#### How do owners ask what their home is worth?
- **With the valuation form** (as built): requests arrive in the Content tab; a broker calls back. Every "Free valuation" button leads to `/sell#valuation`.
- **By phone:** every valuation button becomes a call button (`tel:`); the sell page shows the number large with the hours to call in place of the form; `data/valuations/`, its fragment and `scripts/prefill.js` go.
- **On a booking page elsewhere** (a calendar the agency already uses): every valuation button links to it and says "Book a valuation visit"; the form goes, and the sell page keeps its steps, ledger and questions.

#### Do homes open at their front door?
- **Yes, the open door** (as built): each listing's `number` and `door` paint, the doors on the cards, the hero and each listing's lead photo.
- **No, the photos plainly:** for an agency selling land, commercial premises or apartments off plan, where a front door means little: `scripts/door.js`, the door spans and the `door` field go; `number` stays for the ledger; the reveals carry the motion.

#### One broker, or a team?
- **A team** (as built): the brokers page with a page each, the home page's brokers, and the valuation form's "a broker you'd like".
- **One broker:** the texts speak in the first person; the brokers page type becomes one About page (`/about`) with the portrait, the license, the neighborhoods and their words, without record pages; the home page's brokers become a portrait beside a note (adapted from `apps/desktop/gallery/about/about-founder-note`); the broker fields of the forms and listings go.

#### What does the agency do?
- **Sales** (as built).
- **Sales and rentals:** a "For rent" status with a monthly rent in place of the price, a Rent chip on the homes page and a "Rentals" link; the viewing form stays; the ledger keeps to sales.
- **Rentals and property management:** listings are lettings with their rent and availability date; the sell page becomes "Landlords" (`/landlords`), its form a rental appraisal, its ledger the homes let with their days to let; the hero and promises follow.

### Homes (`/homes`, with each home's page)

#### How are prices shown?
- **The asking price** (as built): "Listed at $789,000", and sold prices beside them.
- **A guide price or "offers over"** (as in the UK or Australia): the price line reads "Guide price" or "Offers over" from a `price_label` field; sold prices are shown or not as the next choices say.
- **On request:** no price on cards or pages, "Price on request" in its place, the ledger without prices (days and neighborhoods only).

#### How do buyers see a home?
- **They ask with the form** (as built): the viewing form on each home's page.
- **They call the listing broker:** the form gives way to the broker's card with a large call button; `data/viewings/` and its fragment go.
- **At open houses, then by request:** the open house line leads on each page, the home page's "Open this weekend" moves to the top of the homes page too, and the form stays for other times.

#### Do sold homes stay on the site?
- **Yes, marked sold** (as built): with their sold price and days, a Sold chip, and their row in the ledger links to them.
- **No, they go once sold:** sold records are deleted when the sale closes, the Sold chip goes, and the ledger keeps only the `sales` rows, without links.

When removed: the homes page type is the template's core; without it the site is a broker's own page. Delete `pages/homes/`, `data/listings/`, `data/viewings/`, `data/home-searches/` and their fragments and navigation record, and `scripts/door.js`; the home page's "On the market" and "Open this weekend" sections go, the hero's photo and caption become a plain photo, and the brokers' pages lose their homes.

### Sell (`/sell`)

#### Do you publish your results?
- **The ledger and the figures** (as built).
- **The figures only:** the ledger goes from the home and sell pages; `sales` goes, `sales-figures` stays.
- **Neither:** sellers' words take their place on the home page; `sales` and `sales-figures` go.

#### Do you say what you charge?
- **Not on the page** (as built): the question "What is your commission?" answers that it is agreed in writing at the valuation.

## When adapting

What the guide will say beyond the choices:
- **Fair housing:** the neighborhoods, the listings and the notes describe homes, streets and distances, never the people who live there or who a place "suits" (families, young professionals, a church or school community): in the United States that is steering, and the same caution holds elsewhere. The agent rewrites the maker's words that way and says why;
- **the doors follow the listings:** each new listing gets its house number and one of the three paints, varied along a row so no two neighbors match; a listing with no number (a lot, a building without one) shows its plate empty;
- **the legal lines:** the license and MLS lines, Equal Housing Opportunity and the disclaimer follow the maker's country; the agent asks for the real numbers;
- **the clients' words and the ledger are invented** and never stay: the agent asks for real ones, or removes them as the choices above say.
