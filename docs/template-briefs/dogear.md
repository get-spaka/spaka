# Dogear: brief

One of wave 5's templates: the independent bookshop with a café corner (`business`). Its motion is gentle and talkative: the week's staff picks laid out as a mosaic of covers, and from behind each cover the bookseller's card rising, with their name and the one line they would say to you at the till. The rest is a neighborhood shop's site, kept useful: a book held or ordered by asking, readings you RSVP to, book clubs you join, gift cards you ask for, and the hours of the counter.

`dogear` is a working name: the folded corner of a page a reader turns down to keep their place, and the folded corner of the booksellers' cards here. A web search found no website template or theme called Dogear or Dog-ear on Framer, Webflow, ThemeForest, Squarespace or Shopify. The nearest are a developer's portfolio concept on Contra, "Dog-Ear Books", built on Shopify's Horizon theme (not sold), and real shops called Dog Eared Books (San Francisco). The bookshop templates on sale go by other names (Framer's Endpaper, Spine Books, Bookish, Buckramwell and The Reading Room; Webflow's Pages, Edition and Readster; Shopify's Publisher and Sitar). The name is the template's, not the business's.

## Concept

**Concept.** A bookshop's site that reads like its front table on a Tuesday, when the new books come in. This week's staff picks lie side by side in a mosaic, at their true sizes: a tall hardcover, a pocket paperback, a wide picture book, a heavy cookbook. From behind each one rises the card a bookseller tucked into it ("Three sisters, one leaking cottage, one terrible summer. I missed my bus stop twice." Nadia), as shelf-talkers do in every good shop and on no shop's website. The covers are drawn by the template from the theme: typographic jackets in ochre book cloth, ink, greyboard and a lavender endpaper, a few with a relief print. The site has a book face revived from a printing museum's matrices over an American gothic, and an uncoated paper white. Everything else is the shop at work: hold a copy or order anything in print by asking, RSVP to a reading, join a club, ask for a gift card, and find the soda counter where the coffee is.

It must not read as Seam, Kindly, Pail or Margin. Seam is a label's cutting table, with manila hang tags on strings, pinked swatches and pleated photos. Kindly is a wedding invitation of envelopes, seals and die-cut arches. Pail is a shop of loose stems priced on enamel plates. Margin is one writer's letter in one green ink. Dogear is a shop of books, each with a person's word on it.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Half Title Books & Coffee** ("Half Title" for short: the half-title is the page at the front of a book that carries its title alone). It is at 58 Gilder Street (an invented street), Providence, Rhode Island 02909, in the Armory District of the West End, a block from the Cranston Street Armory.
- **The place:** a 1912 corner pharmacy with a pressed-tin ceiling. Its oak apothecary drawers now hold the poetry and the postcards, and its marble soda counter, six stools long, is the café corner. The middle shelves are on casters: rolled aside, they make room for 45 chairs on reading nights. About 12,000 books, new, with a shelf of signed copies.
- **The people:**
  - **Nadia Fontes** (she/her) opened the shop in 2019. She grew up in a Cape Verdean family in East Providence and spent eleven years as a publisher's sales rep driving to New England's bookshops. She reads fiction in translation and essays, and leads the translation club.
  - **Owen Pratt** (he/him) runs the events. He taught high school English in Pawtucket for fifteen years. Big novels and history; he leads The Long Read.
  - **Maritza Peña** (she/her) keeps the children's corner and the café. Picture books, young adult and cookbooks; she leads the young readers' club on Saturdays.
  - **Sam Oduya** (they/them) works weekends and the crime and science fiction shelves, and leads First Tuesdays.
  - **Walt Medeiros** (he/him), 71, walked the West End as a letter carrier for thirty years and now works Tuesday and Thursday mornings. Poetry, maps and local history.
- **The week:** Monday to Saturday 10 am to 8 pm, Sunday 11 am to 6 pm; the café corner opens at 8 am Monday to Friday. The front table changes on Tuesdays, when publishers release new books. Readings are most Thursdays at 7 pm.
- **What it offers:**
  - **Holds and special orders:** a copy on the shelf held the same day; anything in print ordered from the distributor, usually in three to five days. The shop calls or texts when it is in and keeps it behind the counter for ten days. It is paid at pickup, with no deposit, or mailed anywhere in the US for $5.
  - **Readings:** free in the shop, with an RSVP so the booksellers know how many chairs to unfold. Bigger nights are at the Dexter Street Grange, a hall two blocks up (an invented venue), for $32 with a copy of the book or $8 without, paid at the door. A signed copy can be held for anyone who cannot come.
  - **Book clubs:** four, free, in the café corner, with 15% off the club's book:
    - First Tuesdays, new fiction, first Tuesday at 7 pm;
    - In Other Words, fiction in translation, third Thursday;
    - The Long Read, one very long book over three months, Sunday afternoons;
    - Young readers, ages 8 to 11, every other Saturday at 10 am.
  - **Gift cards:** any amount from $10, asked for on the site and paid by phone, ready at the counter the same day or mailed free. They never expire and are good for books, coffee, club books and event tickets.
  - **The café corner:** drip coffee $2.75, espresso drinks $3.50 to $5, chai, Portuguese sweet bread from a bakery two doors down, and malassadas on Saturdays.

The name was searched: no bookshop called Half Title came up in Providence or elsewhere. The city's real independent bookshops are Books on the Square and Paper Nautilus (Wayland Square), Twenty Stories and a newer shop (Fox Point), Heartleaf Books (Federal Hill), Symposium Books and Stages of Freedom (downtown), Riffraff (a bookstore and bar in Olneyville) and Small Volume Books. None is in the Armory District, and none is a pharmacy with a soda counter. No template, of the earlier waves or of this one, is set in Providence; the phone in the texts is (401) 555-0163, a number reserved for fiction. The books, their authors and the venue are invented: a search found none of the titles below.

The same structure suits a neighborhood bookshop, a bookshop café, a children's bookshop, a used and rare bookshop (the picks become the week's finds), a comic shop, a record shop (whose staff picks carry the same cards), or a library's friends' shop. The agent rewrites the books and their cards, the booksellers, the readings, the clubs, the café's menu and the town.

## Trends it draws on

These come from twenty-one bookshop, publisher and book club sites opened in November 2026: twelve in a first study (Land-book refused automated reading and McNally Jackson answered 403), and nine more read for this brief with WebFetch, their fonts and colors taken from their own HTML with curl. Several US shops on IndieCommerce (Parnassus, Avid, Greenlight, Skylight, Literati, Porter Square) sit behind a bot check and could not be read. Each trend was seen on at least two sites.

1. **Picks signed by a named bookseller, the name and nothing more.** typebooks.ca ("Jo's Pick", "Philippa's Pick", "Claire's Pick" on the corner of covers); pagesofhackney.co.uk ("BETH RECOMMENDS", "SEB RECOMMENDS" above titles, and a page per bookseller); thirdplacebooks.com (a "Staff Pick" badge, and a staff picks page Book Riot singles out); mrbsemporium.com ("Team B's Top 12s – Laura K, Sue, and Rosa", "Mr B's Bookseller's Dozen"); lighthousebookshop.com (booksellers photographed holding reading lists); stoutbooks.com ("Last copy" tags, gift guides in the shop's voice).
2. **The book as an object in light and shadow, at its true proportions, not a flat thumbnail.** stripe.press (a 3D book turning to its spine); aardvarkbookclub.com (WebGL books); fitzcarraldoeditions.com (single books in hard window light); counter-print.co.uk (a hand holding a book, a row of spines); mendo.nl (a book on a flat block of pale blue, #92b8c0); bookandsons.com (window shadow on grey).
3. **The edit, then dense rows of covers with small type, no cards, no stars.** The first screen given to the week's or the season's selection: yvon-lambert.com ("Objets trouvés"), stoutbooks.com (Holiday 2026 gift guides), doyoureadme.de, shakespeareandcompany.com (Books of the Year). Then rows by shelf: pagesofhackney.co.uk (On Our Tables, Latest Arrivals, Pre-Orders), dauntbooks.co.uk (five rows of four or five covers), therippedbodice.com (New Releases, Brand New to Romance), unitybooks.co.nz (What's New, Top Sellers), counter-print.co.uk and doyoureadme.de (cover grids with tiny metadata).
4. **"Ask us": special orders and recommendations by request, the shop's knowledge as a service.** typebooks.ca ("Looking For Something Specific?" and "Submit a special order"); mrbsemporium.com (two forms, "A recommendation" or "Something specific", answered by "our book experts"); dauntbooks.co.uk ("a curated selection of the titles available in our shops", a phone and an email for the rest); pagesofhackney.co.uk (each book's stock line, "In stock" or "Usually Available Next Day", and collection in the shop); toppingbooks.co.uk ("Coffee with a Bookseller").
5. **Readings as dated cards with their own way in, and clubs and subscriptions as a door of their own.** thirdplacebooks.com (each event's date, time and store, with "RSVP" or "BUY TICKETS"); dauntbooks.co.uk ("Talks & Events" cards, one "Sold Out"); lighthousebookshop.com (dates as dd.mm.yy, "View / Book", a bookclub session among the talks); mrbsemporium.com (event cards with an author's photo; reading subscriptions); toppingbooks.co.uk (monthly signed-first-edition clubs, by genre); pagesofhackney.co.uk ("Book Group" in its top bar); typebooks.ca (subscriptions).
6. **A literary text face with a plain or mono companion, and the shop shown as a place.** The type: doyoureadme.de (Playfair with Roboto Mono); stripe.press (a serif with a mono "[ CART 0 ]"); libreria.io (Courier Sans throughout); mrbsemporium.com (Vollkorn with Open Sans); shakespeareandcompany.com (EB Garamond); dauntbooks.co.uk (Cardo with Gill Sans Nova). The place: stoutbooks.com (its brick façade drawn with lit windows); libreria.io (its shopfront at night, the address, hours and a ticking clock in the corners); typebooks.ca (a full-bleed interior, three branches with their hours); unitybooks.co.nz (each store's hours in its own block).

The gap is the same everywhere: the sites name their booksellers ("Jo's Pick", "BETH RECOMMENDS") and never print what the bookseller would say. The handwritten shelf-talker that sells a book in the shop stays in the shop. Daunt's notes are unsigned Instagram captions, and Mr B's asks you to fill in a form to get one. Dogear makes trend 1 its signature, and the reason for its mosaic: every pick carries its card and its line, signed, rising from behind the cover. Trend 2 becomes covers drawn as objects, with a hinge, a shadow and their true proportions. Trend 4 becomes "Hold a copy" on every book and one order form. Trend 5 becomes readings that know which one is tonight.

Common and left outside: hero carousels with arrows and dots (thirdplacebooks.com, therippedbodice.com, typebooks.ca, unitybooks.co.nz); store cards with "Add to basket" and stock badges on every cover (pagesofhackney.co.uk, lighthousebookshop.com); an Instagram feed (dauntbooks.co.uk); the password-reset banners and genre mega-menus of IndieCommerce shops; preloaders, WebGL books and scroll-driven 3D carousels (aardvarkbookclub.com, stripe.press); the dark academia of burgundy, gold foil and parchment; cozy cream with terracotta under a serif.

**What makes it its own:**
- **the front table:** the hero's mosaic of this week's picks, each tile a panel of greyboard, lavender or ochre with its book standing in it at its own format: hardcover, paperback, pocket, picture book, large. One tile is a photo of the soda counter. The covers are the template's own drawing, never a photo of a real book's cover. They are typographic jackets in five layouts and six tones taken from the theme, with a hinge groove and a contact shadow, their titles and authors live text. Five of them carry a generated relief print. A maker who has photos of the real covers puts them in a field, and the photo takes the jacket's place;
- **the shelf-talkers:** each pick's card, a plain white card with an ochre printed band ("Nadia's pick") and its top corner dog-eared, tucked behind the book. It rises as the picks come into view, and further on hover or focus, showing the line in the bookseller's italic. In the hero only the bands peek above the covers, like cards poking out of books, and the line rises to be read. On the front table section and the Books page the cards stand full. It is a card that rises straight up and stays tucked in: no string, no hole, no tilt, no manila (Seam's tags), no die-cut (Kindly's cards), and nothing sliding out sideways (Encore's sleeve, Spindle's ticket rail);
- **hold a copy:** every book's "Hold a copy" opens the order form with its title and author already written (`/order?title=…&author=…&kind=hold`), and "Order a book" opens it empty. One form for a hold, an order or a signed copy kept for you, with the answer the shop gives at the till: "We'll call when it's in";
- **tonight:** the readings list knows the shop's date. The evening's reading says "Tonight" in a ribbon-red tag, and past readings leave the list on their own, so a list not updated since last month never invites anyone to an evening gone by;
- **the gift card:** drawn as a card of ochre cloth with a pasted paper label, a bookplate, whose amount, "For" and "From" fill in as the visitor types in the request form.

**What it leaves out:**
- online checkout, a cart, payments, stock levels, a calendar widget, an Eventbrite or ticketing embed, an audiobook store embed, an Instagram feed, review stars, a newsletter pop-up, a hero carousel, a genre mega-menu, video and sound;
- photos of real books' covers shipped with the template, and covers made by a model with lettering in them: the titles are always typed;
- the bookshop's clichés: walls of wooden shelves and library ladders, an open-book icon, coffee and an open book seen from above, pages folded into a heart, reading glasses on a book, an armchair under fairy lights, burgundy and gold foil, "Get lost in a good book", a page that curls or turns as you hover, books flying in;
- **other templates' devices:** hang tags, manila, pinking shears and pleats (Seam); envelopes, wax seals, die-cut arches and scallops, a fan of cards (Kindly); a disc sliding out of its sleeve (Encore); stems lowered and lifted, enamel plates, an order-by line (Pail); pictures developing from one ink (Margin); a ribbon or ticker, a turning stamp, arch frames, an "Open now" pill (Corner); a door card turning open or closed, plates set down (Saucer); a pole turning while open, a week chart (Taper); lines that draw themselves (Chalk, Seam, Trestle); a phrase struck through (Codicil); a door swinging open (Aspect); anything scrolling sideways (Reel); a light or a picture following the pointer (Ember, Folio); a huge wordmark across the footer (Corner, Ember, Seam); an italic accent word in a heading (Corner, Folio, Linen); tilted cards (Ember);
- **the wave's other devices:** kitchen tickets slid onto a rail (Spindle: the cards here rise, never slide); a cloth lowered on its batten (Batten); mats unrolling (Bolster); a calendar lit by hovering (Ruled); a paper rota sorting itself (Covered, as the wave sheet names it);
- smooth scrolling, page transitions, a loader: the browser's own scroll, for someone checking on a phone whether the book they ordered has come in.

## Type

Both are files of the template, as `woff2` subset to Latin and Latin Extended (for names such as Fontes, Peña and Oduya, and translated authors), under the SIL Open Font License, from Google Fonts, declared in `styles/site.css`. Each was verified on Google Fonts: **Brygada 1918** ships as two variable files, roman and italic, on a weight axis from 400 to 700, with small caps (`smcp`, `c2sc`), old-style and lining figures, and tabular figures (`onum`, `lnum`, `tnum`). **Libre Franklin** ships as two variable files, roman and italic, from 100 to 900. Its figures are proportional, and its Google Fonts build has no tabular figures, so columns of times, dates and prices are set in Brygada.

Brygada 1918 is a book face: a type cast in Poland in the 1920s, revived in 2018 from a 1954 type-foundry catalogue and from matrices found at the Book Arts Museum in Łódź. It is sturdy, slightly irregular and warm, with a true italic that reads as handwriting without being a script. That italic is the booksellers' voice on every card. Libre Franklin is Morris Fuller Benton's 1912 Franklin Gothic: the American gothic of newspapers, shop signs and library labels, plain at small sizes and solid in caps on the cards' bands.

Ruled out:
- every font the 28 templates ship (the inventory's list) and every font of this wave's sheet: League Gothic, Spectral, Geologica, Golos Text, Besley, Source Sans 3, Victor Mono, Shrikhand, Lexend, Ultra, Karla, Cutive Mono, Gelasio, Mulish, Imbue, Afacad, Castoro, Reddit Sans and Reddit Mono;
- the serifs the bookshops use most (EB Garamond, Cardo, Caslon, Playfair, Vollkorn), which read as any literary site;
- Courier (libreria.io);
- the condensed caps of trend 6 (stoutbooks.com, typebooks.ca), which would read as Corner's, Marquee's and Reel's posters;
- a handwriting font for the cards. Caveat is Commons's, scripts are the trade's cliché, and a handwritten line is hard to read at 15 px. Brygada's italic carries the voice.

**If a sibling takes them,** two pairings keep the same voice (a book face with a true italic for the cards, over a plain sans):
- **Faustina** (Omnibus-Type's face for long reading, variable 300 to 800, with its italic) with **Red Hat Text** (MCKL, variable 300 to 700, with italics);
- **Gentium Book Plus** (SIL's book face, 400 and 700 with their italics) with **Gantari** (Lafontype, variable 100 to 900, with italics).

All four are on Google Fonts under the OFL, with Latin Extended. They are used by no template, by no row of the wave sheet, and by none of the alternatives the other wave-5 briefs name (Alegreya, Piazzolla and Work Sans are among those, so they are left to them).

## Palette

Uncoated paper, greyboard, press ink, ochre book cloth, a lavender endpaper and a ribbon red. Every pair of text and background passes WCAG AA, measured from these values (oklch converted to sRGB, WCAG relative luminance):
- ink on paper 15.4:1, on greyboard 13.0:1, on endpaper 11.7:1, on ochre 6.2:1;
- `text-base-content/80` on paper 8.3:1, on greyboard 7.4:1, on endpaper 6.9:1; `/70` on paper 5.9:1, on greyboard 5.5:1, on endpaper 5.2:1; on ochre only full ink or `/85` (4.9:1);
- light text on the ink bands 14.1:1, `/70` there 7.6:1; ochre on the ink bands 5.8:1 (links and the wordmark in the footer); endpaper on the ink bands 11.0:1;
- ribbon red on paper 5.3:1; paper on ribbon 5.3:1 (the "Tonight" tag);
- on the jackets: ink on the ochre, paper, endpaper and board tones as above; paper on umber 5.8:1; ochre on the ink tone 6.2:1 (a title stamped in ochre on ink cloth).

Ochre on paper is 2.5:1 and on greyboard 2.1:1: ochre is **never text on a light ground**, only a fill (buttons with ink text, the cards' bands, the jackets, the order band, the date blocks) and the 2 px underline of links. Ribbon red is 4.0:1 on endpaper, 4.4:1 on greyboard and 2.8:1 on ink: it is text on paper only, and elsewhere a fill under paper text. Fields are outlined in `base-content/55` (3.7:1 on paper), above the 3:1 a field's edge needs.

**The jackets' six tones** are not theme variables. They are made from the theme in `styles/site.css` (`data-tone`), so the Theme tab recolors every cover with the rest:
- ochre: the primary;
- umber: the primary mixed with the text color, `color-mix(in oklch, var(--color-primary), var(--color-base-content) 45%)`, with light type;
- ink: the neutral, with light or ochre type;
- paper: the surface, with a hairline edge;
- endpaper: the secondary;
- board: the surface alt.

Cloth jackets have a fine weave, a `repeating-linear-gradient` of the tone and a tenth of the text color at two angles. The hinge is a 1 px light line and a soft dark one 6% from the spine. Under each book is a contact shadow in `oklch(from var(--color-base-content) l c h / 0.18)`. **The prints** are black on white images blended into their jacket (`mix-blend-mode: multiply` on the light tones; on ink and umber, `filter: invert(1)` with `screen`), so they take the jacket's color whatever the theme.

Other settings:
- buttons and fields have the box's radius (`--theme-radius-field: 0.25rem`): the main button ochre with ink text, the other outlined in ink;
- relief off (`--theme-depth: 0`);
- density spacious (`--theme-size: 0.28125rem`);
- fine borders (1 px);
- links in text are ink with a 2 px ochre underline offset by 3 px, the underline thickening to the full line's height in ochre on hover (a cloth tape, not a color change);
- the status colors are derived, as always; the forms' errors use them.

It is not Saucer's marmalade with glass blue, Decal's truck yellow with guava, Corner's warm paper with butter, nor Seam's calico with manila. Its endpaper is lavender, not Bolster's chalk pink. It is a cool, almost neutral paper with a blue-black ink, and one deep ochre: the cloth of a hardback, with a lavender endpaper for the inside and a red ribbon kept for what is tonight.

## Motion

Gentle, by a neighborhood shop's character: the cards rise, and little else moves. Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode nothing moves: Spaka's stillness rule stops CSS animations and transitions and no script runs. So every card stands at its rest height (the hero's bands peeking, the others full), every reading is listed with no "Tonight", the gift card shows its sample label, and nothing is prefilled. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too.

- **The jackets** (`data-jacket`, `data-tone`, `data-format` on a book; CSS only):
  - five layouts, `band` (a band across the middle with the title and author), `frame` (an inset rule, the title above and the author below), `type` (the title alone, huge, breaking over lines, flush left), `cloth` (woven cloth with a pasted paper label) and `art` (the print over the top two thirds, the type below);
  - five formats by their true proportions and sizes: `hardcover` (2:3, the full width of its tile's book area), `paperback` (0.65, 88%), `pocket` (0.6, 70%), `picture` (5:4, landscape) and `large` (0.8);
  - with an `image`, the photo of the real cover replaces the drawing (`object-cover` at the format's proportion), keeping the hinge and the shadow. Nothing in a jacket moves.
- **The shelf-talkers** (`data-picks` on a mosaic or a row, `data-pick` on each book, `data-talker` on its card; CSS, and `scripts/talkers.js` with Motion's `inView` and `animate`):
  - in the markup, a pick is a `<figure>`: the jacket, a link to the book's page, then its card as the `<figcaption>`, so a screen reader reads "Low Tide at Conimicut, by Mara Ellison", then "Nadia's pick: Three sisters, one leaking cottage, one terrible summer." The card sits in the same grid cell, behind the jacket (`col-start-1 row-start-1`, the jacket `relative z-10`), 82% of its width and set 8% to the right, as a card tucked in by hand. It is lifted to its rest height by CSS `translate` (`--rise`), and the figure reserves that height above the book;
  - two rest heights: `data-talker="full"` shows the whole card above the book (the band, the line on its two faint rules, the dog-ear); `data-talker="peek"`, in the hero, shows only the band, 1.75 rem, under `(hover: hover)`, and the whole card on a touch screen, where nothing can hover;
  - on hover or focus within a pick (`:hover` under `(hover: hover)`, `:focus-within` always), a peeking card rises to full and a full card lifts 0.375 rem, with a transition of 0.3 s, `ease-out`, under no-preference; under reduced motion it is simply raised. The jacket never moves;
  - as a `data-picks` comes into view (the hero's on load, after 0.25 s), the script lowers each card behind its jacket (`y` its rest height, set in `start`, just before it animates), then raises it to rest: `y: [rise, 0]`, 0.6 s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`, 90 ms apart, in reading order. Motion's `y` is a `transform` that adds to the CSS `translate`, so the rest height stays the stylesheet's. With no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode, every card is at rest;
  - `stop` takes off the styles it set and the `inView` watchers.
- **Tonight** (`data-upcoming` on a list of readings, `data-event` on each with `data-date` and `data-starts`; `scripts/upcoming.js`, no library). This is the one script that reads the clock, and it chooses among the template's words:
  - the shop's time zone comes from `data-tz` on the list ("America/New_York"; without it, the visitor's own clock);
  - it sets `data-when` on each reading: `past` once its day is over, `tonight` on its day when it starts at 5 pm or later, `today` on its day before that;
  - the stylesheet does the rest:
    - each row holds a "Tonight" and a "Today" tag, written in the template and hidden until its state matches (`[data-when="tonight"] [data-tag="tonight"]`), set in paper on ribbon red;
    - past rows are not shown in a list marked `data-upcoming` (`display: none`);
    - a list given a count (`data-upcoming="3"` on the home page) shows only its first three readings still to come (`:nth-child(n + 4 of :not([data-when="past"]))`), so the home page always lists the next three;
  - with no script there is no `data-when`: every reading shows by date, the first three on the home page;
  - it checks again every ten minutes while the page is open, and `stop` clears the timer and removes every `data-when`.
- **The gift card** (`data-gift-card`, `scripts/gift-card.js`, no library): the drawn card holds slots (`data-slot="amount"`, `"for"`, `"from"`) with their sample words ("$50", "Ana", "Lou"). As the visitor types in the request form beside it, the script copies the amount chosen and the two names into the slots: the visitor's own words, never the site's. Nothing moves. `stop` puts the sample words back and removes its listeners.
- **The order form, prefilled** (`scripts/prefill.js`, no library):
  - on `/order` it reads `?title=`, `?author=` and `?kind=` (hold, order, signed) into the form;
  - on `/clubs` it reads `?club=` to choose the club;
  - on `/gift-cards` it reads `?amount=`;
  - it writes only into fields, moves nothing, and with no script the form is simply empty.
- **Reveals** (`data-reveal`, `scripts/reveal.js`, Motion's `inView`): a section's main elements rise a little (`y: [16, 0]`, `opacity: [0, 1]`, 0.7 s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`), children 70 ms apart under `data-reveal="stagger"`, as in the site-scripts skill. They are never used on the picks, whose cards are their reveal.
- **The header** is sticky on paper at 95% with a hairline under it, and does not change as you scroll.

No smooth scrolling, no page transitions, no loader, no WebGL. Library: Motion, pinned on jsDelivr (`motion@14.9.0`), under MIT; everything else is CSS. Because "Tonight" follows the clock, `template.json` sets `"shoot": { "clock": "2027-03-11T11:00:00-05:00" }`: a Thursday morning in March, the day of Mara Ellison's launch, which then reads "Tonight" at the top of the readings. The generated prints make it need `"app": "0.2.0"`.

## Buying, booking and what stays outside

Spaka has no checkout, no payments, no calendar and no embeds, so:
- **a book is asked for, then paid at the till** (as built): a hold or an order is a request; the shop calls or texts when it's in. A shop with an online store (IndieCommerce, Bookshop.org, Shopify) puts its address in each book's `order_link`, and the book shows "Buy online" beside "Hold a copy" (see What varies). It is never embedded;
- **readings are an RSVP,** to count chairs; ticketed nights are paid at the door. A shop that sells tickets elsewhere (Eventbrite, Crowdcast) links each reading's `booking` there;
- **gift cards are asked for and paid by phone,** or linked to the shop's own gift card page;
- **the covers:** the template ships no photo of a real book's cover. The guide tells the agent never to download one. Covers come from the maker, photographed in the shop or taken from the publishers' catalogs for booksellers; until then the drawn jacket stands, with the real title and author typed on it;
- **the time zone:** "Tonight" follows the shop's clock through `data-tz`, which the guide says to set from the maker's city;
- **no map** ("Get directions" links to the address on a map site) and **no Instagram feed** (a link in the footer).

A real book's front cover is someone's artwork: photos are chosen where no cover is legible and spines are small, at a distance.

**Generated** (`pnpm template:image generate`, Q452), because no free image does what they do: the `art` of five jackets (Owl Has a Question, The Weight of Small Birds, Tuesday Soup, one new arrival's moth, and a folded map). They are one style throughout: a one-color relief print, black ink on white paper with the grain and the uneven ink of a linocut, a single motif centered with room around it. There is no letter, number, person, face or logo in any of them, and they are made at 4:5 and 5:4 with the earlier prints given as `--ref`. The print is black on white so that the jacket's CSS blend gives it the jacket's tone under any theme; the jacket's title is always typed. Any print that comes back with letters, or that reads as a glossy model-made cover, is generated again. This makes the template need `"app": "0.2.0"`.

To read as one set, the photos are chosen in daylight, a little cool, with true whites. No golden-hour filters, fairy lights, flat-lays of a book and a mug, or styled reading nooks. No filter is applied: the ochre, the greyboard tiles and the jackets pull them together.
