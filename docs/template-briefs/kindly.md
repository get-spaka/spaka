# Kindly: brief

One of wave 3's four original designs: the wedding, a couple's website (`event`, one of the original, animated designs). Kindly is its working name, from the invitation's "Kindly reply". The author may change it.

**Why `event`:** a wedding site is built around one dated occasion. It has a schedule, a place to reach, rooms to book and a reply to send, as Marquee and Plinth do. A maker looking for a site for their wedding looks among the events.

## Concept

**Concept.** The website is the couple's invitation suite, unpacked from its envelope. It uses cotton paper and letterpress, cards die-cut into arches and scallops, a lilac envelope liner, a wax seal, postage stamps and postcards.
- **The opening:** the first screen is the envelope. As the page loads, the seal lifts, the flap opens on its patterned liner, and the invitation card rises out with the names and the date.
- **The suite:** the weekend, the journey and the reply are cards from the same suite. They are dealt out into a fan as they come into view.
- **The pictures:** every photograph sits in a die-cut window, and the window opens to its full shape as it arrives.
- **The story:** it comes as postcards from the places the couple has been, each with its postmark.
- **The reply:** guests fill in a reply card. Once they send it, the card slides into its envelope and is sealed with the couple's monogram.
- **The look:** joyful and calm at once. One soft, high-contrast serif carries the names and the date, a ballet script appears for single words, and the labels are set in spaced capitals, as engraved.

**The fictional couple** its texts are written for are **Iris Novak and Callum Shaw**. They are getting married on **Mackinac Island, Michigan**, on **Saturday, June 12, 2027**: the last weekend of the island's Lilac Festival (June 4 to 13, 2027).
- **The two of them:** Iris is a landscape architect from Grand Rapids; Callum teaches chemistry at a high school in Chicago. They met in the summer of 2017 as seasonal staff on the island. Callum rented bikes on Main Street; Iris rented one with a bent basket and came back every day to complain about it. They live in Chicago's Logan Square with a dog named Pike.
- **The place:**
  - an island in the Straits of Mackinac with no cars since 1898, reached by an 18-minute ferry from Mackinaw City or St. Ignace;
  - the ceremony and the party are at **Larkspur Cottage**, an invented 1891 summer cottage on the East Bluff, with a lawn over the Straits;
  - the name was searched, and no Mackinac property of that name came up.
- **The weekend:**
  - **Friday:** a welcome fish fry at the harbor boathouse;
  - **Saturday:** a morning bike ride around the island for anyone who wants one. Then carriages up the hill at 4:00, the ceremony on the lawn at 4:30, cocktails on the porch, and dinner and dancing under a tent until the last carriage down at 11:30;
  - **Sunday:** pancakes and goodbyes on the cottage porch before the noon ferries.
- **Travel and stay:**
  - guests fly into Pellston (PLN, twenty minutes from the Mackinaw City docks) or drive from Detroit (about four hours) or Chicago (about six). They leave the car in the ferry lots, and dock porters carry their bags to the hotels;
  - three blocks of rooms are held on the island (invented names, each searched) until March 15, 2027, with the code NOVAKSHAW: the Starling House, Teal Harbor Inn and Bluffside Cottages;
  - there is also a motor lodge on the mainland for those on a budget.
- **The reply:** by May 1, 2027, one reply per invitation. It asks whether you will be there, who comes with you, the dinner (lake whitefish, braised short rib or a spring pea risotto), whether you will join Friday and Sunday, and a song that will get you dancing.
- **Gifts:** "Your being there is plenty." There is a honeymoon fund for a month in Portugal's Azores and a short registry.

The same structure suits any wedding, from a town hall ceremony and a party to a destination weekend. It also suits an elopement with a party at home later, a vow renewal, a commitment ceremony, a milestone anniversary, a big family reunion or a bar or bat mitzvah weekend. The agent rewrites the couple, the place, the weekend, the rooms and the reply.

Two smaller observations shape the palette and the pictures:
- **Bold color:** the bolder sites leave blush behind. wedding20251012-ny.studio.site is acid chartreuse, and wedding.s-tmro.com asks guests to wear green, orange or beige.
- **Candid photographs:** the pictures are flash-lit, grainy and documentary rather than posed (wedding20251012-ny.studio.site, wedding.s-tmro.com).

**What makes it its own:**
- **the envelope:** an envelope drawn in CSS from the theme opens once, at load. The wax seal lifts, the flap turns open on its liner, and the card rises with the real text of the invitation. Without a script it simply stands open, the card out;
- **die-cut windows:** every photograph is cut as an arch, a pebble, a scallop, a wave or a circle, the year's stationery shapes. As a photograph comes into view, its window opens from a small pebble to the full die-cut;
- **the suite, dealt:** the weekend's cards (Friday, Saturday, Sunday, getting here, the reply) are cards of different stocks and shapes. They are dealt from a neat stack into a fan as the section comes into view, and the card under the hand lifts and straightens;
- **the sealed reply:** the answer to the reply form is the reply sealed in its envelope with the couple's monogram;
- **post:** the story is a run of postcards with postmarks, and the way to the island is four postage stamps with perforated edges. Both stay still.

**What it leaves out:**
- a countdown, which four of the sites have: Marquee's "days to go" is already in the templates, so the date here is a still, typeset object;
- loaders and "tap to open" gates (wedding.jongjeonglee.com and togetherwedding.framer.website have them): the envelope opens by itself, and the invitation is readable from the first frame;
- music, falling petals, a 3D globe, embedded maps, a photo upload, and a guestbook wall shown on the site;
- **devices of other templates:**
  - torn paper edges and tear-off tabs (Commons);
  - pinked edges, hang tags and lines that draw themselves (Seam and Chalk);
  - ticket stubs, stickers and tape (Marquee);
  - a sheer curtain, swinging keys and stitched threads (Linen);
  - a ticker, a single arch-framed photo in the hero and a round stamp turning (Corner): here the hero is the envelope, the arch is one die-cut among five, and the seal never turns;
  - a picture following the cursor (Folio);
  - sections pinned while the page scrolls (Ember, Pocket);
  - anything that scrolls sideways (Reel);
  - a giant wordmark across the footer (Corner, Ember, Marquee, Seam);
  - page transitions (wave 2 has four);
  - something sliding out of its sleeve on hover (Encore): the envelope opens once, in the hero, and nothing else slides out.

## Type

All three fonts are files of the template, in `fonts/` with their license files, under the SIL Open Font License, from Google Fonts.
- **Noto Serif Display:** variable, roman and italic, weights 300 to 600 and widths 87.5 to 100. A high-contrast display serif, cut for large sizes, which reads as engraved or letterpressed at poster size. Its italic is kept for the few words a printed invitation sets in italic ("and", "Saturday the twelfth of June").
- **Plus Jakarta Sans:** variable, 300 to 700. A warm, open grotesk for the reading and the engraved capitals.
- **Ballet:** variable, with its optical size axis. A contemporary formal script, used only for single words and the ampersand.

None is used by the sixteen templates, nor by the seven other briefs of wave 3 (Aspect, Codicil, Earshot, Gnomon, Saucer, Taper and Transfer, checked on November 16). Gloock, considered first, is Taper's heading font; Albert Sans appears among the alternatives of three siblings, so it is left to them.

**If a sibling takes them,** two pairings keep the same voice (a high-contrast display serif, a warm sans and a formal script):
- DM Serif Display with Outfit, and Pinyon Script;
- Yeseva One with Urbanist, and Corinthia.

| Role | Font | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| The names on the invitation card | Noto Serif Display | 400 at width 90, `clamp(2.75rem, 6.5vw, 6rem)`, `leading-[0.95]`, debossed (below); each name on its own line, the ampersand between them in Ballet at 1.4 times the size, in the accent |
| The date | Noto Serif Display | 400; on the card at `clamp(1.75rem, 3.5vw, 3rem)` ("June 12, 2027"); on the weekend page each day's number at 6 to 9 rem ("12") beside its weekday in spaced capitals |
| Page titles and headings | Noto Serif Display | 400 (300 at the largest sizes); titles 3.5 to 6.5 rem, `leading-[1]`, sentence case; headings 2 to 3.25 rem; never bold; italic only for the invitation's own italic words |
| Script words | Ballet | the ampersand, the couple's signature under the letter, "and" on the card; never a sentence, never below 1.75 rem |
| Body | Plus Jakarta Sans | 400, 1.0625 rem, `leading-relaxed`, `text-base-content/80`; the letter and the story in a column of about 34 rem |
| Engraved capitals | Plus Jakarta Sans | 600, 0.75 rem, capitals, `tracking-[0.24em]`: "Together with their families", the place line ("Larkspur Cottage · East Bluff · Mackinac Island"), the times spelled out ("Half past four in the afternoon"), labels and postmarks |
| Times in the run-of-show | Noto Serif Display | 400, 2.5 rem ("4:30"), with "pm" in engraved capitals |
| Interface | Plus Jakarta Sans | 600, 0.875 rem: the header's links, buttons and form labels |

**The deboss:** the names and the big date read as pressed into the card. It is a static `text-shadow` made from the theme: a highlight below, `oklch(from var(--color-base-100) calc(l + 0.03) c h)`, and a shade above, `oklch(from var(--color-base-content) l c h / 0.18)`. It is a class (`.pressed`) that works on any paper.

## Palette

Cotton paper and bottle-green ink, with a lilac liner and a vermilion seal. Every pair of text and background passes WCAG AA, measured from these values:
- ink on paper 14.1:1, ink on oat 12.3:1, and `text-base-content/70` about 6:1;
- green on paper 7.5:1, and paper on green 7.5:1;
- ink on lilac 8.3:1. Green on lilac is 4.4:1, so text on lilac is always ink;
- paper on vermilion 6:1, and vermilion on oat 5.2:1;
- paper on night 15.8:1, and lilac on night 9.3:1.

Other settings:
- buttons and fields are pills (`--theme-radius-field: 999px`), like the round seal;
- relief is soft (`--theme-depth: 1`): cards of paper cast a faint shadow made from `--color-base-content` at 8 to 12%, nothing else does;
- density is airy (`--theme-size: 0.28125rem`);
- borders are fine (1px).

The Theme tab still restyles all of it:
- the liner's pattern is an SVG used as a `mask-image` over the secondary color;
- the seals are radial gradients of the accent, their rims lightened with `oklch(from var(--color-accent) calc(l + 0.08) c h)`, and their wavy edge is a mask;
- the stamps' perforations are a `radial-gradient` mask;
- masks count only for their opacity, so none brings a color of its own.

## Motion

Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode nothing moves and everything is in its final place: the envelope open with the card out, every die-cut at its full shape, the suite fanned, the reply sealed. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too.

- **`data-envelope`, the envelope** (`scripts/envelope.js`, Motion), the signature:
  - **the drawing:** the envelope is CSS:
    - a back of oat stock;
    - a liner (`.liner`, the secondary color through the pattern mask: small lilac sprigs and bicycles, `styles/liner.svg`);
    - a front pocket cut in a shallow V;
    - a flap (`.flap`, a triangle by `clip-path`, liner inside, oat outside);
    - the wax seal (`.seal`) with the monogram "I C" in Noto Serif Display, pressed.
    
    The card (`.card`) holds the real invitation text, which the maker edits in place;
  - **the stylesheet's own state** (edit mode, no script, reduced motion): the flap open behind the card (`rotateX(180deg)` from its top edge), the seal resting on its tip, and the card standing out of the envelope with its top three quarters above the pocket;
  - **at load:** the script sets the closed state: the flap down, the seal in the middle, and the card sunk into the envelope with only its edge showing. Then, inside a perspective on the hero:
    - the seal lifts and turns away (`y: [0, -14]`, `rotate: [0, -10]`, 0.45 s);
    - the flap turns open from its top edge (`rotateX: [0, 180]`, 0.9 s, `ease: [0.65, 0, 0.35, 1]`), passing behind the card at 90°;
    - the card rises (`y: ["58%", "0%"]`, 1.1 s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`) and grows a little (`scale: [0.94, 1]`);
    - the envelope settles 2% lower;
    
    In all it takes about 2.2 s;
  - **on a phone:** the same, the envelope as wide as the screen less the gutters, the card's text a size smaller;
  - **`stop`** cancels the animations and removes the styles it set, so the envelope stands open again.
- **`data-diecut`, the die-cut windows** (`scripts/diecut.js`, Motion's `inView`):
  - **the shape is CSS, by class:** `.diecut-arch` (`border-radius: 999px 999px 0 0`), `.diecut-pebble` (an uneven `border-radius`), `.diecut-scallop` and `.diecut-wave` (SVG masks along the top edge, `styles/edges/`), and `.diecut-circle`. The agent changes a picture's shape by changing its class;
  - **as it comes into view:** the script lays a `clip-path` that opens from a small rounded window in the middle (`inset(32% 26% round 999px)`) to nothing clipped (`inset(0% 0% round 0px)`, 1 s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`). Meanwhile the image inside eases from `scale: 1.12` to 1. The die-cut's own shape stays throughout, so the picture opens into its arch or scallop;
  - **with no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode:** the full die-cut. `stop` removes the clip and the scale.
- **`data-suite`, the suite dealt** (`scripts/suite.js`, Motion):
  - **its layout is CSS:**
    - from `lg`, the cards lie in a loose fan, each tilted by its own `--tilt` (−7°, −3.5°, 0°, 3.5°, 7°, from Liquid's `forloop.index`, a number, not a color) and overlapping its neighbor by about 2.5 rem;
    - each card's text keeps clear of the overlapped edge;
    - on a phone the cards are a column, each slightly tilted, overlapping by 1 rem;
  - **as it comes into view:** the script gathers the cards into a neat stack in the middle (`x` to the stack, `rotate: 0`), then deals them out to their places one after another (a spring with a little overshoot, 0.09 s apart). On a phone, each card swings from 0° to its tilt and rises 24 px as it enters;
  - **under the hand (CSS, no-preference):** the card hovered or focused lifts 10 px, straightens to 0° and comes to the front (`z-index` through `:hover, :focus-within`, 0.35 s). The others stay where they are;
  - **with no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode:** the fan, every card readable and clickable. `stop` resets what it set.
- **`data-seal`, the sealed reply** (`scripts/seal.js`, Motion):
  - **where:** on the reply's answer fragment, which htmx swaps in for the form. `start` is called with the fragment, so it runs as the answer arrives;
  - **the fragment's own state:** a closed oat envelope whose front reads like an address, in engraved capitals and Noto Serif Display:
    - "From {{ record.name }}";
    - "Joyfully accepts · a party of 2 · Friday and Sunday too", or "Regretfully declines";
    - "To Iris & Callum, Mackinac Island";
    - the vermilion seal with the monogram on its flap.
    
    Under it, a line thanks the guest ("See you on the island, Ana" or "We'll miss you, Ana") and tells them how to change their reply;
  - **the animation:**
    - a copy of the reply card (`aria-hidden`, holding the same lines) stands above the open envelope;
    - it slides down into the pocket (`y: ["-70%", "0%"]`, 0.7 s), and the flap closes over it (`rotateX: [180, 0]`, 0.6 s);
    - the seal drops onto the flap and presses (`scale: [1.5, 0.92, 1]`, a 0.5 s spring), then the thank-you line rises in;
    - the copy is removed at the end;
  - **with no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode:** the sealed envelope and the line. `stop` removes the copy and the styles.
- **The floating seal (CSS only):**
  - a round vermilion seal, fixed at the bottom right from the end of the hero onward, links to the reply;
  - "Reply" sits in its middle in Noto Serif Display, with "by May 1" in engraved capitals under it. No text runs round its rim and it never turns: a round stamp with text on a circle, turning, is Corner's;
  - it appears through a scroll-driven animation (`animation-timeline: scroll()`, a `from` keyframe at opacity 0 and scale 0.6), which edit mode and older browsers show in place. On hover and on focus it presses down, as a seal does (`scale: 0.94`, its shadow tightening, 0.2 s);
  - the reply page leaves it out, and so does a site with no reply (see What varies). On a phone it is 64 px and sits above the safe area.
- **Postcards and stamps:** still. The postcards hang at their tilt (±2°, layout), and the postmarks and perforations are drawn. Only `data-reveal` moves them.
- **`data-reveal`:** headings, paragraphs and cards rise into view (`y: [24, 0]`, opacity, 0.8 s) with Motion's `inView`, as in the site-scripts skill.
- **The header:** sticky. Once the page is scrolled, it takes the paper at 92% with a hairline under it, through a CSS scroll-driven animation toward the stylesheet's own state, which edit mode and older browsers show.
- **The guide's tabs** (Eat, See, Do) on the travel page: daisyUI radio tabs, with `:has(:checked)` hiding the others in CSS. With no script and in edit mode they work too.
- **No page transition:** pages change plainly.
- **Smooth scrolling:** Lenis, light (`lerp: 0.1`).

Libraries: Motion and Lenis, pinned on jsDelivr, under MIT. No Splitting, no OGL, no WebGL: the paper is drawn in CSS.

## What varies

The choices that change the design, page type by page type; the first option of each is the one recommended.

**Home (`/`)**, with what every page shares:

*How do guests reply?* (the whole website)
- **Online, with the reply card** (as built):
  - replies arrive in Spaka's Content tab, and can be downloaded as a CSV for the caterer;
  - keep `parts/reply-form.liquid`, `data/rsvps/`, `fragments/rsvp-sealed.liquid`, the header's "Kindly reply", the floating seal and the reply bands.
- **By email or text:**
  - the reply page keeps its date and its questions, but its card holds the address and a phone number with "Write to us" and "Text us" buttons;
  - `rsvps`, its fragment and `scripts/seal.js` go;
  - every reply link points to the reply page.
- **Through another wedding service** (Zola, The Knot, Joy or a printed card):
  - one card says where to reply, with a button to it;
  - the form, `rsvps` and its fragment go;
  - the header's button and the floating seal point there.
- **No reply needed** (an open ceremony, a party everyone may join):
  - the reply page and its card go, and so do the header's button and the floating seal;
  - the questions and the gifts move to the weekend page;
  - the reply bands become "We hope to see you there" with a link to the weekend.

*Is the wedding still to come?* (the whole website)
- **Still to come** (as built): the invitation, the weekend, travel and the reply.
- **It has happened** (the thank-you site):
  - the card in the envelope reads "Thank you" over the names, the date and "for being there";
  - the suite becomes the photographs, the story and the thank-you letter;
  - the reply page gives way to **Photos** (`/photos`): a wall of the day's pictures in die-cuts, adapted from `apps/desktop/gallery/gallery/gallery-masonry-wall`, over `gallery`;
  - `rsvps`, the reply form and the floating seal go, and the travel page is left out unless the maker wants it kept as a memory.

When left out: the home page is never left out.

**Our story (`/story`)**

*Who tells the story?*
- **The two of them, as "we"** (as built).
- **Each in turn:** every postcard carries two short paragraphs, "Iris says" and "Callum says", the way the proposal is told. The `story` records get the fields `her`/`his` (or `first`/`second`), and the summary becomes a line.
- **A friend:**
  - the maid of honor or the best man tells it, in the third person, with a short note at the top signed with their name in Ballet;
  - "Ten summers and one ferry" stays as the title.

*Is there a wedding party?*
- **Yes** (as built): the people standing with them, with the officiant.
- **No:** the section goes with `data/party/`. The officiant, if there is one, gets one line under the proposal.

When left out:
- **delete:** `pages/story.liquid`, its parts, `data/party/` and its navigation record;
- **what stays:** the home page's three postcards keep `story`, and their "Read our story" link goes.

**The weekend (`/weekend`)**

*How long is the celebration?*
- **A weekend** (as built): Friday's welcome, the wedding on Saturday and Sunday's goodbyes. The suite's first three cards are the three days.
- **One day:**
  - the run-of-show is the day alone, from the guests' arrival to the send-off, with no day headers;
  - the suite's cards become the ceremony, the dinner and the party;
  - the reply's "The weekend" step goes.
- **A ceremony now, a party later** (two dates, two places):
  - the run-of-show has two days months apart, each with its own place and directions;
  - the envelope's card names both;
  - the reply asks about each.

*What do guests wear?*
- **Colors to wear** (as built): the swatches on lilac, and a sentence of advice.
- **A named dress code** (black tie, cocktail, beach formal): one line in Noto Serif Display, a sentence, and two die-cut photos of the place; no swatches.
- **Nothing to say:** the section goes, and the run-of-show keeps any note in its `dress` field.

When left out:
- **delete:** `pages/weekend.liquid`, its parts, `data/events/` and its navigation record;
- **links:** the suite's day cards point to the reply, and its three day cards become one card, "The day", carrying the times written into it;
- **the envelope:** the card keeps the date, the time and the place.

**Travel & stay (`/travel`)**

*Where do most guests come from?*
- **From away** (as built): airports, the drive, the last leg, rooms to book and things to do.
- **Mostly nearby:**
  - the page becomes "Getting there" (`/getting-there`), with the address, parking and a plain map link;
  - two or three places to stay for the few who travel;
  - the stamps keep only "By road" and "On foot".
- **From abroad:** a "Before you fly" part (passports and visas, the currency, the language, the time zone, plugs and phones) joins the way here, and the stamps carry the countries' airports.

*Where do guests stay?*
- **Blocks of rooms held for them** (as built): each stay with its code and its cutoff date.
- **All at the venue:** one card for the venue's rooms ("Your room is on us; we'll write with your room by May 15"), and the other stays go.
- **Their own choice:** a list of suggestions with no codes or dates, and a line on booking early.

When left out:
- **delete:** `pages/travel.liquid`, its parts, `data/stays/`, `data/guide/`, `data/getting-here/`, the home page's stamps and its navigation record;
- **links:** the suite's "Getting here" card goes; the footer keeps the place's name and a "Directions" link.

**Kindly reply (`/rsvp`)**

Its form follows how guests reply, chosen with the home page.

*What does the reply ask?*
- **Attendance, the party, the dinner and the weekend** (as built).
- **Attendance and the party only** (a buffet or a family-style dinner): the dinner field goes from the form and from `rsvps`, and so does the line on the sealed envelope.
- **A dinner for each guest:** step 02 repeats a name and a dinner for each seat on the invitation (up to four, as `guest_1` to `guest_4`), and the sealed envelope lists them.

*Who comes with a guest?*
- **Those named on the invitation** (as built): "How many of you" goes up to the number invited, and the questions say so.
- **Anyone may bring a guest:** step 02 asks "Bringing someone?" and their name, and the question on plus-ones says yes.

*How do gifts work?*
- **A fund and a registry** (as built): the cards link to them.
- **No gifts, please:** the section becomes one line in Noto Serif Display, "Your being there is the gift", and `registry` goes.
- **A cause:** one card for a charity the two of them chose, with its link.

When left out (the maker answered "No reply needed" or replies elsewhere):
- **delete:** `pages/rsvp.liquid`, `parts/reply-form.liquid`, `data/rsvps/`, `fragments/rsvp-sealed.liquid`, `scripts/seal.js`, the floating seal and its navigation record;
- **what moves:** the questions and the gifts go to the weekend page;
- **links:** every reply link goes where the home page's choice says.

## Images

From Wikimedia Commons and Openverse (CC0, public domain, CC BY, CC BY-SA), credited. About 30 images:
- **the island (about twelve):** the Straits at dusk, lilacs, Arch Rock, the East Bluff's cottages and porches, the ferry at the dock, carriages and bicycles on Main Street, the fort on the bluff, a fudge shop's window, the shore road. Commons has many photographs of Mackinac Island under CC BY-SA;
- **the couple (about seven):** engagement photographs under CC BY from Openverse, best of two people walking, from behind, at a distance or by water, in daylight or flash, with one photographer's series used throughout so that they read as one couple. If no series fits, the pictures turn to hands, two bicycles, two coffee cups or a dog on a dock: the design never needs a face;
- **the story's places (about five):** a college town street in fall, a Chicago balcony with tomato plants, a beach on Lake Michigan's dunes, Arch Rock at dawn, and a ferry's wake;
- **the stays (four):** a Victorian inn's porch, a harbor-front hotel, a white cottage, and a roadside motor lodge's sign;
- **the guide (six):** taken from the island's pictures;
- **the wedding party:** portraits only if fitting photos of people are found; otherwise, their initials on lilac.

To read as one set, they are chosen warm and natural, with a little grain and the candid feel the trend favors. A shared treatment (`[data-print]`: a touch less saturation and a little more brightness, a filter with no color of its own) pulls the island's documentary photos and the couple's together. Faces appear only where the couple and their people would show their own.
