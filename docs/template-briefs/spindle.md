# Spindle: brief

One of wave 5's templates: the neighborhood restaurant, open for lunch and for supper (`business`). Its motion is gentle and useful. The day's specials hang as kitchen tickets on a steel rail, and they slide onto it one after another when the section comes into view. Each ticket gives its dish, its price and its service window ("Lunch 11–2:30"), and a ticket the kitchen has run out of says "86". The restaurant's name is set huge across the first screen, and a cut-out plate of food stands in front of it, breaking its letters.

`spindle` is the working name. It replaces "Docket", the wave sheet's name, which is already sold four times: a law-firm template on Framer's marketplace, a documentation template on Webflow, a Squarespace template by Knapsack Creative, and a preset of Shopify's Azzel theme. "Chit" was set aside next, because ThemeForest sells "Chit Club", a board-game café theme. A search found no template or theme called Spindle on Framer (its address for that name shows "Template unavailable"), ThemeForest (no results), Webflow, Squarespace or Shopify (`themes.shopify.com/themes/spindle` does not exist). The spindle is the lunch counter's ancestor of the ticket rail: the spike or the turning wheel at the pass where the counter's checks waited for the cook. The name is the template's, not the business's.

## Concept

**Concept.** A corner restaurant you read like its own counter at five to eleven. The site is cream and diner turquoise, the colors of the old porcelain sign over the door. The name is set in a wood-type slab across the width, and a plate of pierogi stands in front of it, so the letters disappear behind its rim. The kitchen's day is told in two ways:
- **the rail:** the day's specials, typed on paper tickets, hanging from a steel rail on a pale turquoise wall, the way they hang over the pass. Each ticket has its service window. One is struck through and stamped "86" once it has sold out;
- **the time card:** the week as a staff time card, each day with a lunch shift and a supper shift, so the two services read at a glance.

Under them: the lunch menu and the supper menu side by side as two printed cards, the Friday fish fry, the regulars at the counter by stool number, booths booked for supper, orders picked up at the side door, and the back room hired, or the whole place on a Monday.

It must not read as Decal, Saucer or Ember:
- **Decal** is a food truck's week in road paint. Its special is a pink card pinned to the corner of an asphalt menu board.
- **Saucer** is a daytime café read like its front door. Its round photos of plates, seen from above, are set down on a table as you scroll, and it knows from the clock what is being served.
- **Ember** is a dark evening room lit by the pointer, its hours pinned while the page scrolls.

Spindle is lit by daylight and reads no clock. Its tickets are paper, typed in the morning, hanging plumb and sliding along a rail, never pinned, set down or lit. It is also not Corner (a bakery's poster capitals, cobalt and a ribbon), nor Encore (an aqua stage wash on graphite). Nor is it Covered, its sibling in this wave: Covered is software for restaurant teams, whose messy paper rota tidies itself into a grid.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Halina's**, a restaurant serving lunch and supper at 14 Tamarack Court (an invented side street), on its corner with Lincoln Avenue, Milwaukee, Wisconsin 53215. It is in Lincoln Village, the old Polish south side around the Basilica of St. Josaphat, where most neighbors are now Mexican American.
- **The place:**
  - a 1926 brick corner building. Halina Nowak ran a lunch counter in it from 1961 to 2009;
  - its porcelain sign, cream letters on turquoise enamel, still says HALINA'S LUNCH over the door, and the new owners kept the name with it;
  - inside, her turquoise Formica counter with its chrome edge and fourteen stools;
  - eight booths along the windows, added in 2022;
  - the back room, once the storeroom and the neighborhood's card room, which seats thirty.
- **The people:**
  - **Marisol Ortega** (she/her), the chef, grew up four blocks away and ate at the counter after Mass as a child. She cooked for nine years in Chicago and in Milwaukee's Third Ward before she bought the building in 2021;
  - **Pete Kowalski** (he/him), her partner in the business, runs the floor, the counter and the bar. He tended bar for twelve years at a Bay View bowling alley;
  - **Bernie Sobczak** (she/her) worked Halina's counter from 1994 to 2009, came back for the lunches, and knows every regular's order;
  - **Andre Mitchell** (he/him), the sous chef, runs the grill and Friday's fryers.
- **Two services a day:**
  - lunch, Tuesday to Saturday, 11 am to 2:30 pm, at the counter and in the booths;
  - supper, Wednesday to Saturday, 5 to 9:30 pm, in the booths and at the counter;
  - the Friday fish fry from 4:30 to 9:30, with no reservations;
  - Sunday lunch from 10 am to 2 pm;
  - closed on Mondays, except for private parties;
  - the kitchen takes its last order half an hour before closing.
- **The food:** the south side's two kitchens under one sign.
  - At lunch ($9 to $16): Halina's pierogi plate, the Lincoln (a butter burger), a torta de milanesa, kielbasa on rye, potato pancakes, the soup.
  - At supper ($18 to $29): chicken paprikash with nokedli, pork shank braised in lager with red cabbage, half a chicken in mole poblano, trout in brown butter, stuffed cabbage of mushroom and barley.
  - On Fridays, yellow perch or cod with rye bread, coleslaw and potato pancakes.
  - Kolaczki and a rhubarb kuchen; a brandy old fashioned, sweet, and a lager from down the street.
  - Specials are written up every morning at ten and run until they are gone.
- **How guests sit:**
  - the counter is always walk-in, and so is lunch;
  - booths for supper on Wednesday, Thursday and Saturday are asked for with a form, for up to six, and confirmed by text;
  - on Fridays guests give their name at the counter.
- **Beyond the dining room:**
  - orders for pickup are sent by 10:30 am for lunch or by 4 pm for supper, confirmed by text, and paid for at the side door on Tamarack Court;
  - private hire: the back room for up to thirty from Tuesday to Saturday (a food and drink minimum from $900), or the whole place on a Monday for sixty (from $3,500), with a family-style menu at $38 a guest;
  - gift cards are sold at the counter.

The name was searched. No restaurant called Halina's came up in Milwaukee; the nearest was Halina's Polish Delights in Chicago, which has closed. Saucer is the only other template set in Wisconsin, a daytime café in Madison. Spindle shares none of its devices and none of its dishes: perch, not walleye, and no Door County cherries. The phone in the texts is (414) 555-0163, a number reserved for fiction.

The same structure suits:
- a diner, a luncheonette or lunch counter, a family restaurant, a supper club;
- a neighborhood bistro or trattoria, a taquería with a dining room, a Polish, Hungarian or German restaurant;
- a deli that serves supper, a soul food kitchen, any restaurant whose specials change every day.

The agent rewrites the dishes and the specials, the two services and their hours, the rooms, the people, the regulars and the town.

## Trends it draws on

These come from the ten neighborhood restaurant sites of the wave's trends study (Awwwards' Restaurant & Hotel category of 2025-2026, MaxiBestOf 2026 and Site Builder Report). Four were opened again with WebFetch in November 2026, to confirm the facts quoted below: delice.ca, rayswaterfront.com, slurpramen.dk and tacosmyguey.com. Each trend was seen on at least two of the sites.

**The gap.** On these sites the day's specials are promotions: a carousel of photo cards (delice.ca), a ticker (rayswaterfront.com), star badges (tacosmyguey.com). Spindle turns trend 4 into its rail and trend 3 into its time card, and takes trends 1 and 6 for its first screen: the name huge, a cut-out dish breaking its letters.

**What makes it its own:**

## Type

The wave sheet's display face, **Ultra**, cannot be used. On Google Fonts it is under the Apache License 2.0, not the OFL (it sits in `google/fonts/apache/ultra`), and it covers basic Latin only, with no Latin Extended. Its nearest alternative is **Alfa Slab One** (JM Solé, 2011): like Ultra, it is an ultra-bold slab drawn from the wood-type Egyptians. Its model is the Thorowgood foundry's Six-lines Pica Egyptian, given an extreme stem weight, big serifs and a little more contrast. It is on Google Fonts under the OFL, with Latin, Latin Extended and Vietnamese. **Karla** and **Cutive Mono** hold as the wave sheet set them.

All three are files of the template, as `woff2` subset to Latin and Latin Extended (for names such as Kowalski, Sobczak, kołaczki and pączki), under the SIL Open Font License, declared in `styles/site.css`:
- Alfa Slab One has one weight (400) and no italic, so the headings have none;
- Karla ships as a variable file (weight 200 to 800) with its italic;
- Cutive Mono has one weight (400), and `font-synthesis: none` keeps the browser from faking a bold.

|---|---|---|

The guide tells the agent to set `--chars`, and to set a name over 12 letters on two lines |

Alfa Slab One is the lettering of the porcelain signs and matchbooks of a 1960s lunch counter: black, square-shouldered, friendly. It is set huge, it is not condensed, and the dish breaks it without it falling apart. Karla is a grotesk with a few odd, warm details, plain enough for a menu read in a hurry. Cutive Mono is drawn from the IBM Executive and Smith-Premier typewriters, the face of a ticket typed or printed at the pass, lighter and thinner than the Courier Prime of Seam's hang tags.

## Motion

**Gentle,** by the restaurant's character: neighborly, practical, busy at noon. One movement carries the site, the tickets sliding onto the rail; beside it, a slow drift of the plate in front of the name, and short reveals. Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.

In edit mode nothing moves: Upstart's stillness rule stops CSS animations and transitions, and no script runs. Every ticket hangs in its place, the ones marked gone already struck through, and the plate stands where the stylesheet puts it. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too.

Nothing follows the clock, so `template.json` sets no `shoot.clock`.

- **The ticket rail** (`data-rail` on the list, `data-ticket` on each ticket; CSS, and `scripts/rail.js` with Motion's `inView` and `animate`):
  - **in CSS**:
    - the rail is a `<ul>` whose tickets hang from the top of each row. Each ticket draws its own length of rail above it, its `::before` stretched by half the gap on each side, so the lengths join into one bar on a desktop. On a phone, where the tickets wrap two to a row, each row has its rail;
    - a ticket's top 0.75 rem is hidden behind the bar's lip. Tickets align at their tops and their heights follow their texts;
    - a ticket marked gone (`data-gone`) has its dish struck by a 2px paprika line, and shows the "86 · ALL GONE" strip, written in the template and shown by `[data-gone] [data-86]`. Its other lines fade to 70%, never hidden;
  - **as the rail comes into view** (`amount: 0.4`), the script groups the tickets by row, from their `offsetTop`. It starts each ticket at its row's left end, `x` from minus its distance from that end and 24px more, to 0. In the same animation it fades in, `opacity: [0, 1]` over its first 0.25 s. The tickets go in reading order, 120 ms apart, each in 0.7 s with `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`, so the first slides a little way and the last the length of the rail;
  - each ticket lands with a dip, `y: [0, 2, 0]` in 0.18 s, as the rail's ball takes it. That is all: no swing and no tilt; the tickets hang plumb;
  - with no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode, every ticket is already on the rail;
  - `stop` cancels the animations, takes off the styles it set and unobserves the rail. `start` gives every value where it starts from, so a second start, after a change in the preview, plays the same way.
- **The plate in front of the name** (`data-dish` on the cut-out, CSS only, in `styles/site.css`):
  - under `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`, a scroll-driven animation, `animation-timeline: view()` with `animation-range: exit 0% exit 100%`, moves the plate up by 12% of its height as the hero leaves the screen, while the name stays;
  - only its `to` keyframe is written, so at the top of the page (exit 0%), with no script and in edit mode, the plate stands as the stylesheet sets it;
  - layering is the grid's: the name and the plate share a cell (`col-start-1 row-start-1`), the plate `relative z-10`, never placed absolutely.
- **The time card** (`data-time-card`, CSS only) does not move. It is a table, rows on cream and rules in turquoise, and nothing on it is marked today.
- **Reveals** (`data-reveal`, `scripts/reveal.js`, Motion's `inView`), as in the site-scripts skill:
  - a section's main elements rise a little: `y: [16, 0]`, `opacity: [0, 1]`, 0.7 s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`;
  - children rise 70 ms apart under `data-reveal="stagger"`;
  - used on the regulars, the two menus and the rooms, not on the rail, which has its own entrance.
- **Hover and focus:** buttons and links change in 150 ms. The tickets have no hover: they are read, not clicked.
- **The header** is sticky on cream at 95% with a hairline under it. It does not change as you scroll.

No smooth scrolling, no page transitions, no loader, no WebGL, no Splitting. One library: Motion, pinned on jsDelivr (`motion@14.9.0`), under MIT. Everything else is CSS.

## Reservations, pickup and what stays outside

Upstart has no reservations, no ordering, no payments, no calendar and no embeds, so:
- **a booth is a request** (as built), confirmed by text; the form and its answer say so before and after sending. A restaurant on Resy, OpenTable or Tock links every "Reserve" button to its page there (see What varies), never its widget;
- **pickup is a request too,** confirmed by text with the total and paid at the door. A restaurant that sells ahead online (Toast, Square, ChowNow) links "Order pickup" to that page;
- **the specials are typed facts** the maker changes each morning: no live sold-out count. The rail says "86" because someone ticked it;
- **private hire is answered by phone,** held by a deposit paid outside the site;
- **no map:** "Get directions" links to the address on a map site;
- **no Instagram feed:** a link to the account in the footer;
- **gift cards are sold at the counter;** a restaurant that sells them online links to that page.

## Images

About 22 images: four generated and about eighteen free photos.

**Generated** (Q452), paid by the author's key, four in one style. Each is made with `pnpm template:image generate`, the first given as `--ref` to the next three:
- `pierogi-plate`, the hero's: a heaped plate of pierogi with browned onions, sour cream and dill, on a heavy diner plate with a turquoise band at its rim, seen from a low three-quarter angle, so the plate reads as a wide ellipse and the pile rises above it;
- `the-lincoln`: a butter burger, two thin patties and fried onions, with a pickle spear, on the same plate;
- `paprikash`: chicken paprikash with nokedli in a wide bowl of the same china;
- `perch-fry`: yellow perch with a slice of rye, a paper cup of coleslaw and a potato pancake, on the same plate, for the Friday band.

Why generated: a cut-out dish needs one light, one angle and one plate across the set, which no free photos give. Each prompt asks for the dish alone: no hands, no people, no logo, no lettering, no tablecloth or gingham. The dish is shown in soft daylight from the left, on a perfectly flat, even magenta ground with no shadow.

The ground is then removed, because the generate command sends no transparent-background option today:
- the build session keys out the magenta with a small `sharp` script in its own scratchpad: a color-distance mask, a one-pixel feather and the magenta's spill taken out;
- it writes the cut-out back into `media/` as a WebP with transparency;
- it notes the step in the image's JSON beside the model and the prompt.

Generated images make the template need `"app": "0.2.0"` in `template.json`.

If Commons has a dish already cut out (a PNG with a transparent ground) that fits this plate and light, it is used before generating, credited.

**Free photos** from Wikimedia Commons and Openverse (CC0, public domain, CC BY, CC BY-SA), credited. None are searched yet; the search comes at step 4:
