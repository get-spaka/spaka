# Taper: brief

One of wave 3's four standard templates: the hair salon or barbershop (`business`, not one of the original designs). Its motion is quiet and useful: a small barber pole that turns only while the shop is open, the price grid's steps darkening in turn like a fade, and a hand mirror that turns a cut to show its back.

`taper` is a working name: a taper is the barber's gradual shortening of the hair toward the neck, the same gradient the template draws its levels with. It was searched as a template name: no barber or salon template called Taper came up on ThemeForest, Framer, Webflow or Squarespace (Framer sells a portfolio template called "Taped", another word and another trade). The fictional business has its own name.

## Concept

**Concept.** A neighborhood shop where every head pays for the time it takes, not for its gender. The site is light and plain spoken, like a well-kept shop with the door propped open: a chiseled display serif like the gold-leaf letters on a barbershop window, a sturdy grotesk, towel white and a deep Barbicide blue-green. Prices are a grid of chair time against the level of whoever cuts you, its four columns stepping from pale to deep like a fade, and the same four steps mark every barber and stylist. The header says whether the shop is open right now with a little pole that turns while it is. The cut book shows real cuts with their names, who did them, how long they took and when to come back, and each card turns in a hand mirror to show the back. Asking for a chair takes one short form.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Good Side**, a barbershop and hair studio at 14 Mill Row (an invented street), a step off the Avenue (West 36th Street) in Hampden, Baltimore, Maryland, in a former mill office with tall windows. It was opened in 2019 by **Tasha Greer**, a barber who learned in her uncle's shop on Baltimore's west side and cut for ten years before buying her own chairs, and **Wren Castillo**, a stylist trained in Philadelphia who cuts curly and coily hair dry, curl by curl. Five people work its five chairs and one color bar:

- **Tasha Greer** (she/her), level 4, co-owner: skin fades, tapers, beard shaping, hot-towel shaves;
- **Wren Castillo** (they/them), level 4, co-owner: curly and coily cuts, shags, dry cutting;
- **Inés Duarte** (she/her), level 3: color, grey blending, glosses, lived-in blondes;
- **Mo Bell** (he/him), level 2: fades, lineups, designs, kids' cuts;
- **Sam Ortiz** (he/they), level 1, new talent: classic cuts, buzz cuts, beard trims, at the lowest rate.

Since 2021 every service has been priced by its chair time (15, 30, 45, 60, 90 or 120 minutes) and the level of the person doing it: a 30-minute cut is $34 with Sam, $42 with Mo, $52 with Inés and $60 with Tasha or Wren, whatever the hair or the person under it. Kids under 12 pay a flat $25; a 15-minute consultation is free. The shop is closed on Monday, open Tuesday to Friday from 10 am to 8 pm with walk-ins from 10 to noon, Saturday from 9 am to 5 pm by appointment only, and Sunday from 11 am to 4 pm. A request sent through the site is answered by text message within two hours while the shop is open. The entrance is step-free and one chair lowers for a transfer from a wheelchair. The phone in the texts is (410) 555-0136, a number reserved for fiction.

The name was searched: no salon or barbershop called Good Side came up, in Baltimore or elsewhere. No existing template, and as far as the wave's briefing goes, no sibling, is in Baltimore.

The same structure suits a barbershop, a hair salon, a unisex hair studio, a curly hair specialist, a colorist's studio, a single barber renting a chair, or a braiding salon. The agent rewrites the services and their chair times, the levels and the rates, the people, the cuts and the hours.

## Trends it draws on

These come from fifteen sites of salons and barbershops opened in November 2026 with WebFetch: Awwwards' recent entries for hair (alicehairart.se, Honorable Mention, February 2025; giellygreen.co.uk, Honorable Mention, January 2026; crispmtl.com, Honorable Mention, August 2026; serenityhairblaxland.com.au, Nominee, September 2025; hairmeup.pl, Nominee, 2024), then the shops two 2026 reviews of barbershop and salon websites single out for their design, prices or booking. WebFetch reads a page's text; the fonts, colors and libraries below come from each site's own HTML and stylesheets, read with curl. Each trend was seen on at least two of the sites.

1. **Prices in plain view, set by the level of whoever cuts and by the time a service takes, not by gender.** crispmtl.com (one price list in three columns, Stylist, Junior Stylist and Junior Barber, "Base price + Taxes"), loganparlor.com ("a gender-free pricing structure where services are charged based on timing, hair length, and stylist level", with levels 1 to 6 on its team cards), fellowbarber.com/pages/services (every service with its minutes, and which cuts need a Fellow or Senior barber rather than a Junior), heritagebarbershop.com (every price, a long-hair column, $5 off for kids, seniors and the military), barberandco.us ("from $60", and what every service includes), mrwinstons.com (a "Menu of Services" with its prices).
2. **Named people, each with what they do best, their own handle and a way to book them.** alicehairart.se (three stylists, each with an Instagram handle, years of practice, specialties and a booking link of their own), loganparlor.com (name, pronouns, level, specialties and favorite products), heritagebarbershop.com (eleven barbers, each with their own booking page and schedule), fringeny.com (nine stylists with titles, "Meet Our Passionate Stylists"), palmsunday.co (stylists by first name, "View our stylists"), giellygreen.co.uk/artists (artists filtered by expertise, and a quiz to find yours).
3. **Booking at the top of every page, and the shop's rules said plainly.** Every site keeps a Book button in its header; nearly all hand it to an outside tool: Squire (crispmtl.com, barberandco.us), Boulevard (fellowbarber.com, fringeny.com), Booksy (hairmeup.pl), Mangomint (palmsunday.co), Meevo (loganparlor.com), a booking portal (giellygreen.co.uk), Timma and Bokadirekt, one per stylist (alicehairart.se). The rules come with it: mrwinstons.com (48 hours' notice to cancel, walk-ins welcome), thescotchpine.com (a cancellation notice on the home page and the hours to call), loganparlor.com (its cancellation policy beside the FAQ), barberandco.us ("Hair wash, complimentary drink, grooming consultation & styling are included with all services").
4. **Hours and the place as a practical card, not small print.** crispmtl.com (a card per shop: photo, address linked to a map, hours day by day and its own Book now), barberandco.us (each shop's hours, Miami Monday to Friday 8 to 8), mrwinstons.com (hours per shop, walk-ins accepted), fringeny.com (each location with its phone and hours), heritagebarbershop.com ("Open daily, 9:00 AM to 7:00 PM"), thescotchpine.com (the week day by day).
5. **Warm off-white and near-black with one deep color, often a display serif over a neo-grotesk, and little movement.** giellygreen.co.uk (Sagittaire over GT America, burgundy #5d0f17 on #f2f0ed), fellowbarber.com (Reckless over Neue Haas Grotesk, #363636 on #ebe7df), fringeny.com (Cormorant Infant over Onest, violet ink #292145 with salmon #f6afa1), hairmeup.pl (DM Serif Display with Lora, rust #963e13 on #fbf5ef), crispmtl.com (black on #f3efed, a bold extended Helvetica Neue with a serif, Perfectly Nineties), alicehairart.se (#121212 on #f5f5f5, Px Grotesk), blindbarber.com (Monument Grotesk, #141414 on #f7f7f7), larrykinghair.com (GT America). Their motion is quiet: smooth scrolling with Lenis (giellygreen.co.uk, blindbarber.com), a home animation, a mouse interaction and a looping strip of partners with GSAP (crispmtl.com), carousels (giellygreen.co.uk, hairmeup.pl, blindbarber.com).
6. **The work as a gallery, but rarely explained.** crispmtl.com ("What we do best" and a portfolio page of photos, uncredited and unnamed), serenityhairblaxland.com.au (a gallery page, "lived-in blondes, extensions, balayage"), alicehairart.se (the hair beside the studio's art), fringeny.com and serenityhairblaxland.com.au (an Instagram feed in place of the work). The gap is the same everywhere: a photo of a cut never says what it is called, who did it, how long it took or how to ask for it.

Two things were common and stay outside: an online shop of hair products and gift cards (fellowbarber.com, palmsunday.co, hairmeup.pl, blindbarber.com, giellygreen.co.uk, larrykinghair.com), which Upstart cannot sell, and press quotes and award badges (fellowbarber.com, mrwinstons.com, larrykinghair.com).

**What makes it its own:**
- **the fade:** the four levels are four tonal steps, from a pale wash to deep Barbicide blue-green, like the guard lengths of a fade blended up from the neckline. They are the columns of the price grid, the chip on every barber and stylist, and the choice of level in the request form, so a visitor learns one code: the deeper the step, the more experience in the chair. As the price grid comes into view its columns darken one after another, from the palest to the deepest;
- **the open sign:** a little barber pole, a capsule of diagonal stripes in the theme's colors, beside a line that says whether the shop is open right now ("Open now, until 8 pm" or "Closed now, opens Tuesday at 10 am"). It turns only while the shop is open, and rests when it is closed;
- **the week at the chair:** the opening hours drawn as seven bars on a day running from 8 am to 9 pm, the walk-in hours striped like the pole, today's bar marked and a fine line at the current time;
- **the hand mirror:** each cut in the cut book has its front photo and, in an oval mirror at its lower corner, its back. Under the pointer or the keyboard's focus the mirror grows over the card to show the back, the way a barber turns the chair and holds up the mirror at the end of a cut;
- **the cut card:** each cut has its name, who did it, its chair time and when to come back ("Every 3 to 4 weeks"), and a line to say at the chair ("Ask for a #1 at the sides, blended to finger length on top").

**What it leaves out:**
- the red, white and blue pole as decoration, mustaches, razors and skulls as clip art, "Est." badges and round stamps (Corner's stamp), a dark, moody barbershop: the wave's tattoo studio is the dark room of its artists, this one is a daylit shop;
- an online shop, gift cards sold online, a booking widget, an Instagram feed, review widgets and a map (all third-party embeds), a newsletter discount;
- a ticker or ribbon (Corner's), arch-framed photos (Corner's), a before and after slider (Trestle's), anything that scrolls sideways (Reel's), page transitions, a carousel of photos, italic accent words in headings.

## Type

Both fonts are files of the template, under the SIL Open Font License, from Google Fonts, as `woff2` subset to Latin and Latin Extended (for names such as Inés). Gloock has one weight and no italic, so the headings have none; Funnel Sans ships as a variable file (300 to 800) with its italic.

| Role | Font | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Headings | Gloock | its one weight (400), never synthesized bold; the hero at `clamp(3rem, 6vw + 1rem, 6.5rem)`, `leading-[0.98]`, `tracking-[-0.01em]`, `text-balance`; section headings at `clamp(2rem, 3vw + 1rem, 3.5rem)`; small headings at 1.5 rem; sentence case |
| Large figures | Gloock | the chair times in the grid's first column ("30"), a cut's minutes, the level numbers on the steps, at 2 to 3.5 rem |
| Body | Funnel Sans | 400, 1.0625 rem, `leading-relaxed`, `text-base-content/80`, measure `max-w-[62ch]` |
| Labels, buttons, prices and hours | Funnel Sans | labels 600 at 0.75 rem, uppercase, `tracking-[0.08em]`, like the lettering of a price board; prices and hours 600 at 1 to 1.25 rem with `tabular-nums`, so the grid's columns line up; buttons 600 at 1 rem, sentence case |

Gloock is a high-contrast display serif with sharp, wedge-like serifs: it has the cut of gold-leaf letters painted on a shop window, which suits a barbershop, and the editorial air of the salons' serifs (Sagittaire, Reckless, Cormorant) without their thinness. Funnel Sans is a contemporary grotesk with open, slightly squared curves, plain enough for a price list and with its own character in the figures; it does what Neue Haas, GT America and Monument Grotesk do on the sites above.

None of the sixteen templates uses either: Gloock appears only among Encore's unused alternatives, and Funnel Sans nowhere. **If a sibling takes them:** Prata or Gilda Display (both single-weight display serifs with a window-lettering contrast) for the headings, and Albert Sans or Golos Text for the body. All four are on Google Fonts under the OFL and free of the fonts the wave lists as taken.

## Palette

Towel white and lather, espresso ink, a deep blue-green like the disinfectant jar on every barber's counter, and a vermilion kept for the pole and the open dot. Every pair of text and background passes WCAG AA, measured from these values: ink on towel white 15.1:1, ink on lather 13.4:1, Barbicide on towel white 7.9:1, light text on Barbicide 8.0:1, ink on the pale wash 12.0:1, Barbicide on the wash 6.3:1, vermilion on towel white 4.7:1, light text on vermilion 5.0:1, light text on night 13.7:1. **The four steps of the fade** are not theme variables: `styles/site.css` makes them from the primary and the surface (`color-mix(in oklch, var(--color-primary) 18%, var(--color-base-100))` and so on), so the Theme tab restyles them with the rest. Measured with this palette: step 1 (about oklch(0.885 0.022 215)) and step 2 (oklch(0.77 0.04 215)) carry ink, 11.9:1 and 8.2:1; step 3 (oklch(0.53 0.07 215)) and step 4 (the primary) carry light text, 4.7:1 and 8.0:1. The design notes will say to keep that split if the palette changes.

Other settings: relief off (`--theme-depth: 0`); density spacious (`--theme-size: 0.28125rem`); fine borders (1px); buttons as full pills (`rounded-full`), the one round shape besides the pole and the mirror. The status colors are derived, as always; the form's errors use them.

It is not Mend's lake on warm white with terracotta, Margin's single green, Corner's warm paper with cobalt, nor Trestle's limewash and olive: a cooler blue-green on a white that is only just warm, with one hot mark.

## Motion

Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode nothing moves and everything is in its final place: every step of the fade in its tone, the pole still, the open line showing the week in words, each cut's mirror small in its corner and its back photo replaceable there. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too.

- **The fade** (`data-fade` on a grid or a row of steps, CSS only): each step carries `style="--step: 3"` (no color in the style) and its tone comes from the stylesheet. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference` and `@supports (animation-timeline: view())`, the steps start at the surface's tone and darken to their own as the grid enters the screen, step 1 first and step 4 last (`animation-range` offset by `--step`), the figures in each column fading in with their step. Elsewhere, and in edit mode, the steps are simply in their tones. A thin four-step band (`data-fade="band"`) closes the hero and the price grid, the same steps in a row.
- **The open sign** (`data-open-sign`, `scripts/open-sign.js`, no library):
  - the sign holds the pole and three lines, all written in the template: "Open now, until {{ today.closes }}", "Closed now, opens {{ next.day }} at {{ next.opens }}" for each day, and the neutral line, "Tuesday to Sunday · walk-ins Tuesday to Friday mornings". With no script and in edit mode only the neutral line shows;
  - Liquid writes the week onto the sign as data (`data-week` from the `hours` records' `opens` and `closes`, and `data-tz="America/New_York"`, which the maker's city changes); the script works out from the shop's time zone, not the visitor's, whether it is open, sets `data-state="open"` or `"closed"` and `data-next` on the sign, and the stylesheet shows the matching line. It checks again every minute; `stop` clears the timer and the attributes;
  - the pole's stripes slide upward (a `background-position` animation, 6 s a turn, linear) only when `[data-state="open"]`, under no-preference and `html[data-up-moving]`; closed, or under reduced motion, the pole stands still.
- **The week at the chair** (`data-week-chart`, CSS, and the same module): each day is a row with its bar placed by `style="--opens: 600; --closes: 1200"` (minutes since midnight) on a scale from 8 am to 9 pm, and its walk-in part striped by `--walk-in-from` and `--walk-in-until`; the hours are written in each row as text too. The script marks today's row (`data-today`) and sets `--now` on the chart, which draws a fine vermilion line at the current time; with no script there is no now line and no row is marked. As the chart enters view the bars grow from their opening time to their closing time (`scale: 0 1` from the left, CSS scroll-driven), one row after another.
- **The hand mirror** (`data-mirror` on a cut card holding two `<img>`, the back marked `data-back`; CSS only):
  - at rest the back photo sits in an oval about a third of the card's width at its lower right corner, with a 3px vermilion rim and a short handle drawn in CSS; this is how it shows with no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode, so the maker can replace either photo;
  - on `:hover` and `:focus-within` of the card, under no-preference, the oval grows (`clip-path: ellipse()`, 0.5 s, `ease: cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)`) until the back fills the card, its rim fading out, and the label under the card changes from "Front" to "Back"; leaving the card shrinks it again. Under reduced motion the back simply replaces the front, with no growth;
  - on a phone there is no hover: the mirror stays in its corner and the cut's own page shows both photos large.
- **Reveals** (`data-reveal`, `scripts/reveal.js`, Motion's `inView`): a section's main elements rise a little (`y: [16, 0]`, `opacity: [0, 1]`, 0.7 s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`), their children 70 ms apart under `data-reveal="stagger"`, as the site-scripts skill shows.
- **The request, filled in from the link** (`data-prefill`, `scripts/prefill.js`): "Book with Mo" leads to `/book?with=mo-bell`, a cut's "Book this cut" to `/book?service=skin-fade&with=tasha-greer`, a level's column to `/book?level=2`; the script checks the matching options of the form. It writes no text and moves nothing; with no script nothing is checked in advance.
- **The cut book's filter** (CSS only): the hair type chips (All, Straight, Wavy, Curly, Coily) are radio inputs and `:has()` keeps the cuts of the chosen type; with "All" checked by default, the page shows every cut with no script and in edit mode.
- **The price grid on a phone** (CSS only): four chips, one per level, each in its step's tone, are radio inputs; `:has()` shows the chosen level's column beside the chair times. Level 2 is checked by default; on wider screens all four columns show and the chips are hidden.
- **The header** is sticky on towel white at 95% with a hairline under it; it does not change as you scroll.

No smooth scrolling library, no page transitions, no loader, no WebGL. Library: Motion, pinned on jsDelivr, under MIT. Everything else is CSS.

## What Upstart cannot do here, and how the brief handles it

- **A booking calendar:** a chair is a request with the days and part of day that suit, confirmed by text; a shop with Squire, Booksy, Fresha or another booking page links out to it (see What varies), never embeds its widget.
- **Photos with the request:** a form cannot take files; the note and the answer ask clients to text a photo of the cut they want.
- **Deposits, payments and gift cards:** nothing is paid on the site; there is no deposit, and gift cards are sold at the desk.
- **Products:** sold at the desk, or through the maker's own store elsewhere, linked.
- **Reviews from Google or Yelp:** no widget and no stars; three quotes copied in with permission, and a link to the shop's page there if the maker wants one.
- **A map:** no embed; "Get directions" links to the address on a map site, beside the written directions.
- **The live hours:** the open sign works out "open now" in the visitor's browser from the shop's time zone and the `hours` records; holidays are a note on the day, and a closed day is a record with no hours. The guide tells the agent to set `data-tz` to the maker's city.
