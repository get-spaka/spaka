# Saucer: brief

One of wave 3's four standard templates: the daytime café, breakfast, lunch and coffee (`business`, not one of the original designs). Its motion is quiet and useful: the door card that turns to "Come in, we're open" or "Sorry, we're closed", a menu that opens on what the kitchen is serving, plates set down on the table one by one.

Corrected after the author's decision Q349: no sun, light, shadow or time of day as an image (the sun is Gnomon's); the hours are the café door's glass, with its hanging card.

`saucer` is a working name: what a cup stands on, and the round shape that runs through the site. A web search found no website template or theme of that name (only a design student's concept page, "Saucer & Kettle"); the Flying Saucer restaurants are a beer-hall chain, and the name is the template's, not the business's.

## Concept

**Concept.** A café you read like its front door. The site has a soft, round serif, a typed menu, milk-white pages, one marmalade color like an egg yolk and a pale glass blue. The opening hours are typed on the door's glass, week by week as on a café door, with a two-sided card hanging on its string: "Come in, we're open" on one side, "Sorry, we're closed" on the other. The card turns to the side that is true in the café's own town, and a visitor can turn it by hand to read the other. The whole site knows what is served: the header says "Breakfast till 11:30" or "Lunch till 2:30", and the menu opens on what the kitchen is serving. The dishes are round photos seen from above, plates set down on the table as you scroll.

It must not read as Corner or Ember. Corner is a bakery's shopfront: bread and cakes to take home, poster capitals, cobalt, an arch, a ribbon and a turning stamp. Ember is a dark evening room with a table to book. Saucer is a kitchen with tables, open in daylight only, walk-in, whose menu changes at half past eleven.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Oriel**, a breakfast and lunch café at 2 Larkspur Row (an invented street) in the Marquette neighborhood of Madison, Wisconsin, a block from Williamson Street and two from Lake Monona.
- **The place:** a 1912 corner pharmacy whose upstairs bay window, the oriel, gave the café its name. Inside, 38 seats around the old pharmacy's wooden counter, a long window bench, and a patio of 16 seats from May to October. Bike racks at the door; the lake path two blocks south.
- **The people:** opened in 2018 by **Tessa Brandvold**, a cook who grew up on a dairy farm near Mount Horeb and cooked breakfast in Chicago for ten years, and **Amir Haddad**, who ran a Milwaukee roaster's coffee bar. **June Xiong** bakes from 5 am; **Dario Peña** runs the line at lunch.
- **The day:** open Wednesday to Monday, closed Tuesdays. Weekdays 7 am to 3 pm: breakfast until 11:30, lunch from 11:30 to 2:30, then coffee and the bakery case until 3. Saturday and Sunday 8 am to 3 pm, breakfast all day until 2:30.
- **The food:** eggs from a farm near Mount Horeb, greens and the Hmong growers' lemongrass sausage from the Saturday farmers' market on the Capitol Square, cheddar from a Green County creamery, wild rice from northern Wisconsin, sour cherries from Door County, and bread from its own oven. Breakfast from $10 to $16, lunch from $11 to $17. A special changes each week; on Fridays, a walleye sandwich.
- **The coffee:** espresso and a pour-over of the week, roasted across the isthmus by an invented roaster, Ledgerock Coffee; house chai; fresh orange juice.
- **How it works:** walk in, order at the counter, take a number to your table. No reservations; weekend mornings are busiest from 9 to 11. Laptops welcome on weekdays, not on weekend mornings. Dogs on the patio.
- **Beyond the counter:** pastry boxes and breakfast and lunch for offices, ordered 48 hours ahead with a request form, picked up from 6:30 am or delivered within three miles for orders over $150, paid at pickup or on delivery. Gift cards at the counter.

The name was searched: no café called Oriel came up in Madison. No existing template is set in Madison.

The same structure suits a brunch spot, a café with a kitchen, a diner, a bagel or bakery café that serves lunch, a deli, a juice and breakfast bar, a coffee shop with a lunch counter, a tea room, or a museum's or garden's café. The agent rewrites the dishes, the hours and their services, the suppliers, the people and the town.

This is the home page's first choice in What varies.

**What makes it its own:**
- **the door card:** a two-sided card on a string, the kind every café hangs in its door: marmalade "Come in, we're open" on one side, coffee-brown "Sorry, we're closed" on the other, each with its small typed line ("Breakfast till 11:30", "Back Wednesday at 7"). It turns over to the side that is true when it comes into view, and a tap turns it to read the other side. It turns flat on its string; it does not swing (Linen's keys swing). The trend's touchable object (the swinging lamp, the bagel sticker), made into the site's answer to "are you open?";
- **the door's glass:** the hours typed on a pale blue pane in a narrow wooden frame, as vinyl letters on a café door: the name, then the week day by day in mono, each day with its services ("Wed–Fri 7–3 · breakfast till 11:30 · lunch till 2:30"), today's line marked by a chive dot, the card hanging at the top of the pane. Not ruled rows (Corner's hours board), not bars on a chart (Taper's);
- **served now:** the whole site knows what the kitchen is serving, from one attribute on the page: the header's line ("Breakfast till 11:30", "Lunch till 2:30", "Coffee and the bakery case till 3", "Opening at 7"), a "Now" mark on the menu's tab, and the menu opening on that tab. The times are the maker's own, copied from the hours on the page;
- **plates on the table:** every dish is a round photo seen from above on a pale saucer rim; featured dishes are set down on an oat tabletop one after another as they come into view, each with a small turn, and turn a little more under the hand;
- **the typed menu:** dishes, prices, add-ons and hours set in a monospaced face, like the kitchen's order slips, under headings in a soft, round serif.

**What it leaves out:**
- online ordering, a waitlist and payments (Spaka has none; see below), a map embed, an Instagram feed, video, sound and 3D (Sunbeam's bagel drop, the cake cabinet);
- **other templates' devices:** a ribbon or ticker, a turning stamp, arch frames and an "Open now" pill as a written sentence (Corner); a light following the pointer, an hour pinned while the page scrolls and a tilted menu card (Ember); spinning discs and anything sliding out of a sleeve (Encore: the plates never spin, they are set down); lines drawing themselves (Chalk, Seam); anything scrolling sideways (Reel); page transitions; a huge wordmark across the footer (Corner, Ember, Seam); handwriting (Commons's notes, Haven's lettering); an italic accent word in a heading (Corner, Folio, Linen); the sun, light, shadows or the time of day as an image (Gnomon); hours as a week chart with a now line (Taper); a status band (Aspect);
- smooth scrolling: the browser's own scroll, for a visitor checking the hours on a phone at a bus stop.

## Type

All three are files of the template, as `woff2` subset to Latin and Latin Extended (for names such as Peña), under the SIL Open Font License, from Google Fonts, declared in `styles/site.css`. Caprasimo has one weight and no italic, so the headings have none; Spline Sans and Spline Sans Mono ship as variable files (300 to 700), the mono with its italic for the menu's notes.

| Role | Font | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Headings | Caprasimo | its one weight (400), never synthesized bold; the hero at `clamp(3rem, 6.5vw + 1rem, 6.5rem)`, `leading-[0.98]`, `tracking-[-0.01em]`, `text-balance`; section headings at `clamp(2rem, 3vw + 1rem, 3.5rem)`; small headings (a dish on its own, a box's name) at 1.5 rem; sentence case |
| Body | Spline Sans | 400, 1.0625 rem, `leading-relaxed`, `text-base-content/80`, measure `max-w-[60ch]`; buttons 600 at 1 rem |
| The menu, prices, hours and labels | Spline Sans Mono | 400 and 500: a dish's name at 1 rem in 500, its line at 0.9375 rem in 400, prices `tabular-nums` on the right after a dotted leader; labels above headings and the header's now line at 0.8125 rem, sentence case with `tracking-[0.02em]` rather than spaced capitals; the add-ons and diet marks in the italic |
| The door card and figures | Caprasimo | "Come in, we're open" and "Sorry, we're closed" on the card at 1.75 to 2.25 rem; a box's "serves 12" at 2 to 3 rem |

Caprasimo is a soft, chunky serif in the line of the 1920s fat faces that came back on breakfast menus and coffee bags: round, generous, a little funny, made for display. Spline Sans Mono, drawn beside its sans by the same designers, is a friendly mono without a typewriter's grit, so a long menu stays readable; the pair is the trend's display and mono, with a plain sans for the rest.

**If a sibling template takes them:** Corben (700, a rounded Cooper-like serif) for headings with Albert Sans and Reddit Mono; or Gloock (a softer high-contrast display) with Karla and Sometype Mono. All on Google Fonts under the OFL, none used by waves 1 and 2.

## Motion

Quiet and useful. Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode nothing moves: Spaka's stillness rule stops CSS animations and transitions and no script runs, so the door card shows its plain side, no day on the door is marked, the menu shows its first tab and every plate is in place. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too.

- **What is served** (`scripts/served-now.js`, no library): the one script that reads the clock. Nothing it drives shows a time of day as a picture: it only chooses among the template's own lines, the card's side and the menu's tab.
  - it reads the hours from the page: the footer, on every page, renders each `hours` record as a row with `data-weekday`, `data-opens`, `data-lunch-from`, `data-kitchen-closes` and `data-closes` (24-hour times, as the records hold them), and the café's time zone from `data-tz` on the footer ("America/Chicago"; without it, the visitor's own clock);
  - it sets `data-now` on `<html>`: `before` (not open yet today), `breakfast`, `lunch`, `coffee` (the kitchen closed, the counter open), `after` (closed for the day) or `closed` (closed today), and `data-today` (the weekday). The stylesheet does the rest: the header, the hero and the menu each hold a line per state, written in the template, and show the one that matches (`html[data-now="lunch"] [data-when="lunch"]`). With no script, no `data-now`: each shows its plain line ("Open 7 to 3, Wednesday to Monday");
  - it writes no sentence: the times inside a line are empty slots (`<span data-slot="lunch_from">`) into which it copies the matching time from today's footer row, which the page shows in the visitors' way (7 am, 11:30 am) through a small part, `parts/time.liquid`;
  - it checks the time again every minute while the page is open, and stops that in `stop`, which also removes `data-now` and `data-today`.
- **The door card** (`data-door-card`, CSS, Motion's `animate`, in `scripts/served-now.js`):
  - a card of two faces on a string, in the hero and on the door's glass: the front, marmalade, "Come in, we're open" with its typed line; the back, coffee, "Sorry, we're closed" with "Back Wednesday at 7" (the day and time copied into slots from the next open footer row). The faces are stacked with `backface-visibility: hidden`, the back turned `rotateY(180deg)`; the card itself is a `<button>` with `aria-pressed` and an `aria-label` read from the face shown, so it is reachable and says its side;
  - its stylesheet state is the front, whose plain line, shown without `data-now`, says the week ("Open Wednesday to Monday"), so edit mode and a page with no script never claim the café is open;
  - in visitor mode, when the card comes into view, the script turns it to the true side (`rotateY: [0, 180]` for closed, 0.9 s, `ease: [0.65, 0, 0.35, 1]`, flat on its string, no swing); open, it stays on the front and its line becomes "Breakfast till 11:30";
  - a tap or Enter turns it over (0.6 s) to read the other side, and again to come back; under reduced motion it changes side without turning;
  - `stop` puts it back on its front and removes `aria-pressed`.
- **The door's glass** (`data-door`, CSS only): the pane is a glass-blue panel in a narrow coffee frame, the card hanging from a short espresso string at its top; the hours are typed on it in mono, one line per `hours` record, the closed day struck through, each with its services from the record's times. The script marks today's line (`html[data-today="wed"] [data-weekday="wed"]`) with a chive dot. Nothing else moves on it.
- **Served now on the menu** (`data-served-now`, in `scripts/served-now.js`): the menu's tabs are radio inputs, each with `data-service` (breakfast, lunch, drinks, pastries); the script checks the tab of the service of the hour: breakfast before opening and in the morning, lunch at noon, drinks once the kitchen is closed, breakfast after closing and on a closed day ("what comes first next time"). A chive dot and "Now" show on that tab by CSS (`html[data-now="lunch"] [data-service="lunch"] [data-now-mark]`). With no script, the first tab is checked in the markup and no tab says "Now".
- **The plates** (`data-plate` on a dish's round photo, `data-plates` on their list; `scripts/plates.js`, Motion's `inView`):
  - in CSS, a plate is the photo cropped to a circle (`aspect-square rounded-full object-cover`) inside a rim of oat (`ring-[0.6rem] ring-base-200`) and a hairline beyond it, like a saucer; with no photo, the rim around a sky-to-oat gradient and a small cup drawn in espresso;
  - as the list comes into view, the script sets each plate down in turn: `scale: [1.06, 1]`, `rotate: [-8, 0]`, `opacity: [0, 1]`, 0.7 s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`, 90 ms apart, as if served; the hero's large plate is set down the same way on load;
  - on a pointer that hovers, a plate turns 10 degrees over 1.2 s (a CSS transition, under no-preference and `(hover: hover)`), as a plate is nudged on the table;
  - with no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode, every plate is in place and still. `stop` takes off the styles it set.
- **The steam** (`data-steam` on the cup in the logo, CSS only): three soft, blurred ovals rise 6 px above the cup and fade, twice when the page loads, then rest; they rise again when the logo is hovered or focused. They are shapes, not lines that draw. Under no-preference only; otherwise, and in edit mode, the cup without steam.
- **Reveals** (`data-reveal`, `scripts/reveal.js`, Motion's `inView`): a section's main elements rise a little (`y: [16, 0]`, `opacity: [0, 1]`, 0.8 s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`), children 80 ms apart under `data-reveal="stagger"`, as in the site-scripts skill.
- **The header** is sticky on milk at 95% with a hairline under it, its thin top strip carrying the now line; it does not change as you scroll.

No smooth scrolling, no page transitions, no loader, no WebGL. Library: Motion, pinned on jsDelivr, under MIT. Everything else is CSS.

## Ordering, bookings and what stays outside

Spaka has no ordering, no payments, no calendar and no embeds, so:
- **guests walk in** (as built). A café that takes bookings for weekend brunch gets a booking request form; one that sells online sends every "Order ahead" button to its own ordering page (Toast, Square…), a link that leaves the site, never its widget (see What varies);
- **catering is a request,** confirmed by the café by email, paid at pickup or on delivery; the page says so before and after sending;
- **no map:** "Get directions" links to the address on a map site, beside written directions;
- **no Instagram feed:** a link to the café's account in the footer;
- **gift cards are sold at the counter;** a café selling them online links out to its own page;
- **the time zone:** the door card and the now line follow the café's clock, not the visitor's, through `data-tz`; the guide tells the agent to set it from the maker's town.

## What varies

The choices the agent asks when the maker's messages leave them open, page type by page type, the recommended one first. The hours, the services' times, the prices, the delivery radius and the notice for catering are facts: the agent asks for them once the page is built, or leaves them out rather than guess.

### Home (`/`), with the choices for the whole site

#### How do guests eat with you?
- **They walk in and order at the counter** (as built): no booking and no ordering online; "Find us" is the header's button, and the door says "Walk in: no reservations".
- **They can also order ahead for pickup online:** an "Order ahead" button in the header and on each menu tab links to the café's own ordering page; the hero's second button becomes "Order ahead"; the questions say how.
- **They book a table at weekends:** the Visit page gets a booking request form (adapted from `apps/desktop/gallery/forms/forms-booking-table`, posting to a collected `reservations` with its thank-you fragment), "Book a table" becomes the header's button, and the line beside the door says "Walk in on weekdays; book for weekends". Requests are confirmed by the café.

#### How is the day divided?
- **Breakfast, then lunch** (as built): the menu's Breakfast and Lunch tabs, the header's line switching at `lunch_from`, breakfast and lunch typed for each day on the door's glass.
- **One menu all day** (brunch all day, or a diner): the menu's tabs become its courses (Plates, Sandwiches, Sweet, Sides, Drinks); `lunch_from` goes and the door lists one kitchen time per day; the now line says "Kitchen open till 2:30"; `data-served-now` goes.
- **Coffee and the bakery case only** (a coffee shop with a counter of pastries and a few toasts): the Breakfast and Lunch tabs go, the menu's tabs are Coffee, Tea & other drinks and The bakery case; the door lists the counter's hours only; the plates show pastries; "On the pass today" becomes "In the case today".

When adapting, the time zone in `data-tz` follows the maker's town, and the times are shown as the maker's visitors read them: `parts/time.liquid` switches to 24-hour times (7:00, 14:30) for a site in French, German or most of Europe.

### Menu (`/menu`)

- **The dishes that have one** (as built): plates on the home page and in the menu's rows; a dish without a photo is a typed row only.
