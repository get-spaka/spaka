# Trestle: brief

One of wave 2's two standard templates: the renovation craftsman (`business`, not one of the original designs, though it moves quietly).

`trestle` is a working name: a trestle is the carpenter's sawhorse, what the work stands on.

## Concept

**Concept.** A joiner's drawing laid over the work. The site reads like a measured drawing of an old house put right: calm type on a limewash page, fine dimension lines drawn along the photos with their figures in a small technical type, each project given its own page with its spec, its weeks and its client's words, and the house before and after under one line the visitor can move. The firm's patch is drawn as rings of driving time around the workshop, and asking for a site visit is never more than one tap away.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Ostrom & Kemp**, a joinery and renovation firm in a brick carriage barn at 61 Union Street in Hudson, New York, a river town of Federal and Victorian houses two hours up the Hudson from Manhattan. It was founded in 2013 by Ida Ostrom, a cabinetmaker who spent ten years in a Brooklyn millwork shop, and Calvin Kemp, a carpenter who grew up restoring old houses in Catskill with his father. Nine people work there: four at the benches, four on site, one who runs the office and the calendar. They work on the Hudson Valley's old houses, from Dutch stone farmhouses of the 1700s to Federal and Victorian row houses and 1920s bungalows. In the workshop they make kitchens and pantries, built-in bookcases and window seats, stairs and doors, from white oak, ash and cherry from a sawmill upstate and old-growth pine saved from barns. On site they restore sash windows, rebuild porches, relay floors and take on whole-house renovations with the plumbers and electricians they have worked with for years. They work within about 45 minutes of the workshop and take on ten to twelve projects a year. The first visit is free; a measured drawing and a written price follow within three weeks; they come back a year after handing over, because wood moves with the seasons. They are booking work for spring 2027.

The same structure suits a builder, a carpenter, a cabinetmaker, a kitchen maker, a window restorer, a stonemason, a timber framer, a painter and decorator or a landscaper who builds: any trade that shows its work before and after and travels to its clients. The agent rewrites the projects, the services, the towns and the people.

The name was searched once: no firm called Ostrom & Kemp, in Hudson or elsewhere, came up. Wave 2's physiotherapy template, Mend, is in Kingston, Ontario, so this one is not in Kingston, New York.

**What makes it its own:**
- **the measured photograph:** fine dimension lines with end ticks drawn along the edges of a photo and under figures, their measures in Chivo Mono ("12′ 4″", "11 weeks", "1889"), drawn out from their middle as they come into view;
- **the scribe:** the house before and after in one frame, divided by a fine line with a raw-oak handle; when it first comes into view the line sweeps across once, from the room as found to the room finished, then rests in the middle and follows the visitor's hand or arrow keys. It is shown only where both photos were taken from the same spot; otherwise the two stand side by side;
- **the drive-time rings:** the area served as three rings of 15, 30 and 45 minutes around the workshop, each town a numbered point placed by its minutes and its direction, the rings growing from the center as the section comes into view;
- **the build ruler:** on each project page, the weeks of the job as a tape measure, its stages ticked at their week;
- **the title block:** the footer drawn as the title block of a joinery drawing, ruled cells with small labels (the firm, the address, the hours, the license and insurance, the links), echoed by the spec at the top of each project page.

**What it leaves out:**
- stock contractor pictures (hard hats, handshakes, posed power tools), badges and "free quote" bursts, a chat or WhatsApp button, a map embed, video;
- a ticker or ribbon (Corner's), pictures following the cursor (Folio's), anything that scrolls sideways (Reel's), page transitions (Folio's and Ember's), a huge wordmark across the footer (Corner's and Ember's), handwritten notes (Margin's margin notes).

## Type

Both families are files of the template, under the SIL Open Font License, from Google Fonts, as variable fonts (`Chivo[wght]` and `ChivoMono[wght]`, roman only), declared in `styles/site.css`. A grotesk with its own mono, as on storeyarchitecture.co.uk: the mono is the drawing's lettering.

| Role | Font | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Headings | Chivo | 300 at display sizes, 400 below 2.5 rem, never bold; the hero at `clamp(3rem, 7vw, 6.5rem)`, section headings 2.25 to 3.75 rem, `leading-[1.02]`, `tracking-[-0.025em]`, sentence case |
| Body | Chivo | 400, 1.0625 rem, `leading-relaxed`, `text-base-content/80`; the client's words in 300 at 1.5 to 2.25 rem |
| Labels, specs, dimensions | Chivo Mono | 400 to 500, 0.75 rem, uppercase, `tracking-[0.06em]`, `tabular-nums`: the eyebrow above a heading, a project's spec, the dimension figures, the minutes of the rings, the weeks of the ruler |
| Large figures | Chivo Mono | 300, 3 to 5 rem: a project's weeks, the workshop's figures, the step numbers |

None of the wave's other briefs uses Chivo (Encore names Chivo Mono only among its alternatives). **If a sibling took them,** two pairings with the same character, both OFL and free of wave 1 and of the wave's other choices: **Reddit Sans with Reddit Mono**, another grotesk with its own mono; or **Libre Franklin** (light at display sizes, the American trade sign's gothic) with **Sometype Mono** for the labels.

## Motion

Quiet and exact, like the work. Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode nothing moves and everything is in its final place: every line drawn, every ring and town shown, every pair of photos side by side, editable. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too.

- **The scribe** (`data-scribe` on a figure holding two `<img>`, the before marked `data-before`; `scripts/scribe.js`, Motion):
  - with no script and in edit mode, the two photos stand side by side (stacked on a phone), labeled Before and After in mono, each a plain image the maker can replace;
  - in visitor mode, when the record's `same_view` is on, the script lays the after over the before, clipped at `--split`, and adds a native `<input type="range">` over the frame, named from the figure's `data-scribe-label` (the text stays in the template), so dragging, touch and arrow keys move the line; the handle is a raw-oak tab with a fine line through the frame;
  - the first time the frame comes into view (Motion's `inView`), `--split` goes from 100% (the room as found) to 50% over 1.6 s, `ease: [0.65, 0, 0.35, 1]`; under reduced motion it starts at 50%, and the slider still works;
  - `stop` removes the input and the listeners and puts the photos back side by side;
  - a before photo is always shown a little faded (`[data-before]`: `filter: saturate(0.6) contrast(0.95)`, no color of its own): the house as found.
- **`data-measure`, the dimension lines:** a fine line with a tick at each end and its figure in the middle, along a photo's edge (`data-measure="top"`, `"side"`) or under a figure. In CSS: under `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` and `@supports (animation-timeline: view())`, the line scales from its middle (`scale: 0 1` to its stylesheet state) as it enters the screen, then its figure fades in; the hero's lines play once on load instead (`data-measure-on="load"`, a CSS animation with a 0.3 s delay). Elsewhere, and in edit mode, they are simply drawn.
- **The drive-time rings** (`data-rings`, CSS only): each town is a point with `style="--min: 25; --deg: 60"` (minutes and compass bearing from the workshop, no color in the style), placed by `rotate` and `translate` from the center; the 15, 30 and 45 minute rings grow from 0.85 to full size one after another as the section enters view, and the towns appear nearest first (`animation-delay` from `--min`). A town's name shows beside its point on hover and focus, and in the numbered list beside the rings. With no scroll-driven animations, under reduced motion and in edit mode, all is drawn.
- **The build ruler** (`data-ruler`, CSS only): a line with a small tick for every week (a `repeating-linear-gradient` of `currentColor`) and a tall tick at each stage, placed by `style="--week: 3; --weeks: 11"`; it draws from left to right as it enters view, the stages' labels following. The home page's process uses the same ruler, its steps evenly spaced.
- **`data-reveal`:** a section's main elements rise a little (`y: [16, 0]`, `opacity: [0, 1]`, 0.7 s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`) with Motion's `inView`, as the site-scripts skill shows.
- **The header:** limewash, with a hairline below it once the page is scrolled (a CSS scroll-driven animation on `animation-timeline: scroll()`; the stylesheet's own state, with the hairline, is what edit mode and older browsers show).
- **The work filter** (CSS only): the type chips are radio inputs, and `:has()` keeps the projects of the chosen type; with "All" checked by default, the page shows every project with no script and in edit mode.
- **Smooth scrolling:** Lenis, light (`lerp: 0.12`).

No page transitions, no loader, no WebGL. Libraries: Motion and Lenis, pinned on jsDelivr, under MIT.

## What varies

The choices the agent asks when the maker's messages leave them open, page type by page type, the recommended one first. The prices, the booking season, the drive times, the license, the insurance, the certifications and the reviews' rating and count are facts: the agent asks for them once the page is built, or leaves them out rather than guess.

**Home** (`/`), with what every page shares:

- *How do clients ask for a quote?*
  - **They request a site visit with the form** (as built): the request page's form; requests arrive in Spaka's Content tab; every "Request a visit" button points to `/quote#request`.
  - **They call first**: the phone number set large with a call button (`tel:`) in place of the form; the header's button and every request link call; `quote-requests` goes.
  - **Through the firm's own booking page elsewhere**: every request button links out to it, the form goes, and the request page keeps the steps, the area and the workshop.
- *Do you show your work before and after?*
  - **Before and after** (as built): each project has its before photo; the home page's before and after band and each project's scribe stay, the slider only where `same_view` is on.
  - **Finished work only**: the band shows the first project's finished photo with its dimension lines; project pages show their photos without a comparison; the `before` and `same_view` fields go.
- *Is there a workshop?*
  - **A workshop where the pieces are made** (as built): the home page's workshop section, the services' two columns (made in the workshop, done in your house) and the Workshop page.
  - **On site only** (a builder, a window restorer, a painter): the workshop section becomes the crew, the services stand in one column, and the Workshop page becomes About, the story and the people (`/about`).
  - **The workshop only** (a cabinetmaker, a kitchen maker who fits what they make): whole-house work, porches and windows go from the services and the project types; the area's note says pieces are delivered and fitted further away.

**Work** (`/work`, with the project pages):

- *A page for each project?*
  - **Yes, a page each** (as built): each project opens on its own page with its spec, its before and after, its story, its ruler and its details.
  - **The gallery only**: the projects with their photos and spec lines, and no pages; the cards are no longer links, the record's text and stages go, and its summary shows under its photo.
- *When left out:* the home page keeps its before and after band and selected work over `projects`, without their links to the work page or to the projects' pages; the navigation record goes.

- *Where do the reviews come from?*
  - **Chosen by the maker** (as built): clients' words the maker copies in, with their permission, in the Content tab (`testimonials`), with a link to the firm's page on Google or Houzz.
  - **Clients leave them on the site**: a form with stars below the reviews posts to a collected `reviews` datasource with `review: true`, so the maker approves each one before it shows (adapted from `apps/desktop/gallery/forms/forms-reviews-stars`).
  - **Only a link out**: three chosen quotes and a button to the firm's page on Google or Houzz; the list and the average go.
- *When left out:* the home page's quotes keep `testimonials`; their "All reviews" link goes.

- *Who appears on the page?*
  - **The whole team** (as built): six portraits, or initials on sage until the maker sends photos.
  - **The founders only**: one or two people shown large with a word each (adapted from `apps/desktop/gallery/team/team-founder-featured`).
  - **Nobody by name**: the story, the wood and the credentials only; `team` goes.

## What Spaka cannot do here, and how the brief handles it

- **Photos with the request:** visitors cannot send files through a form. The answer asks them to reply to the confirmation email with photos and plans.
- **A booking calendar:** the site visit is a request with a preferred time (weekday morning or afternoon, Saturday morning), confirmed by phone; a firm with its own online calendar links out to it.
- **Deposits and payments:** nothing is paid on the site; the questions say when the deposit is invoiced (once the drawing is agreed).
- **Reviews from Google or Houzz:** no embed; chosen quotes are copied in with permission, or clients leave them on the site for the maker to approve, and a link goes to the firm's page there.
- **A map:** no embed; the rings are drawn from the `area` records, and "Get directions" links to a maps site.
