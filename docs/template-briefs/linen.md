# Linen: brief

One of wave 2's original designs: the boutique hotel or guesthouse (`business`, one of the original, animated designs). Linen is its working name; the author may change it.

## Concept

**Concept.** A guesthouse seen the way you wake in it: a sheer linen curtain moves in the breeze across the window onto the courtyard, and draws aside as you scroll into the house. The rooms hang as keys on the reception's board, each key dyed in its room's color, and they swing when a draft goes past or a hand reaches for one. Each room has its own page, dyed the same color. The breakfast table, the town and the house's story come next, and a request for your dates is never more than one scroll away.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Casa Hilo**, a guesthouse of five rooms around a courtyard at Calle del Telar 14 (an invented street) in the Barrio de Jalatlaco, Oaxaca de Juárez, Mexico, twelve minutes on foot from Santo Domingo.
- **The house:** it was built in 1871 for the Vásquez family, dyers who boiled cochineal and indigo for the valley's weavers in the stone basins that still stand in the courtyard. From 1932 a tailor cut suits in the front room. It then stood closed for forty years, until Inés Bautista and Theo Marsh bought it in 2018. Inés grew up among her family's looms in Teotitlán del Valle; Theo is an architect from Minneapolis. They restored it with masons from the barrio and opened in November 2021.
- **The rooms:** five, named after the dyes the house once used: Cochineal, Indigo, Marigold, Walnut and Undyed (the roof room, with its own terrace). They cost from $150 to $240 a night with breakfast, and their linen is woven on the Bautista looms.
- **Food:** breakfast every morning on the roof. Mezcal at six in the courtyard, from an honor bar of small producers in Matatlán. On Thursdays, a long supper for twelve cooked by Marisol Cruz.
- **The whole house:** it can be taken by one group of up to eleven guests, for a retreat or a family gathering, and the courtyard by up to thirty people for a dinner.
- **Outings:** the house arranges a few: a day at the looms in Teotitlán, a market morning with Marisol, a palenque visit and Monte Albán at opening time.
- **A letter each season** goes to past and future guests.

**Booking is a request, not a sale.** Upstart has no payments and no booking engine, and the template does not pretend to have them:
- **as built**, guests send a request for their dates through a form: arrival, departure, guests, the room or no preference, and a stay or the whole house. The hosts answer by email within a day with the room and the price. They also say how to pay the deposit of one night, by a payment link or a transfer that they send themselves, outside the site;
- **for a house with its own booking engine**, every "Book" button can link to that outside booking page instead;
- **without either**, guests can be sent to email, phone or WhatsApp links.

There is no availability calendar, no embedded map and no review widget. The hero's booking bar carries its dates to the request form through the address. How to get there is written out, with an "Open in Maps" link. Guests' words are records the hosts write themselves.

The same structure suits a bed and breakfast, a country inn, a farm stay, a seaside guesthouse, a mountain lodge, a city boutique hotel of up to about twenty rooms, or a holiday house let whole. The agent rewrites the house, the rooms and their colors, the table and the town.

**What makes it its own:**
- **the sheer:** a linen curtain over the window's photo, drawn in WebGL, moving in the breeze and drawn aside as you scroll. It is not a light following the pointer (Ember's) but fabric, time and wind;
- **the key board:** the rooms hang as keys on a rail, each fob dyed in its room's color. They swing in turn as the board comes into view and one by one under the hand. Each room's page is dyed in the same color, and its picture grows from the board into the page;
- **the textile details:** a fine weave on the linen ground, buttons with a stitched seam, and a sewn thread lying still between chapters (a line drawing itself as you scroll is Chalk's in this wave).

**What it leaves out:**
- a preloader or an opening of doors (seen on hotelaurelia.online), and a video hero;
- anything an outside service would provide: a booking widget, an availability calendar, a map, a webcam, an Instagram feed or review scores;
- a ribbon or marquee (Corner's), an hour pinned while the page scrolls (Ember's), anything that scrolls sideways (Reel's), and pictures that follow the cursor (Folio's);
- tilted cards and stickers: things here hang straight once they settle.

## Type

Both fonts are files of the template, in `fonts/` with their license files, under the SIL Open Font License, from Google Fonts. Fraunces ships its roman and its italic, both variable; Jost ships its roman.

The name in the header is Jost 500 in spaced capitals (`tracking-[0.3em]`), as on a hotel's linen tag. The header's links and buttons stay on one line (`whitespace-nowrap`) with labels a third longer, and the menu button takes over below the width where they all fit.

**Other settings:**
- buttons and fields have the same small corners (`--theme-radius-field: 0.25rem`); every `.btn` carries a dashed seam inside its edge (`outline: 1px dashed`, currentColor at 55%, `outline-offset: -5px`);
- relief is off (`--theme-depth: 0`);
- density is spacious (`--theme-size: 0.28125rem`);

**A room's color** is a choice field on the room (`tint`: primary, secondary, accent, neutral or linen), written as `data-tint` and turned into `--tint` and `--tint-content` by `styles/site.css` from the theme's variables. It never becomes a class name. The Theme tab still restyles all of it: the fabric takes `--color-base-100` and its shade `--color-neutral`.

## Motion

Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode nothing moves and everything is in its final place: the photo plain, the keys hanging straight, the threads drawn, every picture in view. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too.

- **The sheer** (`<canvas data-sheer aria-hidden="true">` in the hero's window, `scripts/sheer.js`, OGL, with Motion's `scroll`):
  - **the fabric:** the canvas draws only the fabric, over the photo below it. Two panels hang from a rail at the window's top and meet in the middle. They are drawn in the theme's linen, translucent (about 25 to 50% depending on the fold), with vertical folds lit at their crests and shaded in the theme's neutral at their troughs, and a fine weave;
  - **the breeze:** the folds move slowly, more at the hem than at the rail. Every few seconds a gust (noise over time) lifts the hems and opens a gap between the panels;
  - **the scroll:** as the hero scrolls up, the panels draw aside to the window's edges, bunching into tighter folds, until the courtyard is clear;
  - **the pointer:** passing over the window sends a soft ripple through the fabric, which fades within a second. On a touch screen there is only the breeze;
  - **economy:** it renders at a pixel ratio of 1.5 at most, and pauses when the window is off screen or the tab is hidden;
  - **without WebGL**, the script sets `data-sheer-css` on the window. Two CSS panels of `repeating-linear-gradient` folds in `color-mix(in oklch, var(--color-base-100) …, transparent)` then sway with a keyframe, and the script draws them aside through `--sheer-open`;
  - **under reduced motion**, one still frame: the panels gathered at both edges, nothing over the middle;
  - **with no script and in edit mode**, the photo alone;
  - **`stop`** cancels the frame loop and the listeners and clears the canvas, keeping its context for the next `start`.
- **The key board** (`data-keyboard` on the rail, `data-key` on each key, `scripts/keys.js`, Motion):
  - **its shape:** each key hangs from its hook by a ring, with its origin at the hook (`transform-origin: top center`, layout rather than motion). It is an iron key drawn in inline SVG in `currentColor`, and a fob in the room's tint carrying the number and the name;
  - **coming into view:** the keys swing in turn, as if a door had opened down the corridor (`rotate: [0, 9, -6, 3.5, -1.5, 0]`, 1.8 s, 90 ms apart);
  - **under the hand:** the pointer entering a room's card, or the keyboard's focus reaching it, swings that key away from the side it came from, with a loose spring;
  - **on a phone**, each card's key swings as the card scrolls into view;
  - **on a room's page**, the large key swings once as the page opens;
  - **under reduced motion, with no script and in edit mode**, the keys hang straight. `stop` stops the animations and the listeners and clears the rotation.
- **No page transition:** a room's page simply opens, dyed in its color, its large key swinging once. A picture flying from a list into its page is Encore's in this wave.
- **`data-settle`:** pictures and cards settle as they come into view, as if just hung on a nail. Each starts from `opacity: [0, 1]`, `y: [20, 0]` and `rotate: [-1.2, 0]` around its top center, over 1 s with `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`, using Motion's `inView` as the site-scripts skill shows. The script sets the hidden state.
- **`data-stitch`:** a thread between chapters, an inline SVG path with a loop in it, in the primary color (the accent on indigo), drawn as running stitches (a dashed stroke). It does not move: a line drawing itself as you scroll is Chalk's in this wave.
- **The weave** (static CSS on the page's ground): two `repeating-linear-gradient`s of `--color-base-content` at about 3%, crossing. It is there in edit mode too, and absent on the deep bands.
- **The header:** it sticks, and once the page is scrolled it takes the linen at 90% with a hairline beneath. This is a CSS scroll-driven animation from transparent to its stylesheet state, which edit mode and older browsers show.
- **The booking bar's dates** (`scripts/prefill.js`, no motion):
  - the bar is a plain `GET` form to `/book#request`;
  - on the Book page, the script copies `arrival`, `departure`, `guests`, `room` and `for` from the address into the request form's fields;
  - it keeps the departure's `min` a day after the arrival;
  - it writes values into fields only, never text into the page.
- **Smooth scrolling:** Lenis, light (`lerp: 0.1`).

**Libraries:** Motion and Lenis, pinned on jsDelivr, under MIT; OGL, imported in its module from jsDelivr, under the Unlicense. Splitting and Embla are not needed.

- **the people:** `team`;
- **what visitors send:**
  - `stays`, collected: kind (choice: A stay, The whole house), arrival and departure (dates), guests (number), room (choice: No preference and the five rooms' names), name, email, phone and note; answered by `stay-thanks`. Its room choices are written in its definition, since datasources do not link: they follow `rooms` by hand;
  - `newsletter`, collected: email; answered by `newsletter-thanks`.

## What varies

Each choice's first option is the one recommended.

### Home (`/`), for the whole website

**How do guests book a room?**
- **They ask for their dates here** (as built):
  - the booking bar, the Book page's request form and every "Book a room" link remain;
  - requests arrive in Upstart's Content tab, and the house answers and takes its deposit itself.
- **On the house's own booking page:**
  - every "Book a room" and "Ask for this room" goes to the outside page's address;
  - the booking bar becomes one stitched button ("See dates and prices");
  - the Book page keeps how it works, getting here and the questions, with the button in place of its form;
  - `data/stays/` and `fragments/stay-thanks.liquid` go.
- **By message:**
  - the bar and the form give way to the email, the phone and WhatsApp, set large as links (`mailto:`, `tel:`, `https://wa.me/…`);
  - `data/stays/` goes.

**How is the house let?**
- **Room by room** (as built): each room with its key, its page, its price and its place in the form's choices.
- **As a whole,** for a holiday house, a villa or a farmhouse for groups:
  - the hero and the welcome speak of the house and how many it sleeps;
  - the key board shows the bedrooms with what each sleeps, without prices or "Ask for this room", and their pages stay;
  - the form loses its room and kind fields;
  - the Book page's whole-house band goes, since the whole house is what is booked.

### Rooms (`/rooms`)

**Are prices shown on the rooms?**
- **A price from, per night** (as built): on the board and on each room's page, "breakfast included" beside it.
- **No prices:**
  - the price lines go from the board and the room pages, and `price` from `rooms`;
  - the room page says "Rates on request" beside "Ask for this room".

**When left out:**
- delete `pages/rooms/`, `rooms-every` and its navigation record;
- the home page's key board stays over `rooms`, its keys no longer link anywhere, and "All the rooms" goes;
- the form keeps its room choices.

### Book (`/book`)

Its request follows how guests book, chosen with the home page.

**Can guests take the whole house?**
- **Yes, by request** (as built): the whole-house band, and "The whole house" among the form's kinds.
- **Stays only:** the band and the form's kind field go, and the FAQ's question on groups.

**When left out:**
- delete `pages/book.liquid`, its parts, `book-steps`, `book-faq` and its navigation record;
- with the request form, the form moves to the home page under the key board (`id="request"`), and every booking link points to `/#request`;
- with an outside booking page or messages, the links point there;
- the footer keeps the address.

### Story (`/story`)

**Who keeps the house?**
- **Its hosts, by name** (as built): the welcome signed by the two of them, and the four portraits of who keeps the house.
- **A small team:**
  - the welcome is signed by the house ("Everyone at Casa Hilo");
  - the team grid leads with the manager, without the hosts' note.

**When left out:**
- delete `pages/story.liquid`, its parts, `story-milestones`, `story-makers`, `team` and its navigation record;
- the home page's house section keeps its two paragraphs and loses "Read its story".

### The table (`/table`)

**What does the house serve?**
- **Breakfast every morning, supper some nights** (as built): breakfast, the mezcal hour and Thursday supper.
- **Breakfast only:**
  - the mezcal and supper sections go, with `table-supper`;
  - the home page's three moments become breakfast alone.
- **A restaurant open to all:**
  - the menu gets its dishes and prices;
  - "Book a table" is added: a second form, `tables`, adapted from `apps/desktop/gallery/forms/forms-booking-table`, or a link to the restaurant's own booking page;
  - the header gets "Book a table" beside "Book a room".

**When left out:**
- delete `pages/table.liquid`, its parts, `table-breakfast`, `table-supper` and its navigation record;
- the home page's table section stays, without "See the table".

### Around (`/around`)

**Does the house arrange outings?**
- **Yes, a few** (as built): "We can arrange" with `outings`, asked for in the request's note.
- **No, places only:** the section and `outings` go.

**When left out:**
- delete `pages/around.liquid`, its parts, `outings`, `around-seasons` and its navigation record;
- the home page's "From the door" keeps `places` and loses "Around the house".

**Facts, not choices:** the prices, the check-in and check-out times, the deposit, the cancellation terms, breakfast hours and the pickup's price are the house's. The agent asks for them once the pages are built, or leaves them out rather than guess.

Bedrooms need several narrower searches when building.
- **the house:**
  - the arcaded courtyard for the window, which must hold up with the sheer over it: light, arches and plants, no people;
  - stone basins or a fountain, a bougainvillea wall, the roof terrace with the hills;
  - a painted street in Jalatlaco and a carved door;
- **the rooms:** five main pictures and five to ten more. Beamed ceilings, white linen, terracotta floors, woven blankets, shutters open on light, and details: a clay jug, a folded blanket, a tiled shower;
- **the table:** hot chocolate, tamales, fruit, a breakfast table in sun, mezcal in clay cups, a long table set at dusk;
- **around:** Santo Domingo, the market, the ethnobotanical garden's cactus rows, Monte Albán, a loom or rugs in Teotitlán, Hierve el Agua;
- **the story and the seasons:** indigo or cochineal dyeing, hands at a loom, a marigold altar, partly reused for the seasons;
- **the people:** four portraits only if fitting photos are found, otherwise initials on each person's tint.

**Choosing them:**
- **as one set:** daylight, whitewashed and dyed walls, sun and shade, color in the walls and the cloth;
- **left out:** night shots, flash, stock-polished interiors, and faces outside the team;
- **a shared treatment** (`[data-sun]`) pulls them together: a touch of `sepia()` and a little less saturation, filters only, never a color value.
