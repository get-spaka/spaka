# Seam: brief

One of wave 2's original designs: the independent fashion label (`business`, one of the original, animated designs). Seam is its working name; the author may change it (Pleat would name its signature effect).

## Concept

**Concept.** A small label's site made from the things on its cutting table: pattern paper with its notches, a manila hang tag, a swatch cut with pinking shears, and the photographer's contact sheet with the picks circled in grease pencil.
- **The pleats:** its photographs arrive pleated. Each opens from a row of folds into a flat picture, like a pleated skirt shaken out.
- **The opening:** two photographs of the new collection, side by side, each opening from its pleats while the page loads.
- **The pages:** the lookbook is walked look by look. Every piece has its own page, with its cloth, its sizes and a way to ask for it. The studio's page shows how a piece is made, on pattern paper.
- **The look:** quiet and current, with big photographs, tiny type and one narrow serif at poster size. It leaves no doubt that the clothes are made by hand, by four people, in one room.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Ourlet** (French for "hem"), a clothing label and studio in Montreal. The name was searched and no clothing label of that name came up.
- **The studio:** Studio 405, 5590 Avenue de Gaspé, Mile End, Montreal, on the fourth floor of a 1920s shirt factory.
- **The people:** it was founded in 2019 by Margaux Tessier, a pattern cutter who spent nine years in the garment factories of Chabanel Street. Daniel Osei runs the cutting table and production. Lucía Paredes and Huong Tran sew and finish every piece.
- **The clothes:** seasonless clothes for everyone, sized 1 to 5: overshirts, chore jackets, wide pleated trousers, smock dresses, knit vests and a tote cut from offcuts.
- **How they are made:** most pieces are cut when someone asks for one and are ready in about three weeks. A few are kept in stock.
- **The cloth:** washed linen woven in Lithuania, wool flannel from Biella, cotton canvas, a Merino rib knitted by a small mill in the Eastern Townships, and deadstock bought from Chabanel's jobbers.
- **The collections:** two a year, numbered. The current one is Collection 07, *Fieldwork* (Fall–Winter 2026, eight looks). Before it came 06, *Low Water* (Spring–Summer 2026), and 05, *Night Shift* (Fall–Winter 2025). Beside them, the Uniform: three pieces that are always made.
- **Prices:** in Canadian dollars, from $95 for the tote to $480 for the work coat.
- **How people buy:** they request a piece through its page. Ourlet replies within two working days with the timing and a payment link, and nothing is charged on the site. Visitors can also try the clothes on at the studio, open on Saturdays from 11 to 5, with fittings on Thursday evenings by appointment, or at six stockists: in Montreal, Quebec City, Toronto, Vancouver, Brooklyn and Copenhagen.
- **After the sale:** it mends what it makes, free for two years and at cost after that. A letter goes out four times a year, when a collection is ready.

The same structure suits a knitwear label, a bag, shoe or hat maker, a jewelry studio, a vintage reworker, a made-to-measure tailor, or a label of household linens. The agent rewrites the pieces, the looks, the cloth and the studio.

## Trends it draws on

These come from twelve sites of independent fashion, jewelry and accessories labels, studied in November 2026.
- **Opened in a browser** at 1440 pixels wide and scrolled, to read their type, layout and color from the page (nine): ceciliebahnsen.com (Awwwards Honorable Mention, July 2026), serotoninn.com (Site of the Day, August 2026), coutumes.com (E-commerce Honors, September 2026), tillysveaas.co.uk (Honorable Mention, September 2026), agnestoth.com (Honorable Mention, October 2026), apinistudio.com (Honorable Mention, July 2026), palomawool.com, storymfg.com and bode.com.
- **Read with WebFetch only** (two): almadalabel.com (Honorable Mention, October 2026) and baserange.com.
- **Looked at but not counted** (one): atticusfinchclothing.com.au, a multi-brand shop rather than a label, which appears only as a second source for drops.
- **Palettes and tags** come from the Awwwards entries, including those of heirest.com and deadlyponies.com.

**What makes it its own:**
- **the pleats:**
  - a large photograph comes in as a pleated cloth: six vertical folds, each turned on its axis and shaded. They open flat one after another from the left;
  - this happens in the hero at load, and to each look, cover and lead picture as it comes into view;
  - no wave 1 template and no sibling briefed at the same time folds its pictures;
- **the contact sheet:** the lookbook's index is a dark proof sheet of numbered frames, like a photographer's. Its picks get a grease-pencil loop that draws itself, one after another, as the sheet comes into view;
- **the cutting table's shapes, all still:**
  - manila hang tags with a punched hole, which overlap the photos and carry the name, the cloth and the price in typewriter type;
  - swatches and the footer's top edge cut with pinking shears (a zigzag edge);
  - section rules marked with a pattern's notches;
  - the making shown on dot-and-cross pattern paper, with printed pattern pieces (cut line, notches, grain-line arrow, "Cut 2 on fold").

**What it leaves out:**
- a bag, a wishlist, accounts, a currency or country switch, newsletter pop-ups and cookie walls (an Upstart site tracks nothing);
- preloaders, video, an Instagram feed, and product photos cut out on white, which small labels rarely have;
- **effects other templates already have:**
  - a scrolling ticker (Corner's, and Tilly Sveaas's);
  - anything that scrolls sideways (Reel's);
  - sections pinned while the page scrolls (Ember's and Pocket's);
  - a picture following the cursor (Folio's);
  - italic accent words in headings (Corner's and Folio's);
- **effects close to its siblings', briefed at the same time:**
  - stitches or a thread that sews itself, and objects that swing (Linen has both): the tags here hang still, at a slight angle, and the dividers are notched rather than stitched;
  - pattern drawings that draw themselves (Mend's lines draw themselves): here they are printed and still.

## Motion

Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode nothing moves and everything is in its final place: the photos lie flat and the picks are circled. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too.

- **`data-pleat`, the pleats** (`scripts/pleats.js`, Motion):
  - **Setting up:** on a figure holding one `<img>`, the script lays over it six strips (four on a phone). Each strip is a copy of the same `<img>`, clipped to its slice (`clip-path: inset(…)`), so that each shows exactly its part of the picture whatever the crop. The copies are `aria-hidden`, and the script hides the original while they are there.
  - **The folded state:** each strip starts turned 70° on its vertical axis, one way then the other in turn, inside a perspective on the figure. A gradient made from neutral, darker on one side, makes the cloth read as pleated.
  - **The movement:** the strips turn flat one after another from the left (`rotateY: [±70, 0]`, 0.9 s, 0.07 s apart, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`) while the shading fades. The script then shows the original and removes the copies, so the picture is a plain `<img>` again.
  - **When:** in the hero, the two photos one after the other once the page has loaded; elsewhere, with Motion's `inView` as the picture comes into view.
  - **Where:** the hero, the looks, the collections' covers, journal covers, and the studio page's wide photo.
  - **With no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode:** the picture as it is. `stop` removes the copies and restores the original.
- **`data-mark`, the grease pencil** (`scripts/marks.js`, Motion):
  - each pick's loop, a hand-drawn SVG path in the accent color, is drawn (`pathLength: [0, 1]`, 0.6 s, 0.25 s apart) when the contact sheet comes into view;
  - with no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode: the loops are drawn.
- **`data-reveal`:** texts rise a little (`y: [20, 0]`, opacity, 0.8 s) as they come into view, as in the site-scripts skill.
- **A second picture on hover (CSS only):** a piece's card fades from the worn photo to the close-up of its cloth on hover and on focus, under no-preference. It needs no script, and edit mode shows the worn photo.
- **The hang tags (CSS only):** a tag hangs at a fixed slight angle, which is layout, not motion. It straightens to level when its card is hovered or focused, with a 0.3 s transition under no-preference. It never swings.
- **The contact sheet's frames (CSS only):** a frame lifts slightly on hover (`scale: 1.04`) and takes a rose outline.
- **The header (CSS only):** it gains a calico background and a hairline once the page is scrolled, through a scroll-driven animation on `animation-timeline: scroll()` toward the stylesheet's own `bg-base-100`, which edit mode and older browsers show from the start.
- **Page transitions (CSS only):** `@view-transition { navigation: auto }`. The header stays in place while the page beneath cross-fades in 0.35 s, under no-preference.
- **Smooth scrolling:** Lenis, light (`lerp: 0.1`).

Libraries: Motion and Lenis, pinned on jsDelivr, under MIT. There is no WebGL, no Splitting and no carousel.

## Images

From Wikimedia Commons and Openverse (CC0, public domain, CC BY, CC BY-SA), credited. About 34 images:
- **the looks (about 19):** clothes worn against walls, in doorways, on stairs and in plain rooms, in portrait format. Each collection's looks are taken from one photographer's series, so that they read as one shoot. Some example searches:
  - "model wearing a wool coat" finds series of looks shot on city walls, such as a vintage shop's Flickr shoots under CC BY 2.0;
  - "clothes on a rack in a studio" and "tailor cutting fabric" also return usable pictures;
  - "linen shirt hanging on chair" finds nothing;
- **the pieces:** worn photos taken from the looks, plus about eight garments laid flat or on a hanger, and close-ups of linen, wool flannel, canvas and a rib knit (the swatches and the hovers);
- **the studio (about six):** a cutting table with chalk and shears, pattern paper and a pattern piece, hands at a sewing machine, pins in cloth, a rack of samples, the tall windows of an old factory. The Commons series "Cutting materials for tailoring purposes" is a start;
- **mending:** a darned elbow, or a patch being sewn;
- **the journal's covers:** taken from these;
- **the people:** four portraits only if fitting photos of people at work are found; otherwise, their initials on manila.

To read as one set, they are chosen in daylight, with plain backgrounds, natural cloth and few logos. A shared treatment (`[data-print]`: a little less saturation and a little more contrast, a filter with no color of its own) pulls them together. On the contact sheet, each frame has a calico border, like a print. Faces appear only in the looks, which are the label's own lookbook, and on the team.
