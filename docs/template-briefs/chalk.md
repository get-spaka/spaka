# Chalk: brief

One of wave 2's original designs: the bouldering and climbing gym (`business`, one of the original, animated designs).

Named **Chalk** at approval (2026-11-15): the working name "Crux" was dropped, ThemeIsle already selling a "Crux Climbing Gym" template and many real gyms being called Crux.

## Concept

**Concept.** A climbing gym's site drawn like one of its walls. The pages are chalk-white and birch-ply panels dotted with bolt holes, with chunky holds in the gym's circuit colors bolted onto them. As the visitor scrolls down, a line climbs through the page from hold to hold, from START in the hero to TOP in the footer, the way a problem is read on the wall. The walls themselves are drawn in section, each leaning at its real angle. What is new on the walls, the week's classes, the prices and the first visit are each one tap away.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Holdfast Bouldering**, a bouldering gym in a 1912 stove foundry at 1450 Ironside Street, on the Southside of Chattanooga, Tennessee. Two route setters, Rosa Delgado and Jamie Pruitt, opened it in 2022.
- **The walls:** 14,000 square feet of 15-foot walls in six areas (the Slab, the Vert, the Prow, the Comp Wall, the Cave and the Kids' Corner). They hold about 180 problems from VB to V10 in six color circuits. One area is reset every week, so there is a new set every Friday.
- **The rest of the building:** a training room (a spray wall, a board, hangboards and free weights), a yoga room, and the Chalkboard, a café at the front with coffee, local beer and a long table.
- **Hours:** Monday to Friday 6 am to 10 pm, weekends 8 am to 9 pm.
- **Prices:** a first visit for $29 (a 30-minute intro, the day pass and shoes), day passes, ten-visit cards, and memberships with no joining fee.
- **Classes:** from Intro to Bouldering to Strength for Climbers and Yoga for Climbers.
- **Kids and groups:** a kids' club, a youth team, birthday parties and team events.
- **Events:** its winter comp, the Ironside Throwdown, reset nights, and day trips to the boulders at Stone Fort.

The name was searched and no climbing gym uses it. Chattanooga is a real climbing town with real gyms; Holdfast is not one of them.

The same structure suits a rope climbing gym, a gym with several locations, a ninja or parkour gym, a strength or CrossFit gym, a martial arts or boxing club, or a yoga or dance studio that lives by its timetable. The agent rewrites the walls (as rooms or areas), the set, the classes, the prices and the programs.

## Trends it draws on

These come from 15 climbing gym, fitness studio and sports sites opened in November 2026. Each was opened in a browser at 1280 px, looked at screen by screen, and read as text:
- **climbing gyms:** blocshop.com, arkose.com, boulderingproject.com, urbanapes.de, thisisyonder.com, northsideboulders.com, boulderklub.de, movementgyms.com, londonclimbingcentres.co.uk (VauxWall West), climbingdistrict.com, boulderbar.net, hogtownboulders.com and mesarim.com;
- **fitness:** becore.se, an Awwwards nominee of October 2026, and banditrunning.com.

Two climbing gym templates sold on Framer's marketplace, Topout and Belay, were read as what the market already offers. The first of the five pages of Awwwards' Sports category (2026) holds no climbing gym. Each trend below was seen on at least two sites.

1. **The first visit as the front door:** a first-time path in the header, an intro offer, and the steps of a first session said plainly.
   - blocshop.com: a colored "Première fois" pill in the header;
   - arkose.com: "1ère fois" in the menu and a €10 first entry;
   - boulderingproject.com: "Your first visit" as the first card, and a free first visit;
   - hogtownboulders.com: "First visit? What to expect" in the hero, and a waiver button in the header;
   - boulderbar.net: "Erstbesuch" in the menu;
   - northsideboulders.com: two weeks for $39 for new climbers;
   - climbingdistrict.com: a discovery offer of three sessions;
   - thisisyonder.com: "First Visit?" with a registration link and a safety video;
   - londonclimbingcentres.co.uk: "First time?" and pre-registration.
2. **Heavy poster type, wide or condensed, with prices as giant numerals.**
   - blocshop.com: an extended black headline across the whole screen;
   - boulderingproject.com and urbanapes.de: heavy capitals;
   - climbingdistrict.com: "45€" and "18€" in condensed numerals as tall as the card;
   - northsideboulders.com: every price a big figure ("$24 full", "$20 conc").
3. **What is new on the walls, and when.**
   - northsideboulders.com: "New sets Fridays", "on Mondays", "on Wednesdays", one line per gym;
   - boulderklub.de: news of the walls, and its "36 Problems of Berlin" comp;
   - blocshop.com: articles on route setting, and a feed of daily news;
   - boulderbar.net: a monthly challenge and "Flash or Trash";
   - Topout: wall cards marked fresh or resetting, and a reset calendar;
   - Belay: this week's problems as a setting board.
4. **Holds and circuit colors as the graphic language.**
   - boulderbar.net: every illustration built from hold shapes, and event titles set letter by letter on colored hold dots;
   - boulderingproject.com: its "color circuits";
   - Topout: a color-tape grade system from yellow V0-1 to black V7+, on every class and wall;
   - Belay: hold-colored dots on its grade ladders.
5. **A door for each audience: kids, schools, parties and teams.**
   - boulderbar.net: four illustrated doors (first time, kids and youth, schools and groups, courses and workshops);
   - boulderklub.de: team events, school classes and kids;
   - boulderingproject.com and hogtownboulders.com: youth programs and a youth team;
   - londonclimbingcentres.co.uk: kids;
   - arkose.com: kids' packs and birthday parties;
   - thisisyonder.com: kids and families.
6. **The week made visible: timetables, hours drawn as a chart, how busy it is.**
   - londonclimbingcentres.co.uk: opening times as a grid of hours colored off-peak, peak and "magic time";
   - boulderbar.net: each gym's live occupancy;
   - boulderklub.de: the hours, and early-bird prices before 3 pm;
   - becore.se: a class schedule among its featured pages;
   - northsideboulders.com: the hours and set day of each gym, and a calendar;
   - Topout: a "How busy is it?" chart.

Most of these gyms also present themselves as a place to stay, with a café, yoga or a workspace (thisisyonder.com, arkose.com, blocshop.com, boulderbar.net, climbingdistrict.com, boulderingproject.com). The template gives that one section, the Chalkboard.

**What makes it its own:**
- **the line:** a route drawn down the page through its holds as the visitor scrolls, from START to TOP. None of the sites seen does it, and it gives the page the logic of a problem on the wall;
- **holds bolted on:** the holds are the site's illustration, its section marks and its placeholders, in the circuit colors, and they twist into place as if screwed onto the wall;
- **the walls in section:** each wall is drawn as a profile leaning at its real angle (from a 75° slab to a 140° cave), like an architect's section of the gym, and leans into place as it comes into view;
- **volumes as frames:** photos are cut as climbing volumes, angular polygons, rather than the rounded pills and squircles the gyms use;
- **one color system:** the circuits (yellow, green, blue, purple, red, black) also mark the levels of the classes in the timetable, so a beginner learns one code.

**What it leaves out:**
- a marquee or ribbon (Arkose has one, and so does Corner), a custom cursor, stickers, and anything that scrolls sideways;
- a live occupancy meter, and anything else the gym's software would feed live (see the end of this brief);
- a departure-board list of problems (Belay's) and pricing cards in the manner of fitness chains;
- video in the hero, which owners rarely have in a usable form, and free footage of climbing is scarce;
- a giant wordmark across the footer, which Corner and Ember both have: here the footer is the top of the route.

## Type

All three families are files of the template, from Google Fonts, under the SIL Open Font License; none is used in wave 1. Unbounded is wide and round-shouldered: set black, its letters look like a row of holds.

| Role | Font | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Headings | Unbounded | 800 to 900, uppercase for the hero and the section titles, `leading-[0.9]`, `tracking-[-0.01em]`. | Grades, prices and dates | Unbounded | 800 with `tabular-nums`: the "V4" set on its hold, "$79", an event's day number |
| Body | Onest | 400 and 500, 1.0625 rem, `leading-relaxed` |
| Labels, times and setters | Red Hat Mono | 500, 0.75 rem, uppercase, `tracking-[0.08em]`: wall names, angles, the timetable's times, "Set by", "Set Nov 13" |

Encore, briefed at the same time, has Big Shoulders and Azeret Mono, so neither is used here. Encore names Onest among its alternatives; if it ends up with it, this template takes one of the pairings below.

**If a sibling template took these,** two pairings with the same character:
- **Sofia Sans Extra Condensed** (800 to 900: the hero becomes tall stacked capitals) with **Public Sans**, and labels in **JetBrains Mono**;
- **Syne** (800, its wide extra-bold) with **Golos Text**, and labels in **IBM Plex Mono**.

## Palette

The palette is light: chalk and birch ply, graphite ink, and one violet line. Contrast was measured from these values:
- graphite on chalk 15.8:1, on ply 11.6:1;
- violet text on chalk 4.7:1, white on violet 5:1;
- dark on orange 6.4:1, dark on mint 11.4:1;
- chalk on graphite 15.8:1, and `text-base-content/70` about 6.9:1.

Violet on ply (3.5:1) and on graphite (3.3:1) is kept for the line and for large shapes, never for body text: links on ply are graphite and underlined.

|---|---|---|
| `--theme-color-surface` | oklch(0.968 0.004 250) | chalk: a cool off-white, the ground of every page |
| `--theme-color-surface-alt` | oklch(0.87 0.045 75) | birch ply: the hero, the walls in section and the first-visit steps, the panels that carry bolt holes |
| `--theme-color-text` | oklch(0.22 0.018 265) | graphite |
| `--theme-color-border` | oklch(0.85 0.01 250) | hairlines and the mats under the wall sections; the bolt holes are the text at 18% |

Other settings:
- buttons and fields are pills (`--theme-radius-field: 9999px`);
- relief is off (`--theme-depth: 0`);
- density is normal (`--theme-size: 0.25rem`);
- borders are bold (`--theme-border: 2px`), as chunky as the holds.

**The circuits are the theme's colors, never their own.**
- **The mapping:** yellow is `warning`, green `success`, blue `info`, red `error`, purple `primary` and black `neutral`. These are the status colors Upstart derives from the background (Q225-Q228).
- **In the files:** a record's `tone` field holds one of these names, and the element carries it as `data-tone`. `styles/site.css` maps each `[data-tone=…]` to `--tone: var(--color-…)`, which the holds, the tags and the timetable's blocks read. No class name is built in Liquid, which Tailwind would not see.
- **The Theme tab** recolors the circuits with everything else.

## Motion

Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode no script runs: every hold sits at its final place and turn, every wall at its angle, and there is no line. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too. The libraries are Motion and Lenis, pinned on jsDelivr as in the site-scripts skill, both under MIT; there is no WebGL and no Splitting.

- **The line**, the signature (`data-line` on a container, `data-hold` on each stop inside it; `scripts/line.js`, Motion's `scroll`):
  - **the stops:** each stop is a small SVG hold (`<span data-hold data-tone="…" aria-hidden="true">`) beside a section's heading, alternating between the left and right margins on a desktop. The hero's first hold carries START tape, and the footer's last carries TOP;
  - **the path:** the script measures the holds' centers within the container, builds a smooth path through them (a Catmull-Rom curve turned into Béziers), and puts it in an `<svg aria-hidden="true">` behind the content, never over text. The stroke is 6 px wide with round caps, in the primary color;
  - **as the page scrolls:** Motion's `scroll` draws the path (`stroke-dashoffset`) so that its tip reaches each hold as the hold crosses the middle of the screen. Each hold reached lights up (`data-hold-on`): it fills, grows to 1.15 and settles with a quarter turn;
  - **on a phone,** the holds sit in the left gutter and the line runs down it, 4 px wide;
  - **when the layout changes,** the path is measured again (a `ResizeObserver`, and once the fonts have loaded);
  - **under reduced motion,** the whole path is drawn at once and stays still: the route is shown, not played;
  - **with no script and in edit mode,** the holds are there, filled, with no line;
  - **reused:** the first-visit steps carry their own short `data-line`, so the five steps are a route of their own;
  - **`stop`** removes the SVG and the observers and resets the holds.
- **Bolted on** (`data-bolt` on a group of holds, Motion's `inView` and a spring): the holds of the hero, of the fresh set and of the doors come in one by one, from a 0.4 scale and a −90° turn to their resting turn, overshooting a little, as if screwed onto the wall. The hero's start on load, the others as they come into view. With no script they are simply there.
- **A loose hold**, in CSS under no-preference: a hold or hold tag turns 6° on hover, like a hold that needs tightening.
- **The walls in section** (`data-angle` on each wall's profile, Motion's `inView` and a spring):
  - **the drawing is CSS:** each profile is a thick bar standing on a floor line with its mats. It is rotated from its foot by its angle, set inline from the record (`style="--angle: 140"`, a number with no color), with `rotate: calc((var(--angle) - 90) * 1deg)`. The agent changes the record's angle and the drawing follows;
  - **as it comes into view,** the script starts each profile upright and lets it lean to its angle, wall after wall. With no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode, each stands at its angle.
- **Volume frames:** photos are cut by `clip-path` polygons (four shapes, `.volume-1` to `.volume-4`). On hover, a frame shifts one corner by a few percent, as a transition under no-preference.
- **`data-reveal`:** sections' main elements rise into view (`y: [24, 0]`, opacity, 0.7 s), as in the site-scripts skill.
- **Fresh and coming down** (`data-since` and `data-until` on dates; `scripts/fresh.js`):
  - **the dates:** the script turns a problem's set date into "Set 3 days ago", and a wall's reset date into "Comes down in 5 days". A problem set within the week gets a "New" tag in the secondary color;
  - **with no script,** the dates as written ("Set Nov 13", "Reset Nov 20");
  - the script writes only relative dates beside the written ones, never the site's texts.
- **The week** (`data-week` on the timetable; `scripts/week.js`):
  - **today:** the script marks today's column and draws a "now" line across it at the current time. On a phone, it opens today in the day picker;
  - **with no script and in edit mode,** the whole week, with Monday open on a phone. The day picker is radio inputs and works without a script.
- **Page transitions**, in CSS: `@view-transition { navigation: auto }`. The new page rises from 6% below while the old one lifts away, a short climb, about 0.45 s, under no-preference. This is neither Ember's fade through black nor Reel's sideways wipe.
- **Smooth scrolling:** Lenis, light (`lerp: 0.12`), so the line draws evenly.

## What varies

This is the guide's `## Pages` in short: each page type's choices, the first one recommended, and what goes with the page when the maker leaves it out. The prices, hours, set day and grades of the problems are facts, not choices: the agent asks for them.

### Home (`/`)

Its choices are for the whole website.

**How do newcomers start?**
- **They book an intro** (as built): the "First visit" pill, the hero and the first-visit page lead to the booking form. Requests arrive in the Content tab, and the gym confirms by email.
- **They just walk in:** there is no booking. The pill and buttons lead to the first-visit page's steps and the hours. The form goes from the first-visit page in its turn, and the steps gain "Come any time we're open; intros start on the hour".
- **They book on your own booking system:** the pill and buttons link to the gym's booking page (ask its address, or `#` until given), and the form goes.

**Bouldering only, or ropes too?**
- **Bouldering only** (as built).
- **Bouldering and ropes:**
  - the walls gain rope walls: a `kind` field (boulder, top rope, lead), with heights of 30 to 50 feet, drawn taller in section;
  - the first visit gains a belay check, "Learn to Belay" joins the classes, and the prices gain harness rental;
  - the walls, classes, first-visit and prices pages follow in their turns.

**Which grades?**
- **The V-scale** (as built): VB to V10 on the circuits and the tags.
- **The Font scale:** 4 to 8A, for a gym in Europe; the same circuits.
- **Circuits only:** the grades go from the tags. The ladder names each circuit by a word instead ("first steps", "getting there", "strong").

## What stays outside Upstart

- **Payments:** nothing is paid on the site. An intro, a class spot, a membership, an event place or a party is either a request through a form, kept in the Content tab, which the gym confirms by email and takes payment for at the desk, or a link to the gym's own booking or member system (the choices above). The thank-you fragments say so plainly.
- **The waiver:** a legal waiver with a signature is not a form's job. The template links to the gym's waiver page in its own software, or says it is signed at the desk. It never imitates a waiver with a checkbox.
- **Live data:** how busy the gym is now, and problems logged in a route-logging app, cannot come in, since there are no embeds and nothing is fetched. The template shows the quiet hours as the gym writes them, and the set as records the maker edits in the Content tab. A link to the gym's logbook app can sit beside the set.
- **Instagram and video:** links in the footer, no feed and no embedded player.
