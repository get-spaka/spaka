# Transfer: brief

One of wave 3's original designs: the tattoo studio (`business`, one of the original, animated designs). Transfer is its working name, after its signature effect. "Stencil" was set aside because BigCommerce's theme framework carries that name, "Sigil" because Framer sells a "Sigil Labs" template, and "Linework" because Framer sells "Linework Studio". A search found no website template sold as "Transfer".

## Concept

**Concept.** A tattoo shop's site that works the way a tattoo is made. Before a design is inked, a violet thermal stencil is pressed onto the skin and the artist tattoos over it. Here every design does the same: it lands as a violet stencil, a little off register, and then the black ink takes over it. Hovering over a design lifts the ink to show the stencil again.
- **The look:** the site is a sheet of flash on bristol board. A blackletter wordmark sits at poster size, the drawings are numbered and priced, and the flash wall is a catalogue of designs to claim.
- **Around it:** who tattoos here, who is visiting and when, how to ask for a tattoo, and how to heal it, day by day.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Lost Wax Tattoo**, a shop of four artists and an apprentice. It is on the second floor of an old brick tannery warehouse at 17 Tanyard Alley (an invented street) in Butchertown, Louisville, Kentucky.
- **The founder:** June Castellano opened it in 2018. She was a jeweler before she tattooed, and the shop is named after the casting method she learned at the bench.
- **The artists:**
  - June Castellano: fine line, ornamental and botanical work. Her books are on a waitlist.
  - Teo Marsh: American traditional; he draws most of the wall's flash. His books are open.
  - Hana Kobori: large Japanese-influenced pieces (sleeves and backpieces), by consultation. Her books are closed until March.
  - Dez Whitlock: blackwork, dotwork and geometric pieces. His books are open.
  - Rory Pham: the apprentice, who tattoos small flash at apprentice prices and works the Saturday walk-ins.
- **The guest chair:** one chair is kept for guest artists, who stay four or five days each.
  - Mika Lindgren, from Gothenburg: ornamental blackwork, December 3–7, 2026;
  - Paloma Ibarra, from Mexico City: fine-line lettering and botanicals, January 14–18, 2027;
  - Kwame Adjei, from Leeds: neo-traditional animals, February 11–15, 2027;
  - Sadie Fontenot, from Lafayette, Louisiana: American traditional, March 18–22, 2027;
  - Haruka Ino, from Osaka: Japanese pieces, by consultation only, April 22–27, 2027.
- **How people get tattooed:**
  - **Custom work:** by request. The shop replies within five working days with a quote, dates and a deposit link. The deposit ($100 to $200) is not refundable, counts toward the last session, and can be moved once with 48 hours' notice.
  - **Flash:** claimed design by design, with a $50 deposit. Some designs are "one and done", tattooed only once; others can be repeated.
  - **Walk-ins:** Saturdays from 12 to 6, from the wall.
  - **Flash day:** a Friday the 13th flash day: the next one is August 13, 2027.
- **Hours:** Tuesday to Saturday, 12 to 8, by appointment.
- **Prices:** a $100 shop minimum, $180 an hour, $1,100 for a full day; flash from $120 to $450.
- **Rules:** 18 and over with a photo ID. Consent forms are signed at the shop. A free touch-up is offered within three months.
- **The newsletter:** "Fresh flash" goes out when a new sheet goes up.

"Lost Wax Tattoo" was searched: no studio of that name came up. Louisville has real tattoo shops, and Lost Wax is not one of them. No other template, of the earlier waves or of this one, is set in Louisville.

The same structure suits a single tattoo artist, a fine-line or blackwork studio, a piercing studio (the jewelry in place of the flash), a cosmetic tattoo or microblading studio, a henna artist, or a sign painter or illustrator who sells designs. The agent rewrites the artists, the flash, the guests and the healing texts, and keeps the sheet, the transfer and the heal.

**What makes it its own:**
- **the transfer:**
  - every design and drawing arrives as a violet thermal stencil, slightly off register, and the ink then takes over it and settles into place;
  - on hover or focus, the ink lifts to show the stencil underneath;
  - it is made from the theme's colors and works on any image the maker brings, from a line drawing to a phone photo of a painted sheet;
  - no other template prints, stencils or registers its pictures;
- **try it on:** a design is placed on a photograph of a forearm, a calf or a shoulder blade, at its real size in inches, blended into the skin. This answers the trend's 3D bodies with a 2D photograph that a maker can replace, and it is not Mend's jointed figure: there is no drawn body, only skin;
- **the heal:** the aftercare page has a dial of days, from 1 to 30. The same tattoo on the same skin goes from fresh (red and glossy) under its film, to flaking, to cloudy, to settled, with what to do at each stage beside it. It is CSS on radio inputs and works with no script;
- **the guest sheet:** the coming months laid out like a flash sheet, one panel per month, each holding that month's guest: their dates as a big blackletter numeral ("14–18"), their name, home city and mark, so "who's in the chair when" reads at a glance. It is a calendar of visits, not of hours: Taper, a sibling of this wave, draws its week's hours as bars, so there are no bars here;
- **a light shop:** where these studios are black, this one is bristol board and India ink, with flash red, a stencil violet and a touch of flash yellow.

**What it leaves out:**
- 3D bodies, statues and skulls, WebGL and fire shaders (Monolith, Rui, Vitalina and Flatbone have them), loaders, and video in the hero;
- an ink trail behind the cursor (Rui's), and any cursor effect: Ember, Folio and Pocket have them;
- an Instagram feed, a WhatsApp bubble, cookie walls, stats counters and testimonials sliders;
- **effects other templates already have:**
  - a line drawing itself as the page scrolls (Chalk's), and lines that draw themselves (Mend's): the stencil arrives whole, it is never drawn stroke by stroke;
  - rubber stamps (Corner's turning stamp, Marquee's status stamps): a claimed design is marked in type;
  - pinned sections (Ember's and Pocket's), sideways scrolling (Reel's), and a giant wordmark across the footer (Corner, Ember, Marquee and Seam);
  - page transitions (Chalk's rise, Seam's cross-fade, Encore's flight): pages change plainly;
  - tilted cards and pinned notices (Commons, and Nestmade's polaroids): the flash sheet is square to the page.

## Type

Both families are files of the template, in `fonts/` with their license files, from Google Fonts, under the SIL Open Font License. Neither is used by an existing template, nor named in their briefs or in the briefs of this wave written so far (Gnomon has Instrument Sans, which was this brief's first choice).
- **Grenze Gotisch:** variable, 100 to 900. A blackletter drawn to be read, between a roman and a gothic, set heavy and large: the voice of a tattoo shop's sign, without the costume.
- **Encode Sans:** variable, width 75 to 125 and weight 100 to 900. A plain grotesk for the reading, and its condensed width for the labels, so no third family is needed.

- ink on bristol, about 16:1; ink on vellum, about 14:1;
- bristol on flash red, and red on bristol, about 5.3:1;
- bristol on stencil violet, and violet on bristol, about 7:1;
- ink on flash yellow, about 12:1;

## Motion

Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode no script runs and every design shows as plain ink, in place. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too. The libraries are Motion and Lenis, pinned on jsDelivr as in the site-scripts skill, both under MIT; there is no WebGL, no Splitting and no carousel.

- **The transfer** (`data-transfer` on a figure holding one `<img>`, optionally `data-stencil="<url of a line drawing>"`; `scripts/transfer.js`, Motion's `inView` and `animate`), the signature:
  - **The stencil layer:** in `start`, the script adds an `aria-hidden` layer beneath the image, stacked on it by CSS grid, made one of two ways:
    - **from a line drawing** (`data-stencil`, or an image whose file is `.svg` or `.png`): a box in `var(--color-secondary)` masked by that drawing (`mask-image`, set through a `--stencil` custom property holding the URL), so only its lines show, in violet;
    - **from anything else** (a scan or a photo of a painted sheet): a copy of the image in `grayscale(1) contrast(3)` over a box in `var(--color-secondary)`, blended with `mix-blend-mode: lighten`. The paper stays light, and the lines turn violet, like a thermal print.
  - **The movement:**
    - the stencil lands first: opacity `[0, 1]`, from 4 px right and 3 px up, rotated −0.6°, in 0.3 s;
    - after 0.2 s, the ink takes: the image goes from opacity 0 and `blur(2px)` to sharp in 0.6 s;
    - at the same time the stencil slides into register (`x: [4, 0]`, `y: [-3, 0]`) and fades out, leaving the ink alone.
  - **When:** in the hero, the sheet's six designs one after another at load, 0.12 s apart; elsewhere, as each design comes into view.
  - **Where:** the hero's sheet, the flash cards, each artist's mark, a design's page, and "Try it on".
  - **With no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode:** the image as it is, in ink. Under reduced motion the stencil layer is still made, for the lift below, without the entrance. `stop` removes the layers and the styles the script set.
- **The lift** (CSS, on `[data-transfer]` inside a link or a card): on hover and on focus, the ink fades to 0.1 and the stencil to 1 in 0.25 s, so the visitor sees the stencil under the design. It is a transition under no-preference and an instant swap under reduced motion. It needs the stencil layer, so it only happens when the script has run.
- **Try it on** (`data-try`, CSS with radio inputs and `:has()`; `scripts/try-on.js` for dragging only):
  - **The parts:** a photograph of skin, and the design over it with `mix-blend-mode: multiply` and a slight `opacity: 0.92`, so the skin's grain shows through the ink.
  - **The placements:** three radio chips (Forearm, Calf, Shoulder blade) switch the photo. Each placement record gives its photo, the width of skin it shows in inches and a center point. The design's width is set from its size in inches over that width (`--size-in`, `--skin-in`, plain numbers with no color), so a 4-inch swallow is smaller on a shoulder blade than on a forearm.
  - **The script** lets the visitor drag the design a little (pointer and arrow keys) and turn it 15° either way with a second control. With no script, and in edit mode, the design sits at the placement's center.
  - **The design itself** arrives by the transfer.
- **The heal** (`data-heal`, CSS only, radio inputs and `:has()`):
  - **The dial:** five ticks ("Day 1", "Day 4", "Day 10", "Day 18", "Day 30"), radio inputs styled as a ruler in stencil violet, usable with the keyboard's arrows.
  - **The stages:** one design on one skin photo, each stage made of layers drawn from the theme and switched by the checked tick:
    - **fresh:** a soft red halo (a radial gradient of `color-mix(in oklch, var(--color-primary) 35%, transparent)`, blurred) and a glossy streak (a linear gradient of `var(--color-base-100)` at low opacity);
    - **under film:** the streak becomes a rounded, slightly wrinkled sheet;
    - **flaking:** a speckled mask over the ink, as if small flakes were lifting;
    - **cloudy:** the ink at 0.6 behind a pale veil;
    - **settled:** sharp ink and nothing else.
  - **The stage's text:** beside the picture, what you see and what to do, from the `heal` records; only the checked stage's panel shows.
  - **Transitions:** 0.4 s between stages under no-preference, none under reduced motion.
  - **In edit mode:** Day 1 is checked.
- **The guest sheet** (`data-guests` on the sheet, `data-from` and `data-to` on each guest; `scripts/guests.js`):
  - **The panels:** one per month, written by Liquid from the records, in order; the guest's mark transfers as the panel comes into view.
  - **The script:**
    - it labels the current guest "In the chair now" with a yellow dot, and the next one "Arrives in 12 days";
    - it dims past guests and marks them "Been and gone";
    - it writes the same line into the header's strip (`data-guests-strip`).
  - **With no script:** the dates as written, and the strip names the next guest from the records' order.
- **The flash wall's filters** (`data-filter`, CSS with radio inputs and `:has()`): by artist and by style, plus "Available only". The counts ("17 available") are computed by Liquid, so they stay right with no script. Everything shows in edit mode.
- **Prefill** (`scripts/prefill.js`): a link to `/book?artist=teo-marsh` or `?guest=paloma-ibarra` preselects that person in the request form's select. With no script, the visitor chooses.
- **Rises** (`data-rise`, CSS): headings and paragraphs rise into view (`animation-timeline: view()`, a `from` keyframe only), as in the site-scripts skill.
- **The header** (CSS): sticky. Once the page is scrolled, it gains bristol behind it and a hairline under it (`animation-timeline: scroll()`), with the stylesheet's own state for edit mode and older browsers.
- **Smooth scrolling:** Lenis, light (`lerp: 0.1`); stopped while a dialog or the menu is open.

## What varies

The choices that change the design, page type by page type; the first option of each is the one recommended. The prices, deposits, hours and dates are facts, not choices: the agent asks for them.

**Home (`/`)**, with what every page shares:

*How do clients ask for a tattoo?* (the whole website)
- **With the request form** (as built):
  - the book page's form and each design's claim form; requests arrive in Spaka's Content tab, and the shop replies with dates and a deposit link;
  - keep `parts/request-form.liquid`, `parts/claim-form.liquid`, `data/requests/`, `data/claims/` and their fragments.
- **On the shop's own booking system:**
  - for shops whose artists take bookings and deposits through a booking app;
  - every "Request a tattoo" and "Claim" button links to it (the address the maker gives, or each artist's `booking_link`), and the forms, `requests`, `claims` and their fragments go;
  - the book page keeps its steps, prices and questions.
- **By email or direct message:**
  - "Request a tattoo" opens an email (or links to the shop's Instagram), and the forms go;
  - the book page's steps say what to put in the message: the idea, the placement, the size and a few references.

*Is it a shop or one artist?* (the whole website)
- **A shop of several artists** (as built): the roster, the artists' pages, and an artist select in the forms.
- **One artist:**
  - the artists page type becomes "About" (`/about`), the artist's own page: bio, portrait, books and work (the artist record's page, moved);

  - the forms lose the artist select, and "Four artists and an apprentice" leaves the texts.

## Images

About 26 photographs from Wikimedia Commons and Openverse (CC0, public domain, CC BY, CC BY-SA), credited, plus the template's own drawings.

**The flash: the template's own drawings.**
- **What:** twelve designs drawn for the template as SVG files in `media/`, credited to Spaka under CC0. Each is drawn twice: in color (ink lines, with flat flash red, yellow and green fills) for the cards, and as lines only for the stencil (`outline`).
- **The subjects** are those of a classic sheet: a swallow and dagger, a luna moth, a koi in a wave, moon phases, a coiled snake with a peony, a winged hourglass, a panther head, a little lighthouse, a rose, a spider web, an eye in a hand, a ship in a bottle.
- **How they are drawn:** in the manner of traditional flash in general (bold outline, few colors), never traced or adapted from a real artist's sheet or a found image.
- **The artists' marks** are among them, and the footer's "No. 00, Thank you" is a thirteenth, small drawing.

**The photographs:**
- **the skin (3):** a bare forearm, a calf and a shoulder blade, with no tattoo, in even light, of three different skin tones. They are used by try it on and the heal;
- **the shop (6 to 8):**
  - the tattoo chairs and a drawing table by tall warehouse windows, a wall of framed flash seen out of focus, ink caps and a machine on a tray;
  - gloved hands applying a violet stencil to skin, which the "how it goes" steps need most;
  - a brick warehouse's front;
- **the artists (5):** hands at work (tattooing, drawing on a light table, sharpening a pencil), so no stranger's face stands for a named artist. Without them, their marks on vellum;
- **their work:** photos of tattooing in progress, where the design cannot be read, plus the template's own designs set on the skin photos (the try-on blend, rendered as images). The template never presents a real artist's tattoo as the fictional shop's work;
- **the guests (6):** their marks on vellum, or a photo of a tool or a city of theirs;
- **aftercare (2):** hands washing under a tap, and a plain tube of ointment with no brand.

To read as one set, the photographs are chosen warm, clean and calm, not the usual dark, smoky shop. A shared treatment (`[data-print]`: a little less saturation, a little more contrast, a filter with no color of its own) pulls them together.

The guide tells the agent never to fill an artist's work or flash with found images: the maker's own photos and drawings replace them. Until given, the template's drawings stay, or a placeholder in theme colors with the stencil.

## What Spaka cannot do, and how the brief handles it

- **Deposits and payments:** Spaka has no payments. The forms send requests and claims, and the shop sends a deposit link from its own payment service by email.
- **Consent forms and ID:** signed at the shop. The forms ask only that the visitor confirms being 18 or older; they never imitate a consent form.
- **Reference images:** a form takes a link to references (a board, a post, a shared folder) rather than files.
- **Live availability:** the books' statuses and the guests' dates are written by the shop, not read from a calendar.
- **An Instagram feed:** left out; the artists' work comes from records, and Instagram is a link.
- **3D placement:** try it on is a 2D blend on photographs, which the maker can replace with their own.
