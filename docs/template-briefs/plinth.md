# Plinth: brief

One of wave 2's original designs: the museum or gallery exhibition (`event`, one of the original, animated designs). Plinth is a working name.

## Concept

**Concept.** An exhibition that hangs itself. The site opens nose to the canvas, on a detail of the exhibition's lead work, and steps back as you scroll until the whole painting hangs on a white wall with its label. Every work is then shown whole, untinted, at its real size relative to the others and with its museum label. The rooms are painted in their own wall colors. The practical museum is always one line away: the "open today" line, the tickets and the program of talks and tours.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Long Light: Nordic Painters of the Evening, 1885–1915**, an exhibition at the **Hallgren Museum of Art** in Astoria, Oregon, from January 23 to May 16, 2027. The Hallgren is a small museum of Nordic and Pacific Northwest art. It opened in 2016 in a 1911 cannery warehouse on pilings over the Columbia River, at Pier 7, on the collection of a Swedish-Finnish canning family. Long Light is a focused show: twelve paintings by six painters, Peder Severin Krøyer, Anna Ancher, Vilhelm Hammershøi, Eugène Jansson, Richard Bergh and Harald Sohlberg, hung in five rooms. Each room is painted a color of the dusk: Skagen's summer evenings, Copenhagen rooms losing their light, Stockholm's lamps, a winter night in the mountains, and the white nights. Signe Aaltonen, the museum's curator of Nordic art, curated it. The museum opens Wednesday to Sunday from 10 to 5, and until 8 on Thursdays ("Long Thursdays", free after 5). Admission is $16, $12 for seniors and students, and free under 18 and for members. Tickets are sold on the museum's own ticketing page. The program has a curator's talk, slow-looking tours every Saturday, a conservator's talk on the blues of dusk, a family afternoon and a Long Thursday with music. Schools and groups ask for a visit through a form. The works' labels name the real collections that own them ("Collection: Skagens Museum"), never a loan to this invented show. "Hallgren Museum of Art" and "Long Light" were searched: neither is a real museum or exhibition.

The same structure suits a gallery's solo or group show, a photography exhibition, an art center's or a university gallery's show, a design, craft or sculpture exhibition (the works on plinths), a historic house's display, or an artist's own retrospective or open studio. The agent rewrites the exhibition, its rooms, works, artists and program, and keeps the wall, the labels and the way works are shown.

**What makes it its own:**
- **Step back:** the hero opens on a detail of the lead work filling the screen, and scrolling zooms out to the whole work hung on the wall, the title settling beside it. This is the reverse of a zoom into a video, and nothing like a peephole: you end where a visitor stands, a few steps back.
- **Hung to scale:**
  - the works hang on one center line, each frame sized from the work's real height and width, so a 180 cm Sohlberg is three times a 60 cm Hammershøi;
  - a fine line marks the center at 145 cm (57 in.), with a 1 m scale bar at the end;
  - one switch turns the wall into the checklist, and each frame glides into its row, rearranging the page in place (a same-document View Transition).
- **Painted rooms and museum labels:** the rooms are full-width bands painted in the theme's colors, and every work carries its label. In the label, the artist's name and dates come first, then the title in italic, the year, the medium, the size and the collection. Typography does the work that frames and shadows usually do.

**What it leaves out:**
- loaders, any cursor effect (wave 1 has three), sideways scrolling (Reel's), WebGL and 3D rooms, and video;
- any tint, grade, duotone or crop of a work: works are shown whole and true. The hero's detail is the one exception, and it always ends on the whole work;
- a ticker, a giant wordmark in the footer (Corner, Ember and Marquee have one), and an italic accent word in a heading. Italics serve only for the titles of works, as in all writing about art;
- page transitions: two sibling templates of this wave fly a picture from a list into its page, so Plinth's pages change plainly.

## Motion

Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode nothing moves and everything is in its final place. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too.

- **Step back** (`data-step-back` on the hero, `data-focus="34% 58%"` on its picture; `scripts/step-back.js`, Motion's `scroll`):
  - the script makes the hero about 220svh tall (`data-step-back-on`), with its stage `position: sticky`;
  - the picture starts scaled about 2.6 times around its focus point, so the screen shows a detail, brushwork and all. The script caps the scale from the image's `naturalWidth`, so the detail is never blurrier than the file;
  - over the first 120svh of scroll, the scale eases back to 1. As it does, the white wall appears around the frame and the label fades in. The title's two lines come up once the work is in place;
  - on a phone the same, from a scale of about 2;
  - with no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode, the hero is one screen: the whole work on the wall, its label and the title. `stop` removes the attribute and the styles it set.
- **Hung** (`data-hang`, Motion's `inView`): each frame comes down about 14px onto its hook with a slight swing (rotate 1.2° to 0, a low-bounce spring). The frames follow each other 80 ms apart along the line, and each label fades in after its frame. With no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode, the frames are in place.
- **The wall and the checklist** (`data-view`):
  - two radio buttons, "On the wall" and "As a list", switch the works between the hang and the checklist in CSS (`:has(:checked)`). This works with no script, and edit mode shows the wall;
  - `scripts/view.js` wraps the switch in `document.startViewTransition`, so each work glides from its frame to its row and back;
  - each work's `view-transition-name` is on both its frame and its row: only the one shown is rendered, so the name stays unique on the page;
  - under reduced motion, the views switch at once.
- **Look closer** (`data-look`): a link to the image's file under the work. `scripts/look.js` opens the image instead in a full-screen `<dialog>` on night, at the file's full size, panned by dragging or scrolling. Escape or the close button closes it, focus returns to the link, and Lenis stops while it is open. With no script, the link opens the image itself.
- **Painted rooms** (`data-wall="primary"` and the other names): layout, not motion. Each band takes its pair of colors (`bg-primary text-primary-content`), the same with or without a script.
- **Rises** (`data-rise`): headings and paragraphs rise into view in CSS (`animation-timeline: view()`, a `from` keyframe only), as in the site-scripts skill.
- **Open today** (`data-today`, `scripts/today.js`):
  - the script reads the hours on the page (`data-day`, `data-opens`, `data-closes`) in the museum's own time zone (`data-tz="America/Los_Angeles"`, through `Intl`), so a visitor in another time zone reads the museum's day. It writes "Open today until 5", "Open tonight until 8 · free after 5" or "Opens Wednesday at 10";
  - from the exhibition's last day (`data-last-day="2027-05-16"`), it adds "Last 10 days" in the final fortnight and "Now closed" after;
  - with no script, the line written in the page: "Wednesday to Sunday, 10 to 5".
- **Filters** (`data-filter`): the works by room, the events by kind. With no script, everything shows.
- **The header:** sticky; a hairline appears under it once the page scrolls (CSS, `animation-timeline: scroll()`, with the line in the stylesheet's own state).
- **Smooth scrolling:** Lenis, light (`lerp: 0.1`).

Libraries: Motion and Lenis, pinned on jsDelivr, under MIT. View Transitions are the browser's own. No Splitting and no OGL.

## What varies

**Home (`/`).** It carries the choices for the whole website.

*What does the website present?*
- **One exhibition** (as built): the site is the exhibition's own, from its idea to its last work. The museum appears in the header's top line, the visit page and the footer.
- **A space and its exhibitions**, for a gallery or an art center with a changing program:
  - an `exhibitions` list with a page per exhibition (`/exhibitions/<exhibition>`), marked current, upcoming or past;
  - the hero takes the current one, and the header's title becomes the space's name;
  - rooms and works gain an `exhibition` choice;
  - the exhibition page type becomes this list in its turn.

*How do visitors get in?*
- **Tickets on the museum's own ticketing page** (as built): "Buy tickets" in the header, the hero, the visit section and the visit page links to that page, whose address the maker gives. The prices show on the visit page.
- **Free entry:** "Free entry" beside the hours in the top line and in the hero, and the buttons become "Plan your visit". The visit page's admission becomes one line.
- **Free, by time slot:** a request form (date, time, visitors, name, email) whose requests arrive in the Content tab, and the museum confirms by email. The buttons become "Book a free visit".
- **Paid at the door:** the prices on the visit page, no online tickets, and the buttons become "Plan your visit".

**The exhibition (`/exhibition`).**

*How is the exhibition laid out?*
- **In rooms** (as built): a painted band per room, with its works; each work names its room.
- **As one sequence:** no rooms. The essay is followed by every work in catalogue order on one white wall. `rooms`, the works' `room` field, the home's room index and the works page's room filter go.
- **By artist:** each band is an artist's room, with that artist's works. The bands read from `artists`, and `rooms` goes.

When left out: the home's statement keeps the idea. Its "About the exhibition" link and the room index point to the works page (`/works`), and the works keep their `room` for the hang's order.

**Works (`/works`, `/works/<work>`).**

*How are the works hung?*
- **To scale, on a center line** (as built): from each work's height and width, for paintings, drawings and prints.
- **All at one height:** every frame the same height on the line, for photographs and editions, or when the sizes are not known. The sizes stay in the labels when they are known.
- **On plinths:** for sculpture, ceramics and design objects. Each work stands on a block of putty with its label on the block's face, in square frames, with no center line.

When left out: the home's wall, the rooms and the artists' pages show the works without links, the labels in full. "Look closer" moves to the home's wall.

**Artists (`/artists`, `/artists/<artist>`).**

*How much room does each artist get?*
- **A page each** (as built): the list, then each artist's page with their life, portrait and works in the show.
- **All on one page:** for a group show of many artists. Each artist is a row with a short life and their works' thumbnails; the artists' pages go, and links point to the rows' anchors.

When left out (a solo show, or makers who are not named): the labels' names stop linking and the home's artists section goes. For a solo show, the artist's life becomes a section of the exhibition page.

**Program (`/program`, `/program/<event>`).**

*How do people come to an event?*
- **They reserve a seat with the form** (as built): the form on each event's page, the requests in the Content tab, and "Free with admission" or the price shown.
- **Through the ticketing page:** each event's button links to its own page on the museum's ticketing. The form and `reservations` go.
- **They just come:** each event says "No booking, first come". The form and `reservations` go.

When left out: the home's talks and tours section and its navigation record go, along with `events` and `reservations`.

**Visit (`/visit`).** Its admission section follows how visitors get in, chosen with the home page.

*Can groups book a visit?*
- **Yes, with the form** (as built): schools and groups ask by form (`group-visits`), and the museum confirms by email.
- **By email or phone:** a line with the address and the number in place of the form, and `group-visits` goes.
- **No group visits:** the section goes.

## What Upstart cannot do, and how the brief handles it

- **Selling tickets and memberships:** Upstart has no payments, so tickets are a link to the museum's own ticketing page. Free time slots, reserved seats for talks and group visits are requests sent through forms, which the museum confirms by email.
- **The catalogue:** sold "at the museum shop", or through a link to the shop's own page.
- **Zooming into a work:** there is no deep-zoom viewer and no embed. "Look closer" shows the image file at its own size, and step back's scale never goes beyond that file's pixels.
- **Maps:** the address is set large with a "Get directions" link, and there is no map embed.
- **Video, the audio guide and a feed of visitors' photos:** left out.
