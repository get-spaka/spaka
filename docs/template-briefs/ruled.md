# Ruled: brief

One of wave 5's templates: a cohort-based online course opening its enrollment (`launch`). It is the wave's only still template and the first of all 29: nothing moves but what the visitor points at. Point at a week of the syllabus and its two evenings light up on the cohort's calendar beside it, in highlighter. Point at an evening and its week lights up. Hover and focus states, 150 to 200 ms, and no script at all.

`ruled` is a working name. The wave sheet named it Syllabus, but Framer's marketplace sells a template called Syllabus (by Alex & Co) for "a school that teaches in live cohorts", the very subject; its page now reads "This template isn't available on Framer Marketplace right now", but it was sold. The nearest names were taken too: Handout (a fast-food HTML template on ThemeForest), Daybook (a free Framer template for fractional executives), Longhand (a Framer template for essayists), Lectern (two Framer templates, for events and for course sales pages), Quire (Framer and Webflow), Precept (Framer, for manufacturers), and Coursebook, Cohorts, Coursework, Fieldnotes, Applied and Lyceum Academy, all Framer course templates. A web search found no template or theme called Ruled on Framer, Webflow, ThemeForest, Squarespace or Shopify; ThemeForest has Ruleer, an agency template, and three tagged "ruler". The name is a ruled page: the notebook every writing course starts on, and a syllabus ruled into weeks. It is the template's, not the business's.

Of those Framer templates, Coursebook comes nearest the signature effect: on its "Term Map" a visitor taps the evenings they are free and the courses that fit light up on a 12-week timetable, with seats left on each bar. Ruled does neither: it shows one course, and lights a week and its days from each other, as a reader planning their evenings would cross from one to the other in their head. There is no filtering and no seat count in the calendar. Applied, another, has a countdown to the enrollment deadline, which Ruled leaves out with the trend's other clichés.

## Concept

**Concept.** A writing course you read like the syllabus handed round on its first evening. The weeks are numbered and ruled, each with its two dates. The cohort's calendar sits beside them, its course days washed in pale highlighter. The page is ink on paper, and its one color is a teacher's highlighter, marking the one phrase in each section that matters. A wide picture of the instructor's desk opens the page. Under it come the headline and, on one line, everything a reader must know before saying yes: "12 live sessions · Jan 12 – Feb 18, 2027 · Tue & Thu, 7–8:30 pm ET · On Zoom, 14 writers". The button to ask for a place sits beside it. The site is still because the course sells calm attention: six weeks with one piece of writing, two evenings a week. A page that slid and faded would contradict the promise to an adult deciding, late in the evening, whether to give twelve of them. It feels finished and alive through its type, its rhythm and its marks, not through movement.

It must not read as Margin, Cursus, Codicil, Beacon or Earshot. Margin is one writer's weekly letter, with a masthead, notes in the margin and pictures in one green ink; Cursus a résumé that folds to one printed page; Codicil a law firm whose red pen strikes a phrase out; Beacon a dark launch page with a waitlist count and a glow; Earshot lights its captions word by word at the pace of speech. Ruled is a course's dated plan, said plainly, with the evenings it will take.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **The Slow Draft**, a six-week live online course in the personal essay, taught by **Hollis Ouellette** (she/her) from Burlington, Vermont.
- **The teacher:** born in Winooski, the old mill city across the river from Burlington, granddaughter of Quebec mill workers. She spent fourteen years writing features and a weekly column for daily papers in Maine and Vermont. She then taught the personal essay in a college's evening program for six years, and her essays came out as a collection, *The Lower Falls*, in 2022. She has taught The Slow Draft fifteen times since its first cohort of six in January 2021.
- **The place:** a second-floor back room on North Street, in the Old North End, with a desk at the window and a slice of Lake Champlain over the roofs. Every session is taught from there on Zoom.
- **The course:** one essay, written slowly and finished: a first draft by the end of week two, a second by the end of week five, and a reading on the last evening.
  - twelve live sessions, Tuesdays and Thursdays from 7 to 8:30 pm Eastern; Tuesday is a talk and a reading, Thursday a workshop;
  - fourteen writers a cohort, in pods of three who trade pages each week;
  - two letters from Hollis on the two drafts, two pages each;
  - a twenty-minute call with her in week four;
  - about three hours of writing a week between sessions;
  - sessions recorded for the group only, deleted when the course ends;
  - captions on in every session.
- **The weeks of the winter cohort:**
  1. "The story you keep telling" (January 12 and 14);
  2. "Scene and summary" (January 19 and 21), draft one due Sunday, January 24;
  3. "The second voice" (January 26 and 28), on the narrator who knows how it ended;
  4. "Forty to one" (February 2 and 4): cutting, named after Vermont's sugaring, forty gallons of sap to one of syrup;
  5. "Endings that open" (February 9 and 11), draft two due Sunday, February 14;
  6. "Out loud, and out" (February 16 and 18): where to send it, then reading night, guests welcome.
- **Cohorts:** three a year.
  - Winter 2027 (January 12 to February 18) is enrolling now; enrollment closes Monday, January 4; five of its fourteen places are left.
  - Spring 2027 (April 6 to May 13) has a waitlist; its enrollment opens Monday, February 22.
  - Fall 2027 runs September 14 to October 21.
- **Tuition:** $680, in full or in two payments of $340.
  - Four places in every cohort are pay-what-you-can, from $200, with no reasons asked.
  - Refunds: in full until the first evening, half until the end of week one. After that there is no refund, but a place can move to the next cohort, once.
- **Enrolling:** a writer asks for a place with a short form. Hollis writes back within two days with a payment link from her own invoicing, and holds the place for three days. Nothing is charged on the site.
- **The phone** in the texts is (802) 555-0163, "Mondays, 10 to noon, if you'd rather talk it through", a number reserved for fiction.

The names were searched: no course, workshop or newsletter called The Slow Draft came up, in Burlington or elsewhere (the nearest were The Wild Draft, a novel cohort, and Hugo House's "The Practice of Slow Writing"). Nobody called Hollis Ouellette came up. No existing template or brief is set in Burlington or in Vermont.

The same structure suits:
- any course taught to a cohort on set dates: a photography course, a drawing or design cohort, a coding or data bootcamp, a language course;
- a cooking class series online, or a choir's or a pottery studio's term (taught in a room, see What varies);
- a book-writing program over twelve weeks, or a reading group with a syllabus.

The agent rewrites the weeks and their dates, the cohorts, the tuition, the readings, the teacher and the town.

## Trends it draws on

These come from eleven course sites opened in November 2026, read with WebFetch for their text and with curl for their fonts and colors. Many of the 2021-22 cohort brands have closed (Write of Passage, altMBA); those still running are plain, specific and dated. Two sites were read for what not to do (Kevin Mullins's photography course, olivergareis.com/course). Each trend below was seen on at least two of the sites.

1. **The cohort as one line of facts, beside the button.** maven.com (each course card gives its dates, "Jan 30 — Mar 27, 2027"); ship30for30.com (its top bar: "Live Cohort: Jan 18 – Feb 22, 2027 | 11 Live Sessions | Mondays & Thursdays, 12 PM ET"); smashingconf.com/online-workshops ("5 sessions · Nov 9–24"); sfpc.study ("Sun 3pm ET or Wed 6:30pm ET · 10 weeks · Online (Zoom)"); shillingtoneducation.com ("Next Course Starts: January 2027").
2. **A numbered syllabus, with dates, counts and lengths.** maven.com/parlance-labs/evals (Weeks 1 to 9, each session with its date and time, tagged "Optional" or "Free preview"); olivergareis.com/course (chapters 01/08 to 08/08 with their video counts and run times); Kevin Mullins's course (three dated live sessions).
3. **Serif headlines, calm and editorial.** maven.com (a Font Bureau serif at 56 px); interfacecraft.dev (Signifier); ship30for30.com (Georgia); sfpc.study (the Hershey single-stroke plotter font over Times and Garamond).
4. **Classes and modules as cards in a bordered grid, with status tags.** sfpc.study ("applications closed", "online class"); smashingconf.com/online-workshops (category chips and "Early birds!"); interfacecraft.dev (its collections as colored, book-like volumes).
5. **The teacher's face and real work instead of stock.** maven.com (cut-out portraits in the hero); olivergareis.com/course (a portrait video); smashingconf.com (a photo on every workshop card); recurse.com (three documentary photos); shillingtoneducation.com (a band of students' posters).

Their movement says the same thing as their type. Recurse and SFPC do not move at all. Shillington and the School of MA (schoolofma.org) run one slow loop, Interface Craft fans its cards in once, and the heaviest, olivergareis.com, splits every heading line by line and plays twelve videos.

**The gap:** none of them lets a reader see the course in their own evenings. The syllabus is a list of weeks; the dates are a line in the hero, or a table further down. A reader planning six weeks of Tuesdays and Thursdays crosses from one to the other in their head. Ruled sets the weeks beside their calendar and lights each from the other. It takes trend 1 for its hero, trend 2 for its syllabus, trend 3 for its type (Gelasio is the open Georgia) and trend 5 for its pictures. From trend 4 it keeps only the status, as a plain word in the cohorts table.

**Common, and left outside:** countdown timers and "last day to save" bars; struck-out "worth" prices and income promises; crowd counters ("Join 10,000+", stars with a row of avatars, "42 enrolled last week") and "Limited seats" with no number; stock laptops and headphones; "Enroll Now" five times a page, emoji bullets, a chat bubble, quizzes; a monthly or yearly toggle on a course bought once; the dark glowing SaaS look and the "transform your life" tone; the video sales letter (olivergareis.com's dimmed instructor video) and the endless loop of students' work (shillingtoneducation.com).

**What makes it its own:**
- **the picture first, then the facts:** a wide picture of Hollis's desk at the window in January light, across the page under the header, with a caption line under it. Below it come the label, the headline with its one mark, and a short paragraph. Then comes a ruled row with the cohort's facts on one line, the main button beside them, and under them, smaller: "5 of 14 places left · Enrollment closes Monday, January 4 · $680, or two payments of $340". Fifteen of the 28 set a headline beside a picture and Linen sets its headline above one; Ruled is the first to open on the picture and put every word under it;
- **the linked syllabus:**
  - the six weeks as ruled rows, each with its number hanging in pencil left of a fine vertical rule, its title, a line, and its dates ("Tue Jan 19 · Thu Jan 21 · Draft one due Sun Jan 24");
  - beside them on a desktop, the cohort's calendar: the course's weeks as rows of seven days, Monday first, the month named where it changes. Each session day holds its word ("Talk", "Workshop", "Reading night") and its time, in a pale wash; a due day carries an ink tick;
  - hovering or focusing a week lights its sessions on the calendar in highlighter, and hovering or focusing an evening lights its week, in 150 ms;
  - on a phone the calendar steps aside and each week shows its dates inline, as it always does;
  - nothing follows the clock: no week is "this week", no day is "today";
- **the highlighter:** one pale yellow-green mark per section at most, drawn behind a phrase like a real marker swipe, never over the ink. The same marker underlines the links, and it lights the syllabus. It is the site's only color, and it is never text;
- **the ruled page:** rules do the work boxes do elsewhere. The syllabus, the readings, the cohorts and the questions are ruled lists on paper. The week numbers hang in their margin column, and the figures sit in the text (Gelasio's old-style figures), except in the calendar and the prices, where they line up;
- **the first lines:** proof from past cohorts is not praise but what they wrote. Each essay is shown by its opening sentence in large italic, with its title, the writer's first name and town, the cohort, and what became of it ("Read on reading night; published in a Vermont magazine, spring 2026");
- **said plainly:** "It's for you if" and "It may not be for you if", two ruled columns of plain sentences, with no ticks and no crosses. Refunds are said in one sentence each, and the pay-what-you-can places in one ruled note.

**What it leaves out:**
- online checkout and payments, a calendar widget, an "add to calendar" file, a Zoom or video embed, a booking tool for the one-on-ones (booked by email), a chat bubble, and a count of anything that is not a place left;
- the course clichés listed above, and the writing-course ones: a typewriter, a fountain pen on cream paper, a coffee cup on a laptop, handwriting fonts, crumpled paper, a quill;
- **other templates' devices:**
  - words lit one after another, or at any pace (Earshot);
  - a phrase struck through and written over (Codicil);
  - notes in the margin, pictures in one ink developing into color, and a masthead (Margin);
  - a sheet that folds or rises (Cursus);
  - a waitlist count and a launch track of milestones (Beacon);
  - a chartreuse slab and anything bright on black (Reel);
  - links that take a yellow ground on hover, and a week of bays marked today and next (Decal);
  - a month marked "Now" and a line that changes with the hour (Pail);
  - an open-or-closed card and a status dot (Saucer);
  - ticks beside promises (Mend);
  - an italic accent word in a heading (Corner, Folio, Linen) and a huge wordmark across the footer (Corner, Ember, Seam);
  - a headline over a wide picture seen through a sheer, with a booking bar at its foot (Linen);
  - lines that draw themselves (Chalk, Seam, Trestle), a marker climbing (Coteau), a sheet or card that tilts (Ember, Beacon);
- **its siblings' devices:** zones lit as a plan is picked (Overhead), tickets with service windows (Spindle), strings that move as the pointer crosses them (Strum), cards rising from behind covers (Dogear), a paper rota sorting itself (Covered), tabs of commands and racing bars (Heats), things unrolling as they come into view (Bolster), anything lowered from above (Batten);
- all movement but hover and focus: no reveals, no smooth scrolling, no load animation, no page transitions, no parallax, no sticky header that changes, no library and no script.

## Type

Both are files of the template, as `woff2` subset to Latin and Latin Extended (for names such as Bédard, Dvořák and Łucja), under the SIL Open Font License, from Google Fonts, declared in `styles/site.css`. Both were checked on Google Fonts:
- **Gelasio** (Eben Sorkin) ships as a variable file, weight 400 to 700, with its own variable italic, in Latin, Latin Extended and Vietnamese. Its OpenType features include `lnum`, `pnum` and `tnum`; its default figures are old-style, as Georgia's are.
- **Mulish** ships as a variable file, weight 200 to 1000, with its italic, in Latin, Latin Extended, Cyrillic and Vietnamese. It has no tabular or old-style figures (its features are `calt`, `ccmp`, `dnom`, `frac`, `liga`, `locl` and `numr`), so every column of figures is set in Gelasio.

| The second voice | Gelasio italic | 400: the essays' first lines at `clamp(1.5rem, 1.5vw + 1rem, 2.25rem)`, the line under each page's title, essay titles in the reading list; book titles in italic, essay titles in quotes |
| Figures | Gelasio | old-style in running text; `lining-nums tabular-nums` for the calendar's days (500 at 1.125 rem), the tuition ("$680" at 3 rem in 500) and the week numbers (400 at 2.75 rem, pencil) |
| Body | Mulish | 400, 1.0625 rem, `leading-[1.7]`, `text-base-content/80`, measure `max-w-[64ch]`; buttons 700 at 1 rem, sentence case |

Gelasio was drawn to match Georgia's metrics, the serif a generation learned to read on screens and the one Ship 30 sets its headlines in. It is sturdy and warm, with ball terminals, broad letters and a true italic. It is made for reading, so at large sizes it sounds like a teacher, not a luxury brand. Mulish is a plain, open, low-contrast sans, Vernon Adams's Muli grown into a variable family. It keeps the facts, the forms and the calendar's small words quiet, so that the serif and the marks carry the voice.

Ruled out:
- every font the inventory lists;
- the sibling rows' fonts: League Gothic, Spectral, Geologica, Golos Text, Brygada 1918, Libre Franklin, Besley, Source Sans 3, Victor Mono, Shrikhand, Lexend, Ultra, Karla, Cutive Mono, Imbue, Afacad, Castoro, Reddit Sans, Reddit Mono;
- the course sites' own (Signifier is commercial; Times and Garamond read as a university's letterhead);
- EB Garamond, Cormorant, Playfair Display and Lora, the "tasteful default" of section 6 of the web-design skill;
- every handwriting font: the highlighter is the only hand on the page;
- a monospace, which no part of the course needs.

**If a sibling takes them,** two pairings keep the same voice (a warm reading serif with a true italic, a plain sans):
- **Piazzolla** (variable, optical size 8 to 30 and weight 100 to 900, with italics; a compact book face, at its largest optical size for the headings) with **Nunito Sans** (variable, weight 200 to 1000, with italics);
- **Vollkorn** (variable, weight 400 to 900, with italics; dark and bookish) with **Asap** (variable, weight 100 to 900, with italics).

All four were checked on Google Fonts under the OFL, with Latin Extended, and none is used by a template, a brief or the wave sheet.

## Palette

Paper, and a writing teacher's three tools: ink, pencil and one highlighter. The ink is a near-black with a breath of warmth. The paper is a white with the faintest tint of the highlighter's own hue, a copy-paper white, never cream. The pencil is a graphite gray for labels, times and week numbers. The highlighter is a pale yellow-green at h 110, the hue no template owns (the inventory's h 106 to 122 gap). It is owned as a mark, not a ground: never a section's band, never on a dark surface (Reel's chartreuse is neon on black), never text.

Every pair of text and background passes WCAG AA, measured from these values (oklch converted to sRGB, WCAG relative luminance):
- ink on paper 16.6:1, on the second sheet 15.1:1, on the wash 15.1:1, on the highlighter 14.1:1, on the pressed highlighter 11.9:1;
- pencil on paper 6.5:1, on the second sheet 5.9:1, on the wash 6.0:1, on the highlighter 5.6:1;
- paper on ink 14.9:1, paper on pencil 6.5:1;
- `text-base-content/80` on paper 8.9:1, on the second sheet 8.3:1;
- `text-base-content/70` on paper 6.3:1, on the second sheet 6.0:1, on the highlighter 5.8:1.

`text-base-content/60` falls to 4.5:1 on paper and 4.3:1 on the second sheet: no text is set lighter than `/70`. The highlighter on paper is 1.17:1 and the wash on paper 1.10:1. Both are fills behind ink, never text and never a line that must be seen alone. A lit week and its days are also marked by their figures turning 700 and the week's rule turning ink, and every date is written out in text, so the highlight adds and never carries alone.

**The pressed highlighter** (a button under the pointer, a lit day under the pointer) is not a variable. `styles/site.css` makes it from the primary, `oklch(from var(--color-primary) calc(l - 0.055) calc(c + 0.005) h)`, which is oklch(0.87 0.17 110) with these values, so the Theme tab restyles it with the rest.

**The mark** is drawn from the primary too. It is a band from 18% to 90% of the line's height, behind the text, with slightly uneven ends from a small mask (`styles/mark.svg`, shapes only, no color). It uses `box-decoration-break: clone` so that it breaks across lines, and `padding-inline: 0.12em`.

Other settings:
- buttons and fields are nearly square, like a marker's chisel tip (`--theme-radius-field: 0.25rem`); the main button is highlighter-filled with a 1px ink border so its edge holds on paper; the second is outlined in ink;
- relief is off (`--theme-depth: 0`);
- density is spacious (`--theme-size: 0.28125rem`);
- borders are fine (`--theme-border: 1px`);
- links in text are ink, with a highlighter band under their lower half (see Motion);
- the status colors are derived, as always, and the forms' errors use them.

It is not:
- Decal's truck yellow (h 96, saturated, links on a yellow ground);
- Earshot's caption yellow (h 102, lit word by word);
- Trestle's oiled olive (h 105, low chroma, a color of type and panels);
- Reel's chartreuse (h 123, on black);
- the tasteful default: no cream, no muted red, and a reading serif rather than a display one.

## Motion

**Still**, the web-design skill's first level: hover and focus states only, with transitions of 150 to 200 ms. Why: the course sells calm attention, six weeks with one essay, and the page should behave as the course does. Nothing asks for the eye but the words. What answers the visitor answers them only where they point, at once and briefly. Its life comes from elsewhere: the large Gelasio and its italic, the ruled rhythm of every list, generous space (`py-24 md:py-36`), the one mark per section, and the syllabus that lights its evenings.

The template has **no `scripts/` folder, no `scripts/libraries.json` and no library**. Everything below is CSS in `styles/site.css`. The page with no script is the page itself. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` every transition below is left out (they sit in `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`), and each state still appears, at once. In edit mode Spaka's stillness rule stops transitions and no script runs (there is none): the page is exactly as a visitor sees it at rest. A hover there still lights a week, at once, and nothing ever depends on it. There is no `stop` to write, and nothing follows the clock, so `template.json` sets no `shoot.clock`.

- **The linked syllabus** (`data-syllabus` on the section; `data-week="2"` on week 2's row and on each of its calendar days, sessions and due day alike):
  - one rule a week number, from 1 to 16, written out in the stylesheet:
    ```css
    [data-syllabus]:has([data-week="2"]:is(:focus-within, :target)) [data-week="2"]
    ```
    plus its `:hover` twin inside `@media (hover: hover)`. Every element of a week lights when any one of them is hovered, focused or targeted, so the link runs both ways with one rule;
  - lit, a calendar day takes the highlighter fill and its figure turns 700. A week's title takes the mark, its figure turns ink, and its fine vertical rule turns from the border color to ink. Only `background-color`, `color` and `border-color` change, over 150 ms, `ease-out`. Weights change at once, in the calendar's fixed cells only, so no text reflows;
  - under the pointer, a lit day takes the pressed highlighter, so the visitor sees which evening they are on;
  - each calendar day is a link to its week (`<a href="#week-2">`), and each week row's title is a link to the week's part of `/syllabus`. A tap or Enter goes there, and the week stays lit through `:target` while the address holds it. Both kinds of link take focus: the focus ring is 2px of ink, offset 3px, with no transition;
  - the calendar is a `<table>` with its caption ("The winter cohort's calendar"). Each session day's link reads in full to a screen reader ("Tuesday, January 19: Talk, Scene and summary, week 2"). The highlight is only a background, so a screen reader loses nothing without it;
  - on the home page too, each week row carries `id="week-2"`, so a calendar day's link lights its row there, while the row's title leads on to the week on `/syllabus`;
  - on a phone (below 768 px) the calendar is not shown (`hidden md:block`) and each week row keeps its dates inline. Nothing is lost: the dates were always in the rows;
  - the rules stop at week 16; the design notes say so, and how to add one for a longer course.
- **Marks** (`.mark` on a `<span>`): static. The highlighter band behind a phrase is one per section at most: the hero's "finish it", the evening's "in silence", the tuition's "no reasons asked". A maker who marks more than one phrase a section is told by the design notes to choose.
- **Links in text:** ink, with a band of highlighter under their lower half (`background: linear-gradient(var(--color-primary), var(--color-primary)) no-repeat 0 92% / 100% 0.32em`). On hover or focus the band thickens to 0.62 em, half the letters, like a second pass of the marker (`background-size`, 150 ms, `ease-out`). It never becomes a full ground, as Decal's links do. The reading list's, the notes' and the cohorts' rows are links of the same kind.
- **Buttons:**
  - `.btn-primary`: highlighter with its ink border; on hover the pressed highlighter (`background-color`, 150 ms, `ease-out`); nothing moves, nothing grows or casts a shadow;
  - `.btn-outline`: ink outline; on hover it fills with ink and its text turns paper (`background-color` and `color`, 150 ms).
- **Questions** (`<details>`): the summary's plus becomes a minus, with no rotation, and the answer appears with no height animation. The summary's text takes the links' band on hover.
- **Fields:** on focus a field's 1px border turns ink and gains the 2px ink ring, with no transition.
- **The header** is not sticky: it scrolls away with the page, and the button to ask for a place comes again at the foot of every page. On `/syllabus` only, the calendar is sticky beside the long syllabus on a desktop (`md:sticky md:top-8`), which is a position, not a movement.

## Pages

The cohorts are the template's own `cohorts` collection, read by the hero's facts line, the cohorts table, the forms' choices and the footer. So the next cohort's dates are typed once. The weeks are the template's own `weeks` collection. ## Enrolling, and what stays outside

Spaka has no checkout, no payments, no calendar and no embeds, so:
- **a place is asked for** (as built). The teacher answers by email with a payment link from her own invoicing tool, and the form and its answer say so before and after sending. A teacher with her own checkout (a payment link, a course platform) links every "Ask for a place" to it instead (see What varies), never embeds it;
- **places left are a number the maker types:** `places_left` shows "5 of 14 places left", 0 shows "This cohort is full", and empty shows nothing. Nothing counts down, and nothing counts enrollments;
- **the calendar is the course's printed dates,** built from the weeks' records, not a widget. Nobody picks a date in it, and there is no "add to calendar" file: the dates go out with the payment link;
- **time zones are a typed line** ("7 pm Eastern is 4 pm in California and midnight in London"), never the visitor's clock;
- **the one-on-one calls** are booked by email once enrolled, with no booking tool;
- **the sessions** are on Zoom, linked by email to enrolled writers, never on the site;
- **the waitlist** is one email when enrollment opens, said wherever the field is.

## What varies

These are the choices the agent asks when the maker's messages leave them open, page type by page type, the recommended one first. The dates, the number of places left, the tuition, the weeks' titles and the readings are facts: the agent asks for them once the page is built, or leaves them out rather than guess.

### Home (`/`), with the choices for the whole site

#### How do writers enroll?
- **They ask for a place, and you send a payment link** (as built): the request form on `/enroll`; requests arrive in the Content tab and you answer by email. Every "Ask for a place" leads to `/enroll#ask`.
- **On your own checkout:** every "Ask for a place" becomes "Enroll" and links to your checkout page (a payment link, Teachable, Podia, Maven). `/enroll` keeps the cohorts, the tuition, the waitlist and the questions, with a large "Enroll" in place of the form. `data/requests/` and its fragment go. The guide tells the agent to ask for the address, never to invent it.
- **By application:** the form adds "Your first page" (up to 3,000 characters) and "Why now?". The button says "Apply", the answer says when decisions come, and `closes` becomes the applications' deadline. This is for a course that chooses its writers.

#### How is the course priced?
- **One price, with two payments and pay-what-you-can places** (as built).
- **Two levels:** for a course sold with or without extra calls or feedback; the tuition card becomes two cards (adapted from `apps/desktop/gallery/pricing/pricing-two-plans`), and the form's payment choice becomes the level.
- **Free, or by donation:** the price, the payment choice and the pay-what-you-can note go; the tuition card says "Free. Give what you can at the end, if you'd like."

#### Where does the cohort meet?
- **Live online** (as built): "On Zoom" in the facts, the time zones line, the captions.
- **In a room:** the format becomes the place ("At the Old North End studio"). The time zones line gives way to a "Where we meet" section on `/enroll` (adapted from `apps/desktop/gallery/contact/contact-find-us`, without its map placeholder), and recordings leave the questions. This suits a pottery or cooking course.

When adapting, the essays of "What they wrote" are invented and never stay: the agent asks for real first lines, given with the writers' permission, or removes the section and its page part. The hero's places line follows `places_left` and stays empty rather than guessed. The time zones line follows the maker's own zone.

- **Once a week:** each week's `second_date` stays empty; the calendar shows one session a row; the hero says "6 live sessions · Thursdays, 7–8:30 pm ET"; the evening's timeline stays.
