# Cursus: brief

One of wave 4's templates: the personal résumé (`portfolio`), one person's CV and professional profile, for job seekers and freelancers of any trade. Its motion is quiet and useful: a résumé that folds from its full length to one page under the visitor's hand, a sheet whose page ends are drawn on it, and careers drawn as spans of time.

Corrected after the author's decisions Q415 and Q416: the palette moves off purple (the winery, Coteau, keeps it) to a deep indigo, oklch(0.45 0.15 270) (asked around hue 275, set at 270 to keep clear of Transfer's violet at 282), with the sheet, desk, ink and dark retuned around it and the mint and fern kept; the printed CV leaves out the footer, so Upstart's credits link is not printed, which is accepted.

**Why `portfolio`:** the category is where a maker looks for a site about one person and their work. `business` is a place or a service sold, `blog` dated posts. Folio and Reel are already portfolios, of projects: Folio a quiet index of pictures for one photographer, Reel a loud studio's cases. Cursus is the portfolio of a career: what a person has done, where, for how long, with what result, and what they want next. It has no project pictures at its heart and reads first as a document.

`cursus` is a working name: Latin for a course or a run, the root of *curriculum* ("curriculum vitae", the course of a life), and in French one's path through school and work. A web search found no website template or theme of that name on Framer, Webflow, WordPress, ThemeForest or Wix; the closest is Cursio, a Framer template for course sellers.

## Concept

**Concept.** A résumé site that *is* the résumé. One set of records is shown twice: as a profile for people who browse (what this person does, what they are open to, the proof, the career at a glance) and as a typeset sheet, true to the paper, that folds from its full length to a one-page CV with one switch and prints, or saves as a PDF, exactly as it is shown. The maker keeps no Word file in step with the site any more: the site is the file. The page ends are drawn on the sheet, so the maker sees in Upstart, while editing, whether their CV still fits on one page. Above everything, a plain panel of **terms** answers the recruiter's first question: open to what, how, from when, and where.

The type is a warm humanist serif for the name and headings, a clear grotesk for reading and a soft mono for dates and labels; the page is a cool white sheet on a pale blue-grey desk, with one deep indigo ink and a mint panel for the terms.

It must not read as Folio or Reel. Folio is a photographer's numbered index of projects whose pictures follow the cursor, the name split at poster size, a local clock and an "at work" dot. Reel is black, loud and sideways. Cursus has no project index, no clock and no dot: its availability is a set of written terms, its proof is figures and colleagues' words, and its center is a document you can take away.

**The fictional person** its texts are written for is **Hollis Amadi**, an operations lead in Cincinnati, Ohio. She chose an operations career that is not design or code on purpose: the same structure must read as well for a nurse, a teacher or a site manager as for an engineer.
- **Who she is:** thirteen years in operations and logistics, the last eight in food: a produce wholesaler, then a regional food-bank network. She lives in Northside, Cincinnati, and works across the Cincinnati, Dayton and Louisville triangle.
- **Now:** Director of Operations at **Hearthline Food Network** (invented), which supplies 140 partner pantries and meal programs in eleven counties of southwest Ohio and northern Kentucky from two warehouses, Queensgate in Cincinnati and Covington, Kentucky (opened 2025). She runs 46 staff, 22 trucks and 1,900 volunteers a month, and a budget of $9 million.
- **Before:** night operations manager, then promoted from shift supervisor, at **Larch & Dunn Produce** (invented), a produce wholesaler in Dayton serving grocers and school districts (2018–2022); logistics coordinator at **Ninebark Logistics** (invented), a third-party logistics firm in Louisville (2015–2018); food recovery coordinator at **Fieldnote Food Recovery** (invented), Columbus (2013–2015). In 2024, a six-week operations audit for the **Little Miami School Meals Co-op** (invented). Volunteer treasurer of the Northside Community Orchard (invented) since 2021.
- **What she has done, in figures:** late or missed pantry deliveries down from 9% to 2% in fourteen months; 38% less produce and dairy lost to the cold chain (about 310,000 pounds a year); the Covington warehouse, 48,000 square feet and $2.1 million, opened on time and $60,000 under budget; yearly turnover on the Dayton night shift down from 61% to 24%; pick accuracy from 97.1% to 99.4%.
- **Her schooling and credentials:** a B.A. in Geography from an invented Sycamore State University (2013); a certificate in production and inventory management (2020), a Lean Six Sigma Green Belt (2019), OSHA 30-hour general industry (2017) and food protection manager certification (2022). English, Igbo (conversational) and working Spanish, "with drivers and volunteers".
- **What she wants:** a Head of Operations or COO role at a mission-driven organization, full-time, from February 2027, in the triangle or remote with travel. Meanwhile, one two-to-six-week operations audit at a time. Her references are two former managers, given after a first conversation.

The names were searched: no Hollis Amadi came up in operations or logistics, and none of Hearthline Food Network, Larch & Dunn, Ninebark Logistics or Fieldnote Food Recovery exists. No existing template is set in Cincinnati.

The same structure suits anyone whose career is the thing to show: a nurse or a nurse manager, a teacher or a school leader, a project or product manager, a chef de cuisine, an engineer of any kind, a lawyer looking to move firms, an accountant, a researcher or a postdoc, a copywriter or a UX writer, a site manager, a translator, a graduate looking for a first job, and a freelancer or consultant who sells their experience. The agent rewrites the terms, the career, the figures, the highlights and the skills.

## Trends it draws on

Their structure was read with WebFetch, their fonts and colors from their own HTML and stylesheets with curl. Looked at but not counted: drvickimcintosh.com (a family doctor's page on a site builder, older in style), line-cv.com (only a redirect) and Land-book's résumé category (it refused the connection). Most of these people design or build software; rfeasley.io (a content designer), hovighagopian.com (a cinematographer) and balajmarius.com (a managing director) are the others, which is why the template writes for an operations lead. **What makes it its own:**
- **the fold:** a switch above the résumé, "Full · One page". One page folds the sheet down to the CV a recruiter asks for: older roles close to a single line, each role keeps its first two points, sections marked for the full résumé fold away, and the sheet shrinks to its page. The entries fold up in place like paper being creased, and open again. What prints is what is shown. The trend's PDF, made from the same records as the site and never out of date;
- **the sheet:** the résumé is typeset in print units, at the size of a Letter page (A4 as a choice), on a desk, with a hairline and "end of page 1" drawn where each page ends, so the maker sees in edit mode whether it still fits. On the home page the same sheet sits small in the hero, in its one-page form, as the thing to take away;
- **the terms:** what the person is open to, as a short form filled in: Looking for, Kind of work, From, Where, Also. Written words on a mint panel, the same part on the home page, the contact page and the sheet, with no dot and no clock;
- **spans:** every role in the career ledger carries a thin bar on a shared line of years, from the first job to now, so tenure, overlaps and gaps read at a glance, the way a recruiter reads a CV;
- **highlights told as an interview answer:** each a page with its figure, the situation, what she did, what changed and who saw it, with the colleague's words.

**What it leaves out:**
- skill bars, percentages, star levels and charts of skills; a typing headline ("I'm a…"), a terminal or code look; a photo-led grid of projects; a dark-mode toggle; an avatar in 3D; a cursor of its own; a pulsing "hire me" badge;
- **other templates' devices:** a numbered project index with pictures following the cursor, the name split at poster size, a local-time clock and an "at work" dot (Folio); anything sideways or tilted slabs (Reel); a card that turns over (Saucer's door card); a ribbon, a ticker or a stamp (Corner); manila dividers and struck-through words (Codicil); hang tags (Seam); margin notes and a reading-progress rule (Margin); lines drawing themselves (Chalk, Seam); an envelope or a seal (Kindly); a caption marker under spoken words (Earshot); page transitions; a huge wordmark across the footer; an italic accent word in a heading; handwriting;
- smooth scrolling: the browser's own scroll, for a recruiter reading on a phone between meetings.

## Type

All three are files of the template, as `woff2` subset to Latin and Latin Extended (for names such as Bălaj or Peña), under the SIL Open Font License, from Google Fonts, declared in `styles/site.css`. None is used by an existing template.

| The sheet (print units, the same on screen and on paper) | the three above | the name in the serif at 26 pt; the headline at 11 pt; section labels in the mono at 7.5 pt, uppercase, `tracking-[0.08em]`, in the primary color; roles at 10.5 pt in 600; text at 9.5 pt on 1.35; dates in the mono at 8.5 pt |

Hedvig Letters Serif (Kristian Möller, 2024) is a humanist serif with a slight warmth and sturdy serifs, drawn for text sizes but handsome large: it gives the name the dignity of a letterhead without the coldness of a corporate serif. Google Sans Code (2025) is a soft, friendly mono that reads as dates and labels rather than as code, which matters for a nurse's or a teacher's CV.

## Motion

Quiet and useful. Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode no script runs and no animation plays: the switch shows Full, every entry is open, the page-end lines are drawn (they are CSS, and help the maker most there), the small sheet is in place and every span is at its full length. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too. Library: Motion, pinned on jsDelivr as in the site-scripts skill, under MIT. Everything else is CSS. No smooth scrolling, no page transitions, no loader, no WebGL.

- **The fold** (`data-fold-switch` on the switch, `data-fold` on what folds; CSS for the state, `scripts/fold.js` for the movement):
  - the switch is a fieldset of two radio inputs named `length`, "Full" (checked in the markup) and "One page", styled as one segmented control in the mono;
  - the state is CSS, so it works with no script and in the preview: `html:has([name="length"][value="one"]:checked)` closes what is marked: an entry with `data-fold="line"` shows its dates, role and organization on one line; `data-fold="out"` (a section or an entry) is left out; inside an open entry, points after the second are left out (`[data-points] > li:nth-child(n+3)`); a skill with `data-fold="out"` goes;
  - the records say what folds (`one_page` on each experience: full, line or out), so the maker decides in the Content tab, not in the markup;
  - the script animates the change: it measures each entry before and after, then animates the entries' heights from one to the other and moves the entries below by the difference (`y`, 0.45 s, `ease: [0.65, 0, 0.35, 1]`), the closing points fading out first (`opacity: [1, 0]`, 0.2 s); a folded entry keeps a double hairline where its points were, like a crease. It clears every style it set at the end;
  - `/resume#one-page` opens the résumé folded (the script checks the radio on load); with no script, it opens at full length;
  - under reduced motion, the state changes at once; `stop` removes its listeners and the styles it set.
- **The sheet and its page ends** (`data-sheet`; CSS, with the count in `scripts/fold.js`):
  - on screens from 1024 px, the sheet is 8.5 in wide (210 mm for A4) with 0.6 in of padding, typeset in points, and shown with `zoom: 1.15` so its text reads well on a screen while its page lines still fall where the print's do; on a phone it becomes the page itself, one column in rem, with no page lines;
  - the page ends are a repeating hairline in the border color every 11 in (297 mm for A4), each labeled in the mono, "End of page 1", "End of page 2", by elements the part writes (three, enough for a long CV) and that the sheet's height clips: CSS only, so they show in edit mode;
  - the script watches the sheet's height (`ResizeObserver`) and sets `data-pages` on it; the toolbar holds three lines written in the template, "Fits on one page" (with a fern check), "Runs to 2 pages" and the plain line "Letter · lines mark each page's end", and the stylesheet shows the one that matches (the plain one with no script);
  - the guides are approximate by nature: the print breaks between entries (`break-inside: avoid`), not across them. The guide says so to the agent.
- **Print** (`data-print` on a button; `scripts/print.js`, no library): "Print or save as PDF" calls `window.print()`; the browser's print dialog saves a PDF. The button stays visible in edit mode, where it does nothing. Print without the button (the browser's menu) gives the same result, since everything is in the print stylesheet. Links elsewhere ("Save the one-page résumé") go to `/resume#one-page`; nothing prints by itself on load.
- **The small sheet** (`data-sheet-mini` in the home page's hero; `scripts/reveal.js`): the résumé's own parts rendered in their one-page state (a class forces it, whatever the switch) inside an `inert`, `aria-hidden` wrapper at `zoom: 0.42`, linked as a whole to `/resume#one-page`. On load it rises from the desk and straightens (`y: [40, 0]`, `rotate: [-2.5, 0]`, `opacity: [0, 1]`, 0.9 s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`); on hover or focus it lifts 4 px and its shadow deepens (CSS, under no-preference and `(hover: hover)`). With no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode, it is in place and straight.
- **Spans** (`data-span` on a role's bar; CSS only): the ledger gets the first year and today as `--axis-from` and `--axis-to`, and each role its `--from` and `--to` (months counted from year zero, numbers only, computed in Liquid from `start` and `end`; an empty `end` is the date the page was rendered, so a present role runs to the right edge as of the last publish). The bar sits on a hairline track across the ledger, in ink at 35%, the current role in indigo. As the ledger comes into view, each bar grows from its start (`scale: 0 1` to `1 1`, `transform-origin: left`, `animation-timeline: view()`, `animation-range: entry 10% cover 35%`) under no-preference; otherwise, and in edit mode, at full length.
- **Reveals** (`data-reveal`, `scripts/reveal.js`, Motion's `inView`): a section's main elements rise a little (`y: [16, 0]`, `opacity: [0, 1]`, 0.7 s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`), children 70 ms apart under `data-reveal="stagger"`, as in the site-scripts skill. Never on the sheet itself, which must be there at once for a recruiter.
- **The header** is sticky on the sheet's white at 95% with a hairline under it, and does not change as you scroll.

**The print stylesheet** (`@media print` in `styles/site.css`), the other half of the signature:
- `@page { size: letter; margin: 0.55in 0.6in; }` (`size: A4; margin: 14mm 15mm;` for A4); the sheet's own padding and shadow go, as do the desk, the header, the footer (Upstart's credits link included, so a printed CV carries no badge: accepted by the author, Q416), the toolbar, buttons, forms and anything marked `data-print-hide`;
- the sheet prints at its screen sizes in points, in ink on white, the indigo kept for the rule under the name and the section labels (`print-color-adjust: exact` on those only); entries never split across pages (`break-inside: avoid`), section labels stay with what follows (`break-after: avoid`);
- the contact line prints in full (city, email, the site's address, LinkedIn as its address), and other links print as their text;
- what prints follows the switch: full or one page;
- another page printed (the home page, a highlight) prints its content without the header, footer, forms or the small sheet, in the same type.

## Pages

The career is the `experience` collection, the skills `skills`, the schooling `education`, the colleagues' words the gallery's `testimonials`; the résumé sheet and the home page read the same records. The contact form posts to the gallery's `contact` collection. The name and the contact line (city, email, the site's address, LinkedIn) are one part, `parts/contact-line.liquid`; the terms are another, `parts/terms.liquid`; the date of the last update a third, `parts/updated.liquid`: each written once and rendered wherever it appears. ## What varies

The choices the agent asks when the maker's messages leave them open, page type by page type, the recommended one first. The roles, dates, figures, schools, the start date and the places are facts: the agent asks for them, or leaves them out rather than guess (an invented figure on a CV is worse than none). The guide reminds the agent never to publish a home address, a birth date or a phone number unless the maker asks: the city is enough.

### Home (`/`), with the choices for the whole site

#### What are you looking for?
- **A job** (as built): the terms say the role, the kind of work, from when and where; "Read the résumé" leads, and the contact form's first reason is "A role".
- **Clients and projects** (a freelancer or consultant): the terms become "Taking on", "Booking from", "Where", "How I work" (remote, on site, day or project rates "on request"); a services section is added to the home page and the contact page (adapted from `apps/desktop/gallery/services/services-two-columns` over `services`, three to five services with their length and "from" price or "on request"); the form's reasons lead with "A project", and "Get in touch" leads the hero.
- **Both:** the terms keep the role and add "Also" for projects (as built has one such row), with the services section on the contact page only.
- **Not looking, a standing profile:** the terms become "Currently" (role and organization) and "Happy to talk about" (speaking, advising, mentoring); "Open to work" leaves the footer and the sheet's head; the form's reasons are speaking, advice and something else.

#### Which paper, and which word?
- **US Letter, "Résumé"** (as built): `@page` Letter, the sheet 8.5 × 11 in, page lines every 11 in, the word "Résumé" in the header, the page and the buttons; no photo on the sheet.
- **A4, "CV"** (the UK, Europe, most of the world): `@page` A4, the sheet 210 × 297 mm, page lines every 297 mm, "CV" in place of "Résumé" everywhere (the route stays `/resume` unless the maker asks), dates written day-month where they are full.
- **A4, "CV", with a photo** (as is usual in Germany, Austria, France or Spain): as above, and the portrait in the sheet's head, beside the name, a picture from the media library placed in `parts/sheet.liquid` (it prints too); the home page's hero keeps the small sheet.

When adapting, the time zone in the contact page's line follows the maker's town, and dates follow the site's language (`Mar 2022 – now`, `mars 2022 – aujourd'hui`).

### Résumé (`/resume`)

#### Who can read the full résumé?
- **Anyone** (as built): the fold switch, both lengths on the page, both printable.
- **One page only:** the switch goes and the sheet is always folded; the points, roles and sections marked to fold are left out of the site entirely (the agent removes them from the records it shows, with the maker's leave); the page count line stays, for the maker.
- **On request:** the sheet shows its one-page form without the contact line's email; under it, "The full résumé, with references, on request", leading to the contact form with "The full résumé" as its first reason.

When removed: delete `pages/resume.liquid`, `parts/sheet.liquid`, `parts/toolbar.liquid` and `scripts/fold.js`, and its navigation record; point "Read the résumé" to the home page's career (`/#career`), the small sheet goes from the hero (the terms take its place beside the sentence), and every "Save the one-page résumé" goes. The print stylesheet still prints the home page cleanly.

### Highlights (`/highlights`)

#### How are your highlights told?
- **As stories, each with its page** (as built): the situation, what you did, what changed and who saw it.
- **As rows on the home page only:** each highlight keeps its figure, title and sentence; `pages/highlights/` goes, and so do the `situation`, `changed` and quote fields.
- **Pictures first** (for work that is seen: a chef, a carpenter, a site manager, an architect): each record's cover leads its row as a wide picture and its page opens on it, the figure under it; a highlight without a picture keeps the figure first.

When removed: delete `pages/highlights/`, `data/highlights/` and its navigation record; the home page's highlights section goes, the experience records' `highlight` field goes, and the figures on the home page stay.

### Contact (`/contact`)

#### How do people reach you?
- **With the form, and an email to write to** (as built): messages arrive in the Content tab.
- **By email only:** the form, `contact` and its fragment go; the email is set large with "I answer within two working days".
- **By booking a call:** a "Book a 20-minute call" button links to the maker's own scheduling page (a link that leaves the site); the form stays for longer messages.

When removed: delete `pages/contact.liquid`, `data/contact-faq/`, `data/contact/`, `fragments/contact-thanks.liquid` and its navigation record; every "Get in touch" becomes a `mailto:` link to the maker's email, and the terms stay on the home page.

## Résumés, PDFs and what stays outside Upstart

- **No PDF file is made by Upstart.** The visitor's browser makes it, from the print stylesheet ("Save as PDF" in every browser's print dialog). The maker never keeps a separate file in step; the "Updated" date is a line they change in one part.
- **No import** from LinkedIn or a Word document: the maker pastes or tells their CV, and the agent writes the records.
- **Applicant tracking systems:** the printed sheet is real text in one column, in reading order, with plain section names (Experience, Skills, Education), which these systems read well; no promise beyond that.
- **References are not published:** the page says they are given on request.
- **Scheduling** is a link to the maker's own page, never an embedded widget.
- **Privacy:** the city only, no phone, no address and no birth date as built; the guide tells the agent to ask before adding any.
