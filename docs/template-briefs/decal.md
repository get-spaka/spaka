# Decal: brief

One of wave 4's templates: the food truck (`business`). Its motion is short and useful. When the week of stops comes into view, a small yellow truck seen from above pulls into the bay where it is parked today. When the day's stop is over it is back at home base, and the next bay says "Next stop". On the booking page, the guest count works out how long the service will run and what it costs, as the planner types it.

`decal` is a working name. A decal is the vinyl lettering a truck wears, and the road paint the template draws its week with. A web search found no website template or theme of that name on Framer, Webflow, ThemeForest or Squarespace; the nearest were interior design templates called Décorr and Decoral. Framer sells two food-truck templates, Curbside and Truckfare, whose devices this brief stays clear of (see What it leaves out). The fictional business has its own name.

## Concept

**Concept.** A food truck whose website answers the first thing everyone asks: where are you today? The week is drawn as a row of parking bays on a band of asphalt, seen from above. Each bay has its day painted on the road in long white letters, the way road markings are painted, with the stop's place, hours and directions beside it. Days with no stop are yellow cross-hatched "keep clear" boxes. The truck, small, yellow and seen from above, sits in the bay where it is parked today. It goes back to home base at the start of the row when it is not out. The site has a fat, friendly display face, a plain highway-sign sans and its mono, napkin-white pages, a truck yellow, a guava pink and a traffic-cone orange. The menu is the board beside the window, light letters on asphalt. Booking the truck for an event starts from the truck's own data plate (its size, its power, how many it feeds in an hour) and a request form whose guest count works out the service time and the price.

It must not read as Saucer, Corner or Marquee:
- Saucer is a café's front door: it says whether the café is open and what is served now.
- Corner is a shopfront in poster capitals with a ribbon.
- Marquee is a festival poster with stickers.

Decal says where the truck is, in road paint, with lettering like a sign painter's.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Pasada**, a Filipino street food truck in **Durham, North Carolina**. *Pasada* is the word for a jeepney driver's run along his route.

- **The truck:** a 2012 step van, 22 feet long, repainted calamansi yellow and called Nena, after the grandmother whose recipes it serves. It has worked out of a shared commissary kitchen east of downtown since 2021.
- **The people:**
  - **Jo Villanueva** cooks. She grew up in a Navy family in Jacksonville, North Carolina, then cooked on the line in Durham kitchens for eight years.
  - **Theo Marsh** runs the window, the bookings and the truck's upkeep. He was a stage manager for touring shows.
  - **Lola Nena Villanueva**, 81, rolls the lumpia at the commissary on Thursday mornings.
  - **Dre Ruiz** works the grill.
- **The week**, the same every week. Monday is a prep day at home base. Sunday is family day.
  - Tuesday lunch on Parrish Street downtown (11 am to 2 pm), and the evening at Hayloft Beer Garden in East Durham (5 to 9).
  - Wednesday lunch at the Ninth Street lot by East Campus (11 to 2).
  - Thursday lunch at Tradewind Office Park in Research Triangle Park (11 to 2), and the evening at Cotton Mill Commons in Old West Durham (5 to 9).
  - Friday evening at Copperline Bottle Shop (5 to 10).
  - Saturday morning at Durham Central Park, beside the farmers' market (8 am to noon), with the morning menu of silog plates and coffee.
  - The venues are invented, except the public places: Parrish Street, Ninth Street and Durham Central Park. Changes, a rained-out stop or a festival, go up on Instagram by 9 am.
- **The food:**
  - Rice plates and bowls from $12 to $15: longsilog (sweet garlic longganisa, garlic rice, a fried egg, atchara), tocilog, chicken adobo, tofu adobo (vegan), pork sisig with an egg, and pancit bihon.
  - Snacks: pork barbecue skewers glazed in banana ketchup (two for $8), lumpia shanghai (six for $7) and vegetable lumpia.
  - Sides: garlic rice and atchara.
  - Sweets: turon, an ube crinkle cookie, leche flan.
  - Drinks: calamansi lemonade, iced barako coffee, sago't gulaman.
  - A special changes each week; on Fridays, chicken inasal.
- **Booking the truck:**
  - About 70 private events a year: weddings, office lunches, block parties, graduations.
  - Three ways to serve:
    - **the window**: guests order from a short menu, $16 a guest;
    - **merienda**: an afternoon spread of lumpia, turon and calamansi, $12 a guest;
    - **the boodle fight**: a feast laid out on banana leaves down a long table and eaten by hand, $28 a guest, for 40 guests or more.
  - The minimum is $1,400 on weekdays and $1,900 on Saturdays, two hours of service included. Travel is free within 15 miles of downtown Durham, then $3 a mile, up to 60 miles.
  - A request is answered with a quote within two working days. A 50% deposit holds the date.
  - The truck needs a level space of 30 by 12 feet, firm ground (dry grass will do), and either its own quiet generator or a 50-amp outlet. It serves about 150 guests an hour from the window and arrives 45 minutes early.
- **The phone** in the texts is (919) 555-0148, a number reserved for fiction.

The name was searched: no food truck called Pasada came up in Durham, and the Filipino trucks the search found elsewhere have other names (Tatay's Truck, Meryenda, Señor Sisig). No existing template is set in Durham.

The same structure suits:
- a taco, burger, pizza, barbecue, dumpling or coffee truck;
- an ice cream truck or a trailer;
- a cart that moves between markets;
- a pop-up kitchen with a weekly round of breweries;
- a mobile bar.

The agent rewrites the stops, the menu, the truck's facts, the event offers, the people and the town.

**What makes it its own:**
- **the parking row:** the week drawn from above as numbered bays on an asphalt band, with white bay lines and a dashed lane line.
  - Each bay has its day painted on the road in long letters, as road markings are stretched so a driver can read them ("TUE", "THU").
  - Beside the bays, in plain type, each stop's place, neighborhood, hours and "Directions".
  - A day with no stop is a yellow cross-hatched keep-clear box ("Prep day", "Family day"). A stop the maker marks "not this week" is hatched too, with its note ("Not this week: we're at the Bull City rodeo").
  - The row starts with home base, the commissary, where the truck sleeps.
  - It answers "where are you today?" and "where will you be this week?" at once.
  - It is not a map (Truckfare), not a board of rows (Curbside, Saucer's door, Corner's hours board) and not bars on a day (Taper's week);
- **the truck:** a small yellow step van seen from above, drawn in the theme's colors, with "PASADA" painted on its roof.
  - It sits in the bay of today's stop until that stop is over, then at home base. The stop coming next says "Next stop".
  - As the row comes into view, it pulls into its bay from the lane, the way a truck noses into a spot. It does not drive from stop to stop.
  - It knows nothing of open or closed. It says where, and the hours are on the bay;
- **the window:** the hero's photo of the truck's service window, framed as the window itself: a rounded opening cut in a yellow panel, a flat dark awning above it and the counter ledge under it;
- **the board:** the menu as the board beside the window. Dishes in light capitals on asphalt, prices in yellow mono. The week's special is on a guava-pink card pinned to its corner;
- **the data plate:** for the planner, the truck's facts set like the plate inside a vehicle's door. Ruled cells in mono: length, the space it needs, power, guests an hour, setup time, how far it goes, the minimum. It answers "will it fit, and will it feed us?" before anyone writes;
- **the guest count:** in the booking form, the number of guests turns into a line under the field: "About 1 h 20 of service · from $2,880 with the window menu". The numbers come from the plate and the chosen menu.

  - anything sliding out of a sleeve, and a departure board with flipping letters (Encore);

## Type

Bagel Fat One (Kyungwon Kim and JAMO, 2023) is a fat, round, cheerful display face. It looks like the hand-painted letters on a corner store's sign or the side of a truck. It is loud without being condensed: the trend's condensed faces are ruled out with the poster capitals. Overpass is drawn from Highway Gothic, the face of American road signs. Its plain sans keeps long texts calm, its heavy weight stretched makes the road paint, and its mono sets the times and prices. So the whole road system is one family, under one loud face.

## Motion

Short and useful. Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode nothing moves: Upstart's stillness rule stops CSS animations and transitions and no script runs. So the truck sits at home base, no bay is marked, every stop's details are in place to edit, and the guest count shows its plain line. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too.

- **Where the truck is** (`data-parking-row`, `scripts/parking-row.js`, Motion's `animate` and `inView`): the one script that reads the clock. It chooses a bay and nothing else. It never says open, closed or "now".
  - It reads the week from the page. Each bay is rendered from a `stops` record with `data-day` (mon to sun), `data-starts` and `data-ends` (24-hour times, as the records hold them), and `data-off` when the stop is off this week. The truck's time zone comes from `data-tz` on the row ("America/New_York"; without it, the visitor's own clock);
  - it sets `data-state` on each bay:
    - `today`: the day's stop not yet over; the earlier of two if both are left;
    - `next`: the first stop after now, when no stop is left today;
    - `past`: this week's stops already over;
    - nothing on the others.
  - Once the week's last stop has ended, the week starts over: no bay is `past` and the first is `next`, as a new week's row would read;
  - the stylesheet does the rest. Each bay's details hold a line per state, written in the template: "Today, 11 am to 2 pm" and "Next stop", with the day and hours copied from the bay into empty slots. They are shown by `[data-state="today"] [data-when="today"]`. A `past` bay's paint and details fade to half (`opacity: 0.5`), never hidden. With no script, no state, and each bay shows only its day and hours;
  - it writes no sentence. The times inside a line are slots (`<span data-slot="ends">`) into which it copies the time that the page already shows the visitors' way (11 am, 5 pm), through a small part, `parts/time.liquid`;
  - it checks again every minute while the page is open. `stop` clears the timer, removes every `data-state` and puts the truck back at home base.
- **The truck** (`data-truck`, in `scripts/parking-row.js`):
  - one inline SVG in the row: a step van from above, its body `fill-primary` with a 2px `stroke-base-content` outline, the cab's windshield in `neutral`, two roof vents, "PASADA" on the roof (the maker's name, editable), and tail lights in `accent`. It is `aria-hidden`; the plain lines say where it is;
  - in the markup it sits in the home bay at the start of the row, which is how edit mode and a page with no script show it;
  - in visitor mode the script places it in the bay marked `today`, or keeps it at home base. It uses a `transform` worked out from the two bays' boxes, and again on resize, through a `ResizeObserver`;
  - when the row comes into view, if the truck is out, it pulls into its bay from the lane: `y: [64, 0]`, `rotate: [-8, 0]` (a nose-in), 0.9 s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`, its tail lights brightening once at the end (`opacity: [0.4, 1, 0.6]`, 0.4 s). On a phone, where the bays stack, it noses in from the side (`x: [56, 0]`). At home base it does not move. It never drives along the row;
  - under reduced motion it is placed without moving;
  - `stop` takes off the transform and the styles it set.
- **The bays** (CSS only):
  - a grid on `bg-neutral`: white bay lines (2px borders in `neutral-content/80`), a dashed lane line between rows (a `repeating-linear-gradient`), and the day's road paint stretched with `scale-y-[1.8]`. Five to nine bays across on a desktop wrap into two facing rows; on a phone they stack, one bay to a line, with the details beside;
  - keep-clear boxes are a cross-hatching of `primary` on `neutral` (`repeating-linear-gradient` at 45° and −45°), with "Prep day" or the note on a small asphalt plate.
- **The guest count** (`data-headcount`, `scripts/headcount.js`, no library):
  - the form's `guests` field is a number input with a range slider beside it (from 30 to 400), kept in step by the script; with no script, the number input alone;
  - the form carries the plate's figures as attributes: `data-per-hour="150"`, `data-minimum-weekday="1400"`, `data-minimum-saturday="1900"`, `data-hours-included="2"`. Each package option carries its price per guest (`data-per-guest="16"`) and its own minimum guests;
  - under the field, a line written in the template, "About [slot] of service · from $[slot] with [slot]". The script fills its slots from the count, the chosen package and the date's weekday: service time rounded to 10 minutes; the price is guests × price per guest, or the minimum if higher. With no script, or before a count, it shows its plain line: "We serve about 150 guests an hour. Events start at $1,400, two hours included";
  - it writes numbers and the package's own name, never a sentence. Nothing moves; the cone-orange marker beside the line is still. `stop` empties the slots.
- **The window** (`data-window`, CSS only): the hero's photo in a rounded opening of a yellow panel, an asphalt awning above it as a flat band with a 2px ink edge, and a counter ledge below. Nothing in it moves: the awning does not open or swing (Aspect's door swings, Linen's curtain moves).
- **Reveals** (`data-reveal`, `scripts/reveal.js`, Motion's `inView`): a section's main elements rise a little (`y: [16, 0]`, `opacity: [0, 1]`, 0.7 s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`), children 70 ms apart under `data-reveal="stagger"`, as in the site-scripts skill.
- **The header** is sticky on napkin white at 95% with a hairline under it. It does not change as you scroll and carries no status line.

No smooth scrolling, no page transitions, no loader, no WebGL. Library: Motion, pinned on jsDelivr (`motion@14.9.0`), under MIT. Everything else is CSS.

The pictures are taken at the clock its `template.json` sets: `"shoot": { "clock": "2027-04-15T12:15:00-04:00" }`, a Thursday at a quarter past noon. The truck is then in Thursday's lunch bay at Tradewind Office Park, Tuesday's and Wednesday's bays have faded, and the evening stop is next.

## Ordering, bookings and what stays outside

Upstart has no ordering, no payments, no live location, no calendar and no embeds, so:
- **visitors order at the window** (as built). A truck that sells ahead online sends an "Order ahead" link on each stop and in the header to its own ordering page (Square, Toast, Hotplate…). It is a link that leaves the site, never the ordering page's widget (see What varies);
- **events are a request,** answered with a quote by email and held by a deposit paid outside the site. The page says so before and after sending;
- **the truck's whereabouts follow the maker's regular week,** not a tracker. A change for one week is the stop's "not this week" and its note, edited in the Content tab, with Instagram and Monday's email for the rest;
- **no map:** "Directions" links to each stop's address on a map site;
- **no Instagram feed:** a link to the truck's account in the footer;
- **gift cards are sold at the window;**
- **the time zone:** the truck's place follows the truck's clock, not the visitor's, through `data-tz`. The guide tells the agent to set it from the maker's town.
