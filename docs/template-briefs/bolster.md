# Bolster: brief

One of wave 5's templates: the yoga and Pilates studio, a downtown studio that teaches yoga, mat Pilates and reformer classes, thirty-eight a week, sells memberships and class packs, and runs Saturday workshops and one retreat a year (`business`). Its motion is gentle and useful. Class styles and the day's classes unroll like mats as they come into view. The offer for new students sits over the hero's photo, then docks small at the foot of the screen as the visitor reads on, and can be dismissed. The week opens on today.

`bolster` is a working name: the firm cushion of restorative classes, stacked on every studio's prop shelf. The wave sheet named this template Lull, but a search found "Lull" already listed on Framer's marketplace: a free template for therapists, psychologists and coaches, dark and calm, close to this one's world. So the brief takes the nearest free name. A search found no template or theme called Bolster on Framer (its marketplace address answers "Template unavailable"), Webflow, ThemeForest, Squarespace or Shopify. The nearest names were Shopify's Bolt, ThemeForest's Bold and Framer's Boldze, none of them this name. Furl, for the mats, was the other free name. The name is the template's, not the business's.

## Concept

**Concept.** A studio you read like its hall at six in the morning. A deep aubergine field holds the long room's photo, with the offer for new students laid over its edge like a card left on the windowsill. Below it, the day's classes lie on the floor as mats in a row, each unrolling from its end as it comes into view, its roll and the roll's shadow traveling across, until the class lies flat with its time, its teacher and Book. The site is deep, warm and unhurried. It sets a condensed Didone, tall as a standing pose, over a plain sans drawn from a 1940s architect's lettering. Its colors are an aubergine field with a chalk pink and a cork tan, on chalk-white paper. The week opens on today, with the classes already under way dimmed. Every Book goes to the studio's booking app, where the timetable lives. The two ways to pay, by the month or by the pack, stand side by side, so a visitor sees which suits them without a table.

It must not read as Mend, Chalk or Linen. Mend is a physiotherapy practice with a jointed figure, a breathing dot and the first visit's hour drawn as a ring. Chalk is a climbing gym, with a route drawn hold to hold and a seven-column week crossed by a now line. Linen is a guesthouse, with a sheer curtain drawn aside. Bolster is a studio of mats laid on a warm floor. Its answers to a visitor are what is on today, how to start for $49, and which way of paying suits them.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Artesian Yoga & Pilates**, upstairs at 418 Larkin Lane (an invented street), Boise, Idaho 83702. It is downtown, a block and a half from the Basque Block on Grove Street. It is named after the hot water that rises under Boise. The city has heated houses with water from under its foothills since the 1890s, and its geothermal loop, laid in 1983, still runs beneath downtown, where it heats this building.
- **The place:** the second floor of a 1911 brick lodge building. The old lodge room is the **Long Room**, 40 by 64 feet, with its original maple floor, eleven tall windows facing the foothills and 28 mats. The lodge's dining room behind it is the **Reformer Room**, with eight reformers. A small **Corner Room** is kept for private sessions. The building is on the city's loop: from October to April the Long Room's floor is warm underfoot, and on Warm Flow evenings it is held at 85°F ("warm, never hot"). An elevator runs from the street door. There are changing rooms with showers, and mats, blocks, straps, bolsters and blankets for everyone.
- **The people:** the studio was opened in 2021 by **Maite Goikoetxea** (she/her) and **Calvin Oduya** (he/him). Maite grew up in Boise's Basque community, taught yoga in Seattle for twelve years, then came home. Calvin is a former dancer who taught Pilates in Portland for ten years and runs the Reformer Room. Three teachers work with them: **Rosa Nakamura** (she/her) teaches Yin & Restore and the prenatal series, **Inés Arrieta** (she/her) teaches Reformer and Mat Pilates, and **Theo Varga** (he/him) teaches Foundations and the 6:15 am Slow Flow.
- **The classes:** six styles. Foundations (yoga's first months, 60 minutes, gentle). Slow Flow (60 minutes, steady). Warm Flow (60 minutes, the floor at 85°F, steady). Yin & Restore (75 minutes, on bolsters and blankets, gentle). Mat Pilates (50 minutes, strong). Reformer (50 minutes, eight machines, steady, counts as two classes). There are thirty-eight classes a week: six each weekday from 6:15 am to 7:15 pm, four on Saturday morning and four on Sunday. One of Sunday's is "Sunday at four", a Foundations class where students pay what they can.
- **What it sells**, all through its booking app:
  - **New students:** two weeks for $49, every class for fourteen days from the first one, the reformer twice; once per person.
  - **Memberships, by the month:** Every class ($169: yoga and mat Pilates without limit, and four reformer classes), Eight a month ($129) and Four a month ($72). A membership can be paused once a year for up to a month, and stopped with a month's notice.
  - **Class packs:** a drop-in ($24), five classes ($110, used within three months) and ten classes ($200, within six months). A reformer class counts as two.
  - **Private sessions:** yoga $85, reformer $95, a duet on the reformer $120.
  - **Discounts:** students, teachers and over-65s take 20% off memberships.
- **Workshops and the retreat:** a workshop most Saturday afternoons, 18 places, $35 to $45, booked in the app. This season: Arm balances, slowly; Your first reformer ($30, 90 minutes, 8 places); Pilates for runners; Yin by lamplight on the winter solstice. Once a year there is a retreat, "Three days near Stanley": September 17 to 19, 2027, in a lodge in the Sawtooths, 16 places. It costs $1,180 in a shared cabin or $1,480 in a cabin of your own, with the meals, two classes a day and an afternoon at the hot springs. A place is asked for with a form and held with a $300 deposit, which the studio invoices.

The name was searched: no studio called Artesian Yoga & Pilates came up, in Boise or elsewhere, nor any Boise studio called Artesian, and no Larkin Lane in Boise. No template or brief in `docs/template-briefs/` is set in Boise or Idaho. The phone in the texts is (208) 555-0163, a number reserved for fiction. The email is hello@artesianyoga.example and the booking app is at book.artesianyoga.example. Both are `.example` domains, as Ember's and Encore's are, so no real address is named.

The same structure suits a yoga studio, a Pilates or reformer studio, a barre studio, a hot yoga studio, a dance or movement studio with a timetable, a meditation center that teaches classes, or a community studio run by its teachers. The agent rewrites the class styles and their tones, the week, the prices and the offer, the teachers, the workshops and the city.

Two heroes show the overlap move this template opens on: alpinecore.ch lays a rotating photo card over the foot of its mountain panorama, and soarlinepilates.jp sets its offer card at the bottom right of a tight full-bleed photo.

The gap is the same everywhere. The timetable is either a booking widget framed into the page or a link to the tool's own page. Sky Ting alone shows the week on its home page, and there it is a plain table. None of these makes the studio's day look like its room: a floor with the mats laid out. The offer for new students is either a pop-up to close or a tile to find again. Bolster turns trend 4 into the mats, and trend 2 into a card that docks at the foot of the screen, dismissible, never a pop-up.

Common and left outside: booking widgets framed into the page (Acuity, Bsport, Mindbody), video heroes (livingroombrighton.com, urbnpilates.com), marquees (most of them), disco balls and animated grain (livingroombrighton.com), a menu that is the hero with a rotating seal (yogamaya.com), shops of leggings and mats, newsletter pop-ups, press logos.

**What makes it its own:**
- **the mats:** each class style is a mat, a long rounded band in its own tone (aubergine, night, chalk pink, cork, heather or pale chalk) with a fine grip texture and the edge of its thickness. The style's name is in the Didone, with its length, its pace and its room. The day's classes are slimmer mats, one per class: time · length · class · teacher · Book. As they come into view, each mat unrolls from its left end, a roll traveling left to right with its shadow on the floor ahead of it. The roll grows thinner as the mat leaves it, until the mat lies flat. On the classes page they lie at slightly uneven offsets, as mats do on a studio floor, never tilted. It is the trend's timetable, made into the studio's floor;
- **the offer that docks:** "Two weeks for $49" is a chalk-pink card laid over the hero photo's lower edge. Once that card leaves the screen, a small copy of the offer docks at the foot of the screen: the price, "Start" and a cross. It stays there until the visitor dismisses it or reaches the page's end, where it settles into its own place above the footer. One card, never stacked, never over the first screen, and gone for the rest of the visit once dismissed;
- **the week at a glance:** a day strip, Mon to Sun, opens on today in Boise's time, with the classes already under way that day dimmed. Every Book links out to the studio's booking app, and the page says plainly that this is the usual week, while subs and changes are in the app. The full week on its own page adds a filter (All · Yoga · Pilates · Reformer), in CSS;
- **two ways to pay, side by side:** on the aubergine field, the memberships on the left, the class packs on the right, each in its own short column of prices, with one line between them that settles it: "Coming twice a week or more? A membership costs less." The reformer's "counts as two" is a cork mark on every pack;
- **the warm floor:** the studio's one story, on the home page and the studio page. The building is on the city's geothermal loop, which is why the floor is warm, and why Warm Flow is warm rather than hot.

**What it leaves out:**

## Type

Both fonts are files of the template, as `woff2` subset to Latin and Latin Extended (for names such as Goikoetxea and Inés), under the SIL Open Font License, from Google Fonts, declared in `styles/site.css`. Both were checked in Google Fonts' metadata:
- **Imbue** (Tyler Finck, ETC; `ofl/imbue`) ships as one variable file with a weight axis (100 to 900) and an optical size axis (10 to 100), with no italic, so the headings have none;
- **Afacad** (Kristian Möller, Dicotype; `ofl/afacad`) ships as two variable files, roman and italic, with weight from 400 to 700. Both fonts cover Latin Extended.

| Role | Font | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Headings | Imbue | `font-optical-sizing: auto`, so the large sizes take its finest hairlines. The hero at `clamp(3.25rem, 6vw + 1.5rem, 7.5rem)` in 300, `leading-[0.95]`, `tracking-[-0.01em]`, `text-balance`. Section headings at `clamp(2.25rem, 3vw + 1.25rem, 4rem)` in 350; small headings (a teacher, a workshop, a day of the week) at 1.75 rem in 400. Sentence case, never Imbue under 1.5 rem on the aubergine field, where its hairlines would break up |
| Class names, prices and the wordmark | Imbue | a style's name on its mat at `clamp(2.25rem, 2.5vw + 1.25rem, 3.5rem)` in 400; a class's name on a session row at 1.5 rem in 450; "$49" on the offer card at 5 rem in 300, the plans' prices at 3 rem in 350; the wordmark "ARTESIAN" in capitals at 600, `tracking-[0.06em]` |
| Body | Afacad | 400, 1.125 rem (it runs a little narrow), `leading-relaxed`, `text-base-content/80`, measure `max-w-[62ch]`; buttons 600 at 1 rem, sentence case; the teachers' own lines in its italic |
| Times, facts and labels | Afacad | times on the rows at 1 rem in 600 ("6:15 am"), in a right-aligned column of `min-w-[5.5rem]`, so the rows line up whether or not the font's figures are tabular (`tabular-nums` is set, and the builder checks the font has them); facts at 0.9375 rem in 500 ("60 min · Gentle · Long Room"); labels above headings at 0.875 rem in 600, sentence case, without letter-spacing |

Imbue is a condensed Didone: tall, crisp and curved at once, standing in a narrow column like a body in mountain pose. It makes the trend's thin, high-contrast serif without Framer's default, and its narrow set lets "Two weeks for $49" sit large on a card a phone wide. Afacad began as the lettering of a 1940s Swedish architect's housing estate, drawn for its doors' numbers. It is plain, open and slightly narrow, sober next to the Didone. Its figures were drawn for numbers first, which suits a timetable.

Ruled out:
- every heading font a template already ships (Kalnia, Bodoni Moda, Instrument Serif, Fraunces, Young Serif, Gloock, Noto Serif Display, Libre Caslon Condensed, Baskervville, Marcellus, Hedvig Letters Serif, Petrona, Literata, Newsreader, Source Serif 4 and the rest of the inventory's list);
- the wave's own (League Gothic, Spectral, Geologica, Brygada 1918, Besley, Shrikhand, Ultra, Gelasio, Castoro);
- the trend sites' faces: Minion, Italiana, Zen Old Mincho and Instrument Serif, which would copy a site or Framer's default;
- the wellness defaults, Cormorant and Playfair Display;
- any script font for a line under the heading.

None of the 28 templates, nor any row of the wave sheet but this one, uses Imbue or Afacad. Pail's brief listed them only among its alternatives.

**If a sibling takes them,** two pairings keep the same voice (a tall, delicate high-contrast serif over a plain sans with honest figures):
- **Playfair** (the 2023 variable family, not Playfair Display) at its narrowest width (87.5), its light weights and its largest optical sizes, with **Wix Madefor Text** for the body;
- **Gilda Display** (one weight, a light Didone, kept to the large sizes), with **Manrope** for the body.

All four are on Google Fonts under the OFL, with Latin Extended, and used by no template, brief or row of the wave sheet. Antic Didone was weighed and set aside: it has no Latin Extended.

## Palette

Deep, warm and unhurried: an aubergine field that carries the header, the hero, the price band and every page's title, under chalk-white paper with a breath of the same hue, a chalk pink for what is offered, and a cork tan for small marks.

Contrast was measured from these values: each oklch value converted to sRGB, then the WCAG formula. Every pair of text and background passes AA:
- **ink:** on chalk white 15.0:1, on pale chalk 13.3:1, on chalk pink 11.0:1, on cork 7.9:1, on heather 6.2:1;
- **aubergine on light grounds** (links, buttons, plum mats' outlines): on chalk white 11.7:1, on pale chalk 10.5:1, on chalk pink 8.6:1;
- **on the field:** chalk white on aubergine 11.7:1, chalk pink on aubergine 8.6:1, cork on aubergine 6.2:1;
- **on night:** light text on night 15.5:1, chalk pink on night 11.9:1, cork on night 8.6:1;
- **muted text:** `text-base-content/70` 5.8:1 on chalk white and 5.5:1 on pale chalk; `text-primary-content/70` on aubergine 6.5:1.

Cork is never text on a light ground (1.9:1 on chalk white): there it is a fill only, and on the field and on night it may be text. Heather carries ink only (light text on it is 2.4:1). Focus rings are aubergine on light grounds and chalk pink on the field.

**The mats' tones** are not theme variables of their own. A record's `tone` (plum, night, chalk, cork, heather, pale) becomes `data-tone` on the mat, and `styles/site.css` maps each to `--tone` and `--tone-ink` from the theme:
- plum is the primary, under primary-content;
- night is the neutral, under neutral-content;
- chalk is the secondary, cork the accent, and pale the surface alt, each under ink;
- heather is made, `color-mix(in oklch, var(--color-secondary), var(--color-primary) 30%)`, about oklch(0.71 0.06 350), under ink.

The grip texture is a dot grid of `color-mix(in oklch, currentColor 7%, transparent)`, and a mat's thickness a 2 px inner shadow at its foot of the tone mixed with the neutral. The roll's shading comes from the tone mixed with the neutral and the surface. So the Theme tab restyles every mat with the rest. The design notes will say which tones carry light text.

Other settings:
- buttons and fields are pills (`--theme-radius-field: 9999px`): aubergine filled for the main one on light grounds, chalk pink filled on the field, outlined for the other;
- relief off (`--theme-depth: 0`): the dock alone has a soft shadow, drawn in `styles/site.css` from the neutral at 30%;
- density spacious (`--theme-size: 0.28125rem`) and fine borders (1px);
- links in text are aubergine with a 1 px underline offset by 3 px, chalk pink on the field;
- the status colors are derived, as always, and the forms' errors use them.

It is not Coteau's grape must (h 330, lighter and brighter, a button color on limestone), Marquee's hot pink on tangerine with its blush paper, Decal's guava pink, nor Pail's blush beside fern and zinc. It is a deep plum-aubergine used as a field, AlpineCore's trend without its burgundy and bronze, with a matte chalk pink and a cork tan taken from the studio's props.

## Motion

**Gentle**, by the studio's character: people come to slow down, and the site moves the way a teacher lays out the room before class, once and calmly, then stays still. Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode nothing moves: Upstart's stillness rule stops CSS animations and transitions and no script runs. So every mat lies flat, Monday is the day shown, nothing is dimmed, and the dock is a plain band in its place above the footer. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too.

- **The mats** (`data-mats` on a list, `data-mat` on each mat inside a `relative` list item; `scripts/mats.js`, with Motion's `inView` and `animate`):
  - **at rest,** in CSS, a mat is its tone with the grip texture and the inner edge, `rounded-box`. A class style's mat is `aspect-[3/1]` from 768 px and as tall as its content on a phone; a session row's mat is 3.5 rem tall. On the classes page, `[data-mats="floor"]` sets the mats at offsets of 0, 6%, 2% and 8% of the width from 1024 px, like mats laid by hand. On a phone they are all full width;
  - **unrolling:** for each mat, the script sets its hidden state itself, `clip-path: inset(0 100% 0 0 round var(--radius-box))`. Beside the mat, inside its list item, it adds a roll (`<span class="mat-roll" aria-hidden="true">`): a band as tall as the mat, shaded as a cylinder from the mat's tone, with its shadow cast on the floor ahead of it. When the mat is 40% in view, one animation drives both from 0 to 1 through `onUpdate`:
    - the clip opens from the left;
    - the roll travels with the clip's edge;
    - its width shrinks from 2.5 rem to 0.375 rem as the square root of what is left on it, as a real roll's does;
    - in the last tenth the roll fades and its shadow with it.
  - **timing:** a style's mat takes 0.9 s, `ease: [0.3, 0.7, 0.2, 1]`, a push and a long settle. Mats entering together start 90 ms apart, from the top. Session rows take 0.6 s, 50 ms apart, eight at most in a stagger, the rest together. The script removes the roll and the clip at the end of each unroll;
  - **picking another day** in the strip unrolls that day's rows again, quicker: 0.45 s, 35 ms apart;
  - **on a pointer that hovers,** a class style's mat rises 2 px and its arrow moves 4 px, a 200 ms transition, under no-preference and `(hover: hover)`;
  - **reduced motion, no script, edit mode:** the mats lie flat, nothing is clipped and no roll is added; days change at once. `stop` cancels the animations, removes the rolls and clears every style it set.
- **The week** (`data-week` on the section, with `data-tz="America/Boise"`; CSS for the days and filters, `scripts/week.js` for today, no library):
  - **in CSS:**
    - the day strip is a row of radio inputs (`name="day"`, `mon` to `sun`) styled as tabs; `:has()` shows the checked day's list (`[data-day="tue"]`) and hides the others, so it works with no script;
    - in the template, Monday is checked on the home page. On `/schedule`, "All week" comes first and is checked, and every day shows, under its name in Imbue;
    - the filter on `/schedule` is a second set of radios (`name="kind"`: all, yoga, pilates, reformer) hiding the rows whose `data-kind` does not match. A day left with nothing shows its own line, "No Pilates classes this day", by CSS;
  - **the script:**
    - it reads today's weekday and time in the studio's time zone (`data-tz`; without it, the visitor's own clock);
    - on the home page it checks today's radio; on both pages it sets `aria-current="date"` on today's tab and day heading, which CSS marks with a small cork dot;
    - it sets `data-past` on today's rows whose class has begun, which CSS dims to 55% opacity (not a movement, so under reduced motion too);
    - it writes no text, checks again every minute while the page is open, and `stop` clears the timer, restores the radio the template had checked, and removes `aria-current` and `data-past`.
- **The dock** (`data-dock` on `parts/dock.liquid`, `data-offer` on the offer card in the hero, on the prices page and on the new students' page; `scripts/dock.js`, Motion's `inView` and `animate`):
  - **with no script** it is a slim chalk-pink band in its place at the end of `<main>`, above the footer: "New here? Two weeks for $49", "Start your two weeks" and a cross. The cross is a `<label>` around a visually hidden checkbox ("Hide this offer"), and `has-[:checked]:hidden` hides the band without JavaScript, as `banner-dismissible-soft` does;
  - **the script** sets `data-dock="on"`, which turns the band into a small card, `position: sticky; bottom: 1rem`, at the right on a desktop and across the width on a phone. It sets the hidden state itself (`opacity: 0`, `y: 16`, `inert`) while any `[data-offer]` is on screen. The card rises in, `y: [16, 0]`, `opacity: [0, 1]`, 0.4 s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`, once the offer has left the screen above (on a page without one, after the first screen), and sinks out when an offer comes back. At the end of `<main>` it settles into its own place, being sticky;
  - **dismissed**, it is remembered in `sessionStorage` for the visit, so it does not come back on the next page. `stop` removes the attribute, the listener, the styles and the observer;
  - `/new`, where the offer is the page, renders no dock;
  - the page's foot keeps `scroll-padding-bottom: 5rem` while the dock is on, so it never covers a Book button that the keyboard reaches.
- **Rising into view** (`data-rise`, CSS only, as in the site-scripts skill): headings, photos and the price columns rise 24 px and fade in on a `view()` timeline (`animation-range: entry 0% cover 30%`), under no-preference. The mats do not take it: they unroll.
- **The header** is sticky on the aubergine field, solid, with a hairline of chalk pink at 20% under it. It does not change as you scroll.

No smooth scrolling, no page transitions, no loader, no WebGL, no split text. Library: Motion, pinned on jsDelivr (`motion@14.9.0`), under MIT; everything else is CSS. The week follows the clock, so `template.json` sets `"shoot": { "clock": "2027-03-09T07:40:00-07:00" }`: a Tuesday morning in March, Boise on Mountain Standard Time. The strip opens on Tuesday, with the 6:15 Slow Flow and the 7:30 Reformer dimmed and Foundations at 9:30 next. Generated images (below) also make the template need `"app": "0.2.0"`.

## Booking and what stays outside

Upstart has no checkout, no payments, no calendar and no embeds, so:
- **the timetable is a glance** (as built): the usual week, typed in `sessions`. The app holds the real one, with subs, cancellations and how many places are left, and the page says so in one line. Every Book links out to the app, never frames its widget;
- **the booking app's address is asked for, never invented:** the template's `book.artesianyoga.example` stays only until the maker gives theirs. A session's `book` can link to that class in the app, or stay empty and use the address in `navigation`;
- **everything is bought in the app:** the offer, the memberships, the packs and the workshops. The site states prices and terms and never asks for a card;
- **the retreat is a request:** the studio answers by email and invoices the deposit;
- **the dock is the one floating thing,** dismissible without JavaScript, remembered for the visit, never over the first screen, and absent from `/new`;
- **the time zone:** the week follows the studio's clock through `data-tz`; the guide tells the agent to set it from the maker's city, and to show 24-hour times (07:30) for a site in French, German or most of Europe;
- **no Instagram feed:** a link in the footer.

- **Boise:** the Basque Block on Grove Street, downtown seen from Table Rock or the foothills, the Boise River Greenbelt;
- **the retreat and workshops:** the Sawtooth Mountains above Stanley, a hot spring on the Payette River, a cabin among pines; a quiet room at dusk for Yin by lamplight.

To read as one set, they are chosen in early daylight, cool windows over warm floors, true colors, with no golden-hour poses on beaches or summits, no matching sets, no filters and no watermarks. The aubergine field, the mats and the chalk pink pull them together.
