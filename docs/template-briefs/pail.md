# Pail: brief

One of wave 4's templates: the florist, a flower shop and studio that delivers in its city, does weddings and events, runs standing orders and teaches a few evenings a year (`business`). Its motion is quiet and useful: the week's stems lowered into their zinc pails one after another, a stem that lifts out of its pail when you pick it for your bunch, and a line at the top of every page that says whether flowers ordered now go out today.

`pail` is a working name: the galvanized pail every stem stands in, from the market to the cooler to the sidewalk in front of the shop. A web search found no website template or theme called Pail on Framer, Webflow, ThemeForest or Squarespace (Framer sells florist templates called Posy, The Blossom, Florale, Virelle Floral and Stem Florist, none of them this name or this idea). The name is the template's, not the business's.

## Concept

**Concept.** A flower shop you read like its sidewalk on a market morning: this week's stems standing in a row of zinc pails, tall and short, each with its enamel plate saying what a stem costs, and a visitor who picks the ones they like the way they would point at the buckets in the shop, then sends that bunch as an order. The site is light and fresh, like tissue paper and cold water: a Victorian high-contrast display serif, from the age of the language of flowers, over a plain grotesk, a soft mono for the plates and the ZIP codes, a white with a breath of pink, a fern green, a blush and a ranunculus coral, and galvanized zinc for the pails and the footer. The top of every page says "Order by 1 pm for delivery today", or "Order now for delivery tomorrow, Wednesday" once the van has gone. The delivery page answers "Do you deliver to 15217?" from the visitor's ZIP code, and the season page tells a couple planning a September wedding what will be in the pails then.

It must not read as Corner, Saucer or Kindly. Corner is a shopfront of poster capitals with a ribbon and a stamp; Saucer a café door with a turning card and round plates; Kindly a wedding invitation suite of envelopes, seals and die-cut arches. Pail is a shop of loose stems, priced by the stem and by the armful, whose answer to a visitor is what is in this week and whether it can still go out today.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Incline Flowers**, a flower shop and studio at 37 Mercer Steps (an invented street), Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania 15203, on the South Side Flats, a block off East Carson Street, at the foot of the city steps that climb to the South Side Slopes. It is named after the city's inclines, the cable railways up Mount Washington across the river.
- **The place:** a 1905 corner butcher's shop with white tile walls, a marble counter and the old meat locker, now the flower cooler. The pails stand on the sidewalk from April to October; in winter they stand inside the window. The back room, "the locker room", seats twelve for workshops.
- **The people:** opened in 2020 by **Roz Kowalczyk** (she/her), who grew up on the Slopes among her grandmother's dahlias and spent eight years with an event studio in Philadelphia, and **Teo Marchetti** (he/him), a former line cook who does the market runs at 5 am and drives the van. **Nia Boateng** (she/her) leads weddings and events; **Wes Hadley** (he/him) opens the shop at weekends and teaches the wreath nights.
- **The flowers:** from May to October about seven in ten stems are grown within 100 miles: by Hollow Run Farm in Butler County (38 miles: dahlias, zinnias, cosmos, snapdragons), the Two Rivers Flower Collective, six growers around Washington County (peonies, sweet peas, garden roses), and a tulip and branch grower near Lancaster. In winter the shop buys from wholesalers and says where each stem came from, on its plate.
- **The shop's week:** closed Monday, the market and cooler day. Open Tuesday to Friday 10 am to 6 pm, Saturday 9 am to 5 pm, Sunday 10 am to 2 pm. Deliveries Tuesday to Saturday; orders by 1 pm (11 am on Saturday) go out the same day; Sunday is pickup only.
- **What it sells:** the florist's choice from this week's pails in three sizes, a Handful ($45), an Armful ($75) and a Bucketful ($120), wrapped in paper or in a vase for $18 more, with any stems the customer asks for; single stems by the stem ($3 to $9) at the counter; delivery $12, $18 or $28 by zone, free over $150 in the first zone.
- **Standing orders:** a Handful every week ($45 a delivery), every other week or every month, delivered on Thursdays; "The front desk" for offices and restaurants, a large arrangement each week with the vase swapped ($95). Paused or stopped by text; billed by the shop each month, no contract.
- **Weddings and events:** full service from $2,800 (design, delivery, setup and breakdown), and "Market pails": six to twelve pails of loose stems and greens, chosen with the couple and picked up on the Friday, from $450, for couples and friends who arrange their own. The 2027 season is booking; a consultation is asked for with a form.
- **Workshops:** once a month in the locker room, twelve seats, $95 with the stems, the tools and a cup of coffee: branches and bulbs in March, peony night in June, dahlia night in September, wreath nights in December. A seat is asked for with a form and paid at the door.

The name was searched: no florist called Incline Flowers came up, in Pittsburgh or elsewhere. No existing template, nor any brief in `docs/template-briefs/`, is set in Pittsburgh; the phone in the texts is (412) 555-0147, a number reserved for fiction.

The same structure suits a neighborhood florist, a flower studio that delivers, a flower farm's stand or shop, a plant and flower shop, a wedding and event florist with a retail counter, a dried flower studio, or a market flower stall with a website. The agent rewrites the stems and their months, the sizes and their prices, the zones and their ZIP codes, the growers, the people and the city.

## Trends it draws on

These come from twenty-two florist and flower studio sites opened in November 2026. Awwwards' florist search found mostly older entries (Oh les fleurs, Lille, Honorable Mention of September 2023; Flower Dose, Melbourne, Nominee of 2022; Flúr, Toronto), and the 2025-2026 roundups of florist websites (Wix, Colorlib, HubSpot, Site Builder Report, Webcitz) pointed to the shops themselves; each site was read as it stands now, its text with WebFetch, its fonts and colors from its own HTML and stylesheets with curl. Four that answered were dropped (mcqueens.co.uk now hosts something else, saipua.com sells soap, marylennox.com is a placeholder, nolasgarden.com shows no text). Each trend below was seen on at least two of the sites.

1. **The day's cutoff said first, at the very top.** wildatheart.com (its top bar: "Order by 4pm for same day London delivery and next day nationwide"); graceandthorn.com (the hero is "Same Day Flower Delivery in London", "Order by 1pm, Monday to Saturday", then a trio of Cut-off, Coverage and Method); tonicblooms.com (four delivery windows, each with its own order-before time, from 8:30 am for the morning to 3:30 pm for the evening); foxfodderflowers.com ("Same Day Delivery: Last minute can still be thoughtful"); flowerdose.com.au (same-day delivery across Melbourne in its title and its intro); lewismillerdesign.com ("We try and accommodate same-day delivery when we can", and one day's notice asked); flur.ca (its hours, "OPEN Wed-Fri (10-4) & Sat (11-5)", in the announcement bar).
2. **Delivery by area, a fee per zone and a check for your address.** graceandthorn.com (Central London postcodes listed in its questions, £10 to £20 by courier); tonicblooms.com (an address checker, same-day zones and two-hour delivery downtown only, $9.99 on weekdays and $12.99 on Saturdays, a long list of cities served); odealarose.com ("Enter your delivery zip code to view available products!" and a delivery date before any bouquet); flur.ca ("Toronto, GTA and local-Ontario delivery or in store pick up"); urbanstems.com (Same Day, Next Day and Last-Minute Express as its delivery menu).
3. **The florist's choice by size and price, rather than a catalogue.** popupflorist.com ("Designer's choice, delivered", a fresh arrangement "From $98.00"); foxfodderflowers.com (Signature, Large and Bouquet at $305, $440 and $265); lewismillerdesign.com ("Arrangements start at $150", ordered by phone or email); tonicblooms.com (bouquets told by their stem counts, 12, 24, "22+ stems"); graceandthorn.com ("From" prices and one "Pick of the Week").
4. **What is in now: the season named and the week's pick.** wildatheart.com ("In-Season", "Single Stems" and the "Autumn Collection" in its menu); flur.ca ("FALL HITS ON REPEAT", "Gift or keep our seasonal flowers"); foxfodderflowers.com ("What We're Loving: the flowers we're loving, in bunches"); graceandthorn.com ("Pick of the Week"); tonicblooms.com ("Chilean peonies, right this way", centerpieces marked "Late fall"); jamjarflowers.co.uk (a "seasonally led" studio); floretflowers.com ("from fragrant sweet peas in the spring to magnificent dinnerplate dahlias in the fall", and a Year-at-a-Glance); fleurfarm.com ("Ranunculus Shipping Now").
5. **One shop, several doors: delivery, weddings, events, workshops and standing orders given equal room.** foxfodderflowers.com (seven cards: arrangements, events, what we're loving, same-day delivery, classes, journal); wildatheart.com (its hero slides: order flowers, plan your wedding, plan your event); graceandthorn.com (tiles for Ready to Wear wedding flowers, Workshops and Events); rebelrebel.co.uk (Shop online, Weddings, Events, Workshops and courses, Floriculture); jamjarflowers.co.uk (Weddings, Events, Workshops, Contracts, Deliveries); lawlessbotanicals.com (workshops, a weekly flower subscription and event florals side by side); flowerdose.com.au (Workshops, Weddings, Corporate).
6. **Warm off-white with one deep botanical green or one flower's color, under a classic or high-contrast serif and a plain grotesk, and little movement.** flur.ca (deep green #0a4136 with blush #e9ddd5 and #f0e6df; Freight Display and Text with Founders Grotesk); ohlesfleurs.com (deep green #073836, coral #f17970 and blush #f1d6d0 on #f8f4ef; Gulax with Avenir); studiomondine.com (olive black #26290a on #fdfcf8; Fautive with Rational); foxfodderflowers.com (#140e0b on #f6f3e7; Adobe Caslon with Inter); graceandthorn.com (sage #b0bdb4 on #faf6f2, Montserrat); flowermoxie.com (pink #e78596 and green #108474 on #fde9da); fleurfarm.com (oxblood #572323 and sage #7b8c6a); odealarose.com (Ambroise, a Didone, with Montserrat); bloomandwild.com (Tiempos Headline with Unica 77); wildatheart.com (Frenchute High, a tall display face, with Univers Next); lewismillerdesign.com (EB Garamond and Bodoni Moda with a bright yellow #ece70f); floretflowers.com (EB Garamond). Their movement is carousels and tiles (wildatheart.com, foxfodderflowers.com, graceandthorn.com), nothing more.

The gap is the same everywhere: the season is a menu link or a card, and the florist's choice a photo with "From $98". None of these shows what is in the shop this week the way the shop does, stem by stem in its bucket with its price, nor lets a visitor point at the stems they want before the florist chooses. Pail turns trends 3 and 4 into its pails, and trends 1 and 2 into two plain answers: today or tomorrow, and yes or no for your ZIP code.

Common and left outside: an online checkout with a delivery-date calendar, add-ons sold at checkout (chocolates, candles, teddy bears), review widgets and stars (flowermoxie.com, tonicblooms.com's "98% 5-Star reviews"), Instagram feeds (graceandthorn.com, flur.ca), a newsletter discount in a pop-up (graceandthorn.com, wildatheart.com, flur.ca, foxfodderflowers.com) and press logos (graceandthorn.com, wildatheart.com).

**What makes it its own:**
- **the pails:** the week's stems, each a tall photo standing in a galvanized pail drawn in CSS from the theme's colors (a tapered body with two pressed ribs, a rolled rim, a wire handle at rest), on a zinc shelf. The stems are of three heights, as the flowers are (tall branches and delphiniums, garden-height dahlias, short ranunculus), so the row has the uneven skyline of a florist's sidewalk. On each pail a small riveted enamel plate gives the price of a stem ("$4 a stem"); under it, the name, where it was grown and how long it lasts ("Hollow Run Farm · 38 miles · a week in the vase"). The trend's "what we're loving" and "pick of the week", made into the shop's real stock, set by the maker each week with one checkbox per stem in the Content tab;
- **picking a bunch:** every pail is a checkbox. Picked, its stem lifts a hand's width out of its pail and leans a little toward the others picked, a coral ring at its plate, and the counter under the shelf says "4 stems picked". On the Flowers page the pails are the first field of the order form; elsewhere they lead there with the picked stems already ticked. The florist still chooses the stems and how many, within the size the customer pays for: the bunch is a wish, said plainly ("We'll build it around what you picked");
- **the order-by line:** at the top of every page, the shop's own words for the day: "Order by 1 pm for delivery today", "Order by 11 am for delivery today" on a Saturday, "Order now for delivery tomorrow, Wednesday" after the cutoff, "We deliver again on Tuesday; pick up today until 2" on a Sunday. It is a plain line of type in the header's strip, read from the hours, never a sign, a card or a status dot;
- **the ZIP check:** on the delivery page, one field, "Your ZIP code", and the answer typed under it from the zones table: "Yes, 15217 is in Zone 2: $18, same day when you order by 1 pm" or "We don't drive to 15090. Pick up at the shop, or call us: we'll send you to a florist near them." The table of zones, with every ZIP code it covers in mono, stays below, so the page works without the field;
- **the year in stems:** the season page lists the twelve months, each with the stems that are in then as small swatches with their names, the current month marked "Now"; a couple planning a date reads it before a consultation, and each wedding's page links its stems to it.

**What it leaves out:**
- online checkout, payments, a delivery-date calendar widget, add-ons sold at checkout, a cart, a map embed, an Instagram feed, review stars, a discount pop-up, video and sound;
- the florist's clichés: falling petals, a cursor that trails petals, watercolor florals and botanical line drawings as decoration, script fonts, a pink-and-gold "luxury" look, a bouquet in a hero video;
- **other templates' devices:** a ribbon or ticker, a turning stamp and arch-framed photos (Corner); a card that turns to open or closed, round plates set down, the hours typed on a door's glass and a status dot (Saucer); a pole or sign that says open now, hours as bars on a week chart and a hand mirror (Taper); envelopes, wax seals, die-cut arches and scallops, a fan of cards (Kindly); hang tags, manila, pattern paper, pinking shears and photos opening from pleats (Seam); a curtain drawing aside and keys swinging on a board (Linen); lines that draw themselves (Chalk, Seam); anything scrolling sideways (Reel), including the pails on a phone, which wrap two to a row; a light following the pointer (Ember) or pictures following the cursor (Folio); a jar that fills (Commons); rings of driving time and dimension lines (Trestle); a dial (Transfer); the sun, shadows or the time of day drawn as a picture (Gnomon); a huge wordmark across the footer (Corner, Ember, Seam); an italic accent word in a heading (Corner, Folio, Linen); tilted cards (Ember);
- smooth scrolling and page transitions: the browser's own scroll, for someone ordering flowers for a hospital room on a phone in a hurry.

## Type

All three are files of the template, as `woff2` subset to Latin and Latin Extended (for names such as Kowalczyk and Boateng), under the SIL Open Font License, from Google Fonts, declared in `styles/site.css`. Kalnia ships as one variable file (weight 100 to 700, width 100 to 125) with no italic, so the headings have none; Commissioner as a variable file (weight 100 to 900, with its slant axis for the few slanted words, and its flare axis left at 0); Sono as a variable file (weight 200 to 800, with its mono axis at 1, monospaced).

| Role | Font | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Headings | Kalnia | 300 at width 100 for the large sizes, where its hairlines are as fine as a stem; the hero at `clamp(3rem, 6.5vw + 1rem, 6.75rem)`, `leading-[0.98]`, `tracking-[-0.015em]`, `text-balance`; section headings at `clamp(2rem, 3vw + 1rem, 3.5rem)` in 300; small headings (a size's name, a workshop, a wedding) at 1.5 rem in 400; sentence case |
| The name and figures | Kalnia | the wordmark "Incline" at 600 and width 112, its Victorian fat face; the sizes' prices ("$75") at 2.5 to 3.5 rem in 500; the month names on the season page at 1.75 rem in 400 |
| Body | Commissioner | 400, 1.0625 rem, `leading-relaxed`, `text-base-content/80`, measure `max-w-[62ch]`; buttons 600 at 1 rem, sentence case |
| Plates, labels, ZIP codes and times | Sono | the price on a pail's plate at 0.875 rem in 600 ("$4 a stem"), a stem's line at 0.8125 rem in 400; labels above headings, the order-by line and the counter under the shelf at 0.8125 rem in 500, sentence case with `tracking-[0.01em]`; ZIP codes, fees and times in the zones table with `tabular-nums` |

Kalnia is a high-contrast display serif drawn from the Victorian fat faces, at light weights delicate and at heavy ones bold: the type of the years when flowers were sent as messages, and of the old florists' and seed merchants' signs, without the script flourishes the trade usually reaches for. Commissioner is a calm, open grotesk that does what Founders Grotesk, Univers and Unica do on the shops above. Sono is a soft, rounded monospace, plain enough for a list of ZIP codes and friendly enough to be stamped on an enamel plate.

Ruled out: every heading font already used by a template (Caprasimo, Gloock, Fraunces, Young Serif, Bodoni Moda, Instrument Serif, Newsreader, Noto Serif Display, Libre Caslon Condensed, Baskervville, Petrona, Literata, Source Serif 4, Darker Grotesque, Grenze Gotisch, Anybody, Unbounded, Big Shoulders, Archivo, Sora, Fredoka, Parkinsans, Gabarito, Bricolage Grotesque), and the classic Garamonds and Didones the florists use most (EB Garamond, Cormorant, Playfair Display), which a winery or any other sibling would also pick. None of the twenty-four templates, nor any brief, uses Kalnia, Commissioner or Sono.

**If a sibling takes them,** two pairings keep the same voice (a delicate display serif, a plain grotesk, a mono for the plates):
- **Imbue** (a condensed Didone, at 300 to 500, its tall letters standing like stems) with **Afacad** (body) and **Xanh Mono** (plates);
- **Playfair** (the 2023 variable family with optical size and width, not Playfair Display, at its light weights and largest optical size) with **Radio Canada Big** (body) and **Libertinus Mono** (plates).

All six are on Google Fonts under the OFL and used by no template or brief.

## Palette

Tissue white, galvanized zinc, a fern green, a blush and a ranunculus coral. Every pair of text and background passes WCAG AA, measured from these values: stem ink on tissue 14.3:1, on zinc pale 12.5:1, on blush 10.7:1, on coral 5.0:1; fern on tissue 6.3:1, on zinc pale 5.6:1, on blush 4.7:1; tissue on fern 6.5:1; light text on zinc dark 11.2:1, blush on zinc dark 8.8:1; `text-base-content/70` on tissue about 6:1. Coral on tissue is 2.9:1 and on zinc pale 2.5:1: it is a fill and a mark (a picked stem's ring, the counter's badge, the "Now" month), never text on a light ground. Coral on zinc dark is 4.1:1: marks and large figures only there.

**The pails' metal** is not a theme variable: `styles/site.css` draws it from the surface alt and the neutral (`color-mix(in oklch, var(--color-base-200), var(--color-neutral) 18%)` for the lit side, `35%` for the ribs, the neutral for the rim's shadow), so the Theme tab restyles the pails with the rest. **The season's swatches** are six tones made from the theme as well (`data-tone`: white from the surface with a border, blush from the secondary, coral from the accent, deep from the accent mixed with the text color, green from the primary, zinc from the neutral): a stem's color family, not its true color. The design notes will say to keep stem ink on every swatch but deep, green and zinc, which carry light text.

Other settings: buttons and fields are pills (`--theme-radius-field: 9999px`), fern filled for the main one and outlined in stem ink for the other; relief off (`--theme-depth: 0`); density spacious (`--theme-size: 0.28125rem`); fine borders (1px). Links in text are fern with a 1 px underline offset by 3 px. The status colors are derived, as always; the forms' errors use them.

It is not Kindly's deep wedding green with lilac, Margin's single green ink, Trestle's limewash and olive, nor Marquee's hot pink: a cool galvanized gray and a pink-white tissue under a fresh fern green, with one warm flower's color kept for what the visitor picks.

## Motion

Quiet and useful. Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode nothing moves: Upstart's stillness rule stops CSS animations and transitions and no script runs, so every stem stands in its pail, none is picked, the order-by line says the week in words, the ZIP check answers nothing and no month is marked. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too.

- **The shop's clock** (`scripts/shop-clock.js`, no library): the one script that reads the time. Nothing it drives is a picture of the time of day: it only chooses among the template's own lines and marks a month.
  - it reads the week from the footer, on every page, which renders each `hours` record as a row with `data-weekday`, `data-opens`, `data-closes`, `data-delivers` and `data-cutoff` (24-hour times, as the records hold them), and the shop's time zone from `data-tz` on the footer ("America/New_York"; without it, the visitor's own clock);
  - it sets `data-deliver` on `<html>`: `today` (a delivery day, before its cutoff), `next` (after the cutoff, or on a day without deliveries), and `data-month` (jan to dec). The stylesheet does the rest: the order-by line holds a line per state, written in the template, and shows the one that matches (`html[data-deliver="today"] [data-when="today"]`). With no script there is no `data-deliver`, and the line says the week: "Same-day delivery when you order by 1 pm, Tuesday to Saturday";
  - it writes no sentence: the times and days inside a line are empty slots (`<span data-slot="cutoff">`, `<span data-slot="next_day">`) into which it copies today's cutoff and the next delivery day from the footer's rows, shown in the visitors' way (1 pm, Wednesday) by a small part, `parts/time.liquid`;
  - it checks again every minute while the page is open, and `stop` clears the timer and removes `data-deliver` and `data-month`.
- **The pails** (`data-pails` on the shelf, `data-pail` on each; CSS, and `scripts/pails.js` with Motion's `inView` and `animate`):
  - in CSS, a pail is the stem's photo (`aspect-[2/5]`, `aspect-[1/2]` or `aspect-[3/5]` by its `height`, `object-cover`, its top corners at the theme's radius) standing behind the pail's front, a tapered shape (`clip-path: polygon()`) a third of the photo's height, ribbed and rimmed in the metal's tones, with the plate riveted on it; with no photo, a green-to-blush gradient stem in the pail, so the row never has a hole;
  - as the shelf comes into view, the script lowers each stem into its pail in turn, as if set there from the van: `y: [-28, 0]`, `opacity: [0, 1]`, 0.7 s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`, 80 ms apart from the left; the pails themselves never move. With no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode, every stem is in its pail;
  - on a pointer that hovers, a stem sways 1.5 degrees about the pail's rim (`transform-origin: bottom center`, a CSS transition of 0.8 s), under no-preference and `(hover: hover)` only. It is a nudge, not a follow: the stem does not track the pointer;
  - `stop` takes off the styles it set.
- **Picking a bunch** (CSS only, on the same `data-pail`): each pail is a `<label>` around a visually hidden `<input type="checkbox" name="stems" value="Ranunculus">`, so a tap, a click, Space or the keyboard's focus all work and a screen reader says "Ranunculus, $4 a stem, checkbox". `:has(:checked)` lifts the stem 1.25 rem out of its pail and leans it 2 degrees (a transition of 0.35 s, under no-preference; under reduced motion it is simply raised), rings the plate in coral and shows "In your bunch" under the name. A CSS counter under the shelf counts the checked boxes ("4 stems picked"); the words around it are the template's, the figure is the counter's. Nothing is hidden waiting for a script, and in edit mode no box is checked.
- **The bunch carried to the order form** (`scripts/prefill.js`, no library): on the home page the shelf is a small form that sends its checked stems to `/flowers` with a plain GET (`/flowers?stems=Ranunculus&stems=Tulips#order`), which works with no script; on `/flowers` the script checks the matching pails of the order form, and also chooses the size from `?size=armful` (the sizes' "Order an Armful") and the workshop from `?workshop=dahlia-night` (on `/workshops`). It writes no text and moves nothing; with no script nothing is checked in advance and the form still works.
- **The ZIP check** (`data-zip-check`, `scripts/zip-check.js`, no library): a small form with one field; each row of the zones table carries its ZIP codes in `data-zips` and holds its own answer line, written in the template ("Yes, <span data-slot="zip"></span> is in Zone 2: $18, same day when you order by 1 pm"), and the table ends with the "We don't drive there" line. On submit, the script reads five digits, marks the matching row (`data-match`), shows its answer line under the field and copies the typed ZIP into its slot (the visitor's own text, never the site's). With no script the form reloads the page at `#zones` and the table, every ZIP code in it, answers. `stop` removes `data-match` and the listener.
- **The year in stems** (`data-season`, CSS, and the shop's clock): the month rows carry `data-month-row="sep"`; the clock's `data-month` marks the current one with a coral "Now" and opens it if it is folded on a phone (`<details>` with the current one open by the script; with no script the first month, January, is open on a phone and all are open from 768 px).
- **Reveals** (`data-reveal`, `scripts/reveal.js`, Motion's `inView`): a section's main elements rise a little (`y: [16, 0]`, `opacity: [0, 1]`, 0.8 s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`), children 80 ms apart under `data-reveal="stagger"`, as in the site-scripts skill.
- **The header** is sticky on tissue at 95% with a hairline under it, the order-by line in its thin zinc strip above; it does not change as you scroll.

No smooth scrolling, no page transitions, no loader, no WebGL. Library: Motion, pinned on jsDelivr (`motion@14.9.0`), under MIT. Everything else is CSS. Because the order-by line and the "Now" month follow the clock, `template.json` sets `"shoot": { "clock": "2027-05-12T10:30:00-04:00" }`: a Wednesday morning in May, before the cutoff, with peonies and ranunculus in the pails.

## Ordering and what stays outside

Upstart has no checkout, no payments, no calendar and no embeds, so:
- **an order is a request** (as built): the shop calls back to confirm the stems, the day and the address, and takes payment over the phone; the form and its answer say so before and after sending. A shop with an online store (Shopify, Square, a florist platform) links every "Order" button to it instead (see What varies), never embeds its widget;
- **the bunch is a wish:** picking stems tells the florist what you like; the size sets the price; what sold out is swapped for something like it, and the questions say so;
- **delivery zones are typed facts:** the ZIP check reads the zones table on the page, nothing else; no map, no address lookup; "Get directions" links to the address on a map site;
- **standing orders are billed by the shop,** set up by phone; the page never asks for a card;
- **workshops are paid at the door;** a shop selling tickets elsewhere links out to that page;
- **the time zone:** the order-by line follows the shop's clock, not the visitor's, through `data-tz`; the guide tells the agent to set it from the maker's city;
- **no Instagram feed:** a link to the shop's account in the footer.

The choices the agent asks when the maker's messages leave them open, page type by page type, the recommended one first.
