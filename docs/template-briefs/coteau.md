# Coteau: brief

One of wave 4's templates: the winery, a vineyard with its cellar door (`business`). Its motion is quiet and useful: every wine drawn as a small hill from its vintage to its last good year, a marker that climbs the slope to where the wine stands this year, and a plain line that says "Keep it", "Open it" or "Drink it up".

`coteau` is a working name: French for a hillside of vines, the slope the whole site is drawn from. A web search found no website template or theme of that name (the winery templates that came up are Framer's Cellardoor and Ambroise, Webflow's Wino, Themeisle's Maison Cortese and Colorlib's lists). The name is the template's, not the business's.

## Concept

**Concept.** A winery whose site answers the question every bottle raises: open it tonight, or keep it? The site is a limestone-pale page in daylight, set in flared capitals like an engraved label, with one deep grape-must purple and the pale green of unripe grapes. Every wine has its hill: a small vineyard slope, striped with rows, that rises from the year it was picked to the year it is ready, runs flat while it is at its best and falls to the year it should be drunk by. A marker stands on the slope where the wine is this year, and a line under it says what to do: "Keep it: best from 2028", "Open it: at its best until 2031", "Drink it up: good until 2027". The wine list sorts itself into "Open tonight" and "For the cellar", and each wine's page lays its older vintages behind it as a range of hills, so the one to open first is the one on its plateau. The rest is what a visitor needs at a cellar door: four ways to taste with their price and length, a request for a seat on the porch, the hours by season, the club in two sizes, and the barn on the hill.

It must not read as Ember. Ember is an evening wine bar in a dark room: candlelight following the pointer, an evening scrolled from the first pour to the last glass, a tilted cream menu card, a cellar list by the glass. Coteau is the place the wine comes from, in daylight: a hill, its rows and its barn, wines of one estate with their vintages and how they age, and visits by day. No dark pages, no candlelight, no wine list by the glass.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Vossberg Hill**, a family vineyard and cellar door at 3471 South Kettle Road (an invented road), on a glacial hill above the west shore of Lake Leelanau, on the Leelanau Peninsula in northern Michigan, twenty-five minutes north-west of Traverse City.

- **The hill:** a drumlin the glacier left, its south face gravel and sandy loam over clay, half a mile from the lake, whose water keeps the late spring frosts and the early fall ones off the vines. Twenty-two acres of vines in five blocks, named as the family names them: **Kettle Block** (Riesling, planted 1998, the steepest, at the top), **Barn Block** (Blaufränkisch, 2004, below the barn), **Top Block** (Chardonnay and Pinot Noir for the sparkling wine, 2008), **Orchard Block** (Grüner Veltliner, Pinot Blanc and Pinot Gris, 2011, where the last cherry trees stood), **Lower Block** (Cabernet Franc and Gamay, 2015, on the warmer foot of the slope). Nine grapes in all.
- **The people:** the Vossbergs grew tart cherries on the hill from 1931. After the cherry glut of 1997, **Hanne Vossberg**, the third generation, planted four acres of Riesling in 1998; her husband **Daniel Reyes** runs the vineyard and its crew of six; their son **Teo Vossberg-Reyes** has made the wine since 2016, after harvests in the Wachau and in Niagara; **Priya Natarajan** runs the cellar door and the club.
- **The wine:** about 3,900 cases a year, picked by hand, fermented with the vineyard's own yeasts in steel and in old 1,000-liter casks, farmed without herbicides. Mostly dry Riesling and Blaufränkisch, with a traditional-method sparkling wine from Top Block, a pét-nat rosé, a skin-contact Pinot Gris and, in the years the lake freezes early, an ice wine picked on a January night at 14°F. Bottles from $25 to $48; the ice wine $55 the half bottle.
- **The cellar door:** the 1911 fieldstone dairy barn at the top of the hill, with a bar of reclaimed cherry wood, a stove for the winter, and a porch that looks south over the rows to the lake. May to October, every day from 11 am to 6 pm, Fridays until 8 in July and August; November to April, Thursday to Sunday from noon to 5. Closed Thanksgiving, Christmas Day, New Year's Day and the first two weeks of March, when the wine is bottled.
- **How to taste:** at the bar, five wines for $20, half an hour, walk in, waived with two bottles; on the porch, six wines with Leelanau cheese and bread for $38, an hour, booked; up the hill, a ninety-minute walk through the five blocks with Daniel or Teo and a taste from the cask, $55, Saturdays at 10 from May to October, twelve people at most, booked; the library, three vintages of one wine side by side with the current one, $45, an hour, Thursdays and Fridays, booked. Groups of eight or more book. Children welcome with an adult (cherry juice from the last orchard row); dogs on a leash on the lawn, not in the barn; a free cherry soda for the driver.
- **The club:** the Hill Club, two releases a year (April and October), four or eight bottles chosen by Teo, about $110 or $210 a release, 15% or 20% off all wine, picked up at the release party or shipped to the states that allow it, free tastings for two at every visit, first pick of the library and the ice wine. A member joins by request; Priya calls to set up the card, never on the site. Skip a release or leave after two.
- **Buying:** at the cellar door; bottles can be held for pickup with a short request on each wine's page, paid when collected.
- **Events:** the spring release party, a pruning walk in the snow in February, a Blaufränkisch dinner on the porch in August, the harvest lunch for club members in October, and the night of the ice wine pick when the cold comes.

The phone in the texts is (231) 555-0148, a number reserved for fiction.

The name was searched: no vineyard, winery or wine called Vossberg came up (the nearest, Voss Vineyards, is in Napa and South Australia). No existing template is set in Michigan's wine country; Saucer's Madison is the only Midwest one, and Ember's Wick, in Maine, pours Finger Lakes wines, not its own.

The same structure suits a family winery, a vineyard with a tasting room, a natural-wine estate, a cidery or perry maker (the hills work for any bottle that ages), a mead maker, a sake brewery, a farm distillery that releases vintages, or an urban winery that buys grapes from named vineyards. The agent rewrites the wines and their windows, the blocks, the tastings, the club, the hours and the people.

Their motion is mostly carousels (Swiper on monemvasia, kettmeir, imperiale, astemia, franclizer, domainedelacote), smooth scrolling with Lenis (kettmeir, imperialebolgheri, astemia, franclizer) and page transitions with Barba (kettmeir, domainedelacote), and big background images with parallax (astemia). Six of them stop the visitor at the door with an age question: kettmeir.com and franclizer.com (Yes or No), marthastoumen.com and broccellars.com ("Are you 21+"), monemvasiawinery.gr (the birth year, digit by digit) and domainedelacote.com (a birth year); hiyuwinefarm.com, oldwestminster.com and linguafranca.wine do not, the last saying only that someone 21 or older must sign for a delivery. How this template handles it is below, under the age question.

**What makes it its own:**
- **the hill:** each wine's life drawn as a vineyard slope striped with its rows, from its vintage on the left to its drink-by year on the right: it climbs to the year it is ready, runs flat while it is at its best, then falls. A must-purple marker stands on the slope at this year, with the year above it, and one line below says what to do. When the hill comes into view the marker walks up the slope from the vintage to today. It is the trend's drink-or-hold chart, kept in a PDF on every site above, put on the bottle itself as the site's answer to "should I open it tonight?";
- **open tonight or for the cellar:** the wine list sorts by the hills: two chips, "Open tonight" and "For the cellar", keep the wines at their best now or those still climbing. The hero names one wine to open tonight;
- **the range:** on each wine's page its older vintages stand behind it as a range of hills on one line of years, paler the further back, each with its marker at this year, so the one to open first is the one on its plateau. Sold-out vintages stay in the range, marked "Sold out: poured at library tastings";
- **the back label:** each wine's facts as the back of its bottle, a ruled panel in mono: grapes, block, harvest dates, alcohol, residual sugar, acidity, pH, bottles made, closure, serving temperature, with the dry-to-sweet scale that Riesling back labels carry, a must arrow under its step;
- **the typed label:** a wine without a photo is its label, set in the site's flared capitals on a paper panel with its vintage, block and a band in its style's tone, so a winery with no bottle shots still has a whole list, and a bottle photo replaces it when the maker has one.

**What it leaves out:**
- a cart, checkout, prices with tax and shipping, and a booking or club widget (Tock, Commerce7, Vinoshipper, CellarPass): Upstart has none and embeds none (see below); a map embed, an Instagram feed, video heroes, carousels, smooth scrolling, page transitions, a 3D bottle;
- critics' scores and press quotes: they cannot be invented for a fictional estate, and a real winery adds its own as text if it wants;
- an age question in front of the site, as built (see below);
- **other templates' devices:** a dark room, candlelight following the pointer, an evening scrolled by the hour, a tilted menu card and a list of wines by the glass with dotted leaders (Ember); whether the place is open right now, in any form: a door card, a served-now line, an open sign, a week of bars with a now line, an "Open now" pill (Saucer, Taper, Corner): Coteau's hours are a plain table by season and its only clock is the year, for the wines; the sun, shadows or the time of day (Gnomon); round photos set down like plates (Saucer); four tonal steps as a code (Taper); lines that draw themselves (Chalk, Seam): the hills are filled shapes that never draw, and the marker walks on one that is already there; a jar that fills (Commons); a ribbon or ticker, an arch and a turning stamp (Corner); anything sliding out of a sleeve or flipping into place (Encore); anything scrolling sideways (Reel); dimension lines and rings of distance (Trestle); a curtain (Linen); an envelope, a seal or die-cut arches (Kindly); a door swinging open (Aspect); hang tags and pinked edges (Seam); a stencil (Transfer); handwriting; an italic accent word in a heading; a huge wordmark across the footer.

## Type

All three are files of the template, as `woff2` subset to Latin and Latin Extended (for Blaufränkisch, Grüner and names such as Peña), under the SIL Open Font License, from Google Fonts, declared in `styles/site.css`. Marcellus and Marcellus SC have one weight and no italic, so the headings have none and are never synthesized bold. Ysabeau Office ships as a variable file (100 to 1000) with its italic; IBM Plex Mono as its 400 and 500.

| Body | Ysabeau Office | 400, 1.0625 rem, `leading-relaxed`, `text-base-content/80`, measure `max-w-[62ch]`; the tasting notes in its italic; buttons 600 at 1 rem, sentence case |
| Facts | IBM Plex Mono | 400 and 500: the years on the hills, the back label, prices (`tabular-nums`), the hours, a tasting's price and length, at 0.8125 to 0.9375 rem |

Marcellus is a flared serif drawn from Roman inscriptions, close to Albertus and Optima: the cut of letters engraved on a label or carved over a barn door, the glyphic lettering the trend's sites use (Albertus, Optima, Engravers, Historic Roman), here in a face free to ship. Ysabeau Office is a humanist sans after Garamond, with the same calligraphic flare at small sizes, readable in a long tasting note; IBM Plex Mono is the lab sheet's face, plain and exact for a pH or a harvest date.

It is not Seam's oxblood, Linen's red or Ember's wine on a dark room (the must is a purple at hue 330, forty degrees from Seam's 10 and as far from Transfer's violet at 282), nor Marquee's hot pink, Margin's single green or Kindly's forest green with a lilac: here the deep color is the purple and the green is a pale fill. A daylight page whose one strong color is the inside of a grape skin.

## Motion

Quiet and useful. Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode nothing moves: Upstart's stillness rule stops CSS animations and transitions and no script runs, so every marker stands where the page was rendered and every wine shows its stage as Liquid wrote it. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too.

- **The hill** (`data-hill` on a wine's figure; CSS, and `scripts/hill.js` with Motion's `animate` and `inView`):
  - the figure carries the wine's years as numbers, `style="--vintage: 2024; --ready: 2026; --best: 2031; --by: 2035; --now: 2026"` (no color in the style), from the record's `vintage`, `ready_from`, `best_until` and `drink_by`; `--now` is the year the page was rendered, from Liquid's `"now" | date: "%Y"`, as Encore's past shows already use it;
  - the stylesheet draws the slope with `clip-path: polygon()` from those numbers: it rises from the vintage to the ready year, runs flat at 88% of its height to the best-until year and falls to the drink-by year (each point at `calc((var(--ready) - var(--vintage)) / (var(--by) - var(--vintage)) * 100%)` and so on); the rows are a `repeating-linear-gradient` at a slant, so the slope reads as a striped vineyard seen from across the valley. The four years sit under it in mono at their places. It is a filled shape: it does not draw itself;
  - the marker (`data-hill-now`) is a must dot on a thin stem with the year above it; its left is `--at` along the years and its height is computed by CSS on the slope, `min(1, max(0, (at − vintage) / (ready − vintage)), max(0, (by − at) / (by − best)))` of the plateau's height, so it stands exactly on the slope. `--at` is registered with `@property` as a `<number>` and falls back to `--now`;
  - Liquid also writes the stage on the figure, `data-stage` (`keep`, `open`, `drink-up` or `past`), and the figure holds the four lines, written in the template with the record's years: "Keep it: best from {{ wine.ready_from }}", "Open it: at its best until {{ wine.best_until }}", "Drink it up: good until {{ wine.drink_by }}", "Past its best: we keep it for the record". The stylesheet shows the line of the stage (`[data-stage="open"] [data-when="open"]`). So the page is complete with no script and in edit mode: the marker and the line are those of the year it was rendered;
  - in visitor mode the script works out this year to the day (2026.89 in late November) from the visitor's clock, sets `--now` on each hill, and updates `data-stage` if a ready or best-until year has been passed since the site was published; then, when a hill comes into view, the marker walks up from the vintage to today: `"--at": [vintage, now]`, 1.4 s, `ease: [0.33, 1, 0.68, 1]`, the stage line rising after it (`y: [8, 0]`, `opacity: [0, 1]`, 0.5 s). Under reduced motion the marker is set at today without walking;
  - `stop` removes what it set and puts back the stage Liquid wrote, which it kept on the element.
- **The range** (`data-range` on a wine's vintages; CSS and the same module): Liquid writes the range's first and last years on it (`--from`, the earliest vintage, `--to`, the latest drink-by year, with Liquid's `map`, `sort`, `first` and `last`), and each hill in it takes its left and width from its own years on that common line, so later vintages stand further right, each a little lower and in front of the one before, the furthest palest. Every marker stands at this year, one above the other. The current vintage, in front, is the one the page is about; each other one links to its page. On a phone the range keeps its line of years and scrolls nowhere: the hills narrow.
- **Open tonight or for the cellar** (CSS only): the chips over a list of wines are radio inputs; `:has()` keeps the cards whose `data-stage` matches ("Open tonight": `open` and `drink-up`; "For the cellar": `keep`). "All" is checked by default, so the page shows every wine with no script and in edit mode. The same for the style chips (All, Sparkling, White, Rosé & skin contact, Red, Sweet), from the record's `style`.
- **The taste scale** (`data-taste` on the back label's scale, CSS only): four steps, Dry, Medium dry, Medium sweet, Sweet, as on Riesling back labels; the arrow sits under the record's step (`[data-taste="medium-dry"]`). Nothing moves.

It writes no text and moves nothing; with no script nothing is checked in advance.
- **The header** is sticky on limestone at 95% with a hairline under it; it does not change as you scroll.

No smooth scrolling, no page transitions, no loader, no carousel, no WebGL. Library: Motion, pinned on jsDelivr (`motion@14.9.0`), under MIT. Everything else is CSS.

## Buying, booking and what stays outside

Upstart has no shop, no payments, no calendar and no embeds, so:
- **bottles are sold at the cellar door** (as built), and held on request from each wine's page, paid on collection. A winery with an online shop (Commerce7, WineDirect, Vinoshipper, Shopify) links each wine's "Buy" to its page there through the gallery's `order_link`, a link that leaves the site, never its widget (see What varies);
- **tastings and events are requests,** confirmed by email; a winery on Tock, Resy or CellarPass sends every "Book" there;
- **the club is a request:** the card and the shipping address are set up by phone, never typed on the site; the page says so before and after sending;
- **shipping laws** differ by state and country: the texts say "to the states that allow it" and the guide tells the agent to ask the maker, never to list states it has not been given;
- **no map:** "Get directions" links to the address on a map site, beside written directions;
- **no Instagram feed, no critics' widget:** a link to the account in the footer; scores and press quotes only as text the maker gives, with their source;
- **the year:** the hills need no time zone; Liquid writes the year of rendering and the script the visitor's day, so a site left unpublished for a year still shows the right stage to its visitors.

### The age question

The sites above split: six ask the visitor's age at the door, three do not. A question a visitor answers with one click verifies nothing; what the law asks of a winery is at the sale and the delivery (in the US, an adult of 21 to sign for a shipment, checked by the shop or the carrier) and, in some countries, rules on how alcohol is advertised. Upstart sells nothing, so the template, as built:
- asks no question at the door, and says in the footer of every page that tasting and buying are for those of legal age ("You must be 21 or older…", the age the maker's country sets);
- asks for it where it matters, as a required checkbox on every request: "Everyone in my group is 21 or older", "I am 21 or older";
- offers a door question as a choice (below) for the maker whose market, shop provider or own policy wants one. The guide tells the agent never to call either option "compliant" and to tell the maker that the rules of their state or country are theirs to check.
