# Gnomon: brief

One of wave 3's original designs: the architecture practice (`portfolio`, one of the original, animated designs).

`gnomon` is a working name. The gnomon is the pin of a sundial, the part that casts the shadow. Searches for "Gnomon" as a website template (Framer's marketplace, ThemeForest, Webflow, WordPress) found none. The name is best known from a visual-effects school, which sells no templates. "Lintel" and "Eave" were considered first and dropped, because Framer's marketplace already sells templates under both names.

## Concept

**Concept.** A practice's site lit by the desert sun. The practice's name, the headings, the buttons and the photographs stand a little off a plaster wall in one steady light, and each casts a hard, earth-brown shadow that swings and stretches as the visitor walks past it, the way shadows turn on a wall when you move along it. Projects sit in the page like windows punched through a thick earth wall, each with deep reveals that shift as you walk past. The approach is told as one summer day in a house the practice designed.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Ocampo Hale Architects**, a practice of six people in Tucson, Arizona.
- **The studio:** a 1928 adobe ice house at 418 South Meyer Avenue, on the edge of Barrio Viejo.
- **The founders:** Lucía Ocampo, AIA, who grew up in Nogales, Sonora, and spent eight years at a Phoenix firm, and Peter Hale, AIA, who learned rammed earth building houses near Bisbee. They founded the practice in 2014.
- **The work:** houses, additions and restorations, small public buildings, and shade (ramadas, shaded walks and courtyards) for the Sonoran Desert. The practice works within about two hours of Tucson and takes on six to eight projects a year. It has finished 41 buildings.
- **The way they build:** thick walls of rammed earth or adobe, deep shade before any glass, courtyards and zaguanes that hold the evening air, and no windows facing west.
- **How clients begin:** a short brief through the site. A paid site study ($850, credited to the fees) comes first: two visits to the site, one at noon and one at dusk. The practice answers a brief within three working days.
- **Hours:** weekdays 8:30 to 5:30, visits by appointment.

"Ocampo Hale" was searched: no architecture firm of that name came up. The nearest results were an Ocampo & Associates in Florida and Rios Clementi Hale Studios in Los Angeles, both clearly different names. No existing template is set in Tucson or in the Southwest. Linen, in Oaxaca, is a guesthouse in dyed linen and cochineal, and Gnomon keeps apart from it: plaster and earth, brown shadows and no textiles.

The same structure suits a residential architect, a landscape architect, an interior or garden designer, a design-build firm, a passive-house consultant, or a small engineering or planning office: any practice whose work is buildings or places, shown project by project. The agent rewrites the projects, the approach's hours, the people and the place. It also sets the sunset line to the maker's city (latitude, longitude and time zone). The shadows work wherever the maker is.

**What makes it its own:**
- **Shadows that turn as you pass.** The plaster wall keeps one light from the top of the page to the bottom, and scrolling never reads as a time of day:
  - the practice's name, the headings, the buttons and the photographs' openings each cast a hard shadow on the plaster;
  - each shadow swings and stretches while its element crosses the screen: long to one side as it comes up from below, short beneath it in the middle of the screen, long to the other side as it leaves at the top;
  - the shadows are earth brown, mixed from the theme's adobe;
  - a small sundial in the header, with no hour and no figures, turns its own shadow as the page scrolls: a mark of the practice, nothing more.

  None of the sites seen does it. It also stands for the practice's argument: buildings designed around the sun.
- **Address numbers on stucco.** The large type is set like the steel numbers on a Southwest house wall, standing off the plaster on their own shadows. Typography gets depth without any 3D scene.
- **Deep openings.** Every photo sits in a window punched through a two-foot wall:
  - inside the reveal, the sun's shadow falls across one edge of the picture;
  - the picture shifts a little within its opening as the page scrolls, as a view through a thick wall does when you walk past it;
  - on the home page, the projects form a wall of such openings, of different sizes, each with its plaque below.
- **A day in one of our houses.** The approach is five hours of a summer day (6 am, 11 am, 3 pm, 7 pm, midnight). Each hour has a small solid model of a house and the shadow it casts at that hour. Hovering a card runs its sun through the day, on that card only.
- **Rammed-earth strata.** Bands of layered earth tints mark the breaks between parts of a page and the footer's top edge. They settle as they come into view, like a wall being tamped.

**What it leaves out:**
- plans, sections, dimension lines and any blueprint or measured-drawing look (Trestle's);
- any line drawing itself as the page scrolls (Chalk's). The sundial is drawn once and stays; only its shadow turns;
- page transitions: several templates of earlier waves own one, so Gnomon's pages change plainly;
- sideways scrolling (Reel's), a zoom into or out of a picture (Plinth's), folding pictures (Seam's), and any light or curtain following the pointer (Ember's and Linen's);
- a loader, WebGL and 3D scenes: the shadows grow out of their letters on load instead (below), and the only depth is the shadows and openings;
- scroll shown as a time of day: no hour tied to the scroll, no page going from morning to night, no background changing with the scroll (Ember's evening);
- a cursor effect, a ticker, a giant wordmark across the footer, an italic accent word in a heading, and a local-time clock (Folio's): the sundial shows no time at all;
- stock "architect" imagery: hard hats, rolled plans, people pointing at models, renderings with stock people.

## Type

Both fonts are files of the template, under the SIL Open Font License, from Google Fonts, as variable fonts declared in `styles/site.css`. Darker Grotesque gives the solid, upright letters that read like steel numbers off a wall and cast clean shadows. Instrument Sans is a calm grotesk in the spirit of the Suisse and Neue Montreal these practices use, and its width axis gives the specs a condensed voice.

## Motion

Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode nothing moves: every shadow is cast at its resting angle, a little to the lower right, every photo still in its opening and every stratum settled. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too.

- **The sun's angle** (CSS, `styles/site.css`):
  - a registered property, `@property --sun { syntax: "<number>"; inherits: true; initial-value: 0.35 }`, holds the sun's angle for one element, from 0 (low on one side) to 1 (low on the other). The stylesheet's own value, 0.35, is the resting angle: a shadow a little to the lower right;
  - under `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference` and `@supports (animation-timeline: view())`, each `[data-cast]` and `[data-opening]` element runs its own `--sun` from 0.15 to 0.85 while it crosses the screen (`animation-timeline: view()`, `animation-range: cover`). The shadows of a heading and of a photo further down are each at their own angle, set by where each stands on the screen. The page as a whole has no hour, and its light and colors never change with the scroll;
  - with no scroll-driven animations, under reduced motion and in edit mode, every shadow rests at 0.35.
- **The shadows** (`data-cast`, CSS): an element with `data-cast` casts a hard shadow, `filter: drop-shadow(…)` with no blur, its color the adobe brown mixed transparent.
  - The shadow's direction comes from the sun's bearing, `calc((var(--sun) - 0.5) * 160deg)`, through CSS `sin()` and `cos()`. Its length comes from the sun's height (`sin(var(--sun) * 180deg)`, floored so it never runs off the screen), times the element's own stand-off, `--cast` (0.5rem on buttons, 1rem on headings, up to 2rem on the hero's statement).
  - An element coming up from the bottom of the screen casts a long shadow to the lower right. In the middle of the screen its shadow is short and nearly beneath it, and as it leaves at the top it casts a long shadow to the lower left.
  - It is set only on the statement, headings, project names, the main buttons, the openings and the models, never on running text.
- **The shadows grow** (`scripts/cast-in.js`, Motion): on load, a second property, `--light`, goes from 0 to 1 over 1.4 s (`ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`), and every shadow's length and opacity are multiplied by it, so the shadows grow out of their letters. The light on the plaster stays the same throughout. This replaces a loader: nothing is hidden and the text is there from the first frame. Under reduced motion and with no script, `--light` stays at its stylesheet value, 1.
- **The sundial** (`data-sundial` in the header, CSS only): a small disc in rammed earth with its gnomon, a thin upright pin. The pin's shadow, in adobe brown, turns around the disc as the page scrolls (`rotate` from `--sun`, on the dial's own `animation-timeline: scroll(root)`). It has no hour lines, no figures and no time beside it: it is the practice's mark, `aria-hidden`. With no scroll-driven animations, under reduced motion and in edit mode, its shadow rests at the lower right.
- **Deep openings** (`data-opening` on a `<figure>` around one `<img>`):
  - a wrapper with `overflow: hidden` and a rammed-earth inner edge, drawn as an inset box-shadow from `--color-base-200`, which reads as the wall's thickness;
  - a `::after` across the picture draws the reveal's shadow as a hard inset shadow in adobe brown. It is cast from the side the sun stands on, its width following the opening's own `--sun` like every other shadow, with `mix-blend-mode: multiply`, so the picture stays readable and the shadow covers only a strip along one or two edges;
  - under no-preference and with `view()` timelines, the picture is a little larger than its opening (`scale: 1.12`) and moves from `translate: 0 -5%` to `0 5%` as the opening crosses the screen (`animation-timeline: view()`), as a view does through a thick wall;
  - with none of that, the picture fills its opening with the shadow at its resting angle, and the maker replaces it as a plain image.
- **The models** (`data-model`, CSS only):
  - each card of the five hours holds a small solid model: a block with a flat roof and an overhang, in three tints of rammed earth, set in isometric view with CSS 3D transforms. Its shadow on the ground plane is a skewed shape from the card's own `style="--sun: 0.08"` (6 am), `0.42` (11 am), `0.7` (3 pm), `0.95` (7 pm). At midnight there is no shadow, and the model sits on monsoon blue with one window lit in low sun. These hours are the cards' content, never tied to the scroll;
  - on hover and on keyboard focus, the card's `--sun` runs from 0 to 1 over 2.4 s (a transition on the registered property), so its shadow wheels round the model, then settles back to its hour;
  - under reduced motion, nothing turns and each card keeps its hour.
- **Tamped strata** (`data-strata`, CSS only): a band 56 to 120 px tall of eight to twelve horizontal layers of uneven height. They are drawn with a `linear-gradient` of hard stops in tints mixed from `--color-base-200`, `--color-secondary` and `--color-neutral`. As the band comes into view it settles from `scale: 1 1.14` to its own height, from the bottom (`animation-timeline: view()`, a `from` keyframe only). Elsewhere it is simply there.
- **Rises** (`data-rise`, CSS): headings and paragraphs rise into view (`animation-timeline: view()`, a `from` keyframe only), as in the site-scripts skill.
- **The project filter** (CSS only): the type chips are radio inputs, and `:has()` keeps the projects of the chosen type. "All" is checked by default, so every project shows with no script and in edit mode.
- **The header:** sticky on plaster, with a hairline under it once the page scrolls (`animation-timeline: scroll()`, with the line in the stylesheet's own state). On a phone the links fold into a full-screen menu on plaster (a `<dialog>` opened by the menu button, as in the gallery's headers), where the sundial mark stays at the top.
- **Sunset today** (`data-sunset` in the footer and on the contact page, `scripts/sunset.js`): from the studio's `data-lat="32.2155" data-lng="-110.9747" data-tz="America/Phoenix"`, the script works out today's sunset with the usual solar formula. It then writes "Sunset in Tucson today: 5:24 pm" in place of the line written in the page, "Studio open weekdays 8:30 to 5:30". It changes no other text.
- **Smooth scrolling:** Lenis, light (`lerp: 0.1`), which also makes the shadows turn smoothly.

Libraries: Motion and Lenis, pinned on jsDelivr, under MIT. The sun's angle, the shadows, the sundial, the openings, the models and the strata are CSS (registered properties, scroll-driven animations, trigonometric functions), so they cost no script. No Splitting, no OGL, no View Transitions.

## What varies

**Home (`/`).** It carries the choices for the whole website.

*How does a client begin a project?*
- **A brief, through the form** (as built): "Start a project" in the header, the hero, the brief band and every project page leads to the brief on the contact page (`/contact#brief`). Briefs arrive in the Content tab, and the practice answers by email.
- **A first call, booked on the practice's scheduling page:** the buttons become "Book a first call" and open that page, whose address the maker gives. The contact page keeps the studio and the questions, the brief and `enquiries` go, and the line under the title says what the call covers.
- **By email or phone:** the buttons become "Write to us". The contact page sets the email and phone large in place of the brief, and `enquiries` goes.

*What does the practice design?*
- **Buildings, around the sun** (as built): the five hours of the approach, the facts on the sun on each project and the shadows stay as they are.
- **Interiors:** the five hours become the light in a room through a day (the same cards and models, the model's block opened as a room). The facts on the sun become the room's light, materials and furniture makers, and the area is the room's.
- **Landscapes and gardens:** the five hours follow a garden's shade and water, and the models become a tree and a wall casting shade. The facts become the planting, water and shade, and the area is the site's.

**Projects (`/projects`, `/projects/<project>`).**

*Which projects does the list show?*
- **Built and unbuilt, each with its status** (as built): the type chips filter, and each plaque carries its dot for built, on site or in design.
- **Grouped by status:** three walls one after another, "Built", "On site" and "On the drawing table", with no type chips.
- **Built work only:** the status dots, the status chips' meaning and `status` go, and the count says "buildings".

*What does a project's page tell about the climate?*
- **The facts on the sun** (as built): faces, shade, energy and walls, from the maker's own figures.
- **The building's facts only:** for a practice that does not measure its buildings. The sun's facts and their fields go, and the story keeps "How it lives through the day".

When left out: the home's wall of openings shows the projects without links, their plaques in full. "All projects" and the projects' navigation record go, and the hero's "See the projects" points to the wall (`/#work`).

**Practice (`/practice`).**

*Who is the practice?*
- **Two partners and a team** (as built): the partners sign the home's statement, and the people show as portraits in openings.
- **One architect:** the texts move to the first person singular ("I"). The people become one portrait beside a longer bio, the home's signature is one name, and `people` keeps one record.
- **A larger studio:** the people as ruled rows by role (partners, associates, studio) with no portraits, and the partners' portraits only on the home page.

*How does the practice speak of its fees?*
- **One paragraph on how fees work** (as built): a percentage of the cost of building, then a fixed fee, with the site study credited.
- **A fee for each stage:** each of the six stages carries what it costs or how it is charged, and the paragraph goes.
- **Not on the site:** the fees paragraph goes, and the questions on the contact page say fees are given after the site study.

When left out: the home's "About the practice" points to the practice in brief (`/#practice`), the five hours stay on the home page, and the footer keeps the address. The people go with the page, unless the maker asks for them on the home page.

**News (`/news`, `/news/<note>`).**

*What does the news hold?*
- **Notes with a page each** (as built): projects begun and finished, awards, talks and the studio's own news.
- **Press and awards only, linking out:** a dated list whose lines link to the article or the award's own page. The note pages and `detail.liquid` go, and `news` loses its body and cover.

When left out: the home's news section, its navigation record and `news` go. Recognition stays on the practice page.

**Contact (`/contact`).** Its brief follows how a client begins, chosen with the home page.

*What does the brief ask first?*
- **The site** (as built): the site, then the project, then you, since the practice designs from the site.
- **The project:** the kind of work and the budget first, for a practice whose clients often have no site yet. The site part becomes optional.

When left out: every "Start a project" points to the email address (`mailto:`), the footer keeps the address and the hours, and the home's brief band keeps its heading with that link.

## What Upstart cannot do, and how the brief handles it

- **Booking the site study and taking its fee:** Upstart has no payments or calendar, so the brief is a request through a form, and the practice arranges the study and its invoice by email. A scheduling page can be linked instead (a choice of the home page).
- **Files with the brief** (site photos, surveys, plans): the form takes no files, so the answer asks for them by reply to the confirmation email.
- **The real sun:** the shadows turn with each element's place on the screen, not with the clock, and the sunset line is worked out in the browser from the studio's latitude and longitude, with no outside service.
- **Maps:** the address is set large with a "Get directions" link, and there is no map embed.
- **3D models of the projects, walkthroughs and video:** left out; the approach's models are small CSS blocks, not the projects' buildings.
