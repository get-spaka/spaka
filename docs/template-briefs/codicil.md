# Codicil: brief

One of wave 3's four standard templates: the law firm (`business`, not one of the original designs). Its motion is quiet and purposeful, like a careful lawyer's hand: a phrase struck through in red and a plainer one written in, an index whose tabs bring a practice forward, soft reveals.

`codicil` is a working name: a codicil is the short document that amends a will, the firm's corrections in writing. Searched: no website template is sold under that name (Framer sells law templates called Docket, Proviso and Lexmont, and several products called Redline, so none of those).

## Concept

**Concept.** A small firm that writes the law in plain English. The site reads like a well-kept file: a Baskerville on bond paper, a blue-black ink, manila folders, and one red pen. Where a lawyer's phrase would go, the site strikes it through in red and writes the plain words in after it ("Legal advice ~~in legalese~~ you can act on"); the practice areas are a tabbed index, each tab bringing its sheet to the front with who leads it and what it costs; every article opens on a memo heading (To, From, Re, Date); and asking for a first meeting says up front what it costs, what happens next, and why the firm asks who else is involved.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Kesler Lund & Carrow**, attorneys at law, in a three-story 1858 brick townhouse at 23 Haverly Street (an invented street) in Church Hill, Richmond, Virginia, ten minutes from the courts downtown, with a ground-floor meeting room reached step-free through the garden gate. **Eleanor Kesler** (estate planning) and **Henry Lund** (real estate and small business) opened the firm in 2009 in two rooms on Main Street and moved to Church Hill in 2013; **Miriam Carrow** (employment, for employees and small employers) joined as a name partner in 2016, and **Joaquín Ibarra** (probate, estate disputes, elder law and guardianship) became the fourth partner in 2021. Two associates work with them, **Grace Whitlow** (real estate and business) and **Simon Hargrove** (estate planning), with a paralegal and an office manager. The firm acts for families, homeowners and the small businesses they run, across Virginia: wills and trusts, probate and estates, real estate closings and title, forming and selling small businesses, employment, and elder law. A first meeting lasts 45 minutes with a partner, in person, by video or by phone, for a fixed $150 that is credited to the work if the client goes ahead. Most work is done for a fixed fee agreed in a letter of engagement; disputes are billed by the hour against a written estimate. A request sent through the site is answered within one business day, once the firm has checked it can act (a conflict check). The office is open Monday to Friday, 8:30 am to 5:30 pm, until 7 pm on Thursdays by appointment. The name was searched: there is a Kesler & Rust in Salt Lake City and a Lund Law in Minnesota, but no Kesler Lund & Carrow anywhere. The people's names share no surname with another template's (Ashby, first chosen, is Margin's writer).

The same structure suits any small or mid-sized firm with a few practice areas and named people: a law firm, an estate planning or family law practice, a notary or a solicitor's office, an immigration practice, an accounting or tax firm, a financial planner, a mediator, a patent agent, or one lawyer working alone. The agent rewrites the areas, the people and their admissions, the fees, the articles and the regulatory lines.

## Trends it draws on

These come from 16 sites of law firms opened in November 2026: two Awwwards Honorable Mentions (ario.law, May 2026; forthetimes.law, April 2025, whose page renders by script and gave only its styles), an Awwwards nominee (kreisson.com.au), four of Lawyerist's best law firm websites of 2026 (burnhamlaw.com, sidweberlaw.com, alignfamilylaw.ca, es.legal), litigation boutiques known for their sites (selendygay.com, heckerfink.com, keker.com, bravoschrager.com), and firms of every size in London, Frankfurt and the US (kingsleynapley.co.uk, mishcon.com, withersworldwide.com, hengeler.com, erikpelton.com). WebFetch reads a page's text; the fonts, colors and libraries below come from each site's own HTML and stylesheets, read with curl. farrer.co.uk and alhlaw.com refused the connection; lapa.ninja refused too, and Siteinspire's search found no law firm. Each trend was seen on at least two of the sites.

1. **A classical book serif for the headings over a plain grotesk, rather than the old all-caps Trajan or the startup sans.** selendygay.com (Freight Display with Stevie Sans), heckerfink.com (Plantin and Canela with Replica), kingsleynapley.co.uk (EB Garamond with Montserrat), sidweberlaw.com (STIX Two Text with Inter), hengeler.com (Georgia and Lora with Akzidenz-Grotesk Next), withersworldwide.com (Zapf Humanist with Lato). The other way is there for contrast, the bold grotesk of a consumer brand: burnhamlaw.com (Trade Gothic Condensed, chartreuse #d5ff45 on #1a1a1a), mishcon.com (Helvetica Now), ario.law (black and white).
2. **Warm paper and stone grounds, one deep institutional color, and a small bright accent.** selendygay.com (a green-black #202625 on cream #f7f7ed, with a red #ef4852), kingsleynapley.co.uk (oxblood #7d272e on stone #e4e1da), sidweberlaw.com (aubergine #261841 on sand #f4ece2, with taupe #ad9a8a), withersworldwide.com (deep teal #004851 with orange #e36f1e), kreisson.com.au (charcoal #323232 on #f4f3f1 with brass #ceb477), hengeler.com (navy #001a3d with a red #df0000), alignfamilylaw.ca (slate #232c33 and teal #2a5e74 with coral #dd7f73).
3. **Practice areas as a plain index, numbered or grouped by the client's situation, instead of icon cards.** ario.law (four practices numbered 01 to 04, each with its head of practice and team size), kreisson.com.au (three numbered stages, "from contract to completion", each with its list of services), selendygay.com (ten practices as plain links), kingsleynapley.co.uk (fourteen areas as links, with tabs for "Who we help" and "Situations"), withersworldwide.com (by audience: individuals and families, founders and businesses, institutions). Lawyerist's winners bravoschrager.com and es.legal show the older way: grids of nine and seventeen cards.
4. **Named people with their credentials, and firm figures said plainly.** selendygay.com (a featured partner, and a directory filtered by position, school and last name; "more than $40 billion" recovered), hengeler.com (lawyers filtered by role and office), ario.law (the managing partner featured, "10+ years, 30+ lawyers"), es.legal (six headshots linking to profiles, "40+ years of experience"), heckerfink.com ("more than 100 federal and state" trials), kingsleynapley.co.uk (a grid of portraits), withersworldwide.com (220 partners, 650 lawyers).
5. **Articles as a dated feed, labeled by topic and kind, filtered by practice.** mishcon.com (filter tabs, category, format, date and reading time on each item), kingsleynapley.co.uk ("Browse by topic", each item dated and labeled Article), withersworldwide.com (date and "Article"), ario.law (a press center with category and date), selendygay.com and keker.com (the latest headlines on the home page).
6. **The first contact as a request that says what happens next, what it costs, and what it does not mean.** alignfamilylaw.ca (a form, "within one business day", three steps from the first call, a Pricing page in the menu), es.legal ("Request Your Consultation", and a warning that writing does not create an attorney-client relationship and that nothing sensitive should be sent), kreisson.com.au ("Book a consultation", repeated as "Talk to us"), erikpelton.com ("transparent, flat-fee pricing"), bravoschrager.com (a short form beside the office hours), kingsleynapley.co.uk and selendygay.com (the regulator's number and "Attorney Advertising" in the footer).

Their motion is quiet: fades as sections arrive (AOS on selendygay.com, Framer's appear effects on sidweberlaw.com), smooth scrolling (Lenis on kingsleynapley.co.uk and kreisson.com.au), page transitions on ario.law (Barba).

**What makes it its own:**
- **the redline:** a lawyer's phrase struck through in red and the plain words written in after it, in italic, with a red rule under them, as a contract is marked up. The strike draws across from the left, then the new words write themselves in. It is the site's voice made visible, used once in the hero, once in the request band and at most once more per page;
- **the tabbed index:** the practice areas as a file of manila dividers, their tabs staggered along the top like folders in a drawer; choosing a tab slides its sheet to the front, with what the area covers, the situations it answers, the partner who leads it and its fixed fees. It works with no script;
- **the memo heading:** each article opens as a firm's memo, To, From, Re and Date in a label column above a double rule, and the list of articles is a pile of such slips;
- **clauses for sections:** each section's eyebrow carries a section sign and its number, counted by CSS (`§ 3 · The partners`), so a section added later is numbered too;
- **the conflict check, said plainly:** the request asks who else is involved "so we can check we're free to act for you", and says that writing does not make the firm your lawyer yet.

**What it leaves out:**
- columns, gavels, scales of justice and handshakes; navy and gold; stock pictures of lawyers in a glass boardroom;
- star ratings, review widgets, client testimonials and case-result figures (which many state bars restrict; the guide says so), award badges, a chat bubble, a map embed;
- dimension lines and ruled title blocks (Trestle's), margin notes (Margin's), torn paper and tear-off tabs (Commons'), hang tags (Seam's), museum labels (Plinth's), a round stamp turning (Corner's), smooth scrolling, page transitions, a loader, carousels.

## Type

All three families are files of the template, from Google Fonts, under the SIL Open Font License, subset to Latin and Latin Extended (for Joaquín). Baskervville is the ANRT's revival of John Baskerville's type, the face of official printing; it ships as a variable font (400 to 700) with its italic, which sets the redline's inserted words. Baskervville SC, its small capitals, sets the clause eyebrows and the memo's labels. IBM Plex Sans, a grotesk drawn for an institution, keeps the body plain and has tabular figures for fees and dates.

| Role | Font | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Headings | Baskervville | 400 (500 at the smallest sizes), never bold; the hero at `clamp(2.75rem, 5vw + 1rem, 5.25rem)`, `leading-[1.04]`, `tracking-[-0.01em]`, `text-balance`; section headings `clamp(2rem, 2.5vw + 1rem, 3.25rem)`; small headings 1.375 rem; sentence case |
| The redline's new words | Baskervville italic | the heading's size and weight |
| Eyebrows, memo labels, tabs | Baskervville SC | 500, 0.875 to 0.9375 rem, `tracking-[0.04em]`: "§ 2 · What we do", "Re", the index's tabs |
| Body | IBM Plex Sans | 400, 1.0625 rem, `leading-relaxed`, `text-base-content/80`, measure `max-w-[64ch]`; 1.125 rem in an article's text |
| Labels, buttons, fees, dates | IBM Plex Sans | 500, 0.8125 rem, sentence case, `tabular-nums` for fees, dates and hours; buttons 500 at 1 rem |

None of wave 1, wave 2 or the built templates uses Baskervville or IBM Plex (Folio's Newsreader, Corner's and Margin's serifs are other families). **If a sibling takes them:** Libre Baskerville (headings, a heavier Baskerville) with Work Sans (body); or Crimson Pro at 400 (headings, a Garamond-like book face) with Albert Sans (body). All four are on Google Fonts under the OFL.

## Palette

Bond paper, manila folders, blue-black ink and a red pen. Every pair of text and background passes WCAG AA, measured from these values: ink on paper 14.8:1, ink on pale manila 13.2:1, ink on manila 10.8:1, blue-black on paper 10.1:1 (and paper on blue-black the same), blue-black on manila 7.4:1, light text on ink night 14.4:1, red on paper 4.8:1, `text-base-content/70` on paper 5.7:1. Red on pale manila is 4.3:1 and on manila 3.5:1, and on ink night 3.2:1: there it marks lines and the section sign, never small text. The struck words, at 60% ink, are 4.2:1 on paper, only ever at heading size and hidden from screen readers.

It is not Trestle's olive and limewash, Mend's lake on warm white nor Margin's green ink: a cool blue-black on warm paper whose one strong color is a red that only ever marks.

## Motion

Few effects, each with a reason. Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode nothing moves and everything is in its final place: every redline struck and written in, the first tab's sheet in front, every section shown. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too.

- **The redline** (`data-redline` on a heading or a line holding a `<del>` then an `<ins>`; `scripts/redline.js`, Motion):
  - its stylesheet state is the finished markup: the `<del>` at 60% of the text color with a 2 px red line through it (a background `linear-gradient` of `var(--color-accent)`, 2 px high at mid-height and 100% wide), the `<ins>` in italic with a 1.5 px red rule under it (`text-decoration-color: var(--color-accent)`, `text-underline-offset: 0.18em`). The `<del>` carries `aria-hidden="true"`, so a screen reader hears the sentence as it now reads;
  - the script, in `start`, sets the strike's width to 0 and clips the `<ins>` (`clip-path: inset(0 100% 0 0)`), then, when the line comes into view (Motion's `inView`; the hero on load, after 0.4 s), draws the strike across in 0.45 s (`ease: [0.65, 0, 0.35, 1]`), waits 0.15 s, and reveals the new words left to right in 0.6 s, their rule following. Every value says where it starts (`backgroundSize: ["0% 2px", "100% 2px"]`, `clipPath: ["inset(0 100% 0 0)", "inset(0 0% 0 0)"]`); `stop` removes the styles it set. It plays once per page view;
  - under reduced motion, with no script and in edit mode, the markup is simply there.
- **The tabbed index** (`data-index` on the section, CSS only):
  - the tabs are radio inputs and their labels, one per `practice-areas` record, the first checked; `:has()` brings the chosen sheet forward (`[data-index]:has(#area-wills:checked) [data-sheet="wills"]`), the others stay behind it with only their top edges showing, a few pixels apart, like a file;
  - under `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference` and `html[data-up-moving]`, the chosen sheet rises into place (`translate: 0 12px` to 0 and `opacity`, 0.35 s), and a tab under the pointer lifts 3 px; otherwise the sheet simply changes;
  - on a phone the tabs wrap in two columns above a single sheet. The keyboard moves between tabs with the arrow keys, as radios do; each sheet ends with a link to its area's page, so the index is never the only way in.
- **Reveals** (`data-reveal`, `scripts/reveal.js`, Motion's `inView`): a section's main elements rise a little (`y: [12, 0]`, `opacity: [0, 1]`, 0.7 s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`), a list's items 70 ms apart under `data-reveal="stagger"`, as the site-scripts skill shows. The memo slips arrive this way; nothing else moves.
- **The request, filled in from the link** (`data-prefill`, `scripts/prefill.js`, no library): "Request a consultation with Miriam" leads to `/consultation?with=miriam-carrow`, an area's to `/consultation?area=employment`; the script checks the matching options of the form. It writes no text and does not move; with no script nothing is checked in advance.
- **The header** is sticky on paper at 95%, a hairline under it; it does not change as you scroll.

No smooth scrolling, no page transitions, no loader, no parallax: a firm's site should feel steady. Libraries: Motion, pinned on jsDelivr, under MIT. Everything else is CSS.

## What Upstart cannot do here, and how the brief handles it

- **A booking calendar:** the first meeting is a request with a preferred way and time, confirmed by the firm once it has checked for conflicts; a firm with its own scheduling page links out to it.
- **Payments and retainers:** nothing is paid on the site; the first meeting's fee is paid at the meeting or invoiced, and retainers go into the firm's trust account outside the site. A firm with an online payment page (LawPay, Clio Payments) links to it from the footer, a link that leaves the site.
- **Documents and a client portal:** visitors cannot send files through a form, and they should not yet: the note field warns against it, and the answer says what to bring to the meeting. A firm's existing portal is a link in the footer.
- **Confidential intake:** the form asks only what it takes to arrange a meeting and check for conflicts; the guide tells the agent never to add fields for account numbers, dates of birth, Social Security numbers or the facts of a dispute.
- **Reviews and rankings:** no widgets or badges; recognitions, if the maker has them and their bar allows them, are words typed in by the maker.
- **A map:** no embed; "Get directions" links to a maps site, beside written directions.
