# Strum: brief

One of wave 5's templates: the music school, a community school for every age that teaches private lessons and group classes, runs bands and ensembles, rents instruments and puts its students on stage twice a year (`business`). Its movement is expressive: the headline's words land like a downbeat, and every ruled list on the site is strung. Its rules are strings, which pluck once when their section arrives and again when the pointer crosses them, with a short damped vibration and no sound.

`strum` is a working name. It replaces the wave sheet's "Pluck", which ThemeForest sells as "Pluck - Multipurpose Landing Page Bootstrap 5 Template" (EnvyTheme, item 23778441). A strum is what the strings do when a list arrives: each plucked in turn, top to bottom, like a hand drawn across them. ThemeForest's search finds nothing called Strum. Web searches of Framer, Webflow, Squarespace and Shopify's theme store found no template or theme of that name either; the nearest are Stratum, a Framer real estate template and a Webflow SaaS template, and Strada and Strong on Shopify. The trends file's taken names (Tuneo, Fermata, Forte, Solfeggio, MelodyMentor, Rythmo and the rest) are avoided, and so is any Melody-, Rhythm- or Music- compound. The name is the template's, not the business's.

## Concept

**Concept.** A music school whose site you read like the noticeboard by its front desk: who it is for, at what age, on which evening, with whom, and what it costs, in plain lists. Every list is strung: its rules are drawn as strings, thin at the top and thicker down the list, the way an instrument's strings run from the highest to the lowest. They ring for a moment when the list scrolls into view and whenever a hand passes over them. The site is bright and loud in one color: flat bands of tangerine, a sign-painter's heavy slanted display face like the hand-lettered signs of the old Route 66 motor courts, a plain, very readable sans, paper white, and a cool night ink for the concerts and the footer. A parent reads on the first screen that the first lesson is free, that the school rents the instrument and that lessons run after school. A sixty-year-old beginner reads that the headline is talking to them too.

It must not read as Mend, Marquee or Chalk:
- Mend is a calm practice whose friendly checklist sets a serif headline beside a tall photo, with four reassurances in a ruled row of labels under both.
- Marquee is a festival poster in hot pink on tangerine, with halftone dots, stickers and stretching letters.
- Chalk is a climbing wall whose line draws itself down the page from hold to hold.

Strum's strings are never drawn on: they are always there, full length, and only bend and spring back where they stand.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Ristra Music School**, in Nob Hill, Albuquerque, New Mexico. A ristra is the string of dried red chiles that hangs by a New Mexico front door.
- **The place:** Ristra Courts, a 1948 motor court on Route 66, at 16 Oberlin Court SE (an invented lane off Central Avenue), Albuquerque, New Mexico 87106. The motel closed in 2009. Since 2016 its twelve single-story rooms around a gravel courtyard have been twelve teaching rooms, each still with its number on the door. Room 7 holds the drums and has been soundproofed twice. Room 12, with its wood floor, holds the little ones' classes. The old carport, walled in, is "the Carport", a sixty-seat hall for recitals and the teen bands. The neon sign still says RISTRA COURTS, with MUSIC LESSONS hung under it.
- **The people:** the school was opened by **Marisol Archuleta** (she/her), a violinist and fiddler who grew up playing at her uncle's dances in the Española valley, and **Ray Gallegos** (he/him), a guitarist who directs the school's mariachi. Twelve more teachers work there; the site shows eight, among them **Linh Nguyễn** (she/her, piano), **Nadia Haddad** (she/her, cello and the junior string orchestra), **Jonah Kessler** (he/him, drums and the band program), **Celia Baca** (she/her, voice and songwriting), **Walt Ferris** (he/him, trumpet and saxophone, thirty-one years a high school band director) and **Sun-hee Park** (she/her, the little ones' music and movement).
- **Programs, by age:** Little ones (4 to 6): music and movement in groups of eight, then a first instrument from five. Kids (7 to 12): private lessons and the clubs that make them stick (Guitar club, the Piano lab, the junior string orchestra). Teens (13 to 18): bands in the Carport, Mariachi Ristra juniors, songwriting, audition preparation. Grown-ups (18 to 94): evening group classes, private lessons at their own pace, and a recital of their own.
- **Lessons and prices:** private lessons of 30, 45 or 60 minutes at $34, $49 or $64 a lesson, billed by the term ($442, $637 or $832 for the fall term's 13 weeks) or in four monthly payments. Group classes are $195 to $260 a term, ensembles $230 ($120 alongside private lessons). Registration is $35 a year per family; siblings and second instruments are 10% off. The Courtyard Fund pays part of the tuition for about one student in four.
- **The year:** a fall term (September 7 to December 18, 2027, 13 weeks of lessons), a spring term (January 10 to May 6, 2028, 16 weeks), summer camps in June and July. Two student recitals a year, a band night, a winter concert in the courtyard by luminarias, a grown-ups' recital, and one paid concert by the teachers for the Fund.
- **Rentals:** violins (1/16 to 4/4), violas, cellos, guitars, ukuleles, trumpets, alto saxophones and digital pianos, $9 to $42 a month. Every month's rent counts toward buying the instrument, and children change sizes for free as they grow.
- **Starting:** a free first lesson of thirty minutes, asked for with a form; the front desk calls back within two school days. Enrollment for a term is a request too, paid at the desk, by phone or by check. Lessons run Monday to Thursday from 2:30 to 8:30 pm, Friday to 7 pm and Saturday from 9 to 3; the desk answers (505) 555-0164 from an hour before.

The name was searched. No music school or motel called Ristra came up in Albuquerque, where the search found other names: New Mexico School of Music, School of Rock Albuquerque, Albuquerque Institute of Music, Roots & Wings Community Music School, UNM Music Prep School and Sagebrush Music Academy. No template or brief is set in Albuquerque or in New Mexico. The phone, (505) 555-0164, is in the range reserved for fiction, and no brief uses it.

The same structure suits a community music school, a music academy for children, a piano or guitar studio with several teachers, a school of rock or band program, a strings school, a mariachi or folk music school, a voice studio, a music therapy practice with lessons, or a dance school that runs classes by age. The agent rewrites the programs and their ages, the classes and their evenings, the teachers, the terms, the rentals, the concerts, the building and the city.

## Trends it draws on

The gap: trend 4's devices stay in the logo and the hero (Levine's seating chart, Curtis's sliced letters), or turn literal (Lullula's staff lines and note heads). Trend 2's ruled lists, where these sites actually tell a parent what to choose, are ordinary hairlines. Nothing in them is musical, and nothing answers the hand. Strum turns trend 2 into trend 4: the rules of the lists are the strings that most of what the school teaches is played on (violin, cello, guitar, ukulele, the inside of a piano), and they move the way strings move. Trend 1 gives the home page its first list: the four ages, strung from the thinnest string to the thickest. Trend 5 gives it its tangerine and its strip.

Common and left outside: muted autoplay video in every hero (necmusic.edu, curtis.edu, trinitylaban.ac.uk, popskolan.se, levinemusic.org), since Spaka shows photos; the editorial serif display with one italic word (lullula-music.com's "BEGIN *here*", trinitylaban.ac.uk's "Find *your* rhythm", necmusic.edu, lamaisonschoolofmusic.com), the tasteful default the web-design skill warns against, and the italic accent word that Corner, Folio and Linen already have; GSAP reveals and preloaders (levinemusic.org, trinitylaban.ac.uk, lamaisonschoolofmusic.com); circular text around a play button (popskolan.se); faculty carousels and horizontal carousels everywhere (curtis.edu); dark full-bleed video under centered capitals (trinitylaban.ac.uk, popskolan.se); and the headline "Unleash Their Full Potential" (meritmusic.org).

**What makes it its own:**
- **the strings:** every ruled list of the site (the ages, the week's classes, the teachers, the term's dates, the concerts and their running order, the rentals, the hero's three promises) draws its rules as strings with the theme's ink. When the list scrolls into view, its strings are plucked one after another, top to bottom, 60 ms apart: a strum. When a pointer crosses one, it is plucked where it was crossed, harder for a faster hand. Each vibrates briefly, its middle bending and springing back a few times as a real string's does, the vibration dying away within a second. A fainter copy mirrors it while it moves, the blurred lens a vibrating string shows the eye. There is no sound, ever. The strings have gauges: the ages' list runs from the thinnest (the little ones) to a thick wound string (the grown-ups), as an instrument's highest string is its thinnest, and the thin strings ring fast and short, the thick ones slower and longer;
- **the friendly checklist on a tangerine band:** the headline, "Start at four. Or at sixty-four.", set huge across the band's width, over three promises each with a tick and strung between them, with the free lesson's button under them and one photo of a lesson beside the promises, under the headline. The first screen shows the move and the signature together: the words land, then the three strings are strummed and each tick bounces as its string rings;
- **the ages first:** the home page asks "how old?" before "which instrument?". Each program is a wide row with its ages in the display face ("4–6"), what it holds, its price from, and a page of its own with its classes, its teachers and its questions;
- **the term at a glance:** the term's dates as a strung list, with this week marked and the next date marked "Coming up", from the school's own clock (`America/Denver`);
- **the motor court:** the twelve rooms as a strung list on the About page (Room 7, drums, soundproofed twice), and every class with its room number, as a parent reads it on the door.

## Type

Both fonts are files of the template, as `woff2` under the SIL Open Font License, from Google Fonts, declared in `styles/site.css`. Both were checked in the Google Fonts repository (`ofl/shrikhand`, `ofl/lexend`) and in their files:
- **Shrikhand** (Jonny Pinhorn, 2016) has one style, 400, with no italic and no other weight. Its subsets are Latin, Latin Extended and Gujarati: it is subset to Latin and Latin Extended (ñ, ó, ā, ł, ő are drawn). It has no Vietnamese, so a teacher's name (Nguyễn) is never set in it.
- **Lexend** ships as one variable file, weight 100 to 900, roman only, no italic. It is subset to Latin, Latin Extended and Vietnamese, so Nguyễn and Española are drawn. It has no tabular figures: its digits are proportional and it has no `tnum` feature, so `tabular-nums` does nothing. Times and prices line up by their cells (a right-aligned column of fixed width), never by the font.
- Neither draws arrows (→, ↓): the "Little ones →" links and the arrows of the two ways in are small inline SVGs in `currentColor`.

Shrikhand comes from the hand-painted shop signs of Gujarat: a fat, slanted, cheerful display face with tight round counters and ball terminals. In Albuquerque it reads like the sign-painted lettering of the Route 66 motor courts, without their neon script, and it is loud in the way a school full of children is loud. Lexend was designed by an educational therapist, Bonnie Shaver-Troup, to make reading easier, with open, even letters drawn so as not to be confused with one another. It suits a site read by parents in a hurry, children learning to read and students in their seventies and eighties. Lexend carries every list, form and price; Shrikhand only headings and big figures.

## Palette

Paper, a warm shade of it, tangerine, an apricot, a rust for small type and a cool night ink. One color dominates, the tangerine, in large flat bands with straight edges: never a gradient, a wave or a diagonal. Contrast was computed for every pair: each value converted from oklch to sRGB, then the WCAG formula; for text with an opacity, the color composited on its ground first.
- **Ink:** on paper 15.5:1, on shade 14.2:1, on apricot 11.4:1, on tangerine 6.8:1, on the border 11.5:1.
- **Ink at `/80`:** on paper 8.3:1, on shade 7.8:1, on apricot 6.8:1. On tangerine it falls to 4.9:1 (and to 4.4:1 at `/75`), so text on tangerine is full ink, or `/85` at least (5.4:1).
- **Rust:** on paper 5.5:1, on shade 5.1:1; paper on rust 5.5:1. Rust on apricot is 4.0:1, so it goes there only at 1.5 rem or more (large text, 3:1).
- **On night:** paper 14.4:1, paper at `/70` 7.8:1, tangerine 6.3:1 (a concert's date on the night band), apricot 10.6:1.

Tangerine on paper is 2.3:1 and on shade 2.1:1: it is a fill on light grounds (the bands, the main buttons with ink text, the ticks' disks, the "This week" tab), never text there. The strings are not text but must be seen (3:1 for graphics): ink at 60% on paper is 4.3:1, ink at 70% on tangerine 4.0:1, paper at 55% on night 5.4:1.

## Motion

Expressive, by the business's character. A music school is noisy in the best way: a dozen lessons behind twelve doors, a teen band in the Carport, the little ones clapping in Room 12. The site moves more than a shop's: the headline's words land, every list is strung and answers the hand, and the page glides. A parent booking a lesson on a phone at a red light still gets a calm, complete page. On a phone nothing answers a pointer, the scroll is the phone's own, and no form ever moves.

Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode nothing moves: Spaka's stillness rule stops CSS animations and transitions and no script runs. Every string lies straight and still, the headline is whole, no row of the calendar is marked, and every text is in place to edit. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too. The libraries are Motion, Lenis and Splitting, pinned on jsDelivr as in the site-scripts skill, all under MIT.

- **The strings**, the signature (`data-strings` on a list, `data-string="1"` to `"4"` on each string, `scripts/strings.js`, Motion's `animate` and `inView`):
  - **the drawing is SVG, in the markup:** `parts/string.liquid` renders `<svg class="string" data-string="2" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false" viewBox="0 0 1000 24" preserveAspectRatio="none">` with a straight `<path d="M0 12 H1000">`, full width, 1.5 rem tall to leave room to swing. Its `stroke` is `currentColor`, with `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke` and `stroke-linecap: round`. Its width is its gauge: 1px, 1.5px, 2px and 3px. Gauge 4 is a wound string: a second path over it with `stroke-dasharray: 1 2` in the ground's color at 45%, the winding of a low string. A second, fainter path, the ghost, lies exactly under the string at 30%. Every ruled list renders its rules with this part, one above each row and one under the last, so with no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode, the strings are simply drawn, straight;
  - **gauges:** 2 unless the list says otherwise. The ages' list goes from 1 (the little ones) to 4 (the grown-ups), by position (`forloop.index`), so the agent never sets it;
  - **a pluck:** the path bends into one smooth curve (`M0 12 Q…`) whose deepest point lies where the string was plucked, kept between a quarter and three quarters of its length. The displacement runs from its amplitude back to 0 on Motion's spring (`animate(amplitude, 0, { type: "spring", stiffness, damping, onUpdate })`), with stiffness 1400, 1100, 850 and 600 and damping 12, 11, 10 and 9 by gauge. A thin string swings about six times a second and settles in 0.67 s; the wound one about four times a second, settling in 0.89 s. While it moves, the ghost takes the mirrored curve at an opacity that fades with the amplitude, so the string shows the lens of a vibrating string, then the two lie as one again;
  - **the strum on arrival:** as a list comes into view (`inView`, a third of it shown), its strings are plucked once each, top to bottom, 60 ms apart, at 30% of their length, 6 px deep (4 px on strings shorter than 480 px, as on a phone). Once per page view;
  - **the hand:** on a fine pointer that hovers (`(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`), one passive `pointermove` listener checks the strings in view (an `IntersectionObserver` keeps the set). When the pointer's path from its last position to this one crosses a string within its length, that string is plucked at the crossing, 2 to 9 px deep by the pointer's vertical speed, in the direction it moved. While the pointer rests over a list and the page scrolls under it, the strings passing under it are plucked too, more gently (2 to 6 px), like a hand held over a harp. A string is plucked at most every 120 ms. On a touch screen, tapping a row plucks the string above it (4 px, where the finger was); with the keyboard, focus moving into a row plucks the string above it (3 px, at 30%). Nothing follows the pointer: a string bends only where it was crossed, and only for a moment;
  - **the hero** (`data-strings-at="load"` on its list): its three strings are strummed 0.55 s after the page opens, after the headline's words, not on view. As each rings, the tick of the promise below it bounces (`scale: [0.7, 1.15, 1]`, 0.45 s, `ease: "easeOut"`);
  - **no sound, ever**, and no color change: a string is the same color moving and still;
  - **`stop`** stops every animation, puts each path back to `M0 12 H1000` and the ghost under it, removes the listeners and disconnects the observers, so `start` strings the same elements again.
- **The hero's words** (`data-split="words"` on the hero's headline, `scripts/words.js`, Splitting and Motion's `animate`): Splitting cuts the headline into words. Each lands `opacity: [0, 1]`, `y: [28, 0]`, `rotate: [-3, 0]`, 0.6 s, `ease: [0.34, 1.4, 0.64, 1]` (a small overshoot, like a downbeat), 70 ms apart, from 0.15 s after load. Only the hero's headline: section headings arrive with the reveals. `start` saves the headline's markup and `stop` puts it back, so the next start splits the maker's text again. With no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode, the headline is whole and still. They land as whole words, once, never letter by letter (Folio) and never lit in turn (Earshot).
- **The school's clock** (`data-term` on a list of dates, `scripts/term-clock.js`, no library): the one script that reads the time. It marks rows and nothing else.
  - Each row carries `data-starts` and, for a span, `data-ends` (ISO dates, as the `dates` records hold them); the list carries the school's time zone in `data-tz` ("America/Denver"; without it, the visitor's own clock).
  - It sets `data-state` on each row: `now` when today falls in it ("This week", or "Today" for a single day), `next` on the first row after today ("Coming up"), `past` on the rows before, which fade to 55%, never hidden. Each row holds its own labels, written in the template; the stylesheet shows the one that matches (`[data-state="now"] [data-when="now"]`). With no script, no row is marked.
  - It writes no sentence, checks again every hour, and `stop` clears the timer and every `data-state`.
  - The concerts' rows carry the same attributes, so a concert already played fades until the maker removes it.
- **Smooth scrolling** (`scripts/smooth-scroll.js`, Lenis): light, `new Lenis({ autoRaf: true, lerp: 0.12, anchors: true })`, so "Book a free first lesson" glides to the form. Touch screens keep their own scroll (Lenis's default), and under reduced motion it does not start. `stop` calls `lenis.destroy()`. Lenis's few CSS rules are copied into `styles/site.css`.
- **Reveals** (`data-reveal`, `scripts/reveal.js`, Motion's `inView`): a section's heading and main elements rise a little (`y: [16, 0]`, `opacity: [0, 1]`, 0.7 s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`), children 70 ms apart under `data-reveal="stagger"`, as in the site-scripts skill. A strung list is not revealed: it is there, and it rings.
- **The forms' prefill** (`scripts/prefill.js`, no library): `?teacher=linh-nguyen` chooses the teacher in the free lesson form, `?program=kids` the program and `?class=guitar-club` the class in the enrollment form, and `?instrument=cello` the instrument in the rental request. It writes no text and moves nothing; with no script the forms start empty and work.
- **Rows under the pointer** (CSS only): a strung row takes the shade ground in 150 ms on hover and focus, under no-preference (at once under reduced motion).
- **The header** is sticky on paper at 95% with a hairline under it, the night strip above it; it does not change as you scroll.

No page transitions, no loader, no WebGL, no carousel (Embla stays out). Because the term's rows follow the clock, `template.json` sets `"shoot": { "clock": "2027-11-10T16:30:00-07:00" }`, a Wednesday afternoon in mid-November after lessons have started. Recital week is "This week" and Thanksgiving week is "Coming up". The pictures are taken once the strings have settled (within 0.9 s).

## Lessons, enrollment and what stays outside

Spaka has no checkout, no payments, no calendar and no embeds, so:
- **a first lesson is a request** (as built): the desk calls back within two school days to set the time; the form and its answer say so;
- **enrollment is a request too:** the desk confirms the time and the teacher, then takes the term's tuition at the desk, by phone or by check, never on the site. A school that uses an outside school management system (My Music Staff, Opus1, TutorBird) links "Enroll" to it instead (see What varies), never embeds it;
- **the calendar is typed facts:** the `dates` records, marked by the school's clock; no calendar widget, no feed;
- **rentals are paid at the desk** when the instrument is fitted;
- **concerts are free** but one, whose tickets are sold at the door; a school selling tickets elsewhere links to that page;
- **no donation widget:** "Give to the Fund" links to the school's outside giving page, if it has one;
- **the time zone:** the term's marks follow the school's clock through `data-tz`; the guide tells the agent to set it from the maker's city;
- **no map, no Instagram feed:** "Get directions" links to the address on a map site, and the school's account is a link in the footer.
