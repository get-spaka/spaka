# Batten: brief

One of wave 5's templates, and its immersive one: the season of a theater, a repertory company that plays six productions a year with one ensemble (`event`). The whole site moves as a show changes scene. Each production is a painted cloth that comes down from above on its batten as the visitor scrolls. It lands at its mark inside a fixed portal in the house color and settles with a slight swing. The next cloth comes down in front of it. Between pages, the next page comes down the same way. On a phone and under reduced motion, the productions are stacked one after another, plain and complete.

`batten` is a working name. A batten is the pipe a cloth hangs from: it is flown in and out on its lines from the fly tower above the stage. A web search found no website template or theme called Batten: Framer's marketplace has no template at that address, ThemeForest's search for the word returns only a roofing kit, Shopify's theme store lists none, and no Webflow or Squarespace template of that name came up. The theater templates sold now are Framer's Stageo, Opening Night and Marquee Cinema, Webflow's Thespo and Theatre 128, and WordPress's Theatre WP, Bard, OsTende and Cultera; Spaka's Marquee and Encore are taken too. The name is the template's, not the business's.

## Concept

**Concept.** A theater's season you watch as you would its stage. The first screen is a black stage under a portal of flat house magenta, with one statement on it. Then the six productions come in one at a time, the way scenery comes in from the flies. Each is a cloth painted in grays like a scenic artist's drop, with its title painted large across its foot, its credit line and its dates. It drops in on its pipe, its lines running up out of sight, lands at its trim, swings a little and is still. The rest of the site is the program and the box office:
- the season at a glance, month by month;
- every production's page, with its cast and credits as a program prints them, its dates, its running time and its advisories;
- an access performance of each kind for every play;
- a subscription asked for with a form;
- the building, the classes and the ways to give.

The site is black and work-light white. One magenta frames everything, and every picture is in black and white. The type is a condensed poster gothic for the titles and a screen serif for reading.

It must not read as Encore, Marquee, Plinth or Beacon. Encore is a band on graphite, with a departure board of shows and covers that fly between pages; Marquee a festival poster in hot pink on tangerine, with stretching letters and diagonal wipes; Plinth a museum's exhibition that steps back from a detail of a painting; Beacon the other dark statement, a violet glow around an app window. Batten is a working stage, the one place where scenery travels up and down on ropes, and it tells its season with that machinery and nothing else.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Bottineau Rep**, the Bottineau Repertory Theater in Northeast Minneapolis, Minnesota. It is named after its neighborhood, which is named after Pierre Bottineau, the Métis guide and surveyor.
- **The building: the Sash Works,** a sash-and-door factory built in 1912 at 212 Sawmill Row NE (an invented street), Minneapolis, Minnesota 55418, between the river and the rail yards. The **Mainstage** (284 seats, an end stage) has a 62-foot fly tower, built into the old boiler house in 2006, with 38 counterweight line sets run by hand; **the Loft** is a 96-seat black box upstairs in the old drying loft; the **paint frame**, a 40-foot wall, is where every cloth is painted; the lobby bar is in the loading dock.
- **The company:** founded in 1998 by seven actors in a storefront on Central Avenue; today an ensemble of **twelve resident actors** on a 36-week contract who play the whole season, with guest directors and designers.
  - **Inés Halvorsen** (she/her), artistic director since 2015 after twenty years acting in Chicago's storefront theaters, directs two plays a season; **Marcus Bell** (he/him) is the managing director; **Ola Bergquist** (they/them), the technical director, runs the rail; **June Thao** (she/her) is director of learning and community.
  - The ensemble includes Teddy Okonkwo, Mae Lindgren, Rosalind Cheng, Samir Haddad, Dagny Moe and Darnell Price.
- **The 2027–28 season**, six productions:
  - **Uncle Vanya** (Chekhov, in a new version by Petra Lund, directed by Halvorsen; Mainstage, September 9 to October 10);
  - **Ice Out** (a world premiere by Wren Haugland: three sisters in their late father's fish house on Mille Lacs, the last weekend before the ice goes out; The Loft, October 21 to November 14);
  - **The Snow Queen** (after Andersen, adapted by the company, for 6 and up; Mainstage, December 2 to 30);
  - **Hedda Gabler** (Ibsen, translated by Arne Vik) and **Short Days** (a new play by Hodan Ismail, set in a Northeast corner bar in January), played **in rep**, on alternating nights on the same stage, from January 20 to March 19. Hedda's cloths fly out and the bar flies in;
  - **Twelfth Night** (Mainstage, April 13 to May 21).
- **The schedule:** Tuesday to Saturday at 7:30 pm, and Saturday and Sunday at 2 pm. The first two performances of each run are previews.
  - Every production has an ASL-interpreted, an audio-described (with a touch tour 75 minutes before) and an open-captioned performance. The Snow Queen and Twelfth Night each also have a relaxed performance.
- **Tickets** are sold through the company's outside box office, online and on (612) 555-0163, open Tuesday to Saturday from noon to 6 pm and from two hours before each performance: previews $25, weeknights $38 to $52, Friday to Sunday $46 to $68, under 30 $20; every Thursday is pay-what-you-can, forty seats at the door from 6:30.
- **Subscriptions,** asked for with a form and set up by phone: the Full Season (all six plays, $186 on weeknights, $252 on Friday and Saturday nights), Pick Four (from $132), Under 30 (all six for $90) and the Flex Pass (six tickets used any way, $240).
- **Learning and community:** the Teen Company (ages 14 to 19, free, auditions in October, a play on the Mainstage in May); student matinees ($12 a student, weekday mornings at 10:30, with a study guide and a talkback); eight-week acting classes for adults ($240, sliding scale); rail tours on the first Saturday of the month, to watch the crew fly a cloth.
- **Support:** giving once on an outside giving page; the Rail Club ($20 a month); sponsoring a line set ($1,000 puts your name on its label at the rail for a season); volunteer ushers. "Tickets pay for 58 cents of every dollar a play costs."

The names were searched: no theater called Bottineau Rep or Bottineau Repertory came up, in Minneapolis or elsewhere, nor any "Sash Works" there; Wikipedia's list of Minnesota's theaters has no company with "Rep" in its name. No template or brief is set in Minneapolis.

The same structure suits a regional or repertory theater, a touring company, a Shakespeare festival, an opera or dance company's season, a children's theater, a puppet or physical theater company, or a community theater with a season of plays. The agent rewrites the productions and their credits, the stages, the prices and packages, the company, the programs and the city.

## Trends it draws on

These come from thirteen theater and dance sites opened in November 2026 at 1440 by 900, five of them rebuilt recently: donmarwarehouse.com (by SomeOne, 2026), druid.ie (by Path, 2025), Nederlander Theatres in London (nederlander.com/london, by Fiasco), lyrictheatre.co.uk in Belfast (by Eyekiller, 2025-26) and pina-bausch.de for Tanztheater Wuppertal (by Convoy, an Awwwards Honorable Mention in June 2024); and ita.nl, malthousetheatre.com.au, colline.fr, operaballet.nl, hebbel-am-ufer.de, volksbuehne.berlin, stannswarehouse.org and ballet.org.uk. Two American companies were read with WebFetch to complete them, steppenwolf.org and seattlerep.org (guthrietheater.org refused the fetch, osfashland.org showed no text). Each trend below was seen on at least two of the sites.

1. **The production's title set like a poster, in a condensed or compressed display face, 72 to 180 px, often in several widths of one family.** donmarwarehouse.com (Fusiona, condensed); ita.nl (Zed Display Semi Condensed at 135 px, with lettering drawn for each show); pina-bausch.de (Origin Super Condensed at 120 to 180 px); Nederlander (Marr Sans Condensed); druid.ie (Diatype Bold at 80 px).
2. **The credit line set as a poster's billing, with the run and a status.** donmarwarehouse.com ("by Danny Lee Wynter", "Until Sat 28 Nov", "On now" or "On next" on each card, with "Book now"); ita.nl ("ITA / June Yanez / Leila Sahir / …", "Nov 25 – Dec 4"); steppenwolf.org ("Now thru Dec 13, 2026"); seattlerep.org ("Begins Nov. 27"); druid.ie and malthousetheatre.com.au.
3. **One house color frames everything, the photos kept neutral.** donmarwarehouse.com (a red frame and a vertical logo rail); pina-bausch.de (violet panels, #4900B8, on black, with black-and-white photos); colline.fr (a mustard header, each production its own flat color); operaballet.nl (lavender and peach bands); druid.ie (black-and-white stage photos).
4. **The calendar given room, with big day numbers.** hebbel-am-ufer.de (a strip of days, "Tue 24"); ita.nl ("25.11 / 20:00 - 21:45 / title / company"); volksbuehne.berlin (a month grid with ticket rows); pina-bausch.de (date cards with the venue, time and price).
5. **One layout shared by every production's page:** pinned tabs (Overview, Booking info, Dates and times) with Book always in reach, a large intro paragraph, the cast with their roles, the credits, then "Also interesting": donmarwarehouse.com (its Ilford Boy page) and ita.nl (its Wuthering Heights page). The access performances sit under the visit pages: seattlerep.org's Accessible Options, steppenwolf.org's Accessible Performances, ita.nl's Accessibility.
6. **The company, learning and giving as doors of their own, beside the season.** steppenwolf.org ("Meet The Ensemble" in its hero; For Educators, For Teens, For Community; Ways to Give); seattlerep.org (Young Rep classes for ages 13 to 20, student matinees, "Subscribe Today"); ita.nl (Education, Support ITA, Friends); donmarwarehouse.com (Take Part, Support Us).

Their movement: none at all on druid.ie, colline.fr, lyrictheatre.co.uk, stannswarehouse.org and hebbel-am-ufer.de; soft on donmarwarehouse.com, malthousetheatre.com.au and operaballet.nl; expressive on Nederlander (Lenis, clip-path wipes upward, a background video), ita.nl (smooth scrolling, every production a full-screen panel, ticker bands, clip-path transitions) and pina-bausch.de (a band of month names, sliding titles, a progress bar between pages). No WebGL anywhere.

The gap is the same on all of them: the productions slide, fade and wipe in like the slides of any carousel, and the pages change as an app's would. None uses the machinery the theater itself uses to change a scene: a cloth flown in from above, landing at its trim inside a portal that never moves. Batten turns trend 3's frame into that portal, trend 1's titles into the painting on each cloth, and the expressive sites' panels and page wipes into the flies.

Common and left outside: an embedded ticketing widget or seat map (Spektrix, Tessitura, AudienceView), a basket and an account; a trailer playing as the hero's background (Nederlander, malthousetheatre.com.au); press quotes as headlines and star ratings (donmarwarehouse.com, stannswarehouse.org), since a template's would be invented and a play not yet opened has none; a rotating hero slideshow (donmarwarehouse.com, ita.nl); an Instagram feed; a newsletter pop-up.

**What makes it its own:**
- **the portal:** a show portal built as a stage's is, flat, in the house color: a header piece across the top that holds the wordmark and the links, and two legs down the sides of every page. It has no arch, no sill, no ornament and no curtain. It never moves, while the cloths and pages travel behind it;
- **the cloths:** each production is a cloth.
  - Its painting is a grisaille, painted in grays as scenic artists paint a drop, shown in black and white.
  - Its title is painted across its foot in a condensed gothic, its credit line set as a billing.
  - It hangs from a black pipe that runs past its edges, with its three pick-up lines rising out of sight.
  - It drops in as the visitor scrolls, lands at its mark, swings a little and settles.
  - At the stage's foot, the rail numbers the six cues with their titles, like the labels of the line sets on a fly rail;
- **the next page coming in from the flies:** a link followed lowers the next page in front of the last, inside the portal, with the same swing. It does not rise from below (Chalk), wipe sideways (Reel), cut through black (Ember) or fly a cover across (Encore);
- **the run:** each production's tag ("On now", "Next", "On sale", "Closed") is chosen by the date from four written in the template, and the season at a glance marks today with a hairline;
- **the season at a glance:** the months from September to May as columns, each production a magenta bar from its opening to its closing, the two in rep side by side. It answers "what's on when" in one look;
- **access first:** a table of every production's ASL, audio-described, captioned and relaxed performances, in its own band on the season page and again on each production's page, with what each one is on the visit page;
- **the program:** a production's cast and credits set as a printed program sets them, the role and the actor, the job and the name, on muslin, the one light band of the page.

**What it leaves out:**
- online checkout, seat maps, an embedded box office, a basket, payments, a calendar widget, a map embed, a video trailer, sound, an Instagram feed, a newsletter pop-up, press quotes and stars;
- the theater's clichés: red velvet curtains or any curtain that parts or draws, a gold proscenium, comedy and tragedy masks, spotlight beams, marquee bulbs and the ghost light, perforated ticket stubs, Art Deco gold on black, a Playbill script, a countdown to curtain-up, rows of red seats or an applauding audience, "experience the magic of live theater";
- **other templates' devices:**
  - a departure board of shows, a disc sliding from its sleeve, covers flying between pages (Encore);
  - letters stretching on their width axis, stickers, halftone, diagonal wipes (Marquee): Batten's titles keep the width they are set at;
  - a page rising from below, a line drawn down the page (Chalk); anything scrolling sideways, a cursor disc, a sideways wipe (Reel); a light carried by the pointer, a fade through black, a huge wordmark across the footer (Ember); photos opening from pleats, cross-fades (Seam);
  - stepping back from a detail on a sticky hero (Plinth): Batten's pinned first screen on a production's page rises away, it does not zoom; a sheer drawing aside (Linen): Batten's cloths only ever travel up and down;
  - an aperture glow (Beacon); an italic accent word in a heading (Corner, Folio, Linen); a status dot or a card turning open or closed (Saucer); bars of hours on a week chart (Taper): the season at a glance spans months and productions, never hours;
- WebGL and split text.

## Type

Both fonts are files of the template, as `woff2` subset to Latin and Latin Extended (for names such as Bottineau, Halvorsen and Bergquist, and for makers in French or Polish), under the SIL Open Font License, from Google Fonts, declared in `styles/site.css`.
- **League Gothic** ships as one variable file: weight 400 only, a width axis from 75 to 100, no italic, Latin and Latin Extended.
- **Spectral** ships as static files, 300, 400, 500 and 600 with their italics, Latin and Latin Extended. It has small caps (`smcp`, `c2sc`) and lining, oldstyle and tabular figures.

| Section headings | League Gothic | width 88, uppercase, `clamp(2.5rem, 4vw + 1rem, 4.5rem)`, `leading-[0.92]` |

League Gothic is a revival of the American wood-type gothics of the early twentieth century, the face of playbills and boxing posters. It is drawn in caps and fits a title the length of *The Snow Queen* across a cloth. Its width axis does in one free family what the trend sites do with commercial condensed faces. Spectral is a serif drawn for reading on screens, sober and literary, with the small caps a credit line needs. A program's cast list reads in it as it would in print.

Ruled out:
- every font already used by a template, among them Big Shoulders (Encore), Anybody (Marquee), Archivo (Reel) and Bodoni Moda (Ember);
- every font of the wave sheet;
- Anton, Bebas Neue and Oswald, the condensed faces of every second theater and cinema site, which would make it any of them;
- script faces, Art Deco faces and the "Playbill" lettering of the clichés.

None of the 28 templates, nor any brief, uses League Gothic or Spectral.

**If a sibling takes them,** two pairings keep the same voice, a condensed poster sans over a reading serif:
- **Sofia Sans Extra Condensed** (variable, weight 1 to 1000, with italics) with **Alegreya** (variable, 400 to 900, with italics);
- **Saira** at its narrowest widths (variable, width 50 to 125, weight 100 to 900, with italics) with **Crimson Pro** (variable, 200 to 900, with italics).

All four are on Google Fonts under the OFL, with Latin Extended, and none is used by the inventory or the wave sheet.

## Palette

Stage black, work-light white, a house magenta, a pale tint of it, and the color of raw muslin, the cotton cloths are painted on. Every pair of text and background passes WCAG AA, measured from these values (oklch converted to sRGB, then the WCAG formula):
- work-light white on stage black is 17.3:1, on the wings 15.7:1;
- `text-base-content/70` on stage black is 8.6:1, and `/60` is 6.5:1;
- house magenta on stage black is 5.5:1, on the wings 5.0:1, on flies black 5.7:1;
- stage black on house magenta is 5.5:1;
- the pale pink on stage black is 12.6:1;
- stage black on muslin is 13.8:1, and at 70% it is 6.4:1;
- a cloth's texts in white over its scrim, where it is thinnest (60% of the surface), are 10.1:1 on a mid-gray painting and 4.6:1 on pure white; where the credit line and buttons stand (80% and more), 14:1 and 9.8:1.

Work-light white on magenta is 3.2:1: white is never text on the house color, only stage black is. Magenta on muslin is 2.5:1: on muslin, buttons and links are stage black. The pale pink on magenta is 2.3:1, so it is never used there.

Other settings:

**Pictures are black and white** (`.bw` on every picture's frame, `filter: grayscale(1) contrast(1.05)` in `styles/site.css`): the cloths, the photos and the portraits, so that the house color is the only color on the site, as trend 3's companies keep theirs. **The cloths' scrim** is made from the surface (`linear-gradient(to top, var(--color-base-100), color-mix(in oklch, var(--color-base-100), transparent 40%) 35%, transparent 65%)`). **The weave of the hero's black cloth** is a faint `repeating-linear-gradient` of the surface and the surface alt. So the Theme tab restyles all of it.

It is not Marquee's hot pink (h 352) on tangerine, Coteau's deep grape (h 330) on light paper, Kindly's lilac liner, nor Beacon's violet glow on blue-black. It is a bright, flat magenta on true stage black, the only color of the site.

## Motion

**Immersive**: the whole site moves the way a show changes scene. Scenery comes down from the flies and lands inside a portal that never moves, and the next page comes in the same way. The web-design skill's "one orchestrated moment a page" does not hold. Yet:
- every page reads on a phone, where nothing is pinned;
- everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`: under `reduce` nothing flies and nothing swings; the productions are stacked, and the tags and today's line are still marked;
- in edit mode no script runs and Spaka's stillness rule holds CSS animations: the hero's statement is in place, the cloths are stacked one after another, each complete with its title, credits, run and buttons, no tag is chosen and no line marks today.

Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too. The libraries are Motion and Lenis, pinned on jsDelivr as in the site-scripts skill, both under MIT. There is no WebGL and no Splitting.

- **The flies**, the signature (`data-flies` on the season section, `data-flies-stage` on what sticks, `data-cloth` on each production, `data-flies-rail` on the list of cues; `scripts/flies.js`, Motion's `scroll` and `animate`):
  - **the cloth, in CSS:**
    - its painting (`object-cover`, `.bw`) fills it, the scrim lies over its foot, and the title, credit line, run, tag and buttons stand on the scrim, at the left edge;
    - its pipe (`data-batten`) is a bar of the neutral 0.4 rem tall, 1.25 rem wider than the cloth on each side;
    - its three pick-up lines rise from the pipe to the top of the stage, 1 px of the border color, as pseudo-elements;
    - its hem at the foot is 0.5 rem of the surface alt;
  - **as built in CSS, the stack:** the cloths stand one after another, each a full-width panel between the legs (`min-h-[85svh]` from 1024 px, its painting at 4:5 above its texts on a phone), every one complete. This is what shows with no script, in edit mode, under reduced motion and below 1024 px;
  - **flying, from 1024 px under no-preference:**
    - the script sets `data-flying` on the section and gives it the height of one screen per cloth;
    - the stage sticks under the portal's header, one screen tall less the header, and is clipped at its top edge, so a cloth above it is out of sight in the flies;
    - the cloths stand in it on top of one another, 1 rem inside each leg, so that the black of the wings shows beside them. The first is at its trim and the others are up in the flies (`y: "-104%"`);
  - **a cue a cloth:**
    - Motion's `scroll` reads the section's progress, and each equal part of it is a cue;
    - when the visitor scrolls into the next cue, its cloth flies in, in front of the last: `y: ["-104%", "0%"]`, 0.85 s, `ease: [0.3, 0, 0.15, 1]`, the way a flyman hauls a line set fast and brakes it to its trim;
    - then it settles, swinging about its pipe (`transform-origin: 50% 0`): `rotate: [0, 0.8, -0.45, 0.2, 0]` with the hem a little late (`y: ["0%", "0.6%", "0%"]`), 1.2 s, `ease: "easeOut"`. The pick-up lines swing with it;
    - the cloth behind dims (`filter: brightness(0.55)`, 0.5 s) and, once covered, is hidden (`visibility: hidden`, `inert`);
    - scrolling back, the front cloth flies out (`y: ["0%", "-104%"]`, 0.6 s, `ease: [0.5, 0, 0.75, 0]`) and the one behind brightens;
    - a visitor who scrolls past several cues at once sees only the last one fly in; the others are set at their trim without moving;
  - **the rail:** at the stage's foot, on the wings' color, the cues are numbered with their titles ("1 Uncle Vanya … 6 Twelfth Night"), the current one in magenta. In the stack they are plain links to each cloth's `id`. While flying, a click scrolls to its cue (Lenis's `scrollTo`), and a link focused by the keyboard on a cloth still up in the flies brings its cue in;
  - **held from outside:** as `reel.js` does in Reel, the script returns the section to the stack the moment its stage is held in place from outside, as a full-page capture does, so every picture shows all six cloths. It measures again on resize and when late images arrive;
  - **`stop`** removes `data-flying`, the height, the styles and `inert` it set, its listeners and its `scroll`.
- **The statement flown in** (`data-fly-in="load"` on the home's hero cloth, the same script):
  - once the page has painted, the black cloth of the statement comes down on its pipe and settles, with the same motion as a cloth, 0.2 s after load;
  - it is the first thing a visitor sees move: the house cloth in, before the season;
  - with no script it hangs at its trim.
- **The cloth flown out** (`data-fly-out` on a production page's first screen, the same script, Motion's `scroll`):
  - from 1024 px, the cloth of the production holds the first screen; as the visitor scrolls the first 70% of a screen, it rises into the flies (`y: 0` to `-100%`, linked to the scroll), its lines with it, and uncovers the overview standing on the black behind it;
  - with no script, under reduced motion, on a phone and in edit mode, it is a first screen like any other, followed by the overview.
- **The next page's cloth** (page transitions, CSS in `styles/site.css`, under no-preference):
  - `@view-transition { navigation: auto; }`;
  - the portal keeps still: the header and the legs carry `view-transition-name: portal-header` and `portal-legs`. The legs are drawn twice in one place: as the borders of the page's frame, so that every full-page picture and the page with no script show them full length, and as a fixed `aria-hidden` element named for the transition, which covers them. The two pages' pictures are clipped inside the legs (`clip-path: inset(0 var(--leg))`), so nothing of them swings past the portal;
  - `::view-transition-new(root)` comes down from above, in front of the old page: `translate: 0 -100%` to `0` over the first 60% of 0.95 s, `cubic-bezier(0.3, 0, 0.15, 1)`, then swings, `rotate: 0.4deg`, `-0.15deg` and `0`, about its top edge, in the remaining 40%;

## Tickets and what stays outside

Spaka has no checkout, no payments, no seat map, no calendar and no embeds, so:
