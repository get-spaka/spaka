# Earshot: brief

One of wave 3's original designs: the podcast (`blog`, one of the original, animated designs). Earshot is a working name.

**Why `blog`:** a podcast is a body of dated episodes, the newest first, each with its own page of notes, filed by topic, with an archive behind it. That is a blog's shape, with audio where the articles would be. `portfolio` shows finished work, `event` one dated event, and `business` a place or a service. A maker looking for a site for their show looks among the blogs.

**The name** was searched: no website template or theme called Earshot came up among the podcast templates sold by ThemeIsle, Dorik, Colorlib, HubSpot or the WordPress theme lists. The best-known Earshot is Earshot Jazz, a Seattle nonprofit, which is not a template.

## Concept

**Concept.** A podcast's site that you can almost hear. Its quotes are not set as quotes but as captions: the words light up one after another at the pace of the voice that said them, a yellow caption marker running under the word being spoken, with the speaker's name and the minute it was said. Each episode has its own sound print, a waveform drawn by the page from the episode's number, in the episode's own color. It ripples when you point at it and is cut into the episode's chapters on its own page. Section titles are set like subtitles, on dark boxes, line by line. A small on-air lamp in the header lights up on release day. Where to listen is always one tap away, on every episode.

**The fictional show** its texts are written for is **Say Again?**, a weekly podcast about how people talk, made in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania.
- **The hosts:** Dr. Marisol Ibarra, a sociolinguist who teaches at a university in the city, and Teddy Kowal, a former public radio producer who asks the questions everyone else is too embarrassed to ask. Hana Sato produces and edits the show.
- **The studio:** the old switchboard room of a 1914 telephone exchange in Lawrenceville, where the show has recorded since March 2021.
- **The show:** a new episode every Tuesday at 6 am, about 40 minutes long; 142 episodes so far. Each takes one thing people say without thinking ("yinz", "um", the angry period at the end of a text, baby talk, names nobody can spell) and finds out where it came from, usually with a guest who studies it. Every fourth week is the Mailbag, built from listeners' questions and voicemails.
- **The topics:** Accents & dialects, Words & origins, Kids & learning, Texting & tech, Names, and the Mailbag.
- **Listeners take part:** a question form on the site, and a voicemail line, (412) 555-0142, whose best messages are played on the show.
- **Members:** the Party Line, $5 a month or $50 a year, on the show's membership page: an ad-free feed, a bonus episode every month, first tickets to the live tapings, and their name in the credits once a year.
- **Live:** two live tapings a year at a theater in the city.
- **Where it is heard:** Apple Podcasts, Spotify, YouTube, Pocket Casts, Overcast and its RSS feed.

The name was searched: no podcast called "Say Again?" came up, among the linguistics shows (Lingthusiasm, Because Language and Linguistics After Dark are real ones, and this show borrows nothing from them). Pittsburgh is used by no other template. The guests are invented, as are their books and papers.

The same structure suits an interview show of any subject (business, design, science, food, sports), a narrative or documentary series in seasons, a company's or a nonprofit's podcast, a church's weekly sermons, a local radio show's site, a book club or an audio drama, or a talk show that lives on YouTube. The agent rewrites the episodes, the topics, the hosts, the quotes and the ways to listen.

Two of them, rectangles.fm and dive.club, lead with video: the episode is a YouTube conversation first. The template keeps that as a choice (below).

**What makes it its own:**
- **the read-along:** a quote is played as a caption. Its words light up one after another at the pace of speech, with longer pauses at commas and periods and a yellow caption marker under the word being spoken. Beside the speaker's name, a small level meter moves while they talk. None of the sites does it; it turns a site that cannot play sound into one you read the way you would hear it;
- **the conversation:** the hosts' portraits sit in round frames like a video call's. A short exchange between them plays as a read-along, line by line, and the ring of whoever is speaking pulses with their voice;
- **sound prints:** every episode has a waveform of its own, drawn by the page from its number, so no two look alike and an episode needs no picture. It ripples under the pointer, and on the episode's page it is cut into its chapters;
- **caption boxes:** section titles are set like subtitles, each line on its own dark box;
- **the on-air lamp:** a lamp in the header that lights on release day ("On air today: episode 142"), and otherwise says when the next one comes. It is a small touch, not the signature: Taper, briefed at the same time, has a barber pole that turns only while the shop is open, also read from the clock. If the author wants that idea in one template only, the lamp stays an outlined dot beside "New episodes every Tuesday", and `scripts/on-air.js` goes.

**What it leaves out:**
- an audio player of its own, autoplay and a sound toggle: Upstart serves no audio, and sound that starts by itself sends visitors away (see the end of this brief);
- scroll-jacking (CTRL SHIFT's), a headphone cable or microphone drawn as the site's emblem, a wall of episode tiles as the hero (Acquired's), and a giant wordmark across the footer (Corner's, Ember's and Seam's);
- **other templates' devices:** a ticker (Corner's), letters that stretch, flip or rise one by one (Marquee's, Encore's board), anything pinned while the page scrolls or scrolling sideways (Ember's, Pocket's, Reel's), a line drawing itself as the page scrolls (Chalk's), a reading-progress rule (Margin's), a picture following the cursor (Folio's), WebGL, and page transitions (Chalk, Seam, Ember, Reel and Encore each have theirs).

## Type

All three families are files of the template, in `fonts/` with their license files, from Google Fonts, under the SIL Open Font License. None is used by an existing template.
- **Parkinsans** (variable, 300 to 800; Red Stone for the charity Parkinson's UK, 2024): a round, bold grotesque with clipped terminals, loud and friendly at poster size.
- **Atkinson Hyperlegible Next** (variable, 200 to 800, with its italic; Braille Institute, 2025): a text face drawn so that no two letters can be confused. Captions are made to be read at a glance, so the read-alongs use it bold.
- **Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono** (variable; 2025): its mono, for timecodes, episode numbers and speakers' names.

No class name is built in Liquid. The Theme tab recolors every episode with the rest.

## Motion

Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode no script runs: every word of every read-along is lit, no marker shows, the rings are still, the sound prints stand at full height and the lamp shows the usual line. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too. The libraries are Motion, Splitting and Lenis, pinned on jsDelivr as in the site-scripts skill, all under MIT; there is no WebGL.

- **The read-along**, the signature (`data-readalong` on a quote's text; `scripts/readalong.js`, Splitting by words and Motion's `inView`):
  - **setting up:** the script keeps the element's markup, splits it into words, and dims every word (opacity 0.3, set by the script);
  - **as it comes into view,** the words light one after another. Each word stays current for 110 ms plus 55 ms a letter, with 250 ms more after a comma and 450 ms after a period, a question mark or a dash, which gives the pace of someone talking. The current word carries `data-now`: a caption marker in the secondary color slides under it (a `::before` box whose position eases from word to word);
  - **the speaker's meter:** a speaker label holding `data-level` (three small bars, `aria-hidden`) gets `data-speaking` while its quote plays; the bars move in CSS, at uneven heights, like a voice;
  - **at the end,** every word is lit and the marker is gone. It plays once a visit; pointing at a played quote, or focusing it, plays it again;
  - **with no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode:** the quote as written, every word lit;
  - **`stop`** puts back the markup it kept and removes its observers, so the maker's text is never changed.
- **The conversation** (`data-conversation` on a container, `data-turn="<speaker>"` on each line and `data-voice="<speaker>"` on each portrait; the same module):
  - the lines are read-alongs played one after another, a short pause between them;
  - while a line plays, the portrait whose `data-voice` matches its speaker gets `data-speaking`: its ring, in the accent color, grows and shrinks a few percent at uneven intervals, like a call's active speaker; the other portrait dims a little;
  - **with no script and in edit mode,** the lines as written and both rings still.
- **The on-air lamp** (`data-on-air="tuesday"` in the header; `scripts/on-air.js`):
  - the header holds both lines, "New episodes every Tuesday" and "On air today: episode 142";
  - on the release day, in the visitor's time, the script sets `data-live`: the lamp fills with the primary color and glows slowly (CSS, under no-preference), and the script shows the second line and hides the first. It never writes a text;
  - **with no script and in edit mode,** the lamp is an outlined dot beside "New episodes every Tuesday".
- **Sound prints** (`data-soundprint`; drawn by `parts/soundprint.liquid`, moved in CSS):
  - **the drawing:** the part draws 64 bars (40 on a phone, in a smaller part) from the episode's number, each bar's height computed in Liquid from the number and the bar's place, with quiet stretches every ten to fourteen bars, so it looks like speech and never changes between two renderings. Each bar is a `<i>` with `style="--h: 42; --i: 7"`, numbers only, in the episode's `--tone`. The agent never draws one by hand: a new episode has its print as soon as it has a number;
  - **pointing at an episode** (hover or focus on its row or card): its bars ripple, each a little after its neighbor (`animation-delay: calc(var(--i) * -40ms)`), under no-preference;
  - **coming into view** on an episode's page, the bars grow from a tenth of their height, left to right (`data-reveal-bars`, Motion's `stagger`); with no script they stand at full height;
  - **the chapters:** on an episode's page, the print is cut into its chapters by thin gaps, each segment as wide as the chapter is long. Pointing at a chapter in the list lights its segment, and pointing at a segment lights its chapter, with `:has()` and no script.
- **`data-reveal`:** sections' main elements rise into view (`y: [24, 0]`, opacity, 0.7 s), as in the site-scripts skill.
- **Smooth scrolling:** Lenis, light (`lerp: 0.12`).

## What varies

This is the guide's `## Pages` in short: each page type's choices, the first one recommended, and what goes with the page when the maker leaves it out. The release day, the hosts' names, the price of membership and the episodes themselves are facts, not choices: the agent asks for them.

### Home (`/`)

Its choices are for the whole website.

**How do visitors play an episode?**
- **On the platforms** (as built): each Play button opens the episode on the main platform (`listen`'s first link, or the show's), and the listen row gives the others. Nothing plays on the site.
- **With your host's player on each episode's page:** each episode gains a `player` field, the address of the episode's player that the maker's podcast host gives to embed (ask for it, or leave the field empty until given). The episode's page shows it under the head, in a rounded frame; the Play buttons elsewhere lead to that page.
- **Video first, on YouTube:** each episode gains a `video` link and its `cover` becomes the video's picture. Play becomes "Watch", the hero's frame shows the latest picture, the listen row puts YouTube first, and the frame's corner reads "WATCH · 44 MIN".

**Who is on the mic?**
- **Two hosts** (as built): the conversations alternate between them, and the hero's captions are an exchange.
- **One host:** the conversations become one host's lines, or a host and a guest from the latest episode. One ring is shown, and the hero's captions are the host's line alone.
- **A panel of three or four:** the rings sit in a row, the conversations go round them, and About lists them all.

**How does the show pay for itself?**
- **Members** (as built): the Party Line on the home page, the Listen page and the header, linked to the membership page (ask its address, or `#` until given).
- **Sponsors:** the membership sections go, each episode gains `sponsor` and `sponsor_link` fields shown as "Supported by…" under its notes, and work with us leads with "Sponsor the show".
- **Neither:** the membership sections, the header's link and the perks go.

### Episodes (`/episodes`)

**What kind of show is it?**
- **A guest each episode** (as built): each episode names its guest and their role, in the rows, the latest episode and its page.
- **A series in seasons:** each episode gains a `season` number, the archive is grouped by season with "S2 · E4" in the mono, the topic filters become season chips, and the guest fields go.
- **The hosts alone:** the guest fields go, and each row shows the topic and a line from the episode instead.

**Transcripts?**
- **On each episode's page** (as built): the `transcript` field, folded under "Read the transcript".
- **Elsewhere:** the field becomes a `transcript_link` to the host's transcript page.
- **None:** the field and its section go.

**When left out** (a show that keeps its archive on its host): delete `pages/episodes/` and its navigation record. The home page keeps its latest episode and recent rows, which link to the episodes on the main platform; the quotes link there too.

### About (`/about`)

**How do guests, sponsors and press reach you?**
- **One form with a reason** (as built): requests arrive in the Content tab.
- **By email:** the form and `inquiries` go, and three addresses are given by purpose (guests, sponsors, press).
- **Guest suggestions only:** the reason goes, and the form asks who, why and how to reach them.

**When left out:** delete the page, its parts, `inquiries`, `about-steps`, `about-conversation` and its navigation record. The home page's hosts section keeps its portraits and conversation, without its link; the producer leaves `team`.

### Ask us (`/ask`)

**How do listeners send their questions?**
- **The form and a voicemail line** (as built).
- **The form only:** the voicemail number goes from the page, the home page and the footer.
- **Voicemail only:** the form and `questions` go; the number stays, large, with "Up to two minutes".

**When left out:** delete the page, its parts, `questions`, `ask-answered` and `ask-faq`, and its navigation record. The home page's ask band and the footer's voicemail line go, and the Mailbag's rows keep their envelope.

### Listen (`/listen`)

Its Party Line follows how the show pays for itself, chosen with the home page.

**Do you tape live?**
- **Yes, a few times a year** (as built): "Hear it live" lists the tapings with a ticket link each.
- **No:** the section and `tapings` go, and members' first tickets leave the perks.

**When left out:** delete the page, its parts, `tapings`, `listen-perks` and `listen-faq`, and its navigation record. The header's Listen pill keeps its list of platforms, "All the ways to listen" goes, and the footer keeps the platforms and the newsletter.

## What stays outside Upstart

- **Audio:** Upstart serves no audio and its media library takes none (decision Q169), and the template check refuses `<audio>`, `<video>` and `<iframe>` from another site in the template's own files. The episodes live on the maker's podcast host, which makes the RSS feed the apps read; the site links to them. A host's player can be embedded only by the maker's own address in an episode's `player` field (the choice above), never in the template as built.
- **The feed:** the site does not make the podcast's RSS feed; it shows its address.
- **Membership:** payment and the ad-free feed are the membership platform's; the site links to its page.
- **The voicemail line:** a phone number from a service the maker already has; the site shows it as a `tel:` link.
- **Transcripts and chapters:** written or exported by the maker's host or editing tool and pasted into the records; nothing is fetched.
- **Downloads and listener numbers, reviews in the apps:** not live. The reviews are records the maker copies in.
