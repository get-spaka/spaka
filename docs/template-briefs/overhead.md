# Overhead: brief

One of wave 5's templates: the independent coworking space, one floor of desks, small offices and two meeting rooms in an old building, sold by the day, the month and the hour (`business`). Its motion is expressive and useful. The floor is drawn from above, and the rooms a plan opens light up one after another from the elevator when a visitor picks that plan. The first screen shows the building's front with the day pass's price, the address, the hours and the bus stop set on it, its floor's windows switching on as the page opens. A walk across the floor is dragged by a thumb, and the scrolling is lightly smoothed.

`overhead` is a working name: the floor seen from overhead, as the plan draws it, and the overhead that a desk by the day saves a one-person business. The wave sheet named it Annex, but ThemeForest sells "Annex - Responsive Bootstrap 4 Admin Dashboard" (by Mannat-Themes, $28, still on sale), an HTML template, so the brief takes the nearest free name. A search found no template or theme called Overhead on Framer (its marketplace address answers "Template unavailable", as it does for any unknown name), Webflow, ThemeForest (its search for "overhead" lists two unrelated items), Squarespace or Shopify. Mezzanine was weighed and is free too; Floorplate was set aside, sold on Framer for a residential building. Framer and Webflow sell coworking templates called CoSpace, Deskfolk, Floffice, FlowSpace, Nexus Coworking, Planta, Mullion, CoOffice, Union Space, Arch Space and WorkScape: none has this name or this idea. The name is the template's, not the business's.

## Concept

**Concept.** A coworking floor read the way its members learned it on their first morning: from the elevator, room by room. The site is bright and practical, drawn in fine ink lines on warm paper, with one color, a window cyan, kept for light:
- in the first screen, the lit windows of the fourth floor on the building's front, beside the day pass's price, the address, the hours and the bus stop;
- then the floor seen from above, with its hot desks by the river windows, its bench of fixed desks, six small offices, two meeting rooms, four phone booths and the kitchen's long table. Picking a plan switches on the rooms it opens; pointing at a room says what it holds and what it costs.

A plain Dutch-style serif speaks for the place, a friendly grotesk does the reading, and a mono sets the facts, the prices and the plan's labels. Availability is never live: the offices say what the maker typed, and when ("Offices as of Monday, November 16"), and the hot desks say plainly that they are first come. The direction is "practical, bright and plainly drawn".

It must not read as Decal, which draws a week of parking bays from above on asphalt, with road letters, hatching and a truck that parks; nor Trestle, a joiner's measured drawing with dimension lines and their figures along photos; nor Saucer and Taper, local shops whose first screens say whether they are open. Overhead draws a floor plan the way an architect draws one for a lease (walls, doors, columns and desks, never a measure written on it), and its light means "this is yours with this plan", never "open now".

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Sample Floor**, an independent coworking space on the fourth floor of the old Holloway furniture works, 61 Tailrace Street NW (an invented street), Grand Rapids, Michigan 49504, on the West Side, a block from the Grand River and the Blue Bridge.
- **The place:** the Holloway Furniture Company (invented) built its works in 1906 and kept the top floor for samples: twice a year, in January and July, buyers from across the country walked between the season's chairs, desks and sideboards up here and wrote their orders (the city's furniture market ran from 1878 to 1965). The company closed in 1962; the floor was a printer's stockroom, then empty for twenty years. It is 120 by 64 feet, with tall windows on three sides, a maple floor and twelve cast-iron columns. The ground floor has a bike room and the river door; a roof deck is open from May to September.
- **The people:** **Lena Brouwer** (she/her) designed office desks and chairs for twelve years at one of the city's office-furniture makers; she leased the floor in 2019, sanded the maple and filled it with floor models and samples from local showrooms, so almost every desk here was a sample once. **Andre Whitfield** (he/him), a former branch librarian, runs the events and Thursday's show and tell. **Rosa Ibarra** (she/her) keeps the front table, the rooms and the mail on weekdays. **Sam Okafor** (they/them) looks after the Wi-Fi, the fobs and the elevator's moods.
- **The floor:** the river room, 28 hot desks under the east windows; the bench row, 12 fixed desks along the north windows; six offices on the south side (two for 2 people, three for 4, one for 6); the Showroom (14 seats, among the old columns) and the Sample Room (5); four phone booths; the kitchen with a long table for 12; the front table by the elevator.
- **The hours:** the front table is staffed and day passes run from 8 am to 6 pm, Monday to Friday; full-time, fixed-desk and office members come in at any hour with their fob, weekends and holidays included.
- **What it sells:** a day pass, $25, paid at the front table, no booking needed for one or two people; Part-time, $155 a month for ten days in staffed hours; Full-time, $265 a month, a hot desk any day, any hour; a fixed desk, $365 a month on the bench row, with a locker and a mailing address; an office, $790 a month for 2 people, $1,250 for 4 and $1,690 for 6, lockable and furnished, with the business's name on the directory downstairs. Every plan but the day pass includes 2, 5, 8 or 12 meeting-room hours a month; all are month to month, with 30 days' notice.
- **Rooms by the hour,** for anyone, members or not: the Showroom, $45 an hour, $160 a half day, $290 a day, with a screen, a video bar and a whiteboard wall; the Sample Room, $25 an hour or $90 a half day; the whole floor on evenings and Saturdays, for workshops and launches, from $650, 80 standing or 50 seated.
- **Events and the floor:** every week, coffee at 9 on Mondays, a lunch walk along the river on Wednesdays, show and tell at 5:30 on Thursdays; a few dated events a month (a freelancers' tax hour with a CPA, a holiday potluck, an open floor day when a first desk is free, a grant-writing workshop for small studios); about sixty members, from fourteen businesses in the offices and on the bench row.

The name was searched: no coworking space or other business called Sample Floor came up, in Grand Rapids or elsewhere, nor a Holloway Building or a Tailrace Street in the city; the real coworking spaces found there have other names (The Factory, Spaces, Regus). No template, and no other row of the wave sheet, is set in Grand Rapids. The phone in the texts is (616) 555-0162, a number reserved for fiction. The city's facts are real: the furniture market, The Rapid's Laker Line and its Pew Campus stop, the Blue Bridge (the old railroad bridge, now for walking) and the ZIP code.

The same structure suits an independent coworking space or a shared office floor, a café with desks upstairs, a building that rents desks and rooms to makers, a writers' room, a small-town business center, a nonprofit hub sharing an office, or rooms by the hour with a few desks. The agent rewrites the floor (its rooms and their sizes), the plans and their prices, the rooms by the hour, the events, the building, the people and the city.

## Trends it draws on

These come from sixteen coworking sites opened in November 2026: single-site independents (Llum in Valencia, The Rumbl on the Mornington Peninsula, Indie Desk in downtown Los Angeles, Anticafé in Strasbourg, Desk Space in Sydney, Mountain Club in Queenstown, Primary in New York) and small groups (Patch, Second Home, The Commons, Runway East, betahaus). The design galleries hold few coworking sites: Awwwards' newest, Llum, is from May 2024. Indie Desk and Llum were opened again with WebFetch for this brief, to confirm their first screens and look for a floor plan. Each trend was seen on at least two of the sites.

1. **Prices up front, often in the first screen.** indiedesk.com ("Day pass · $20" as the hero's second button, beside a booking card asking "What brings you in?" with the tabs Work today, Work here monthly, Host something and Visit first; plans from $150 to $750 a month); llumcoworking.com ("See prices" first; a day at €25, desks from €205, offices from €480, rooms from €18 an hour); runwayea.st (a sentence to fill in, "offices in [London] for [8 people]", with a slider); thecommons.com.au ($80 a month with "Buy now"); anticafe.eu (its prices in a ticker).
2. **The practical facts set as design, not left to the footer.** llumcoworking.com ("Since 2018 · Calle Colón 10, Valencia · 24/7 access" under its buttons); indiedesk.com (a top strip with its tower, "Suite 3310" and its phone, the 33rd floor named in the hero, "Public hours 9a–6p" in its checklist); thecommons.com.au (the tram lines on each location's card).
3. **Several doors in the first screen, rooms and events level with desks.** therumbl.com (Work or Play); morning.fr (Bureaux, Événementiel, Salles); patch.work and Primary (Coworking, Offices, Meetings, Events, four up); indiedesk.com (Host something among its tabs, and conference rooms by the hour from $50 to $125); llumcoworking.com (rooms for 8 and 10, a soundproof booth at €10 an hour).
4. **Warm paper grounds with one strong color.** llumcoworking.com (cream #fbf6ea and a whole ground of mustard); indiedesk.com (cream, brown and red); therumbl.com (bone and tomato red); thecommons.com.au (#f6f4f0 and sage); anticafe.eu (terracotta).
5. **A plain serif beside a mono "utility" voice.** thecommons.com.au (Chapman with Circular); Mountain Club (PP Fragment, with Roboto Mono for its menu and labels); secondhome.io (Avenir with Courier).

The gap is the same everywhere. Every site describes its floor in cards and photos (a hot desk, a dedicated desk, a private office for 1 to 4), each beside a stock interior. None shows the floor itself, though a newcomer's first questions are about space: where would I sit, is the fixed desk by a window, how far is the phone booth, which rooms does my plan open? Floor plans do exist, but inside the operators' booking software (nexudus.com, spacebring.com, optixapp.com, deskbird.com), behind a member's login, as tools for booking a desk live; neither indiedesk.com nor llumcoworking.com shows one. Overhead puts the plan on the public page as a drawing: trends 1 and 3, the prices and the doors, become its picker (pick a plan, see its rooms); trend 2 is its first screen; trends 4 and 5 are its paper and its type, under a cyan of its own.

Common and left outside: huge condensed display type across the width (deskspace.com.au's Manuka Condensed capitals, patch.work's FK Screamer over full-bleed photos, therumbl.com's full-width wordmark, runwayea.st's Barlow Condensed), the poster capitals of Corner, Marquee and Reel, and in this wave of Batten; looping videos of the space (Mountain Club, secondhome.io, therumbl.com); tickers (anticafe.eu's prices, Llum's address), rotating badges (llumcoworking.com) and pulsing live dots (runwayea.st); client logos in a marquee; page transitions (deskspace.com.au); and the 2026 AI-built look seen on anticafe.eu and indiedesk.com: numbered mono eyebrows ("— 02 · OUR VENUE"), Fraunces with one italic accent word, a fact ticker, a stat row, pill chips.

**What makes it its own:**
- **the front:** the first screen's object, the local details made a drawing: the building's street front in fine ink lines from the theme (a cornice, five floors of tall windows, the river door), its fourth floor's windows lit cyan and switching on one after another as the page opens. Beside its floors, in the site's type, stand the facts a newcomer asks first, each at the level where it is true: "Day pass $25" and its hours at the fourth floor, the address and the step-free door at the street, the Laker Line and the bike room at the curb. Not a card of facts (the gallery's local-shop hero, Indie Desk's booking card) nor a row of chips;
- **the plan:** the floor from above, an inline SVG drawn from the `zones` records' numbers, so the agent redraws the maker's floor by editing rooms in the Content tab: walls, doors, windows, columns, and in each room its furniture. Beside it a picker (Day pass, Part-time, Full-time, Fixed desk, Office, A room by the hour, each with its price): the rooms the picked plan opens light up one after another from the elevator, the light spreading across each room from the side nearest the door. Pointing at a room, on the plan or in the list of rooms beside it, lights it fully and opens what it holds, costs and includes. A raspberry dot at the elevator says "You come in here". The picking itself is CSS, so it works with no script and in edit mode;
- **availability said honestly:** Upstart has no live data, so the plan never says "free today" or counts seats. An office shows the state the maker typed (free from a date, taken, waitlist), its door drawn open when it is free. The caption carries the day the maker last typed it: "Offices as of Monday, November 16. We don't count desks live: hot desks are first come, and on a full Tuesday the long table takes the overflow." The guide tells the agent to have the maker retype that line whenever the offices change, and never to write a state the maker did not give;
- **one color for light:** cyan is never decoration. It is a lit window on the front by day, the same floor lit at night in the members' band ("The lights stay on for members"), and the rooms a plan opens; buttons wear it because a button is where you switch something on;
- **the floor's directory:** "Who's on the floor" lists member businesses the way an old building's lobby directory does, each with its name, its trade and where it sits ("Office 2", "The bench row"): the community shown by who is here, not by a marquee of logos or a count of members. Real names go only with the members' permission; the invented ones never stay.

**What it leaves out:**
- live availability or desk booking ("3 desks left today", "free now"), a booking calendar, a checkout for passes, online payments, a member portal, a map embed, an Instagram feed, a chat widget, a newsletter pop-up, video and sound;
- the coworking clichés: stock interiors (laptops, monstera, hanging plants, people laughing at events, a woman on the phone in a booth); the slogans ("Where work and culture collide", "Workspace redefined", "Not your ordinary office") and Community, Collaboration, Connection, Innovation; "Trusted by" marquees, stat bands ("200+ members"), three pillars (Workspace, Community, Culture), rows of amenity icons, hive and hexagon motifs, pop-ups; Llum's six numbered plan cards;
- the 2026 AI-built look: numbered mono eyebrows, Fraunces with one italic accent word, a fact ticker, a stat row, pill chips. Labels here are plain sentence case and never numbered, the picker's options are a list, not chips, and buttons have the drawing's crisp corners, not pills;
- **other templates' devices:** Decal's parking bays on asphalt, painted road letters, keep-clear hatching, its data plate of ruled mono cells and a vehicle that moves into place (the plan is ink on paper with an architect's conventions, and nothing on it travels); Trestle's dimension lines with ticks and figures, rings of driving time and before-and-after scribe (no measure is written on the plan or the front); lines that draw themselves (Chalk, Seam, Trestle: the plan's and the front's lines are there from the start); Saucer's door card, status dot and hours on the glass, Taper's open-now pole and week bars, Corner's ribbon, ticker, "Open now" pill and arches (the lit windows are always lit: they say where, never whether it is open); Gnomon's sun and shadows; Aspect's swinging door (the plan's doors are drawn, still); Linen's keys on a board and its curtain; Transfer's dial; Commons's jar and tear-off tabs; Codicil's dividers; Pail's order-by line and ZIP check; Kindly's envelopes; Encore's departure board; Folio's cursor preview and Ember's light following the pointer (a pointed room lights where it is; no light tracks the pointer); Reel's sideways scroll (the walk is an ordinary carousel, which the page's scroll does not drive); a huge wordmark across the footer (Corner, Ember, Seam), an italic accent word in a heading (Corner, Folio, Linen), tilted cards (Ember);
- in this wave: Ruled lights a week's sessions on a calendar on hover, where Overhead lights rooms on a drawn floor, chosen by plan and spreading from the elevator; Batten's cloths flown in, Heats' racing bars, Strum's strings, Spindle's ticket rail, Covered's tidy-up, Dogear's cards behind covers, Bolster's (the sheet's Bolster) mats unrolling;
- page transitions, a loader, WebGL and cursor effects.

## Type

All three are files of the template, as `woff2` subset to Latin and Latin Extended (for names such as Brouwer, Ibarra and Okafor, and a maker's Wójcik or Nguyễn), under the SIL Open Font License, from Google Fonts, declared in `styles/site.css`, each checked in Google Fonts' METADATA and CSS API on November 23. Castoro (Tiro Typeworks, 2020) now ships as a variable file, weight 400 to 700, with its italic; it has small caps, fractions and `lnum` but no `tnum`, and its default figures are old-style, so prices in Castoro turn `lnum` on. Reddit Sans ships as a variable file, weight 200 to 900, with its italic, `tnum` and stylistic sets; Reddit Mono as a variable file, weight 200 to 900, with no italic, which it does not need.

| Role | Font | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Headings | Castoro | the hero at 400, `clamp(2.75rem, 4.6vw + 1rem, 5.5rem)`, `leading-[1.02]`, `tracking-[-0.02em]`, `text-balance`; section headings at `clamp(2rem, 2.6vw + 1rem, 3.25rem)` in 400; small headings (a room, a plan, an event) at 1.375rem in 500; sentence case, never an italic word |
| The name and the figures | Castoro | the wordmark "Sample Floor" at 600; the front's "Day pass $25" and the Plans page's prices at 2 to 2.75rem in 500, `font-variant-numeric: lining-nums` |
| Quotes | Castoro italic | members' words only, at 1.5 to 2rem in 400 |
| Body | Reddit Sans | 400, 1.0625rem, `leading-relaxed`, `text-base-content/80`, measure `max-w-[62ch]`; buttons and the picker's names 600 at 1rem, sentence case |
| Facts, prices, the plan | Reddit Mono | the front's fact lines and the hours at 0.875rem in 400; the plan's labels (a room's name and price) at 0.75rem in 500, upright, sentence case; prices in the picker, the rooms' rates and the event dates `tabular-nums`; the hero's one label, "Coworking on the West Side, Grand Rapids", at 0.8125rem in 500, never numbered |

Castoro is a sturdy Dutch-style text serif, plain and well built, with a calligraphic warmth at large sizes: the voice of a city settled by Dutch families, and of a floor of honest desks, without a Didone's luxury. Reddit Sans is a friendly, open grotesk that reads well small, and Reddit Mono, drawn as its partner, gives the trend's utility voice (Courier, Roboto Mono) a softer tone; being one family, they share the plan's labels and the picker.

Ruled out: every font the 28 templates ship (the inventory's list) and every font of the wave sheet (League Gothic, Spectral, Geologica, Golos Text, Brygada 1918, Libre Franklin, Besley, Source Sans 3, Victor Mono, Shrikhand, Lexend, Ultra, Karla, Cutive Mono, Gelasio, Mulish, Imbue, Afacad); Fraunces most of all, the AI-built look's own face; and the trend's condensed display faces (Barlow Condensed, and Manuka Condensed and FK Screamer, not on Google Fonts), which would read as Corner's, Marquee's, Reel's and Batten's poster capitals.

**If a sibling takes them,** two pairings keep the same voice (a plain text serif, a friendly sans, a mono for the facts):
- **Manuale** (a sturdy text serif, weight 300 to 800, with italics) with **Sarabun** (body) and **Fira Code** (facts);
- **Domine** (a plain, robust serif, weight 400 to 700, with no italic, so the members' words go roman) with **Assistant** (body) and **Noto Sans Mono** (facts).

All six are on Google Fonts under the OFL, with Latin Extended, checked in its METADATA, and named by no template, no row of the wave sheet and no sibling brief written so far, alternatives included (Piazzolla, Nunito Sans, Sometype Mono, Andada Pro, Be Vietnam Pro, JetBrains Mono, Wix Madefor Text and Manrope were set aside for that reason).

## Palette

Plan paper, a darker board, graphite ink, window cyan, a maple from the old floorboards, a raspberry dot for "you come in here", and the graphite of the floor after hours. Every pair of text and background passes WCAG AA, measured from these values (oklch converted to sRGB, then the WCAG formula, in a scratchpad script): graphite on paper 14.1:1, on board 12.7:1, on cyan 8.1:1, on maple 10.6:1, on a lit room (cyan mixed 45% into paper) 10.7:1; `text-base-content/80` on paper 7.7:1, `/70` on paper 5.6:1, on board 5.3:1, on maple 4.8:1; light text on after hours 14.6:1, `/70` 7.8:1; cyan on after hours 8.5:1 and maple 11.2:1, so both may be text on the dark band; paper on raspberry 4.9:1; the plan's furniture lines (graphite at 55%) on paper 3.5:1, over the 3:1 asked of graphics.

Cyan on paper is 1.75:1 and on board 1.6:1: it is never text on a light ground, only a fill (lit windows and rooms, buttons with a 1px graphite edge so their outline holds at 14:1, a link's underline, the picked plan's ground). Raspberry on paper is 4.7:1 and on board 4.2:1: it is the elevator's dot and the short "Waitlist" tag with paper text on it, never body text. `/60` falls under 4.5:1 on maple and board, so small text never goes below `/70`.

**The lit tones** are not theme variables: `styles/site.css` mixes a lit room as `color-mix(in oklch, var(--color-primary) 45%, var(--color-base-100))`, takes the primary itself for a pointed room, and the primary over the neutral for a window at night, so the Theme tab relights the whole site with the rest.

Other settings: fields have the box's radius (`--theme-radius-field: 0.25rem`), not pills; relief off (`--theme-depth: 0`); density spacious (`--theme-size: 0.28125rem`); fine borders (1px; the plan's outer walls 1.5px). Links in text are graphite with a 2px cyan underline offset by 3px, taking a cyan ground on hover. The status colors are derived, as always; the forms' errors use them.

It is not Taper's deep Barbicide (h 215 at L 0.40, a dark, muted blue-green for text and buttons), Mend's deep lake (h 220), Encore's aqua on graphite (h 195) or Pocket's lagoon teal (h 200): a light, saturated cyan at h 214 that is never text on paper, on a warm paper ground, with a maple and a raspberry kept rare.

## Motion

Expressive, because a coworking floor is a place people move through and choose from, and its site should show that rather than describe it; the moment is the plan, and around it everything is quiet. Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode nothing moves (Upstart's stillness rule stops CSS animations and transitions, and no script runs): the front's windows are lit, the plan shows the first plan's rooms lit (a checked radio is state, not movement), every line of the rooms' list is closed, and the walk is a plain row that snaps. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too.

- **The plan** (`data-plan` on the section, `data-plan-picker` on the picker, `data-zone` on each room of the SVG, `data-zone-row` on each line of the list; CSS, and `scripts/plan.js` with Motion's `animate` and `inView`):
  - **the drawing:** `parts/plan.liquid` draws an inline SVG in feet (`viewBox="0 0 120 64"`). From assigns at its top it takes the floor's width and depth, which walls have windows, the columns' grid, the core (stairs, elevator, restrooms, the front table) and the elevator's door (`data-entrance`); from each `zones` record, the room's box (x, y, w, h in feet from the north-west corner), its kind and its seats. Each kind draws its furniture: desks 5 by 2.5 ft with a chair, in as many rows as fit; an office as a room with its desks and a door leaf, drawn open as a quarter circle when its state is free, shut otherwise; a meeting room as a table and its chairs; phone booths as 4 by 4 ft cells with a seat; the kitchen as a counter along one wall and the long table. Walls are 1.5px graphite, partitions 1px, furniture 0.75px at 55%, all `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke`, so the lines stay fine at every size; windows are a thin double line in the outer walls, the cast-iron columns small circles; labels in Reddit Mono give a room's name and price on two lines. On a phone the part draws the same floor a quarter turn round, standing portrait, from the same numbers, its labels upright; one of the two SVGs shows per width. The SVG is `aria-hidden`: the list of rooms beside it (below on a phone) is what a screen reader and the keyboard use;
  - **picking, in CSS:** each room's group carries `data-plans="2 3 5"`, the positions of the plans that open it, worked out by the part from each plan's `zones` list. The picker is a fieldset of radios, the first checked in the markup. `styles/site.css` holds eight rules of the form `[data-plan]:has([data-plan-picker] > :nth-child(3) :checked) [data-plans~="3"]`, which fill the checked plan's rooms with the lit tone and show that plan's details under the picker (`data-plan-detail`, by the same position): eight plans at most, as the guide says. A browser without `:has()` (`@supports not selector(:has(*))`) shows every plan's details and lights no room. A room under a hovering pointer takes the full cyan in 150ms, under `(hover: hover)`;
  - **the lights coming on, by the script:** once, as the plan comes into view, the script turns the lit rooms dark and back on, one after another in the order a visitor walks from the elevator (by the distance from `data-entrance` to each room's nearest corner), 80ms apart; each room's light spreads from that corner across it, a circle in its clip path growing over 0.5s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`. On every change of plan, the newly lit rooms come on the same way and those going dark fade in 0.2s. The script sets the dark state itself, just before animating;
  - **pointing, by the script:** pointing at or focusing a line of the list lights its room at full cyan. Each line is a `<details>` whose summary gives the room's name and price ("Hot desks, the river room · from $25 a day") and whose body says what it holds, what it includes and its typed state. Pointing at a room on the plan marks its line (`data-active`), and a tap on a room opens its line. With no script the lines still open as `<details>` do, and a room under the pointer still lights through CSS;
  - under reduced motion, rooms switch on at once and the walk from the elevator is skipped; picking and pointing still light rooms, which involves no movement. `stop` removes the observer, the listeners, the clip circles' values and `data-active`.
- **The front** (`data-front`, CSS only): `parts/front.liquid` draws the street front as a CSS grid from assigns at its top: `floors` (5), `our_floor` (4), `bays` (6, and 4 on a phone) and `look` (`old`, with its cornice and tall windows; `storefront`; `modern`). Each window is a cell with a 1px graphite frame and a mullion; our floor's are `bg-primary`. The facts are HTML in the grid's rows beside the drawing, at the row where each is true; on a phone they stack under it, top floor first. Under `no-preference`, our floor's windows switch on one after another as the page opens: a keyframe `from { background-color: transparent }`, 0.3s each, 90ms apart (`--i` on each window), from 0.4s after load, `ease-out`. The stylesheet's state is lit, so edit mode, reduced motion and a page with no script show them lit. In the members' band the same part draws the front at night (`night: true`: lines in `neutral-content/50`, our floor lit on the neutral), still.

No page transitions, no loader, no WebGL, no cursor effects. ## Availability, bookings and what stays outside

Upstart has no live data, no payments, no calendar and no embeds, so:
- **availability is typed, and dated** (as built): each office's state is what the maker set in the Content tab, and the plan's caption says the day it was last set; the hot desks are first come, and the page says so, with the overflow table. Nothing on the site counts seats, says "free now" or reads a booking tool; a space that wants live availability links to its booking tool's own page (see What varies), never embeds its floor-plan widget;
- **a day pass is paid at the front table:** one or two people just come up before 11; a larger group or a tour sends the visit form, and the space confirms by email;
- **plans are joined in person,** after a tour or a day, and billed by the space; the site never asks for a card;
- **rooms are a request,** confirmed by email within a working day; members' hours come off first, the rest goes on the month or is paid at the front table;
- **events are free or paid at the door,** with an RSVP only to count chairs; a space selling tickets elsewhere links out;
- **no map:** "Get directions" links to the address on a map site, and the front and the getting-here cards say the rest in words;
- **no Instagram feed:** a link to the space's account in the footer.

## What varies

The choices the agent asks when the maker's messages leave them open, page type by page type, the recommended one first. These are facts, not choices: the prices, the hours, the rooms' sizes and seats, the offices' states, the bus line and the events. The agent asks for them once the page is built, or leaves them out rather than guess.
