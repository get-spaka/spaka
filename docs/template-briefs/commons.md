# Commons: brief

One of wave 2's six original designs: the local nonprofit or association (`business`, the closest category; one of the original, animated designs).

## Concept

**Concept.** A neighborhood nonprofit's site made like the noticeboard by its door: big, friendly type on newsprint paper, flat bright colors, notices torn from a pad with tear-off tabs at their foot, and a few lines written by hand. The first screen is two doors, "Lend a hand" and "Take what you need", because a nonprofit's visitors come either to help or to be helped. The impact is told in a few huge figures on slips of colored paper and in short stories of neighbors. The current appeal is a jar that fills to where it stands. Giving, volunteering and the next event are never more than one tap away.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Bread & Bolts**, a neighborhood nonprofit in the North End of Dubuque, Iowa. It works out of a former hardware store at 41 Couler Lane, "the shed", with a pantry on the porch and a garden on the vacant lot next door. Dolores "Dee" Hartwig, a retired shop teacher, and Samuel Adeyemi, a school nurse, founded it in 2017, after the block had spent two winters borrowing Dee's tools and Sam's soup pot. It has been a 501(c)(3) since 2019. Today it has three staff, a board of five and 186 volunteers, and it runs five programs:
- **the Tool Library:** 1,400 tools to borrow for a week, free with a North End address and $20 a year for everyone else;
- **the Repair Café:** volunteer fixers mend lamps, toasters, zippers and bikes on the second Saturday of each month, from 10 to 1;
- **the Porch Pantry:** a fridge and shelves on the porch, open day and night, restocked every day with food rescued from three grocers and two bakeries;
- **the Winter Swap:** coats, boots, snow pants and mittens, every Saturday in November;
- **the Lot:** 24 raised beds and a Wednesday supper from June to September.

Last year it lent 7,412 tools, mended 1,083 things (4.1 tons kept out of the landfill), shared 52,600 pounds of food and found new owners for 1,940 coats, boots and mittens. Its appeal this winter is a walk-in cooler for the Porch Pantry: $36,000 by March 1, 2027, of which $22,480 has come from 304 neighbors. It takes gifts on its own donation page and sends a monthly email, the Porch Letter. A search for "Bread & Bolts" found no nonprofit of that name.

The same structure suits a food bank, a community center, a neighborhood or tenants' association, a youth program, an animal rescue, a literacy or language program, a refugee welcome group, a park or river conservancy, a mutual aid network or a library's friends. The agent rewrites the programs, the figures, the stories and the appeal.

## Trends it draws on

These come from 15 nonprofit, foundation and campaign sites opened in November 2026. They were found through Awwwards' Social responsibility listing (honorable mentions and nominees of 2025-2026), the Webby Awards' Charitable Organizations/Non-Profit winners and honorees of 2025 and 2026, and the 2025 WebAward for the best nonprofit website. WebFetch read their text; the browser showed the colors, type and movement of seven of them; the fonts were read from their pages' source. The sites are hopecancerresources.org, re-cycle.org, cobaltaf.org, fondation.canadiens.com, opportunity.waltonfamilyfoundation.org, woonpact.gent, omni.org, stillhere.hdfund.org, givingkitchen.org, mauifoodbank.org, bigthought.org, fineacts.co, licensedtradecharity.org.uk, thehighline.org and aya.nl. Each trend below was seen on at least two of them.

1. **Two doors from the first screen, give or get help, with Donate always in reach:**
   - hopecancerresources.org: "I want to help" and "I need help" side by side in the hero, and "Apply for Assistance" beside "Donate" in the header;
   - givingkitchen.org: "Ask for help" and "Donate", in the header and again in the hero;
   - mauifoodbank.org: "Give Now" and "Find Food";
   - cobaltaf.org: "Get Support" and "Donate";
   - licensedtradecharity.org.uk: "Need help?" and "Want to get involved?" right under the hero;
   - a Donate button that never leaves the header on re-cycle.org, thehighline.org (with a side tab too), fondation.canadiens.com and bigthought.org. The gift itself is made on the organization's own payment page, never on the site: Qgiv and GoFevo on hopecancerresources.org, a donor portal on stillhere.hdfund.org.
2. **Impact as a few huge figures, each with a short label:**
   - hopecancerresources.org: 5.8k people, 304k miles, 1,269 hours of counseling;
   - re-cycle.org: four figures divided by vertical rules, 29 years, 377 containers, 171,227 bikes and 7 partners;
   - fondation.canadiens.com: seven figures, $54.4M among them;
   - givingkitchen.org: 40,000, 238K and +70%;
   - mauifoodbank.org: "Over 7.6M pounds";
   - stillhere.hdfund.org: "1,096 days" and a ledger of totals;
   - fineacts.co: five counters;
   - opportunity.waltonfamilyfoundation.org: one figure per screen ("7 in 10").
3. **Stories of people told short: a photo, one line in their words and a name:**
   - givingkitchen.org: photo cards with names and quotes;
   - re-cycle.org: quotes with the person's name and program;
   - fondation.canadiens.com: "A child building their confidence / one smile at a time" beside a photo;
   - stillhere.hdfund.org: a grid of portraits with dated captions;
   - hopecancerresources.org: "Stories of Hope";
   - licensedtradecharity.org.uk: one man's story offered to visitors who hesitate to ask.
4. **Paper and the hand on a flat, bright palette (rough-torn edges, marker lines, drawn outlines, illustrated figures):**
   - givingkitchen.org: rough-torn edges between navy, mustard and pale blue sections, and a red marker line under "We Help";
   - re-cycle.org: a torn photo edge on forest green and lime;
   - woonpact.gent: pastel outlines of a house drawn by hand behind a black headline, on cream and lilac;
   - fondation.canadiens.com: illustrated skaters, and red (#FF364B) and blue (#333EF0) panels set between photos like a checkerboard;
   - hopecancerresources.org: a huge brush-drawn "h" across the hero's photo;
   - cobaltaf.org: an illustration of its own for each service.
5. **Three equal ways to get involved, events as dated cards, a newsletter close by:**
   - hopecancerresources.org: Volunteer, Donate and Grant Support, then event cards with a date badge and a category;
   - givingkitchen.org: Donate, Volunteer and Partner, then events with date and place;
   - re-cycle.org: Donate a Bike, Fundraise and Partner;
   - mauifoodbank.org: dated campaign cards, and food drives and volunteering;
   - fondation.canadiens.com: six ways to help, and "Stay in the game" for its newsletter;
   - newsletter sign-ups also on woonpact.gent, fineacts.co and omni.org.
6. **An appeal with a goal and where it stands, and the accounts in the open:**
   - thehighline.org: a $200,000 matching challenge in a bar above the header;
   - re-cycle.org: "Make your donation go twice as far!", a matched Christmas appeal;
   - woonpact.gent: five numbered stages with the "current step" marked;
   - mauifoodbank.org: its food insecurity report to download, and charity ratings in the footer;
   - hopecancerresources.org: its 990 filings from 2021 to 2024 and a transparency seal in the footer.

**What makes it its own:**
- **the two doors:** the first screen splits into "Lend a hand" (tomato) and "Take what you need" (sky). The door under the pointer opens wider, its photo comes forward and its ways in slide toward you, while the other steps back;
- **the jar:** the appeal is a tall jar outlined in ink. When it comes into view it fills with marigold to where the appeal stands, its surface sways and settles, and the goal is marked at its brim;
- **the tear-off tabs:** events, volunteer roles and the address are notices torn from a pad, each ending in a fringe of tabs cut by a dashed line, like a flyer on a laundromat's board. The tabs flutter once as the notice comes into view, as if in a draft from the door, and the tab under the pointer lifts away from its notice;
- **paper and the hand:** photos, slips and a few section edges are torn like paper, which also pulls the documentary photos a nonprofit has into one set. A line written by hand is pinned beside a figure or a notice now and then ("that's 4 tons kept out of the landfill!").

**What it leaves out:**
- lines that draw themselves (underlines, loops, outlines): Seam, briefed beside it, draws its grease-pencil loops. Commons takes the trend's paper and handwriting rather than its pen strokes;
- preloaders and splash screens (woonpact.gent and the Walton site have them);
- video, and any feed or map embedded from another site, which Spaka does not allow: a "Get directions" link replaces the map;
- figures that count up from zero, which would have a script rewrite the maker's numbers: the figures are always the real text;
- carousels; condensed poster capitals (Corner, Marquee, Reel); tilted stickers and slabs (Marquee, Reel); a ticker (Corner); duotoned photos (Margin); a word set on a pill or in an italic serif inside a heading (Pocket, Corner, Linen);
- a "quick exit" button and a crisis line: they matter for some nonprofits, but not for this one. The design notes say how to add them.

## Type

The three fonts are files of the template (`fonts/`), from Google Fonts, under the SIL Open Font License. None is used in wave 1 or chosen by the other wave 2 briefs.

| Role | Font | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Headings, the wordmark and the figures | Gabarito (variable, 400 to 900) | 800 for the hero and the figures, 700 for section titles, 600 for card titles; sentence case, `leading-[0.92]`, `tracking-[-0.03em]`; the hero at `clamp(3rem, 9vw, 8.5rem)`; the figures at `clamp(4rem, 11vw, 9rem)` with `tabular-nums`; the ampersand of "Bread & Bolts" in tomato |
| Body, labels, buttons and forms | Public Sans (variable, 100 to 900) | 400 and 600, 1.0625 rem, `leading-relaxed`; labels 0.8125 rem, 700, uppercase, `tracking-[0.08em]`, `tabular-nums` for dates and hours. It was made for the U.S. government's public websites, plain and readable: the voice of a public service |
| Handwritten notes and the tabs | Caveat (variable, 400 to 700) | 600, 1.5 to 2 rem, `leading-tight`; a note is set at -2° beside a figure or a notice, at most one per section, and never the only place a fact is said; the tabs' labels at 1.125 rem |

Gabarito's round, sturdy shapes carry the figures like headlines; Public Sans keeps everything else plain. The handwritten notes are one class, `hand`, in `styles/site.css`.

Chalk names Public Sans among its alternatives. **If a sibling took them,** two other pairings in the same roles, also OFL and also free of wave 1 and of the other wave 2 briefs:
- Lilita One, Lexend and Gochi Hand: the same friendly poster voice, chunkier;
- Bitter, Mulish and Kalam: a civic slab serif, with a rounder body.

Other settings:
- buttons and fields are pills (`--theme-radius-field: 9999px`);
- relief is off (`--theme-depth: 0`);
- borders are bold (`--theme-border: 2px`), like a printed flyer's;
- density is normal (`--theme-size: 0.25rem`): the type is already big.

Contrast, measured from these values:
- ink on paper 14.9:1, ink on kraft 13:1, ink on sky 9.6:1, ink on marigold 10:1, ink on tomato 4.7:1 (button labels are set at 600);
- paper on deep ink 16.5:1, marigold on deep ink 11:1;
- `text-base-content/70` on paper about 5.8:1.

Tomato on paper is 3.15:1: enough for the ampersand, a door's arrow and words at display size, never for running text.

The Theme tab still restyles all of it, since every color, the jar's and the notices' included, comes from the theme.

## Motion

Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode nothing moves and everything is in its final place: both doors the same width, the jar at its level, every tab hanging straight. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too.

- **The doors** (`data-doors` on the hero's row; CSS only):
  - from `lg`, the two doors share the row. The one under the pointer or holding the focus grows (`flex-grow` from 1 to 1.7, 0.6 s, `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1)`, through `:has(:hover, :focus-within)`). Its photo scales from 1 to 1.04, its links slide 0.5 rem toward the center one after another (`transition-delay` by `--i`) and its large "→" moves forward; the other door's photo eases back and its links fade to 60%;
  - on a phone and a tablet, the doors are stacked, full width and fully open;
  - under reduced motion, nothing changes size or place, only the other door's fade. With no hover (touch), a tap simply follows the link.
- **The jar** (`data-jar`, `scripts/jar.js`, Motion):
  - Liquid computes the level from the `appeal` record and writes it into the style attribute (`style="--level: 62%"`, from `raised` and `goal`). The stylesheet fills the jar to it: the page is right with no script;
  - when the jar comes into view, the script empties it and raises the fill to `--level` (1.4 s, a spring with a little overshoot). The fill's wavy surface sways twice before it settles (a CSS animation under no-preference, stopped after 3 s); the handwritten note ("62% there!") fades in last;
  - with no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode, the jar is at its level.
- **The tear-off tabs** (`data-tabs` on a notice's fringe; `scripts/tabs.js`, Motion, and CSS):
  - a notice ends in four to six tabs under a dashed cut. They are one link (the event's page, "Save a spot", the volunteer form, directions), each tab a `<span>` with its label set vertically (`writing-mode: vertical-rl`) in Caveat; screen readers hear the link's text once (`aria-label`);
  - as the notice comes into view, its tabs flutter once from their top edge (`rotate` from 6° to 0, a soft spring, 0.05 s apart), like paper in a draft;
  - on hover and focus, the tab under the pointer lifts away (`rotate: 8deg` around its top, a shadow from `--color-base-content` at 15%), in CSS under no-preference;
  - with no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode, the tabs hang straight; hover only darkens the tab.
- **Torn paper** (`data-torn="top|bottom|both"`; CSS only): a `mask-image` from SVG files in `styles/edges/` (alpha only, no color of their own), three variants so that two neighbors do not tear alike. The photos in cards and doors are torn at the bottom, the notices at the top (torn from the pad), and the figures' slips, the count's band and the footer at the top. Static.
- **`data-reveal`:** sections' main elements rise (`y: [24, 0]`, opacity) with Motion's `inView`, as in the site-scripts skill. Cards, slips and notices in a grid follow each other by 0.08 s.
- **Links** (CSS only): text links' underline thickens and moves closer on hover (`text-decoration-thickness` and `text-underline-offset`, 0.2 s); the current page's link in the header keeps the thick underline.
- **No page transition:** a page rising over the last is Chalk's in this wave.
- **Smooth scrolling:** Lenis, as it comes (`autoRaf: true`).

Libraries: Motion and Lenis, pinned on jsDelivr as in the site-scripts skill, under MIT. No WebGL: the paper is the effect.

## What varies

The choices the agent asks for each page type, the first one recommended. Facts (the donation page's address, the figures, the appeal's amounts, the hours) are not choices: the agent asks for them once the page is built, or leaves them out rather than guess.

### Home (`/`)

It carries the choices for the whole website.

**How do people give money?**
- **On your own donation page** (as built): every Donate button opens, in a new tab, the page where the nonprofit already takes gifts (its bank's, PayPal, Givebutter, Donorbox, Zeffy…); Spaka takes no payments. Its address goes into `appeal.link`, which every Donate button, the jar and the give levels read; until it is given, they lead to `/give`.
- **With a pledge form:** visitors say what they mean to give, once or monthly, and leave their name and email; the nonprofit then sends them how to pay. This adds a `pledges` datasource (amount, frequency, name, email, note, collected) and its thank-you fragment, puts the form on the give page in place of the levels' buttons, and points the Donate buttons to `/give#pledge`.
- **By check or in person only:** the give page says to whom to write the check, where to send it, and when the shed takes gifts in person. The Donate buttons lead to `/give#ways`, and the levels become examples without buttons.
- **Things and time, not money:** "Donate" becomes "Give things" and leads to `/give#things`. The jar counts things instead of dollars ("120 winter coats by November 30: 74 so far"). The levels and the dollar bar go.

**How do people ask for help?**
- **They just come** (as built): each program says when it is open and who it is for; nobody signs up. The "Take what you need" door links to the programs.
- **With a request form:** a short, private form asks for a name, a way to reach them, the program and what they need, and says who reads it and when they will hear back. Requests arrive in the Content tab. It adds a `requests` datasource (collected, `parts/ask.liquid` on the programs page, `#ask`) and its fragment, and the door's links lead to it. It asks only what is needed.
- **By phone:** a help line set large with its hours. The door's links and "Not sure where to start?" lead to `tel:`.

**Does it send a newsletter?**
- **Yes, sign-ups kept in Spaka** (as built): the footer's Porch Letter form posts to `newsletter`. Sign-ups arrive in the Content tab, to be copied into whatever tool sends the email; Spaka does not send it.
- **On another service:** the form gives way to a "Get the Porch Letter" button to the maker's Mailchimp, Buttondown or other page; `data/newsletter/` and its fragment go.
- **No newsletter:** the form goes, and the footer keeps the address and the hours.

When a page type is left out, the home page keeps its rhythm: the doors, one band of slips, one sky noticeboard, the jar, and a deep ink footer.

### Programs (`/programs`)

**Does each program have its own page?**
- **A page per program** (as built): the programs page lists them, and each has its page with its steps, its figure, its story and its next event.
- **All on one page:** for one or two programs. `pages/programs/[programs].liquid` goes; each program's row on `/programs` shows its text and steps in full, and the cards on the home page link to `/programs#<program>`.

When left out: delete `pages/programs.liquid`, `pages/programs/`, and its navigation record. The home page's program cards keep `programs` and show When and Where without links; the doors' links point to `/#programs`. The ask-for-help section, if chosen, moves to the about page.

### Volunteer (`/volunteer`)

**How do volunteers sign up?**
- **A form, then a call** (as built): the form says what they would like to do and when they are free, and the coordinator calls them. Records arrive in the Content tab.
- **They pick a shift:** the page lists the coming shifts as notices (a `shifts` datasource: date, time, program, spots), and the form asks for one. It is still a request that the coordinator confirms by email.
- **An orientation first:** the form books a seat at the next orientation, chosen among the `events` of kind Orientation; shifts come after.
- **On a volunteer platform:** a button to the maker's SignUpGenius, VolunteerHub or other page replaces the form, and the roles' tabs lead there; `data/volunteers/` and its fragment go.

When left out: delete `pages/volunteer.liquid`, its parts, `data/volunteer-roles/`, `data/volunteer-steps/`, `data/volunteer-faq/`, `data/volunteers/`, `fragments/volunteer-thanks.liquid` and its navigation record. Point the header's Volunteer pill, the "Lend a hand" door's Volunteer link and the program pages' "Volunteer here" to the coordinator's email (`mailto:`), or remove them.

### Give (`/give`)

Its giving sections follow how people give, chosen with the home page.

**Is there an appeal running?**
- **Yes, the jar** (as built): one goal with an end date, filled to where it stands from the `appeal` record. It appears in the header's strip, on the home page and here.
- **Not now:** the jar and the header's strip go, and the page opens on what a gift does. `appeal` keeps only the donation page's address.

When left out: delete `pages/give.liquid`, `parts/give-levels.liquid`, `parts/give-things.liquid`, `parts/give-ways.liquid`, `parts/give-spending.liquid`, `data/give-levels/`, `data/give-things/`, `data/give-ways/`, `data/give-spending/` and its navigation record. The Donate buttons lead straight to the donation page, or go when there is none; the jar's "Other ways to give" goes.

### Events (`/events`)

**How do people come?**
- **They save a spot** (as built): each event's page has a short form posting to `rsvps`, so the nonprofit knows how many to expect; the notices' tabs lead to it.
- **They just come:** the form and `data/rsvps/` go; each notice says "Free, no need to sign up", and its tabs carry the date and place, linking to the event's page.
- **Tickets elsewhere:** for a benefit with paid seats, each event gets a `ticket_url` field. The page's button and the notice's tabs open the maker's own ticket page; the form goes.

When left out: delete `pages/events/`, `data/events/`, `data/rsvps/`, `fragments/rsvp-thanks.liquid`, the home page's noticeboard and its navigation record. The program pages lose their next event; the volunteer page's next orientation becomes a line of text.

### News (`/news`)

No choice: the posts are the maker's. With none to tell yet, the agent keeps one, written from what the maker says, rather than inventing three.

When left out: delete `pages/news/`, `data/news/`, the home page's "From the porch" and its navigation record. The stories stay on the home page.

### About (`/about`)

**Who runs it?**
- **Staff and a board** (as built): the team in two groups, staff then board.
- **Volunteers only:** no staff. "Who runs it" becomes the volunteers who coordinate each program, with "Run entirely by volunteers since 2017". The `team` records lose their group.

When left out: delete `pages/about.liquid`, `data/about-milestones/`, `data/about-figures/`, `data/about-logos/`, `data/team/`, `data/contact/`, `fragments/contact-thanks.liquid` and its navigation record. The count's "Our year in numbers" link goes, and the footer keeps the address, the hours and the email.

To read as one set they are chosen in daylight and natural color: documentary, not stock, never posed smiles. People appear at work, from behind or at a distance, or as hands; no recognizable face is given an invented name or quote. The torn edges and the cards' colors do the rest: no filter, no duotone (Margin's).
