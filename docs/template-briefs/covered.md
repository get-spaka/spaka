# Covered: brief

One of wave 5's templates: the marketing site of a software product for non-technical teams, here the schedule, the timesheets, the swaps and the payroll export of small restaurant and café crews (`app`). Its one orchestrated moment is on the home page. A paper schedule is taped up crooked, covered in sticky notes, crossed-out names, arrows and a coffee ring. As it comes into view it tidies itself, once, into the product's clean week: every name flies into its slot, each note turns into what it asked for, and the clutter goes. Everywhere else the site is calm and useful. The product is shown one job at a time in tabs, built in HTML from the theme, and the same proof line sits under every trial button.

`covered` is a working name: what a manager wants to say about Saturday, and the line the tidy-up ends on. The wave sheet named this template Sorted, but a search found "Sorted" already listed on Framer's marketplace: a free SaaS landing page template for an AI productivity tool, the same world as this one. So the brief takes the nearest free name. Squared, the American "squared away", is a Webflow agency template. A search found no template or theme called Covered on Framer (its marketplace address answers "Template unavailable"), Webflow (no `covered-website-template`), ThemeForest (the search lists Grippable's "Covers", Cover Image, nothing called Covered), Squarespace or Shopify (no `themes/covered`). The nearest names were Webflow's Cover (insurance) and Coverly, Framer's Covix, and Shipshape, which Framer also answers "unavailable". The name is the template's, not the business's.

## Concept

**Concept.** A scheduling product's site that starts where its visitors are: Sunday night, the sheet on the walk-in door, three sticky notes and a text that says "can anyone cover Saturday?". The first screen is split down the middle. On one half, a baker at work in a café-bakery, with a small card from the product floating over the photo: "Swap approved · Jo takes Maya's Friday". On the other half, a deep jade panel holds the promise, the trial button and the proof line. Under it, that café's paper schedule sorts itself into the product's week. The product is then shown one job at a time: the schedule, the timesheets, the swaps and availability, the payroll export. Every screen of it is HTML drawn from the theme, so the Theme tab restyles the product with the site. The site is sturdy, clear and warm, like a well-run back of house. Its fonts are a wide, constructed humanist sans with a pen-cut edge, over a sans drawn for public-service websites. Its colors are a warm order-pad white, one deep jade, an espresso ink, a pale jade and a ticket coral. Nothing on it reads as a developer tool.

It must not read as Pocket, Beacon or Kindly:
- Pocket is a consumer app: three phones in a fan over a glow, then one phone that stays while its screens change.
- Beacon is a launch: a window tilting flat out of a violet glow on a dark page.
- Kindly is a wedding: an envelope opening, in a bottle green on warm paper close to this one's ground.

Covered is a work tool for people who stand all day. It answers what its visitors are asking: will this end the Sunday night, will my crew use it, what does it cost per place, and can I talk to a person.

**The fictional business** its texts are written for is **Staff Meal**, a scheduling and timesheet product for restaurants, cafés, bakeries and bars of 5 to 60 people. It is made in **Kansas City, Missouri**. Staff meal is the meal before service when the whole crew sits down together, the one time a day everyone is in one place. Now the week is too.

- **The company:** founded in 2021, 22 people, on the second floor of an old printing house at 1717 Pressman Alley (an invented street) in the Crossroads Arts District, Kansas City, Missouri 64108.
- **The people:** **Mariela Ochoa** (she/her), co-founder, ran the counter, and the schedule, at her family's café on Southwest Boulevard for twelve years; the schedule was a sheet taped to the walk-in door. **Grant Lindqvist** (he/him), co-founder, wrote payroll software for a Kansas City firm for ten years and closed a Westport bar on weekends through college. Support is answered by people from 7 am to 10 pm Central, seven days a week: "Everyone who answers has worked a Saturday double."
- **The product:** managers use it in a browser and on their phone; the crew use a free phone app (iPhone and Android) in English and Spanish.
  - the **schedule**: copy last week, see who can't work while building, a warning before anyone goes over 40 hours, open shifts, one tap to publish to everyone's phone;
  - **timesheets**: clock in and out on the phone, only within 150 yards of the door; breaks, early and late starts flagged; tips declared at clock-out; the day's hours approved in one go;
  - **swaps and availability**: a crew member offers a shift, a coworker who can work that role takes it, the manager approves it; availability set once; time off asked for and answered in the app;
  - **payroll export**: at the end of the pay period, the approved regular hours, overtime and tips per person go out as one file, in ready-made formats for the payroll services most US restaurants use or as a plain spreadsheet. Staff Meal is not a payroll service, a point of sale or an HR suite, and says so.
- **The price:** per location, with the whole crew included. **Counter**, $29 a month, up to 15 people: the schedule, swaps and availability, the crew app. **Full house** ("Most asked for"), $59, up to 60 people: everything in Counter, plus timesheets, the time clock and the payroll export. **Group**, $45 a location from three locations: one view of every place, people shared between places, set-up with a person. Yearly billing gives two months free ($24, $49 and $38 a month). The trial is 30 days of Full house with no credit card; at its end the account waits for a plan and nothing is charged. A person sets up each trial's first week on a call, or types it in from a photo of the paper schedule.
- **Its customers**, all invented, are the stories' four: **Bramble & Bun**, a bakery café in Waldo, Kansas City, 11 people, owned by Tess Okonjo, whose week is the one the tidy-up sorts; **Taquería Dos Puentes** in South Omaha, Nebraska, 18 people (Lupe Cárdenas); **Kettle Ten Ramen** in Tulsa, Oklahoma, two kitchens and 22 people (Danny Phommachanh); **Windbreak Coffee** in Wichita, Kansas, three cafés and 27 people (Kelsey Brandt).

The names were searched. No scheduling product called Staff Meal came up, and no business of that name in Kansas City; the product names in the trends' list (Jobber, Homebase, 7shifts, Deputy, Sling, Planday, Skello, Combo, When I Work and the others) are avoided. Short Order, considered first, is a recipe app on the App Store, and SideWork a gig-work app. No bakery called Bramble & Bun came up, nor a Kettle Ten Ramen, a Taquería Dos Puentes in Omaha or a Windbreak Coffee in Wichita (Prairie Hour, considered first, was too near Wichita's Prairie Fire Coffee Roaster). No existing template, and no brief of this wave, is set in Kansas City. The phone in the texts is (816) 555-0134, a number reserved for fiction, and the email hello@staffmeal.app, invented.

The same structure suits any work tool sold to small teams who are not at a desk: the booking tool of salons and clinics, the job scheduler of a cleaning or landscaping crew, a volunteer rota, a delivery round planner, an inventory app for small shops, a staff training app for retail. The agent rewrites the product's parts and the records that fill them (the week's shifts become bookings, jobs or rounds; the paper becomes the appointment book, the whiteboard or the clipboard the product replaces), the plans, the stories and the help.

The gap is the same everywhere. Every site shows the product already tidy, or building itself from an empty grid. None shows where its visitor is coming from: the sheet on the wall, the sticky notes, the group text. And none turns that mess into the product before the visitor's eyes. Covered's tidy-up does, with a real-looking week of a real-looking café. ## Type

All three are files of the template, as `woff2` subset to Latin and Latin Extended (for names such as Phommachanh, Cárdenas and Lindqvist), under the SIL Open Font License, from Google Fonts, declared in `styles/site.css`. Each was checked in Google Fonts' own metadata (google/fonts, `ofl/`), and its OpenType features read from the file:
- **Geologica** (Monokrom, on Google Fonts since 2023) is one variable file with four axes: weight 100 to 900, sharpness (`SHRP`) 0 to 100, cursive (`CRSV`) 0 to 1 and slant 0 to −12. Its cursive italic is drawn, not slanted. It has `tnum`, `case` and `frac`;
- **Golos Text** (Paratype, 2019) is one variable file, weight 400 to 900, with no italic. It has `tnum`, `lnum`, `case` and `frac`;
- **Shantell Sans** (Arrow Type with Shantell Martin, 2023) is a variable file with weight 300 to 800, bounce (`BNCE`), informality (`INFM`) and spacing (`SPAC`), and an italic.

| Role | Font | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Headings | Geologica | 700 for the hero, 600 for sections, with `SHRP` at 0 and `CRSV` at 0. The hero at `clamp(2.5rem, 3.4vw + 1rem, 4.75rem)` within its half panel, `leading-[1.02]`, `tracking-[-0.02em]`, `text-balance`. Section headings at `clamp(1.875rem, 2.2vw + 1rem, 3rem)`, small headings (a job, a plan, a story's business) at 1.375 rem in 600. Sentence case |
| The wordmark | Geologica | "Staff Meal" at 800 with `SHRP` at 60, its corners cut like a pen stroke, beside a mark of two stacked rounded bars (two shifts) |
| Figures | Geologica | prices ("$59") at 3 rem in 600, the figures row at 2.75 rem in 600, totals and hours inside the product in 600, all `tabular-nums` |
| Body | Golos Text | 400, 1.0625 rem, `leading-relaxed`, `text-base-content/80`, measure `max-w-[62ch]`. Emphasis in 600, since Golos has no italic. Buttons 600 at 1 rem, sentence case |
| Labels | Golos Text | 600 at 0.75 rem, uppercase, `tracking-[0.08em]`: above headings, a plan's limit, a story's facts |
| Inside the product | Golos Text | 500 and 600 at 12 to 15 px with `tabular-nums`, to read like an interface. Names in 600, times in 500 ("6a–2p"), the product's own headings ("Week of March 8") in Geologica 600 |
| The paper | Shantell Sans | the tidy-up's before only (`aria-hidden`): the names, days and notes at 500 with `INFM` 100 and `BNCE` 40, at 1 to 1.4 rem in the text color, like a marker on a sheet; the sheet's title at 700 |

Geologica is grounded in humanist letters but built from geometric, constructed shapes: wide, steady and evenly colored, sure of itself without shouting, like a sturdy kitchen tool. Its sharpness axis gives the wordmark a mark no other template has. Golos Text was drawn for public-service websites and made for reading on screen. It is plain and very legible at the 12 px of a shift chip, as an interface should be, and it does what Inter and DM Sans do on the trend's sites without being them. Shantell Sans was made from an artist's marker handwriting, and its informality axis turns it from neat to scrawled: the manager's hand on the sheet by the walk-in.

**A third face.** The wave sheet names Geologica and Golos Text. Shantell Sans is added for the paper alone, which must read as handwritten at a glance, or the tidy-up loses its before. This is for Matt to decide. Without it, the paper's writing is Geologica's drawn cursive (`CRSV` 1, slant −12, weight 500), and the script and the layout are unchanged.

**Ruled out:**
- every font the 28 templates ship (the inventory's list), and this wave's other faces (League Gothic, Spectral, Brygada 1918, Libre Franklin, Besley, Source Sans 3, Victor Mono, Shrikhand, Lexend, Ultra, Karla, Cutive Mono, Gelasio, Mulish, Imbue, Afacad, Castoro, Reddit Sans, Reddit Mono);
- the trend's body faces (Inter, Geist, DM Sans, Manrope, Poppins), which make any SaaS site;
- condensed capitals (Corner, Marquee, Reel, Batten's League Gothic, the trend's Homebase and Housecall Pro);
- the handwriting faces already shipped, Caveat (Commons) and Ballet (Kindly);
- any mono: nothing here should look like code.

**If a sibling takes them,** two pairings keep the same voice (a sturdy display sans with character, a plain interface sans, a marker hand for the paper):
- **Anek Latin** (variable, width 75 to 125 and weight 100 to 800; headings at 700 and width 112, wide and steady) with **Mukta** (body and interface, 200 to 800), and **Patrick Hand** for the paper;
- **Signika** (variable, weight 300 to 700 with a grade axis; drawn for wayfinding signs; headings at 700) with **Cabin** (variable, width and weight 400 to 700, with italics; body and interface), and **Playpen Sans** (variable, 100 to 800) for the paper.

## Palette

Order-pad white, a deep jade, an espresso ink, a pale jade, a ticket coral and espresso. ## Motion

Orchestrated: one moment, on the home page, around which the page is built. It is the product's promise shown rather than told. Everywhere else the site is calm, since its visitors are reading between two rushes. Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. In edit mode nothing moves: Spaka's stillness rule stops CSS animations and transitions and no script runs. So the week is clean and the paper hidden, the first tab is open, the hero card is in place, and no tab of the product page's rail is marked. Each effect is turned on by an attribute, so a new section can have it too.

- **The tidy-up** (`data-tidy` on the section, `scripts/tidy-up.js`, with Motion's `inView` and `animate`):
  - **in the markup**, the stage holds two layers rendered from the same `shifts` records:
    - **the week** (`data-tidy-week`), the product's clean week as a `<table>`: a row per person (initials, name, role, the week's hours), a column per day, and a shift chip in each worked cell (`data-shift="<key>"`). An "Open shifts" row sits on top. Its frame, header bar, grid lines and totals carry `data-tidy-chrome`, and the "Published · sent to 11 phones" line carries `data-tidy-toast`;
    - **the paper** (`data-tidy-paper`), a layer over the week with the HTML `hidden` attribute and `aria-hidden="true"`. It holds the sheet, its title ("SCHEDULE wk of 3/8"), seven pencil-drawn day zones (`data-paper-day`) and two strips of tape (`data-tape`);
    - inside the paper's day zones, a scrawled name per shift (`data-scrawl="<key>"`), tilted by a few degrees from its record's index;
    - the sticky notes (`data-note="<key>"`), each written from its shift's `paper_note`;
    - the crossed-out names (`data-clutter`), each a scribble over its shift's `paper_struck`;
    - the curved arrows (`data-clutter`), inline SVG beside a name written on another day (`paper: moved`);
    - the coffee ring (`data-ring`);
    - the texts are the template's records: the script writes none and copies none. Being `hidden`, the paper is not hidden by the stylesheet, so the page with no script shows the clean week;
  - **at `start`**, unless reduced motion asks otherwise, the script sets the before. It removes `hidden` from the paper and sets, inline, opacity 0 on every chip, the chrome and the toast. Then it waits with `inView` (amount 0.45);
  - **once in view**, it plays one sequence of about 2.9 s, then stops observing:
    - **0 to 0.6 s:** the paper holds still, to be read;
    - **0.6 s:** the crossed-out names and the arrows fade (`opacity: [1, 0]`, 0.3 s), and the tape lifts (`y: [0, -6]`, `opacity: [1, 0]`, 0.3 s);
    - **from 0.8 s**, by day then by row, 35 ms apart: each scrawl fades (`opacity: [1, 0]`, 0.15 s). Its chip starts where the scrawl was, its offset measured from the two boxes (FLIP: First is the scrawl, Last the chip's slot), and travels home: `x: [dx, 0]`, `y: [dy, 0]`, `rotate: [tilt, 0]`, `opacity: [0, 1, 1]` at times `[0, 0.2, 1]`, 0.7 s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`. There is no scale, so the text stays sharp;
    - **from 1.2 s:** each sticky note flies to the chip it asked for, with the same measuring. It goes to its center, `rotate` to 0, `scale` to the chip's width over its own, and fades out on arrival (0.8 s) as the chip fades in. "Need 1 more Sat AM!!" becomes the Saturday open shift with "2 offers", "Priya: no Sundays (class)" becomes Priya's hatched Sunday, and "Who closes Fri??" becomes Kenji's "12p–6p · closes";
    - **1.0 to 1.8 s:** the sheet straightens and gives way to the card (`rotate: [-1.5, 0]`, `opacity: [1, 0]`), while the chrome fades in (`opacity: [0, 1]`, 0.6 s);
    - **2.0 s:** the coffee ring fades last (0.5 s);
    - **2.4 s:** the toast rises at the card's corner (`y: [12, 0]`, `opacity: [0, 1]`, 0.5 s);
    - at the end the paper gets `hidden` again and every inline style is cleared, so nothing of the before stays to be measured or resized;
  - **on a phone** (below 768 px) the week shows Friday to Sunday, the busy days where the notes are. The paper's zones stack as three rows of a portrait sheet, and the measuring is the same; Monday to Thursday, their cells, chips, zones and scrawls alike, are left out below `md` by Tailwind's responsive classes, never by the script;
  - it never replays (no "Run again", which is Heats'), and nothing in it follows the pointer or the scroll;
  - `stop` cancels the animations and timers, sets `hidden` on the paper and clears every style it set;
  - with no script, under reduced motion and in edit mode, the clean week alone, its toast in place.

Under no-preference, the new panel's piece of the product rises 8 px as it appears (`@starting-style` on `translate`, 0.25 s). In edit mode the first tab, Schedule, is open, and the maker opens the others to edit them.
- **The product page's rail** (`data-tab-rail`, `scripts/tab-rail.js`, no library): a sticky row of pill links to the four chapters. The script marks the link of the chapter in the middle of the screen with `aria-current="true"` (an `IntersectionObserver`), and the stylesheet fills it in jade. Nothing moves. With no script the links still jump to their chapters. `stop` disconnects the observer and removes the marks.
- **Reveals** (`data-reveal`, `scripts/reveal.js`, Motion's `inView`): a section's main elements rise a little (`y: [16, 0]`, `opacity: [0, 1]`, 0.7 s, `ease: [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1]`). Under `data-reveal="stagger"`, children follow 70 ms apart, as in the site-scripts skill. They are used sparingly: the stories, the plans and the crew's phone, never every block.
- **Small states, in CSS under no-preference:** a shift chip lifts 1 px with its shadow on hover or focus, and buttons darken (150 ms). The pricing switch (the gallery's, which works with no script) changes the prices at once.
- **The header** is sticky on order-pad white at 95%, with a hairline under it. It does not change as you scroll.

No smooth scrolling, no page transitions, no loader, no WebGL, no split text. Library: Motion, pinned on jsDelivr (`motion@14.9.0`), under MIT. Everything else is CSS. ## Trials, demos and what stays outside

Spaka has no accounts, no checkout, no calendar and no embeds, so:
- **a trial is a request** (as built). The form collects what a person needs to open the account, and the answer says a person will text the log-in within a working hour. That is the offer: a person sets up each first week. A product with its own sign-up page links every trial button there instead (see What varies), and never embeds its form;
- **a demo is a request** for a 20-minute call at a part of the day, never a booking widget. A product that books demos with an outside tool links to it;
- **"Log in" is a link.** As built it leads to the help article that says where managers and the crew log in. The agent points it at the product's real address once the maker gives it, and never invents one;
- **the product is pictures made of HTML,** with no live demo, no video tour and no interactive sandbox beyond the tabs. The tidy-up is the tour;
- **no logos:** no customers', press, integrations' or payroll services'. The payroll export names no service until the maker says which ones the product supports;
- **no stores' badges:** the crew app is named, not linked; once the maker gives its store pages, plain pills with the store's name in text (as Pocket's are) link there;
- **no chat widget:** the phone, its hours and the support form;
- **no legal pages:** the guide tells the agent to ask the maker for their privacy policy and terms and to add them as pages then, never to write them; the footer links only pages that exist.

## Images

About 11 images. Four are generated (`pnpm template:image generate`, paid by the author's key), and the rest are free photos from Wikimedia Commons and Openverse (CC0, public domain, CC BY, CC BY-SA), credited. None has been searched yet; the search comes with step 4.

**Generated, where the style is better served than by any free photo:** the four customers' places, the stories' covers. The businesses are invented, and free photos of real cafés and kitchens carry their own names on signs, menus and cups. These are scenes of mood, with no person, no logo and no text, in one light: early morning, documentary, a 35 mm look, warm but not golden, with true colors. The later three are made with the first as `--ref`. They make the template need `"app": "0.2.0"`.
- a bakery café's counter at 6 am, trays of buns on a rack, the chairs still up;
- a taquería's kitchen line, a comal and stacked tortillas, steel shelves;
- a small ramen bar's counter with its stools, bowls stacked at the pass;
- a coffee bar's long counter, an espresso machine and cups upside down on a towel.

**Free photos:**
- **the hero:** a baker or a barista at work in a café, seen from the side or from behind, the face turned away or out of the frame. Sliding a tray onto a rack, pulling a shot or plating at a pass are the best finds. It is cropped tall to fill half the screen;
- **the product page:** one wide photo of a café or restaurant's back of house at service, under its title;
- **the start page:** a paper work schedule taped or pinned to a wall, the before the trial offers to type in;
- **the stories' work:** three or four photos of hands at work or a cook's back at the line (shaping dough, folding tortillas, pouring milk, wiping a counter), each with no recognizable face;
- **people:** no portraits. The owners and the crew in the product are initials, since a photo of a recognizable person goes only where the business would show its own people, and these customers are invented.

To read as one set, the free photos are chosen in daylight or morning light, with steel, flour, wood and white tile, no flash, no filter and no stock smiles. No filter is applied: the jade panel, the kraft-light sections and the order-pad cards pull them together.
